Method

How SkinTiers grades

SkinTiers grades every product on two axes, effect size and evidence quality, relative to other products in its category. Grades are relative and always name their comparators.

A grade is not a star rating. It answers two separate questions. How much measurable difference does the product make, and how much can we trust that the difference is real. A product can score a strong effect on weak evidence, or a small effect that has been proven well. Those are different situations, and the two axes keep them apart.

Every grade is relative to a named comparator, usually a plainer or cheaper product in the same category. We say what the product is being measured against, so "notable" always means notable versus something specific, not notable in the abstract.

Effect size

Effect size is how much measurable difference the product makes versus a plainer or cheaper comparator. It is shown as filled segments out of four.

Evidence quality

Evidence quality is how much we can trust that the effect is real. It runs from a single anecdote to repeated, well-controlled trials.

Anecdotal Anecdotal
Personal reports or single cases, with no controlled test.
Preliminary Preliminary
Early or small studies that point in a direction but are not conclusive.
Mixed Mixed
Studies disagree, or a trial shows one benefit but not another.
Solid Solid
Consistent controlled evidence, though not the strongest possible.
Gold-standard Gold-standard
Large, well-run, independent randomized trials, ideally more than one.

The two axes are read together. A strong effect backed only by anecdotes is graded differently from a modest effect proven in good trials. When a product shows a large difference but the evidence is weak, the effect bar can be high while the evidence chip stays low, and that gap is the point of the grade.
