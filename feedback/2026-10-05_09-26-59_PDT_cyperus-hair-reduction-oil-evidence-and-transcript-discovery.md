## Primary evidence and interpretation

- Mohammed 2012, *Role of Cyperus rotundus oil in decreasing hair growth*, reports 91 completers, including facial hirsutism and axillary-hair participants, comparing oil with saline. Methods specify twice-daily use over six months, with sugaring/threading every three weeks. Do not characterize all Cyperus evidence as underarm-only, but do not treat this as a robust, independently replicated PCOS-specific trial. The oil was hydrodistilled from Egyptian tubers; a commercial carrier-oil blend is not automatically equivalent.
- Mohammed 2014, PMID 24408514, reports 65 randomized participants with unwanted axillary hair and 60 completers, comparing oil, saline, and Alexandrite laser. A nonsignificant difference from laser is not proof of clinical equivalence. The accessible abstract and search excerpts were sufficient to establish design limitations; the publisher DOI open failed and the full 2014 protocol was not independently read here.
- Tirabassi 2013, PMID 23211454, randomized 24 women with mild idiopathic hirsutism to lavender/tea-tree spray or placebo, twice daily for three months. This is preliminary evidence and a different population from longstanding PCOS. It does not justify a DIY essential-oil recipe or a once-weekly schedule.
- A targeted search did not identify convincing controlled human evidence for ordinary coconut oil suppressing unwanted facial hair. Phrase this as the finding of the search, not proof that no study could exist.

The practical assessment is that possible slowing/thinning with continued treatment is distinct from removing existing hair or demonstrating permanence. No reviewed protocol supports application only every 7–10 days. That interval could refer to a marketer's claimed reduction in shaving frequency rather than application frequency; product identity is needed.
