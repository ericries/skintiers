## Findings worth preserving

- AZ15 is labeled 15% azelaic acid and 1% MSM. Supporting ingredients include panthenol, ceramide NP, hyaluronate, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, centella/madecassoside, allantoin, and a licorice-derived ingredient.
- TIRTIR is labeled 12% azelaic acid. Its supporting blend includes panthenol, ceramide NP, zinc PCA, tocopherol, betaine, and a hyaluronic-acid copolymer.
- Neither published ingredient declaration lists niacinamide, cholesterol, added parfum, or essential oils. Do not infer that either formula cannot irritate, or that the ceramide alone makes it equivalent to a complete barrier moisturizer.
- Supporting-ingredient amounts are mostly undisclosed. Ingredient order cannot establish a comparative clinical advantage or quantify how much barrier support either delivers.
- TIRTIR advertises quantified hydration/redness improvements. The accessible page text does not provide sufficient study design, population, comparator, duration, and uncertainty to assess those figures as rosacea treatment evidence or superiority over AZ15. Record them as manufacturer test claims requiring appraisal, not as either established clinical efficacy or proof that no testing occurred.
- Both list sodium hydroxide. Its presence alone does not prove inactive azelaic acid or establish either product's final pH or skin delivery. The studied Finacea formulation also uses sodium hydroxide for pH adjustment. Avoid repeating social-media chemistry conclusions without formulation data.

## Evidence and recommendation lessons

The difference between 15% and 12% is not a measured difference in benefit. Matching Finacea's nominal percentage does not establish AZ15's equivalence to the prescription gel. Formulation, delivery, tolerability, and actual use matter. No direct comparison of these two finished products was identified in the materials reviewed; this was not an exhaustive literature search.

The current local best-of list states categorically that no cosmetic azelaic product has its own clinical trial. That requires an auditable search scope and date; prefer that none was identified in the reviewed evidence. Manufacturer tests must be assessed separately from controlled disease-treatment trials. Similarly, the AZ20 page assigns solid acne evidence by borrowing from a prescription formulation at the same concentration. Distinguish confidence in azelaic acid as an ingredient from confidence in a particular cosmetic's clinical performance.

For this case, trying already-owned AZ15 is a reasonable practical preference, not an evidence-based declaration that AZ15 is superior. TIRTIR becomes attractive if its actual comfort or cosmetic wear improves consistent use. Neither fills an obvious niacinamide or hydration deficiency in the existing routine. Select one standalone azelaic product and account for the azelaic acid already present in the tretinoin compound.
