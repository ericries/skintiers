# Topical antiandrogens: evidence gaps and patient-relevant expectations

They ask which topical options have evidence, particularly antiandrogens, and what observable differences successful treatment would produce. Preserve the no-pill preference in future iterations. Do not assume that refusing pills means refusing prescription creams.

## Findings to reconcile

1. **Incorrect absolute claim about head-to-head evidence.** The clascoterone and Winlevi profiles repeatedly state that no trial compares clascoterone with established retinoids. Trifu et al. 2011, PMID 21428978, DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2133.2011.10332.x, is a randomized, double-blind pilot comparing clascoterone/cortexolone 17alpha-propionate 1% with vehicle and tretinoin 0.05%. It enrolled 77 men, treated once nightly for eight weeks. This is not strong evidence of superiority for a 48-year-old woman, and it does not quantify the benefit of adding Winlevi to her existing compounded tretinoin. Nevertheless, a comparator trial exists. Add aliases such as cortexolone 17alpha-propionate and CB-03-01 and index older trials under the modern ingredient name. The abstract reports superiority versus vehicle and describes a numerical/clinical advantage versus tretinoin; do not promote this into established comparative superiority without examining the full statistical analysis.

2. **Topical spironolactone is a coverage gap, not an evidence-free treatment.** Afzali et al., PMID 20964565, DOI 10.3109/09546634.2010.488260 (online 2010; print 2012), randomized 78 patients to 5% spironolactone gel or placebo. Total lesion count favored spironolactone (p=0.007), while the acne severity index difference was not statistically significant (p=0.052). This supports a preliminary signal, not equivalence to oral spironolactone or to clascoterone. Record the exact topical formulation and study design, and distinguish evidence for that gel from arbitrary compounded creams or multidrug formulations. A nonsignificant endpoint does not prove zero benefit. Further search results also identified PMID 34646657 and a 2021 pilot cream study; these were not fully appraised here and should be treated as follow-up leads.

3. **Avoid conflating approval with the entire mechanistic evidence base.** “Only FDA-approved topical androgen receptor inhibitor for acne” is a different claim from “the only topical shown to act on the receptor.” The latter wording on the hormonal-acne page overlooks investigational/off-label topical antiandrogens. Also, a locally acting receptor blocker still acts within the pilosebaceous unit; avoid framing receptor action and follicular action as anatomically separate alternatives.

4. **Separate acne outcomes from hair outcomes.** Winlevi has controlled evidence for acne lesions; its acne trials do not establish facial-hair reduction or correction of systemic PCOS. Eflornithine is a distinct topical option for unwanted facial hair and works through a different, non-antiandrogen mechanism. The historical Vaniqa 13.9% label describes two randomized studies with 594 women: marked improvement or better at 24 weeks in approximately 32% versus 8% with vehicle, onset as early as 4–8 weeks, and return toward baseline within eight weeks after stopping. It slows growth rather than removing existing hair. Trials excluded severe inflammatory acne. Do not infer current pharmacy availability from an archived label, or assume any compounded formula is clinically interchangeable with the studied product. Current local availability was not verified in this iteration.

5. **Translate trial outcomes into things the patient can observe.** For Winlevi, useful expectations are fewer inflammatory papules/pustules and fewer comedones over about 12 weeks. The pivotal publication reports strict clear/almost-clear success with at least a two-grade improvement in 18.4%/20.3% versus 9.0%/6.5% on vehicle. Mean incremental inflammatory-lesion reductions were 3.8 and 7.4 lesions beyond vehicle. Patients began with 30–75 inflammatory and 30–100 noninflammatory lesions and were mostly young; these counts are not forecasts for someone with a handful of residual hormonal pimples on tretinoin. The 2026 age/sex analysis reuses pivotal trial data and is not a PCOS/perimenopause-specific replication. Label-population efficacy figures differ slightly because the age inclusion differs; keep the publication and label denominators separate.

6. **Distinguish demonstrated effects, plausible effects, and untested effects.** Less oiliness or fewer cyclical flares would be welcome observations, but should not be promised from acne lesion-count endpoints. The trials do not establish scar removal, collagen stimulation, rosacea control, facial-hair reduction, or the incremental benefit on her exact compounded routine. Use weekly new-lesion counts, tenderness, and standardized photographs to assess an individual trial, rather than judging success by immediate changes in a single lesion.

7. **Keep safety proportionate and formulation-specific.** The Winlevi label supports twice-daily use and cautions about local irritation with other irritating products. It also records reversible adrenal-axis suppression in a small pharmacokinetic study; topical use is not zero systemic exposure. Large application areas and occlusion increase absorption, relevant if considering face plus back. Do not convert this into a claim that the same percentage of ordinary facial users will develop endocrine effects, or invent routine laboratory requirements absent a clinical indication. A prescriber should decide how to integrate it with her existing tretinoin and exfoliant.

## Reflection for the next iteration

The directory made the main treatment easy to find, but absolute absence-of-evidence claims were less reliable than its positive trial summaries. Prefer “no study cited here” over “no study exists,” preserve historical drug-name aliases, and connect patient concerns to distinct endpoints (acne, oiliness, hair growth, flushing). Add formulation-specific topical spironolactone and eflornithine records before expanding a generic hormonal-acne recommendation list. Retain the no-pill preference, and evaluate any proposed topical addition against the regimen already in use rather than recommending all options together.
