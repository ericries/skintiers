Hair loss is common and usually falls into one of two patterns. Androgenetic alopecia (male or female pattern hair loss) is genetically driven miniaturization of follicles, "the most common type of diffuse thinning of the hair and balding that occurs in adult males," with a similar but less severe pattern in women.[^mphl] Telogen effluvium is a different mechanism: a temporary shift of hair follicles into their resting phase after a physical or emotional shock, causing diffuse shedding 2 to 4 months later that usually reverses on its own.[^te] Skincare products sit at the edge of what can help here; most of the real treatment options are medical.

**This is a medical topic.** Patterned thinning, sudden or patchy loss, scarring, itching, or scalp redness should be diagnosed by a dermatologist before you spend money on treatment, both to confirm the type of hair loss and to rule out reversible causes like thyroid disease or iron deficiency.[^te]

## How to know you have this

Male pattern hair loss "is characterised by a receding hairline and hair loss on the top and front of the head."[^mphl] Female pattern hair loss instead causes diffuse thinning over the crown and a widening part, with the frontal hairline usually preserved; "around 40% of women by age 50 show signs of hair loss."[^fphl] Telogen effluvium looks different again: diffuse shedding across the whole scalp, often noticed on a hairbrush or in the shower, following a trigger such as childbirth, illness, major weight loss, high fever, or a stressful event a few months earlier.[^te] It can affect "up to 50% of the scalp hair" at its peak and, paradoxically, heavy shedding is a sign that new hair is pushing the old resting hairs out.[^te]

## What is happening in the skin

In androgenetic alopecia, the hormone DHT progressively shortens the growth (anagen) phase of genetically susceptible follicles and shrinks them over successive cycles, producing thinner, shorter hairs until some follicles stop producing visible hair at all.[^mphl] In telogen effluvium, a shock to the system pushes an abnormally large share of anagen (growing) hairs into telogen (resting) at once; normally about 15% of scalp hairs are resting, but a trigger can push "as many as 70%" of them into that resting phase together, so they shed in an unusually large wave a few months later.[^mphl][^te]

## What actually works

There is no OTC skincare or cosmetic product with strong evidence for regrowing hair. The one topical with real trial evidence is minoxidil, an FDA-approved medication (not a cosmetic ingredient), applied directly to the scalp; a 48-week randomized trial in men found 5% topical minoxidil produced "45% more hair regrowth than 2% topical minoxidil," both of which beat placebo.[^minox] Minoxidil is not a page on this site yet; it must be used indefinitely, since gains reverse when it is stopped, and results take months to appear. Prescription options such as finasteride and spironolactone, and procedures like PRP injections and hair transplantation, are outside this site's skincare scope. For telogen effluvium, DermNet's guidance is that the condition is "self-correcting": gentle hair handling, treating any underlying trigger (thyroid disorder, iron or vitamin deficiency), and time are the actual treatment, not a topical product.[^te] Anti-dandruff shampoos containing [[ketoconazole]] have some supporting evidence as an adjunct in androgenetic alopecia, though the mechanism is unclear.[^mphl]

## What's overhyped

Biotin gummies, "hair growth" shampoos, scalp massage tools, and most topical serums marketed for thinning hair lack trial evidence of regrowing hair. Repeated bouts of telogen effluvium can also uncover or accelerate underlying female pattern hair loss, so shedding that does not resolve in several months warrants a dermatologist visit rather than another product.[^te]

If irregular periods, acne, and excess facial or body hair accompany the hair loss in a woman, this can point to [[pcos]] rather than ordinary pattern thinning.[^mphl]

## The Evidence

Male pattern hair loss affects the large majority of men over a lifetime, "up to half of male Caucasians will experience some degree of hair loss by age 50, and possibly as many as 80% by the age of 70."[^mphl] The minoxidil dose-response data come from a 48-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter trial of 393 men, in which 5% topical minoxidil was "significantly superior to 2% topical minoxidil and placebo" on scalp hair counts, with the 5% arm showing 45% greater hair regrowth than the 2% arm.[^minox] Telogen effluvium's course, "gradually tapering back to normal over 6-9 months in most cases," is described by DermNet as generally self-limited once the trigger resolves, though "recovery may be incomplete in some cases" and repeated episodes can evolve into female pattern hair loss.[^te]
