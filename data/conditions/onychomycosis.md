Onychomycosis is a fungal infection of the nails (usually the toenails) that, as a Cochrane review puts it, "causes damage to the nail's structure and physical appearance" and, when severe, "can interfere with normal daily activities."[^oral] It is caused by the same dermatophyte fungi behind [[athletes-foot|athlete's foot]] and is managed with antifungal drugs, not skincare cosmetics; it is notably harder to treat than skin fungus because the nail plate is a barrier to topical medicines.[^oral]

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. A nail that is thickened, discolored, or crumbling has several possible causes, and a definitive onychomycosis diagnosis is confirmed by lab testing of a nail sample; treatment choice and oral antifungal safety are decisions for a clinician.

## How to know you have this

Onychomycosis mainly affects the toenails, and in the population studied in the Cochrane review the fungus was usually subungual, growing under the nail rather than on its surface, so what you see is damage to the nail's structure and appearance rather than a rash.[^oral] Because several non-fungal problems can look the same, the diagnosis is confirmed with a laboratory test of a nail sample, by culture, direct microscopy of fungal elements, or histological examination of a nail clipping, rather than by appearance alone.[^oral] A new, spreading, or bothersome nail change is worth a clinician's evaluation, especially before starting an oral antifungal.

## The Evidence

Oral antifungals are first-line: as the Cochrane review notes, "traditionally topical treatments have low success rates due to the nail's physical properties," while oral treatments "appear to have shorter treatment times and better cure rates."[^oral] Across 48 randomized trials in 10,200 people, oral terbinafine achieved clinical cure far more often than placebo (risk ratio 6.00) and, in head-to-head trials, was probably more effective than the oral azoles (moderate-quality evidence); the azoles in turn clearly beat placebo.[^oral] The graded treatments are in the tier list above. On the topical side, a separate Cochrane review of foot and nail antifungals found that topical ciclopiroxolamine has poor cure rates for nails, consistent with the poor penetration of the nail plate.[^topical]
