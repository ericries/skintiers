Intertrigo is a rash in a skin fold. A 2014 clinical review defines it as "a superficial inflammatory dermatitis occurring on two closely opposed skin surfaces as a result of moisture, friction, and lack of ventilation", worsened by perspiration, urine and faeces.[^review] The fold is the cause; *Candida* is the frequent passenger.

> **The antifungal on the shelf is not labelled for this.** An over-the-counter clotrimazole cream's Uses section reads in full: "Cures athlete's foot (tinea pedis), jock itch (tinea cruris), ringworm (tinea corporis)."[^otclabel] Those are three dermatophyte infections. Candidal intertrigo is a different organism and is not on that list. Meanwhile nystatin, the first topical the clinical review names, is prescription-only: all 50 topical nystatin records returned by openFDA's drug listing directory are ANDAs, and many of them pair it with a steroid, triamcinolone acetonide, at 100,000 USP units per gram of nystatin with 1 mg/g of triamcinolone.[^ndc]

## How to know you have this

You probably do not know, and that is the honest answer. The review says candidal intertrigo "is commonly diagnosed clinically, based on the characteristic appearance of satellite lesions", with diagnosis confirmable "using a potassium hydroxide preparation".[^review] Satellite lesions are the small separate spots scattered beyond the main red patch; a KOH prep is a bedside microscope test. Neither is something you can do at home.

What makes this worth a clinician rather than guesswork is that the fold rash has several possible causes with different treatments, and the review lists them together: a break in the skin can admit "Candida, group A beta-hemolytic streptococcus, and Corynebacterium minutissimum".[^review] Each gets different drugs. Per the review, fungal lesions are treated with "topical nystatin, clotrimazole, ketoconazole, oxiconazole, or econazole", resistant cases "require oral fluconazole therapy", streptococcal superinfection with topical mupirocin or oral penicillin, and *Corynebacterium* with oral erythromycin.[^review] Bacterial superinfection is identified by culture or Wood lamp.[^review] Treating all of those with one antifungal cream from the pharmacy is a coin flip.

General inflammation of fold skin without infection is closer to [[contact-dermatitis]]; the groin dermatophyte infection that this is most often confused with is [[tinea-cruris-jock-itch]].

## The Evidence

No trial of any treatment for this condition was read for this page, and the grades above say so. What exists here is a clinical review's list of agents and one epidemiological anchor.

On how common it is, a 2016 survey of dermatomycoses in Japan analysed 6,776 cases: 757 (11.2%) were candidiasis, and within those, candidal intertrigo was the most frequent presentation at 181 cases, 98 male and 83 female.[^japan] That is one country in one year among patients who reached a dermatologist, so it measures the shape of referred disease rather than how common fold rash is in the general population.

The steroid-antifungal combination deserves naming rather than recommending. Nystatin with triamcinolone is widely dispensed and appears repeatedly in the openFDA listing.[^ndc] Nothing read for this page compares it against nystatin alone in intertrigo, so this hub reports that the combination exists and is common, and grades neither.
