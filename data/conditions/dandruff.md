Dandruff is the mild, everyday form of [[seborrheic-dermatitis]] confined to the scalp: in DermNet's words, "Dandruff (also called 'pityriasis capitis') is an uninflamed form of seborrhoeic dermatitis on the scalp."[^dermnet] It shows up as loose white-to-yellow flakes and mild itch without the marked redness of fuller seborrhoeic dermatitis. The same Malassezia yeast, biology, and full treatment evidence are covered on [[seborrheic-dermatitis]] and ranked on [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]].

> Dandruff is not a hygiene problem and it is not curable, but it is very controllable. The flaking is driven by a normal scalp yeast, so the treatments that work are antifungal shampoo actives (ketoconazole is the best-evidenced), used regularly and left on for a few minutes before rinsing. Keratolytics like salicylic acid lift the flakes you already have but do not address the cause, so they are add-ons rather than the main event.

## How to know you have this

Dandruff reads as loose, powdery white-to-yellow flakes in the hair and on the shoulders, usually with some itch, on a scalp that is otherwise not markedly red. If the scalp is instead visibly inflamed and greasy-scaly, or the flaking spreads to the eyebrows, sides of the nose, ears, or chest, that points to fuller [[seborrheic-dermatitis]] rather than simple dandruff. Isolated thick, silvery, well-defined plaques on the scalp suggest scalp [[psoriasis]] instead. This is a self-manageable everyday complaint, not a medical diagnosis to make from a web page: persistent, severe, or spreading scalp symptoms are worth a clinician's assessment.

## What actually works

The evidence-ranked treatments are in the tier_list above and covered in full on [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]]. The pattern is simple: an antifungal shampoo active suppresses the Malassezia yeast that drives the flaking, so [[ketoconazole]], [[selenium-sulfide]], [[zinc-pyrithione]], [[ciclopirox]], and [[piroctone-olamine]] are the first-line options; [[coal-tar]] and [[salicylic-acid]] help with scale but do not target the yeast. Because dandruff recurs when treatment stops, ongoing maintenance use is normal rather than a sign of failure.

If the scalp is markedly red, weeping, or intensely itchy, or the flaking extends onto the face and ears, that is fuller [[seborrheic-dermatitis]] rather than simple dandruff and is worth a clinician's assessment.
