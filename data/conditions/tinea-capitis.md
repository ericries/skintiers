Tinea capitis, or scalp ringworm, is a dermatophyte fungal infection of the scalp and hair shafts. A 2018 systematic review calls it "the most common cutaneous fungal infection in children."[^gupta] Unlike ringworm on the body, it is treated with oral prescription antifungals, not over-the-counter creams, because the medicine has to reach the fungus growing in the hair; it is diagnosed and treated by a clinician.[^gupta]

> Scalp ringworm is a physician-treated fungal infection, most common in children, not a skincare problem. The reason it needs a prescription is that topical creams cannot clear fungus inside the hair, so treatment is an oral antifungal: usually [[griseofulvin]], [[terbinafine]], or itraconazole, with the best choice depending on the fungus. A scaly, itchy, or patchy-bald area of a child's scalp should be seen by a clinician for diagnosis, because it is both treatable and contagious.

## How to know you have this

Tinea capitis shows up on the scalp as scaly patches with broken hairs or patchy hair loss, and in some cases an inflamed, boggy swelling called a kerion. It is far more common in children than adults.[^gupta] Diagnosis and the identification of the specific fungus are done by a clinician; the review notes that sampling with a hairbrush, toothbrush, or cotton swab is "the least invasive and most efficient method" of collecting the organism, and that *Trichophyton tonsurans* "was the most common organism found in North America."[^gupta] A persistent scaly or bald patch on a child's scalp warrants medical evaluation rather than self-treatment.

## The Evidence

Treatment is with oral antifungals, and the evidence favors a species-matched choice. In the 2018 systematic review, griseofulvin and terbinafine had the highest complete cure rates (72% and 92%), and continuous itraconazole and terbinafine had the highest mycological cure rates (79% and 81%); importantly, "griseofulvin more effectively treated Microsporum infections" while "terbinafine and itraconazole more effectively cured Trichophyton infections."[^gupta] A separate meta-analysis of randomized trials comparing the two most-used options found no significant overall difference between griseofulvin and terbinafine, but the same species split: terbinafine more effective for Trichophyton and griseofulvin more effective for Microsporum.[^tey] The full drug-by-drug detail is on [[griseofulvin]] and [[terbinafine]].

Oral antifungals for tinea capitis are generally well tolerated: the review reported that "only 1.0% of children had to discontinue medication based on adverse events."[^gupta] None of this is over-the-counter skincare; it is prescription treatment chosen and monitored by a physician, which is why the species identification above matters.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a diagnosis. Scalp ringworm is treated with prescription oral antifungals and is contagious between people; a child (or adult) with a scaly, itchy, or balding scalp patch should be evaluated by a clinician rather than treated with over-the-counter products.
