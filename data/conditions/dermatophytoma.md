A dense clump of fungus sitting under the nail, and the reason it has its own page is narrow and practical: the published cure rates for nail-fungus treatments were measured in people who were screened OUT if they had one. General nail fungus is on [[onychomycosis]].

> **If you have a dermatophytoma, the headline number is not about you.** Both phase 3 trials behind [[efinaconazole]] enrolled toenails "without dermatophytomas or lunula (matrix) involvement".[^label] Those are the trials that produced its complete-cure figures of 17.8% against 3.3% and 15.2% against 5.5%.[^label] A reader with a dermatophytoma is reading a result from a population they were excluded from.

## How to know you have this

Not by looking in a mirror, usually. A 2022 review describes dermatophytomas as "a hyperkeratotic fungal mass in the subungual space, showing as dense white or yellow, typically in longitudinal streaks or patches", adding that masses "can be visualized by traditional microscopy or histology" and that dermoscopy and optical coherence tomography also give diagnostic features.[^gupta] A dense white or yellow streak running the length of the nail, rather than diffuse discolouration, is the clue worth taking to a clinician. The distinction matters because it changes what treatment is likely to work, not because it is a different infection.

## Why it resists the usual treatment

The review's explanation is mechanical rather than microbiological: "The density of fungal mass, and lack of adherence to the nail structures, as well as possible biofilm development, may play a role in the reduction in drug penetration and subsequent lack of efficacy with traditional oral therapies such as terbinafine and itraconazole."[^gupta] A drug that reaches the nail may still not reach the inside of a packed clump. [[terbinafine]] and [[itraconazole]] carry their own cases.

## The Evidence

**What is recommended, in the review's own words.** "A combination of drug treatment with mechanical or chemical debridement/avulsion has been recommended to increase efficacy."[^gupta] Recommended, not demonstrated: the sentence reports clinical practice rather than a trial result, and no effect size for the combination appears in the abstract.[^gupta]

**The topical argument is pharmacological, not clinical.** The same review says topical solutions "such as tavaborole, efinaconazole, and luliconazole may reach the dermatophytoma by both the transungual and subungual routes, due to low affinity for keratin and low surface tension", and that "Current data indicates these topicals may provide efficacy for dermatophytoma treatment without debridement/avulsion".[^gupta] Note the hedge the authors chose twice, may. Reaching a target is a mechanism; clearing it is an outcome, and the efinaconazole trials that measured outcomes excluded these nails.

**Nothing here is graded.** This page carries no ranking, because one narrative review is not enough to tier treatments against each other, and the strongest evidence in the field systematically left this group out. [[efinaconazole]], [[terbinafine]], [[itraconazole]] and [[ciclopirox]] are each graded on their own pages for ordinary onychomycosis; those grades should not be read across to a dermatophytoma.

**Resistance is part of the picture.** The review closes by noting that "The availability of improved treatments for dermatophytomas is crucial, as resistance to traditional therapies is on the increase."[^gupta] Two of the PubMed case reports indexed under this term describe dermatophytomas caused by terbinafine-resistant organisms, which is consistent with that, though neither was read for this page.[^pubmed]

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. A dermatophytoma is diagnosed by a clinician using microscopy, histology or imaging, not by a photograph, and the management described above involves prescription drugs and physical removal of nail. If a nail infection has not responded to a full course of treatment, that is a reason to ask whether the diagnosis is complete rather than to repeat the same course.
