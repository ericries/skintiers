Melanoma is a cancer that forms in melanocytes, the pigment-making cells in the skin, and it is the most serious common type of skin cancer because it is more likely than other skin cancers to invade nearby tissue and spread elsewhere in the body.[^nci] This is a medical disease diagnosed and treated by physicians, through surgery and, in more advanced cases, oncology-led treatment such as immunotherapy or targeted therapy, and it is entirely outside the scope of skincare products.[^nci]

> The AAD advises prompt evaluation by a board-certified dermatologist for any new or changing mole, because catching it early is what makes it highly treatable.[^aad]

**Medical disclaimer.** This is educational content, not medical advice, and it does not diagnose or treat any individual. Only a dermatologist can diagnose melanoma, including with a biopsy where needed. Nothing here should be used to delay evaluation of a new or changing mole.

## How to know you have this

The American Academy of Dermatology teaches the ABCDE rule for the signs of melanoma: Asymmetry (one half of a spot unlike the other), Border irregularity (a scalloped or poorly defined edge), Color variation (mixed tan, brown, or black, or patches of white, red, or blue in one spot), Diameter usually larger than about 6 millimeters, though a melanoma can be smaller when caught early, and Evolving, a spot that is changing in size, shape, or color.[^aad] The AAD advises seeing a board-certified dermatologist for any new spot, one that differs from a person's other spots, or one that is changing, itching, or bleeding.[^aad]

## Risk factors

The National Cancer Institute lists the main risk factors for melanoma as a fair complexion (skin that freckles or burns easily, light eyes, red or blond hair), exposure to natural or artificial sunlight including tanning beds, a history of many blistering sunburns, especially in childhood, having several large or many small moles, a family history of atypical moles, a family or personal history of melanoma, being White, and having a weakened immune system.[^nci] Anyone can develop melanoma, including people with darker skin.[^nci]

## Prevention and this site's role

Because melanoma is a physician-managed disease, this site's contribution is prevention and prompting early evaluation, not treatment. Because UV exposure is the main modifiable risk factor the NCI lists, sun protection is the lever most within a person's control; sunscreen is a well-studied way to reduce cumulative UV exposure, with the randomized evidence set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^nci] Most dark spots people notice day to day are benign hyperpigmentation, the common dark-spot causes covered on [[hyperpigmentation]], but a mole or spot that is changing should still be checked by a dermatologist rather than assumed to be cosmetic.
