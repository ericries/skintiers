Keratosis pilaris is a common, benign skin condition in which small, rough plugs of keratin form inside hair follicles, giving the skin a stippled, bumpy texture that some call chicken skin.[^statpearls] It shows up most on the outer upper arms, thighs, cheeks, and buttocks.[^poskitt][^statpearls] The plugging is a follicular keratinization problem rather than the inflamed, bacteria-driven comedones of [[acne]], and StatPearls lists acne vulgaris among the look-alikes clinicians rule out when diagnosing it.[^statpearls] It runs in families in a meaningful share of cases, is linked to dry skin and atopic conditions, and is medically harmless.[^poskitt][^statpearls]

## How to know you have this

These are the features clinicians use to recognize keratosis pilaris. They are for recognition, not self-diagnosis: only a clinician can make the diagnosis, and it is made by examination rather than a lab test or biopsy.[^statpearls]

- **Small, rough, flesh-colored or red bumps, not single spots.** The lesions are described as "numerous small inflammatory papules with follicular involvement," symmetric and clustered rather than scattered as isolated blemishes.[^statpearls]
- **Not painful.** StatPearls describes the typical presentation as "red bumpy skin without pain or pruritus," an asymptomatic eruption that people mostly notice by look and feel rather than discomfort.[^statpearls]
- **Symmetric, on the outer arms, thighs, and buttocks.** The classic distribution is "the extensor surfaces of the proximal upper and lower extremities," with the face, trunk, and other areas possible too.[^statpearls] A 1994 survey of diagnosed patients found the arms affected in 92% of cases, legs in 59%, face in 41%, buttocks in 30%, and eyebrows in 8%.[^poskitt]
- **Worse in winter, better in summer.** The same survey found 80% of patients noticed seasonal change, with 49% improving in summer and 47% worsening in winter, a pattern attributed to lower humidity in cold months.[^poskitt][^statpearls]
- **Often tied to dry skin and a family history.** In the same survey, 37% of patients had a personal history of atopy and 16% had associated dry skin, while 39% had another affected family member.[^poskitt] StatPearls similarly describes keratosis pilaris as most commonly associated with atopic dermatitis (covered on [[atopic-dermatitis]]) and an autosomal dominant inheritance pattern.[^statpearls]

**Medical disclaimer.** This is educational content, not medical advice, and it does not diagnose or treat any individual. Keratosis pilaris is benign, but red, bumpy skin can also signal other conditions (folliculitis, milia, or, rarely, scurvy are on the same differential diagnosis list), so a new or unusual eruption is a reasonable reason to see a clinician rather than assume keratosis pilaris.[^statpearls] Nothing here should be used to delay appropriate medical care.

## Management, not a cure

There is no cure. StatPearls states plainly that "there are no controlled clinical trials or a cure for keratosis pilaris," and that treatment, when pursued, is for cosmetic improvement rather than eliminating the condition.[^statpearls] It also tends to improve on its own with age: a 1994 survey found 35% of patients improved over time, 43% stayed about the same, and 22% got worse, with a mean age of improvement around 16.[^poskitt] The two mainstays reflect the two things going wrong at once, a plugged follicle and dry surrounding skin: keratolytics that loosen the keratin plug, and moisturizers, covered in general on [[moisturizing]], that address the dryness. Both are ongoing maintenance, not a one-time fix.

## Topical treatments with evidence

The best-controlled comparison is a split-side trial in 50 patients who completed 12 weeks of treatment, applying 10% lactic acid to one arm and 5% salicylic acid to the other, with the examining dermatologist blinded to which side got which cream.[^kootiratrakarn] Both [[lactic-acid]] and [[salicylic-acid]] significantly reduced papule counts from baseline (66% and 52%, respectively, at week 12), and lactic acid's edge over salicylic acid was itself statistically significant, a 14-point gap (P<0.05).[^kootiratrakarn] Skin conductance, an instrumented hydration measure, rose significantly on both sides, though the trial's own transepidermal water loss (TEWL) barrier measure did not improve significantly on either side, and the only reported adverse effect was mild, transient irritation.[^kootiratrakarn] Ammonium lactate lotion, sold over the counter as AmLactin, is the widely used lactic acid form for this use. Salicylic acid is oil-soluble, so alongside loosening the keratin plug it also reaches the sebum inside the follicle, the same property that makes it useful for acne.[^kootiratrakarn] How these acids compare in general, by molecule size and depth of penetration, is covered on [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]].

[[urea]] is both a humectant and, at higher concentrations, a keratolytic, and a small open-label trial of a 20% urea cream found a statistically significant improvement in skin texture after one week and four weeks of once-daily use, with high participant satisfaction and no significant adverse events.[^mccormick] That trial had no comparison group, so it shows improvement against baseline rather than against a vehicle or another treatment.[^mccormick] [[glycolic-acid]] has the least applicable evidence of the acids here: the one trial used a much higher, professionally applied concentration (50% or 70%) than a retail leave-on product, in an uncontrolled single-arm design, and while treated papules cleared progressively over 80 days, a five-year follow-up in the patients who could be reassessed found no significant difference from baseline in papule count, pigmentation, or redness.[^tian]

[[adapalene]] and [[tretinoin]] work by normalizing follicular shedding, the same mechanism thought to drive the keratin plugging in keratosis pilaris, and a clinical review lists topical retinoids as a step-up option after keratolytics.[^kodali] But neither has a controlled keratosis pilaris trial behind it; the supporting evidence is case reports of a related topical retinoid, 0.01% tazarotene, applied nightly, in which lesions were reported to fade within two weeks and resolve within four to eight.[^statpearls]

## Honest limits

None of these treatments is curative, and a recent systematic review of the keratosis pilaris literature found "no standardized approach exists for selecting one treatment over another."[^beyron] Improvement depends on continuing the treatment: the one trial that followed patients for years past a course of acid treatment found the gains had faded back toward baseline by five years.[^tian] A separate review of the AHA/BHA/urea literature for keratosis pilaris concluded that "the overall evidence base is constrained by small sample sizes, heterogeneous outcome measures, limited blinding, and short follow-up."[^dampa] Physically scrubbing or picking at the bumps is a specific way to make things worse: StatPearls names traumatic rupture of the lesions, from patients manipulating them, as a cause of scarring, and instructs that patients be counseled to avoid it.[^statpearls] Aggressive acid or retinoid use on top of already-dry, plugged skin can also cause irritation. Laser treatments and other in-office procedures for keratosis pilaris are prescriber-managed decisions outside the scope of this page.

## The Evidence

The clinical picture, prevalence, and natural history come from two convergent sources: a 1994 survey of diagnosed patients in the UK, the primary source for keratosis pilaris' body distribution, family history rate, and seasonal pattern,[^poskitt] and the StatPearls clinical reference, which adds the diagnostic and differential-diagnosis framework and states outright that no controlled trials or cure exist.[^statpearls] The treatment evidence is thin across the board. The best single trial is a split-side, assessor-blinded comparison of 10% lactic acid against 5% salicylic acid in 50 patients, which is the only comparative, blinded evidence among the options graded here.[^kootiratrakarn] A 20% urea cream and a high-concentration glycolic acid protocol each have one supporting trial, both without a control arm, and the glycolic acid trial's own five-year follow-up found the improvement had not persisted.[^mccormick][^tian] Topical retinoids carry a mechanistic rationale and case-report-level support but no controlled keratosis pilaris trial.[^kodali][^statpearls] A 2025 systematic review of the field found lactic and glycolic acids the preferred topicals but concluded no standardized treatment approach exists,[^beyron] and a separate 2025 review of the AHA/BHA/urea literature specifically attributed the field's uncertainty to small samples, inconsistent outcome measures, limited blinding, and short follow-up.[^dampa]
