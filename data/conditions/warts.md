Warts are benign growths caused by human papillomavirus infecting the basal keratinocytes of the skin.[^nma26] The useful thing to know before buying anything for one is that the two treatments everybody reaches for first, salicylic acid and freezing, did not beat doing nothing in the best plantar-wart evidence read for this page.

## How to know you have this

This site cannot tell you whether a lump on your sole is a wart, and the packaging will not either. The 40% salicylic acid plaster sold for this carries a single purpose line reading "Corn Remover Wart remover", so the same product is marketed for two different things, which is set out on [[calluses-and-corns]].[^label] The distinction matters because the evidence differs by site and by wart type, and because the one prescription option graded here is indicated for genital warts and nothing else.

What the drug listing shows is how uniform the shelf is: a search of the federal drug listing for products whose brand name contains "wart" returns 346 records, and the active is overwhelmingly salicylic acid at 17% in liquids or 40% in plasters and pads, submitted as over-the-counter monograph drugs rather than approved under their own applications.[^ndc] One record in the returned set is an unapproved homeopathic product.[^ndc]

## The Evidence

**The classics did not beat no treatment.** A 2024 Bayesian network meta-analysis pooled 33 randomised trials of plantar wart treatments. Only three were significantly superior to no treatment: a topical combination of 1% cantharidin, 20% podophyllotoxin and 30% salicylic acid, microneedling plus bleomycin, and intralesional bleomycin injection. The combination ranked highest with a SUCRA of 0.9363. Salicylic acid on its own and cryotherapy were not superior to no treatment, and the authors write that those classic modalities "may play a less important role in the clinical practice of plantar warts."[^nma24]

**In the intralesional literature, saline won.** A 2026 network meta-analysis of intralesional therapies pooled just 5 randomised trials and 295 patients, comparing agents including BCG, MMR vaccine, purified protein derivative, Candida extract, vitamin D3, interferon alpha, zinc sulphate and acyclovir. Saline had the highest probability of being the best treatment for complete response, 96%, and for partial response, 98%, and the authors conclude for "the superiority of intralesional saline." The forest plot showed no statistically significant differences among the treatment arms except saline against purified protein derivative for no response. Blister formation was higher with cryotherapy and with Mycobacterium w vaccine.[^nma26]

Read those two findings together carefully. Five trials and 295 patients is a very small base, and a placebo injection topping a ranking is not proof that nothing works. What it does establish is that the comparative evidence is thin enough that an inert comparator can come out on top, and that warts commonly resolve or respond to procedures whose active element is unclear.

**A gap worth naming.** The treatment that ranked highest for plantar warts is a compounded cantharidin combination. The one FDA-approved cantharidin product covered on this site, [[ycanth-cantharidin-topical-solution]], is indicated for molluscum contagiosum rather than warts, which is on [[molluscum-contagiosum]]. The best-ranked option in the plantar literature is therefore not something a reader can buy as an approved wart treatment in the United States.
