Molluscum contagiosum is a common viral skin infection of childhood that a 2026 pediatric network meta-analysis says "affects up to 11.5% of children".[^nma] **Bottom line:** two prescription drugs are now FDA-approved for it, but the treatment that ranked highest in the pediatric evidence is a cheap keratolytic combination, and a substantial fraction of children clear on placebo alone.

## How to know you have this

- **Firm, small, dome-shaped bumps, often in clusters.** This is a clinical diagnosis made by a clinician, and this page does not attempt to let you self-diagnose. If you are looking at new bumps on a child, the useful step is a clinician visit, not a product purchase.
- **It spreads, which is the practical problem.** The trials behind both approved drugs randomized children **by household**, which tells you how the condition is managed in practice: as something that moves between people living together, not as an isolated lesion.[^ycanth]
- **It often resolves without treatment.** In the berdazimer trials, complete clearance at week 12 on **vehicle alone** was 19.7%, 20.3% and 22%.[^zelsuvmi] Any treatment you are offered has to beat that, and the honest reading of these numbers is that waiting is a real option to discuss with a clinician.
- **See a clinician rather than self-treating if** the bumps are spreading rapidly, are inflamed, are near the eye, or occur alongside eczema. Molluscum co-occurring with atopic dermatitis is a recognized combination, which is how this page was surfaced from [[eczema-herpeticum]] and why [[atopic-dermatitis]] is worth reading alongside.

## What the evidence actually shows

**The pediatric ranking does not crown either new drug.** The 2026 network meta-analysis pooled 18 controlled trials and 2,377 children across 15 treatments. Among treatments studied more than once, three beat placebo: "salicylic acid + lactic acid 16.7% (RR 3.16, 95% CI 1.81-5.52, SUCRA 86.0%), cantharidin 0.7% (RR 3.25, 95% CI 1.39-7.58, SUCRA 81.2%), and potassium hydroxide 10% (RR 3.05, 95% CI 1.77-5.24, SUCRA 78.6%)".[^nma] Cantharidin 0.7% is the strength of the FDA-approved YCANTH.[^ycanth] Potassium hydroxide 10% has no page here yet. The analysis also warns that its highest-ranked entries overall came from single studies and "require validation", and that potassium hydroxide "demonstrated concentration-dependent efficacy and adverse events", meaning stronger is not automatically better.[^nma]

**Cantharidin, the professionally applied drug, has the larger effect.** YCANTH is cantharidin 0.7% topical solution, approved for molluscum in patients 2 years and older, and it is applied by a clinician: the label directs that "All healthcare professionals should receive instruction and training prior to preparation and administration".[^ycanth] In its two trials, complete clearance of treatable lesions at Day 84 was 46% versus 18% on vehicle (difference 29%, CI 19% to 38%) and 54% versus 13% (difference 40%, CI 30% to 51%).[^ycanth] Both trials cleared placebo decisively.

**Berdazimer, the at-home drug, did not replicate.** ZELSUVMI is berdazimer topical gel 10.3%, "a nitric oxide (NO) releasing agent" that its label says is "indicated for the topical treatment of molluscum contagiosum (MC) in adults and pediatric patients 1 year of age and older", applied once daily for up to 12 weeks.[^zelsuvmi] Across its three vehicle-controlled trials, complete clearance at week 12 was 32.4% versus 19.7% (difference 12.8%, CI 7.1% to 18.6%), 30.0% versus 20.3% (difference 9.2%, CI -0.04% to 18.4%) and 26% versus 22% (CI -5% to 14%).[^zelsuvmi] **Only the first of the three excluded no effect.** That is on the drug's own label, and it is the single most useful thing to know before buying it: the newer, more convenient option has the weaker and less consistent evidence.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. Both drugs named here are prescription-only, cantharidin is applied in a clinical setting, and molluscum in or near the eye needs specialist assessment.
