## How to know you have this

Small, soft, skin-coloured or slightly darker growths on a narrow stalk, typically where skin rubs skin: neck, armpits, eyelids, groin, under the breasts. They hang rather than sit flat, which is the feature that distinguishes them from the flat-based lesions at [[seborrhoeic-keratosis]]. They are benign.

Anything that is growing, changing colour, bleeding without being caught, or firmly attached rather than stalked is a different question and belongs to a doctor, not to this page.

> **Twenty-six over-the-counter products carry "skin tag" in their brand name, and not one is an approved drug.** A query of the FDA's drug listing directory on 2026-11-15 returns 26 such products, all human OTC drugs.[^ndc] Their marketing category is recorded as "OTC MONOGRAPH DRUG" for 23 of them and "UNAPPROVED HOMEOPATHIC" for the other three.[^ndc] Neither category means the product was reviewed and approved for removing skin tags; a monograph listing means it is marketed under FDA's general rules for a class of over-the-counter ingredients.

## What is actually in them

Of the 26 listed products, **20 declare salicylic acid** as an active ingredient.[^ndc] The rest of the field: thuja occidentalis in four products, lactic acid in two, and one each of acetic acid, asiatic acid, *Platycladus orientalis* leaf, tea tree oil and **Apis mellifera venom**, which is bee venom.[^ndc] The three homeopathic listings are the thuja and bee venom ones.

A separate query for products whose brand name contains the word acrochordon returns no matches at all.[^ndc] The medical name sells nothing.

## The medical point most people miss

Skin tags are a recognised cutaneous marker worth taking to a doctor, not for the tag but for what it may accompany. A 2017 systematic review of skin signs in diabetes and prediabetes reviewed 34 original articles and found an association with diabetes documented in "five articles with ST", meaning skin tags, alongside eight for acanthosis nigricans.[^diabetes] Its recommendation is explicit: the results "indicate a benefit of diabetes screening in individuals presenting with AN, ST or BD", and until better studies exist "it is advisable to screen individuals with the skin lesions presented by measuring their glycated haemoglobin".[^diabetes]

That is a far more useful thing to do about a crop of new skin tags than buying any of the 26 products above.

## Removal

Removal is cosmetic. The established methods are in-office procedures, snip excision, cryotherapy and electrocautery, performed by a clinician who can also confirm the diagnosis. This site has no page grading them and does not assess at-home removal, which is the subject of most of the consumer market and none of the evidence reviewed here.

**No tier list on this page.** Nothing this site grades treats skin tags, and ranking 26 unreviewed products by brand-name popularity would be worse than saying so.
