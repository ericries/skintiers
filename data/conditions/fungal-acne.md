"Fungal acne" is not acne. The name is popular shorthand for Malassezia (formerly Pityrosporum) folliculitis, an itchy eruption caused by overgrowth of the Malassezia yeast that normally lives on skin, inside hair follicles, rather than the bacterial, comedone-driven process behind [[acne]].[^saunte] It produces monomorphic, 2 to 4 mm itchy papules and papulopustules, most often on the chest, back, upper arms, neck, and face, and StatPearls describes a characteristic "cape-like distribution over the patient's shoulders, back, and neck."[^saunte][^statpearls] It is routinely mistaken for acne or bacterial folliculitis, and it does not respond to the antibiotics that treat ordinary acne because those drugs do not target yeast; StatPearls specifically flags clinical suspicion in patients diagnosed with acne "that has failed to respond or even worsened, after antibiotic treatment," and broad-spectrum antibiotic use is itself listed among the condition's predisposing factors.[^statpearls][^saunte]

## How to know you have this

These are the features clinicians use to recognize Malassezia folliculitis. They are for recognition, not self-diagnosis: it is confirmed by microscopy, culture, or biopsy, not by appearance alone.[^statpearls][^saunte]

- **Itchy, uniform bumps, not classic acne blemishes.** Saunte and colleagues describe "monomorphic, approximately 2-4 mm, erythematous itchy papules or papulopustules," adding that "comedones are absent and itching is a common symptom," a distinction acne lesions do not typically share.[^saunte]
- **Trunk and shoulders as much as the face.** Saunte and colleagues list the chest, back, upper arms, neck, and face; StatPearls describes a "cape-like distribution over the patient's shoulders, back, and neck."[^saunte][^statpearls]
- **Worse with heat, sweat, and occlusion.** Predisposing factors include "occlusion and sweating," and the condition is "more frequent in, or after visiting, tropical areas or hotter climates."[^saunte] It also tends to track with skin that runs oily, covered in general on [[oily-skin]].
- **Shows up or worsens after antibiotics, or in anyone immunosuppressed.** Predisposing factors include "immunosuppression (e.g., immunosuppressive medication, broad spectrum antibiotics, diabetes, HIV, hematological malignancies)."[^saunte]
- **Confirmed under the microscope, not by eye.** A standard KOH preparation shows yeast spores (rarely hyphae) inside the follicle, and biopsy shows follicles distended with a large number of Malassezia organisms.[^statpearls][^saunte]

**Medical disclaimer.** This is educational content, not medical advice, and it does not diagnose or treat any individual. Malassezia folliculitis is diagnosed clinically and confirmed with microscopy, culture, or biopsy, and the reviews note it is commonly misdiagnosed as acne or bacterial folliculitis.[^saunte] Nothing here should be used to delay appropriate medical care.

## Management

Because the driver is yeast rather than bacteria or clogged pores, the treatments with real evidence behind them are antifungal. Topical options with reported benefit include azole antifungals, selenium sulfide applied once daily for three days and then weekly, and propylene glycol 50% applied twice daily.[^saunte] For cases that do not clear topically, systemic treatment is used: itraconazole 100 to 200 mg daily for one to four weeks has been reported with a clinical effect of 69 to 100%, and fluconazole at the same dose and duration with a clinical effect of 80%.[^saunte][^statpearls] StatPearls notes itraconazole has more supporting evidence, but "fluconazole is often the treatment of choice secondary to its better side effect profile."[^statpearls] A 2023 position statement from the European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology's Mycology Task Force built a treatment algorithm around confirming the diagnosis first, with separate pathways for patients who are immunocompetent, immunocompromised, or have impaired liver function.[^henning]

Recurrence is common once treatment stops. Saunte and colleagues report that clinicians use maintenance therapy, weekly topical or monthly oral antifungals, to prevent it.[^saunte] Standard acne antibiotics do not treat the underlying yeast, and broad-spectrum antibiotic use is itself one of the condition's listed predisposing factors, alongside heat, sweating, and occlusion.[^saunte]

One over-the-counter acne active has laboratory evidence of activity against Malassezia specifically. An in vitro study exposed *Malassezia furfur*, *M. restricta*, and *M. globosa* to [[benzoyl-peroxide]] for one hour and found viable fungal counts "markedly decreased," with electron microscopy showing destruction of *M. restricta* cell walls at the highest tested concentration (2 mmol/L).[^okamoto] That is laboratory evidence of biocidal activity, not a clinical trial in fungal acne patients, and it sits well below the antifungal drugs above.

## The Evidence

The clinical picture, risk factors, and topical and systemic treatment regimens come primarily from a 2020 review of Malassezia-associated skin diseases, which draws together the diagnostic and treatment literature and states plainly that the condition "may be misdiagnosed as acne or bacterial folliculitis."[^saunte] The StatPearls chapter on folliculitis converges on the same clinical picture and treatment options, and adds the specific diagnostic cue clinicians use: suspecting Malassezia folliculitis when a patient's "acne" has failed antibiotics or worsened on them.[^statpearls] A 2023 position statement from the European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology's Mycology Task Force, the only source here representing a formal specialist consensus rather than a single review, adds a structured diagnostic and treatment algorithm without changing the underlying drug evidence.[^henning] The over-the-counter tier list rests on a single in vitro microbicidal study, laboratory rather than clinical evidence, and is the only benzoyl peroxide evidence specific to Malassezia in the literature.[^okamoto]
