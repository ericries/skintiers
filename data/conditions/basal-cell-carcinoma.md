Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) is a cancer that starts in the basal cells, the round cells found under the squamous cells of the epidermis, and it is the most common type of skin cancer.[^nci] It usually grows slowly and rarely spreads to other parts of the body, but left untreated it can invade and destroy the surrounding skin and tissue.[^nci] This is a medical disease diagnosed and treated by physicians, typically with surgery such as simple excision or Mohs micrographic surgery and other physician-directed options, and it is entirely outside the scope of skincare products.[^nci]

**Medical disclaimer.** This is educational content, not medical advice, and it does not diagnose or treat any individual. Only a dermatologist can diagnose basal cell carcinoma, including with a biopsy where needed. Nothing here should be used to delay evaluation of a new or changing spot.

## How to know you have this

The American Academy of Dermatology describes basal cell carcinoma as appearing in several ways, most often as a firm, round, raised growth, but also as a circle-shaped area that dips in the center, a scaly spot or patch, or a sore that does not heal or that heals and returns.[^aad] Color varies: on lighter skin tones the growth is often shiny and pink or red, while on darker skin tones it can be brown, black, or the same color as the surrounding skin.[^aad] As it grows, the AAD notes it may bleed, crust over, and break open, sometimes repeatedly.[^aad] The AAD advises anyone concerned about a spot or growth on their skin to see a board-certified dermatologist, who can examine it and determine the next step.[^aad]

## Risk factors

The National Cancer Institute lists the main risk factors for basal cell carcinoma as long-term exposure to natural or artificial sunlight, including tanning beds, a fair complexion (skin that freckles or burns easily, light eyes, red or blond hair), a history of sunburns, a personal or family history of basal cell carcinoma or other skin cancer, certain inherited gene changes, long-lasting skin inflammation, a weakened immune system, and older age.[^nci] People of all skin colors can develop basal cell carcinoma.[^nci]

## Prevention and this site's role

Because basal cell carcinoma is a physician-managed disease, this site's contribution is prevention and prompting early evaluation, not treatment. The key action for any new, changing, or non-healing spot is seeing a board-certified dermatologist rather than watching and waiting.[^aad] Because cumulative UV exposure is the main modifiable risk factor the NCI lists,[^nci] sun protection is the lever most within a person's control; sunscreen reduces UV exposure, and its randomized evidence, clearest for squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].
