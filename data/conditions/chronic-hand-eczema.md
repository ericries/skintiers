Chronic hand eczema (CHE) is a long-lasting, often relapsing eczema of the hands. A 2025 review describes it as "a highly prevalent dermatology-related occupational hazard" and, importantly, "a distinct diagnosis from atopic dermatitis," even though the two overlap.[^gupta] It is diagnosed by a clinician and, in moderate-to-severe forms, treated by one; the skincare-relevant part is the everyday barrier care and irritant avoidance underneath the medical treatment.

> The honest split here is between what skincare does and what medicine does. Everyday management of CHE, as with other eczemas, rests on barrier repair and avoiding the wet work, irritants, and allergens that trigger it, and the general evidence for that is set out on [[moisturizing]] and [[atopic-dermatitis]]. The treatments with the strongest CHE-specific approval are prescription drugs, not skincare: two agents, oral alitretinoin and topical delgocitinib, are specifically approved for CHE, and stubborn cases are a clinician's job.

## How to know you have this

CHE is eczema localized to the hands that persists or keeps returning. The 2025 review frames it as an occupational hazard that "can be quite symptomatic with implications for one's personal and professional life," and stresses that it is a distinct diagnosis from atopic dermatitis rather than simply eczema that happens to be on the hands.[^gupta] Common drivers are repeated wet work, irritants, and contact allergens, so it shows up most in hands-in-water and hands-in-gloves occupations.

## The Evidence

Everyday, non-prescription management is the same barrier-first approach used for eczema generally: frequent emollients and reducing the irritant and wet-work exposure that provokes flares, with the moisturizer evidence set out on [[moisturizing]] and [[atopic-dermatitis]]. This manages the skin; it does not resolve moderate-to-severe or treatment-resistant disease.

For that more severe end, two treatments are specifically approved for CHE by the FDA and EMA: "alitretinoin 30 mg once daily (oral) and delgocitinib 20 mg/g twice daily (topical)."[^gupta] A 2025 network meta-analysis by Gupta and colleagues pooled 5 eligible studies with 10 active comparators, using the 12- to 16-week change in the Hand Eczema Severity Index (HECSI) as the outcome, and set out to rank the newer treatments' relative efficacy; the authors note it is the first published network meta-analysis of CHE treatments.[^gupta] Because it is a network meta-analysis of only five studies, the comparisons are indirect and the evidence base is thin, so it points to which agents are approved and studied rather than settling a single best treatment.

**Medical note.** Persistent hand eczema, especially with cracking, pain, or a workplace exposure behind it, is worth a clinician's assessment. The CHE-specific drug treatments (oral alitretinoin, topical delgocitinib, and the topical corticosteroids and calcineurin inhibitors used off the back of eczema care) are physician-managed, not over-the-counter skincare.
