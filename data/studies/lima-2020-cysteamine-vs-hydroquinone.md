A head-to-head clinical trial in facial melasma found 4% hydroquinone produced a significantly greater reduction in pigmentation score than 5% cysteamine at both 60 and 120 days.[^1]

**Design:** Quasi-randomized, multicenter, evaluator-blinded clinical trial; both groups also used a tinted sunscreen (SPF 50, PPD 19) nightly alongside the assigned active.[^1]

**Population / n:** 40 women with facial melasma, followed for 120 days.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** 5% cysteamine cream applied nightly vs. 4% hydroquinone cream applied nightly.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** mMASI (modified Melasma Area and Severity Index) reduction: at 60 days, cysteamine 24% vs. hydroquinone 41% (p = 0.015); at 120 days, cysteamine 38% vs. hydroquinone 53% (p = 0.017). Blinded photographic assessment showed "up to 74% improvement for both groups, without statistically significant difference" (p = 0.087). MELASQoL (quality-of-life) scores decreased progressively in both groups, with hydroquinone showing a greater reduction at 120 days (p = 0.018). "No severe adverse effects" occurred; erythema and burning were the most important local adverse effects with cysteamine, with frequency not differing significantly between groups (p > 0.170).[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Hydroquinone outperformed cysteamine on the mMASI clinical severity score at both timepoints (statistically significant), while the two were statistically indistinguishable on blinded photographic improvement. The authors' conclusion, verbatim: "Cysteamine proved to be safe, well-tolerated, and effective, despite its inferior performance to hydroquinone in decreasing mMASI and MELASQoL in the treatment of melasma."[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Small (n=40), quasi-randomized (not fully randomized) trial in women with facial melasma over 120 days; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text, so methodological detail behind "quasi-randomized" and any subgroup data could not be verified. Provides a direct, sourced comparator for [[cysteamine]]'s real-world efficacy against the [[hydroquinone]] benchmark in [[hyperpigmentation]]/melasma treatment.
