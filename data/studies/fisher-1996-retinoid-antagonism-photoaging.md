A mechanistic study in human skin found that UVB irradiation rapidly induces the transcription factors AP-1 and NF-kappa B and downstream matrix-degrading metalloproteinases, and that pretreatment with all-trans retinoic acid substantially reduced this induction.[^1]

**Design:** Human in-vivo mechanistic study measuring metalloproteinase mRNA, protein, and activity levels, and AP-1/NF-kappa B transcription factor activation, in skin exposed to ultraviolet-B (UVB) irradiation.[^1]

**Population / n:** Human skin studied in vivo; the abstract does not state the number of subjects.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** UVB-irradiated skin versus unirradiated skin; and skin pretreated with all-trans retinoic acid applied to the skin before UVB irradiation versus (implied) untreated/vehicle skin.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Matrix-degrading metalloproteinase messenger RNAs, proteins and activities are induced in human skin in vivo within hours of exposure to ultraviolet-B irradiation (UVB)," at doses "well below those that cause skin reddening." "Within minutes, low-dose UVB upregulated the transcription factors AP-1 and NF-kappa B, which are known to be stimulators of metalloproteinase genes." "All-trans retinoic acid, which transrepresses AP-1, applied before irradiation with UVB, substantially reduced AP-1 and metalloproteinase induction."[^1] The abstract gives no fold-change or percentage figures for these effects.

**Effect size / interpretation:** Establishes the mechanism by which UV exposure activates AP-1 and NF-kappa B, which drive metalloproteinase induction; the authors propose these elevated metalloproteinases in turn degrade collagen and elastin to produce photoaging, and show that retinoic acid pretreatment blocks the pathway upstream at AP-1. This is a molecular mechanism, not a measured clinical wrinkle outcome.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** This is a molecular/mechanistic in-vivo study of UVB-irradiated human skin over a short (hours-to-minutes) timeframe, not a clinical trial of wrinkle improvement, and it reports no sample size or quantitative fold-changes in the abstract; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the Nature full text, so no additional numbers can be verified. It underlies the general mechanistic case for [[retinoids]] and [[tretinoin]] in [[anti-aging]], covered on those pages.
