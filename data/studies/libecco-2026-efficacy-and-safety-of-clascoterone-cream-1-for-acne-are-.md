A post hoc subgroup analysis of two pooled Phase 3 trials found clascoterone cream 1% produced significantly greater IGA treatment-success rates than vehicle across adolescent, adult, male, and female [[acne]] patients alike, with adults showing larger lesion-count reductions than adolescents and no male/female difference.[^1]

**Design:** Post hoc subgroup analysis, by age (adolescent vs adult) and sex (male vs female), of pooled data from two Phase 3 randomized, vehicle-controlled trials (NCT02608450 and NCT02608476) of clascoterone cream 1%.[^1]

**Population / n:** Patients ≥12 years of age with mild-to-moderate acne vulgaris. Subgroup n's given in the abstract: adolescent 287 (drug) vs 306 (vehicle); adult 330 (drug) vs 309 (vehicle); male 226 (drug) vs 252 (vehicle); female 391 (drug) vs 363 (vehicle). The adolescent+adult totals (593 + 639 = 1,232) match the male+female totals (478 + 754 = 1,232), so the pooled trial population is 1,232 patients; the abstract does not state this total directly.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Clascoterone cream 1% applied twice daily for 12 weeks vs vehicle cream applied twice daily for 12 weeks.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Treatment with clascoterone cream 1% vs vehicle resulted in significantly greater IGA treatment success rates for all subgroups: at week 12, 47/287 (16.4%) vs 12/306 (3.9%) adolescent, 77/330 (23.3%) vs 29/309 (9.4%) adult, 32/226 (14.2%) vs 13/252 (5.2%) male, and 92/391 (23.5%) vs 28/363 (7.7%) female patients achieved IGA treatment success." "Patients treated with clascoterone cream 1% vs vehicle in all subgroups also experienced significantly greater lesion count reductions." "From baseline to week 12, clascoterone cream 1% treatment resulted in significantly larger reductions in lesion counts in adult vs adolescent patients; there were no statistically significant differences between male and female patients." "Adverse events were similar across subgroups."[^1] The abstract does not report exact lesion-count figures or p-values for any of these comparisons; it only states that differences were "significant."

**Effect size / interpretation:** IGA treatment-success rate differences (drug minus vehicle), computed from the stated figures rather than quoted directly: +12.5 percentage points in adolescents, +13.9 in adults, +9.0 in males, +15.8 in females. All four subgroups favor clascoterone over vehicle by a broadly similar margin, and the authors' stated conclusion is that efficacy and tolerability hold "across the spectrum" of age and sex within the ≥12-year label population; the abstract gives no confidence intervals or p-values to gauge precision.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to the labeled population (patients ≥12 years with mild-to-moderate acne) already covered by clascoterone's Phase 3 approval trials; this analysis re-slices those same two trials by age and sex rather than testing a new population. Key limitation: it is a post hoc subgroup analysis (not pre-specified or independently powered for subgroup comparisons), and only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full J Drugs Dermatol text, so no p-values, confidence intervals, or lesion-count numbers beyond what is quoted above could be verified.
