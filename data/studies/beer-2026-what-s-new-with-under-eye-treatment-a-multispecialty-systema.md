A multispecialty systematic review of 50 studies (post-2010) organizes infraorbital treatments by modality and states which modality fits which underlying cause, without pooling any quantitative outcome data.[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review; "a systematic PubMed search was conducted in March 2025 per Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses guidelines for English-language studies published after 2010."[^1]

**Population / n:** Not human subjects directly: "fifty studies were selected and categorized by treatment type," each "evaluated for efficacy, safety, and patient suitability." No pooled patient count across the 50 studies is given.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** No single intervention vs. comparator; treatments are grouped into "injectables, energy-based devices, microneedling, peels, topicals, polynucleotides, platelet-rich plasma/platelet-rich fibrin, neuromodulators, and surgery," each mapped to a cause of infraorbital hollowing/hyperpigmentation.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Fillers remain foundational for volume loss; lasers address melanin, vascularity, and skin laxity. Microneedling and polynucleotides target texture and fine rhytides. Chemical peels and topicals are effective for superficial dyschromia. Botulinum toxin benefits orbicularis oculi hypertrophy and sequelae. Surgery is preferred for herniated fat and poor lower eyelid support."[^1] The abstract gives no percentages, p-values, or fold-changes for any modality.

**Effect size / interpretation:** No quantitative effect size is reported or poolable from the abstract; this is a narrative categorization of which modality class suits which infraorbital problem (volume, pigment, vascularity, laxity, texture, muscle hypertrophy, fat herniation), not a meta-analysis of outcome magnitude.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies broadly across dermatology, plastic surgery, and cosmetic practice for infraorbital hollowing and pigmentation, per the authors "patient-specific factors, including fat pad integrity, skin quality, and eyelid support, are critical for optimal results." Key limitation: it is a qualitative, modality-by-modality review of 50 heterogeneous studies with no pooled efficacy/safety statistics in the abstract, and the underlying included studies were not individually appraised here; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the Dermatologic Surgery full text, so no additional numbers can be verified. The topicals/peels arm bears on [[hyperpigmentation]], covered on that page.[^1]
