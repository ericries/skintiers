A randomized, double-blind trial in 50 subjects with epidermal melasma found cysteamine 5% cream produced a modestly greater reduction in melasma severity than modified Kligman's formula (MKF) over 4 months, with better tolerability.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind clinical trial.[^1]

**Population / n:** 50 subjects with epidermal melasma; mean age 34.96 (SD 6.17) years in the cysteamine group and 35.76 (SD 5.23) years in the MKF group. The abstract does not state the per-arm n (i.e., how the 50 subjects split between groups).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Cysteamine 5% cream, applied once daily with a 15-minute exposure duration, versus modified Kligman's formula (4% hydroquinone, 0.05% retinoic acid, 0.1% betamethasone), applied nightly; treatment duration was 4 consecutive months.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "The mean mMASI score after 4 months was 7.04 (2.23) in the MKF group and 6.09 (2.01) in the cysteamine group." "At both prospective evaluation points (2 months, 4 months), the percentage reduction in mMASI score was approximately 9% greater by cysteamine cream as compared to MKF, and these differences were statistically significant (P = .005 and .001 respectively)." The abstract does not state the baseline (pre-treatment) mMASI scores, so the absolute magnitude of improvement in each arm cannot be verified from it. Outcome measures also included Investigator's Global Assessment and patient questionnaires, but the abstract gives no verbatim IGA or satisfaction figures.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Cysteamine outperformed MKF by roughly 9 percentage points of mMASI reduction at both the 2- and 4-month evaluations, a difference the authors report as statistically significant (P = .005 at 2 months, P = .001 at 4 months). The authors conclude cysteamine 5% cream "showed greater efficacy as compared to MKF...with the advantage of being significantly better tolerated," though the abstract does not give specific adverse-event rates or numbers for either arm.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults with epidermal melasma treated for 4 months under direct dermatologic comparison; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text, so per-arm n, baseline mMASI values, and specific tolerability/adverse-event figures could not be verified and are not stated here. Relevant to [[hyperpigmentation]] and [[hydroquinone]], where MKF is discussed as a comparator treatment.[^1]
