A multi-biobank GWAS meta-analysis of hidradenitis suppurativa found 9 genome-wide-significant loci (5 novel) and used variant-to-gene mapping to prioritize 11 lead candidate genes (including SMPD4 and PSMA4) and eQTL/pQTL Mendelian randomization to nominate additional genes (notably MPO), alongside a reaffirmed role for KLF5.[^1]

**Design:** Genetic association study: a genome-wide association meta-analysis of [[hidradenitis-suppurativa]] combining the Million Veteran Program, UK Biobank, and FinnGen, followed by variant-to-gene mapping and expression/protein quantitative trait loci (eQTL/pQTL) 2-sample Mendelian randomization to nominate causal genes.[^1]

**Population / n:** 3,941 cases and 1,435,603 controls, pooled across the three biobanks.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** No treatment intervention: this is a case-control genetic comparison (hidradenitis suppurativa cases vs. controls) across the pooled cohorts, not a drug or ingredient trial.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "9 genome-wide significant loci, 5 of which have not been previously reported." Variant-to-gene analysis "mapped these variants to 11 lead genes," including novel candidates SMPD4 and PSMA4, reported as "differentially expressed in hidradenitis suppurativa skin lesions." eQTL/pQTL Mendelian randomization identified "additional genes likely involved in the pathogenesis," notably MPO. The abstract does not give per-locus effect sizes, odds ratios, or p-values beyond describing the loci as genome-wide significant.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** No quantitative effect sizes (odds ratios, fold-changes, or exact p-values) are stated in the abstract for individual loci or genes. The study's conclusion is qualitative: it "reaffirm[s] the previously suspected role of KLF5" and implicates pathways "related to lipid and skin barrier homeostasis, proteasome function, and oxidative stress" in hidradenitis suppurativa pathogenesis.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** This is a genetic discovery/prioritization study, not a treatment trial: it identifies candidate disease genes and pathways rather than testing any intervention, so it has no direct product or ingredient application. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Journal of Investigative Dermatology text; the abstract omits per-locus statistics (odds ratios, p-values, confidence intervals), so those numbers cannot be verified or reported here.[^1]
