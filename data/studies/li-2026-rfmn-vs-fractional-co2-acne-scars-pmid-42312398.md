The direct head-to-head behind a question two systematic reviews answered differently: does radiofrequency microneedling match a fractional CO2 laser on atrophic acne scars. It finds that it does on efficacy, and beats it on recovery.[^1]

**Design:** Prospective, randomised, split-face, evaluator-blinded, and labelled by its own authors "exploratory". Each side of each face was assigned to one modality, so every patient is their own control. Three treatment sessions at 8-week intervals.[^1]

**Population:** 33 patients enrolled, 30 completed, at the Department of Medical Cosmetology, Shanghai Skin Disease Hospital, Tongji University School of Medicine. The abstract gives no age range, no sex breakdown and no Fitzpatrick distribution, which matters because one of the findings is about pigmentation.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Radiofrequency microneedling on one facial side against fractional carbon dioxide laser on the other. No vehicle or untreated arm, which is the correct design for a comparison and means the trial cannot say how much either beats nothing.[^1]

**Primary result:** Mean scores on the Echelle d'Evaluation Clinique des Cicatrices d'Acne "decreased from 54.73 to 30.53 with RFMN and from 53.67 to 31.03 with FCO2, without a significant between-treatment difference".[^1] Physician-rated improvement was "comparable".[^1]

**Effect size:** Not reported as such. The abstract gives before and after means with no standard deviations, no confidence intervals and no p-values, so the within-modality change is visible, roughly 24 and 23 ECCA points, while the precision of the between-modality comparison cannot be judged from it.[^1]

**Where the two modalities actually differed.** Not on efficacy. "RFMN was associated with shorter erythema, pain, swelling, and scab-detachment durations and fewer post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation events", and patients reported "higher efficacy satisfaction after the first session and higher safety satisfaction at all follow-up assessments".[^1] None of those differences is quantified in the abstract.

**Generalizes to:** Facial atrophic acne scars treated over three sessions at a single Chinese dermatology hospital. The pigmentary finding is the part most likely to depend on the population, and the abstract does not describe it.

**Key limitation:** Thirty completers in a trial its authors call exploratory, with no numeric between-group comparison published in the abstract. It is strong on design and thin on reporting, so it supports a conclusion of no detected difference rather than a conclusion of equivalence, which needs a margin nobody states.

**Why it is indexed here:** [[rf-microneedling-acne-scars]] and [[fractional-radiofrequency-microneedling]] both turn on whether the parity claim holds, and the two 2026 systematic reviews disagree, one of them written from a device manufacturer's medical affairs department. This is the direct trial, and it is independent of both.
