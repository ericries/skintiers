A double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial found that a topical cocoa butter lotion did not reduce the development or severity of pregnancy stretch marks (striae gravidarum) versus a placebo lotion, the key negative result behind the [[stretch-marks]] tier list and the [[palmers-cocoa-butter-massage-lotion-stretch-marks|Palmer's cocoa butter]] review.[^1]

**Design:** Multicentre, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial (Beirut and Tripoli, Lebanon).[^1]

**Population / n:** Nulliparous women presenting for prenatal care in the first trimester; 210 enrolled, 175 (83%) completed (91 cocoa butter, 84 placebo).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** A lotion containing cocoa butter versus a placebo lotion, applied daily from the first trimester until delivery.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "There was no difference in the development of SG (45.1% versus 48.8%; P = 0.730) or the severity of SG between cases and controls. The results did not change when presence of stretch marks at enrolment or compliance with the regimen were taken into account."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** No effect. Stretch-mark incidence was essentially identical between the cocoa butter arm (45.1%) and placebo (48.8%), a trivial 3.7 percentage-point gap that was far from statistically significant (P = 0.730), and severity did not differ either. The authors concluded cocoa butter "does not appear to reduce the likelihood of developing striae gravidarum."[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to preventing pregnancy stretch marks in first-time mothers, the population and use case cocoa butter products are most marketed for. Key limitation: it tests a cocoa butter lotion for the prevention endpoint during pregnancy, so it does not speak to treating established (older) stretch marks, and only the abstract was accessed, so per-arm severity distributions and adverse events were not verified.[^1]
