A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial found that topical bacteriophage treatment significantly reduced Cutibacterium acnes and increased skin microbiome diversity in [[acne]] over 8 weeks, though the abstract reports only significance thresholds, not effect-size figures.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study; three arms (placebo, phage, phage + salicylic acid); clinic visits at baseline, week 4, and week 8 collecting safety data, VISIA imaging, lesion counts, IGA scores, and microbiome samples.[^1]

**Population / n:** 90 participants total, males and females aged 12-35 with mild to moderate acne; the abstract does not state the per-arm n.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Topical phage alone, and phage + salicylic acid (SA), each compared against placebo.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Participants in both phage and phage + SA arms showed significant improvement (P<0.05) over baseline at 8 weeks." "Phage application resulted in a statistically significant (P<0.05) decrease of C acnes on the skin compared to placebo." "Phage use over 8 weeks significantly increased microbiome diversity, a marker of long-term skin health." The abstract states only P<0.05 thresholds and gives no lesion-count percentages, fold-changes, or per-arm effect sizes.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Directionally positive for phage and phage+SA versus placebo on lesion improvement, C acnes reduction, and microbiome diversity, but the abstract supplies no magnitude (e.g., no percent reduction in lesion count or C acnes load) beyond "P<0.05," so the size of the clinical benefit cannot be assessed from this source alone.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adolescents and adults (12-35) with mild-to-moderate acne over an 8-week course; key limitation is that only the PubMed abstract was accessed (no free full text/PMC link available), so per-arm n, exact lesion-count/IGA changes, and confidence intervals underlying the P<0.05 claims could not be verified.[^1]
