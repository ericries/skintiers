A randomized placebo-controlled trial of the biologic lebrikizumab, added to topical corticosteroids, in moderate-to-severe [[atopic-dermatitis]] that ciclosporin had failed to control or that could not take ciclosporin.[^1] This is an injected prescription biologic, not topical skincare; it informs [[systemic-atopic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]].

**Design:** "a placebo-controlled, randomized phase IIIb clinical study (ADvantage; NCT05149313)". Patients were randomized 2:1 to lebrikizumab 250 mg plus topical corticosteroids, with a 500 mg loading dose at baseline and week 2, or placebo plus topical corticosteroids, both subcutaneously every 2 weeks for a 16-week induction. A 36-week open-label maintenance period followed in which **all** patients received lebrikizumab.[^1]

**Population:** 331 randomized, 220 to lebrikizumab plus steroids and 111 to placebo plus steroids. Adults and adolescents with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis "who had a history of inadequate response to CsA or for whom CsA was not medically advisable".[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Lebrikizumab plus topical corticosteroids against placebo plus topical corticosteroids. Both arms received steroids, so this measures what the biologic adds on top of topical treatment rather than against nothing.[^1]

**Primary result:** EASI 75 at week 16, "68.4% vs. 40.8%; P < 0.001".[^1] Secondary endpoints at week 16 all favoured lebrikizumab: EASI 90 "42.9% vs. 20.8%; P < 0.001", IGA 0/1 with at least a 2-point improvement "42.0% vs. 24.5%; P < 0.01", and at least a 4-point improvement in the pruritus numeric rating scale "49.9% vs. 29.7%; P < 0.05".[^1]

**Effect size:** A 27.6 percentage-point absolute difference in EASI 75, roughly 4 additional responders for every 10 treated beyond what steroids alone achieved. Worth noting in the other direction: the placebo arm reached 40.8% EASI 75 on topical corticosteroids alone, so a substantial share of the response in both arms is attributable to the steroids. Adverse events were "62.3% in the lebrikizumab plus TCS group vs. 53.2% in the placebo plus TCS group at week 16", with serious events and discontinuations low in both.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Adults and adolescents with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis who have already failed ciclosporin or cannot take it, under specialist care, using topical steroids alongside. It says nothing about milder disease, about lebrikizumab versus other biologics or JAK inhibitors, or about anything topical, which is on [[atopic-dermatitis]].

**Key limitation:** The striking week-52 figures are **not controlled**. By then every participant was receiving lebrikizumab in an open-label maintenance phase with steroids at investigator discretion, so "EASI 75 and EASI 90 further increased to 88.9% and 71.7%" describes a single-arm cohort with no comparator and no blinding.[^1] Those numbers cannot be read as the drug's effect over placebo; only the week-16 figures support that. The trial also has no active comparator, so it does not establish where lebrikizumab sits against dupilumab or the oral JAK inhibitors. Six of the authors are employees of Almirall, which markets lebrikizumab, and the remaining authors report extensive pharmaceutical consultancy.[^1]
