A randomized, blinded, split-body trial comparing two in-office ultrasound skin-tightening devices for laxity and crepiness of the upper inner arm.[^1] Both arms of the comparison are energy-based procedures performed by a clinician, not topical products, so this informs [[skin-firmness-elasticity]] only as procedural context.

**Design:** Prospective, single-center, blinded, split-body randomized trial. Each subject received a single treatment to the upper arms, with one device per side, so each person serves as their own control. Assessments compared Day 30 and Day 90 follow-up against baseline.[^1]

**Population:** "Fourteen female subjects completed the study."[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** High-intensity parallel-beam ultrasound (HIUS) versus microfocused ultrasound with visualization (MFU-V).[^1]

**Primary result:** "HIUS was as effective as MFU-V in improving arm laxity and crepiness. Investigators and subjects rated improved arm laxity/crepiness at Day 90. Most subjects were satisfied with the improvement of arm laxity/crepiness. There was no statistically significant difference in pain scores observed between the HIUS and MFU-V. The MFU-V treatment took more time to perform than the HIUS treatment."[^1]

**Effect size:** Not reportable from this abstract. No rating-scale scores, change-from-baseline figures, confidence intervals or p-values for the efficacy comparison are given, so the size of either device's improvement cannot be stated here, only its direction.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Women seeking cosmetic improvement of upper-arm laxity through an in-office energy-based procedure, delivered in a cosmetic dermatology practice. It says nothing about topical treatment of skin laxity, which is covered on [[skin-firmness-elasticity]] and [[anti-aging]].

**Key limitation:** The headline is an equivalence claim that the design cannot support. With 14 subjects, a trial is badly underpowered to detect a real difference between two active devices, and **absence of a statistically significant difference is not evidence of equivalence**. Read "as effective as MFU-V" as "no difference was detected in a very small sample" rather than as demonstrated parity. The shorter treatment time for HIUS is a throughput observation about the operator, not a patient outcome. Both author practices are cosmetic laser dermatology centers and the abstract states no funding source, so commercial interest is unresolved from the record cited here.
