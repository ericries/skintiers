A stratum-corneum lipid analysis found ceramides significantly reduced in both lesional and non-lesional skin of atopic dermatitis patients compared with age-matched healthy controls, the founding evidence for ceramide-depletion in [[atopic-dermatitis]]-prone skin discussed generally at [[ceramides]].[^1]

**Design:** Stratum corneum sheets were stripped from forearm skin with cyanoacrylate resin, lipids extracted (hexane/ethanol), stratum corneum mass measured after membrane filtration, and ceramides quantified by thin-layer chromatography as micrograms per milligram of stratum corneum.[^1]

**Population / n:** Healthy individuals, n = 65 (forearm skin); atopic dermatitis patients, n = 32-35 (the abstract states this as a range and does not disaggregate).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Observational comparison, not an intervention: lesional forearm skin vs. non-lesional (uninvolved) forearm skin in atopic dermatitis patients, each vs. forearm skin of age-matched healthy individuals.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "In the forearm skin of healthy individuals (n = 65), the total ceramide content significantly declined with increasing age. In atopic dermatitis (n = 32-35), there was a marked reduction in the amount of ceramides in the lesional forearm skin compared with those of healthy individuals of the same age. Interestingly, the non-lesional skin also exhibited a similar and significant decrease of ceramides. Among six ceramide fractions, ceramide 1 was most significantly reduced in both lesional and non-lesional skin."[^1] The abstract gives no percentage or fold-change figures for the magnitude of reduction.

**Effect size / interpretation:** No numeric effect size is stated; the abstract reports statistical significance ("marked," "significant") without magnitude. Authors' interpretation, stated directly: "these findings suggest that an insufficiency of ceramides in the stratum corneum is an etiologic factor in atopic dry skin."[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Only the PubMed abstract was accessed (the full 1991 JID paper is likely paywalled), so no additional numbers beyond those quoted above could be verified. The abstract reports no percentage or fold-change magnitude and no p-values, limiting how precisely "reduced" can be characterized. It is an observational lipid-chemistry study, not a clinical or interventional trial, and is the primary source underlying the general ceramide-depletion claims made on [[ceramides]] and [[atopic-dermatitis]].
