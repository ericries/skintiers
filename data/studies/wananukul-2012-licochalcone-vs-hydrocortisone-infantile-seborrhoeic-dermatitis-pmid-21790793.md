A head-to-head of a cosmetic moisturiser against a steroid, in babies, with each treatment on one side of the same rash. The ingredient is graded at [[licochalcone-a]] and the condition is at [[cradle-cap]]; neither is retold here.[^1]

**Design:** "randomized, prospective, split-side, double-blind".[^1] The split-side design is the reason this study is worth more than its size suggests: each infant is their own control, so severity, age and care are matched by construction.

**Population:** 75 infants between two weeks and one year old with a clinical diagnosis of infantile seborrhoeic dermatitis; 72 completed.[^1] Conducted by a paediatric dermatology division at Chulalongkorn University, Bangkok.

**Intervention vs comparator:** a moisturiser containing 0.025% licochalcone against 1% hydrocortisone, applied twice daily, in the paper's words "simultaneously with either moisturizer containing 0.025% licochalcone or 1% hydrocortisone on opposite sides of the lesion".[^1] Severity was scored on erythema, scale and crusts at day 0, 3-4, 6-7 and 14.

**Primary results:** at day 3-4 the licochalcone side had "a higher clearing rate (42%) compared to 1% hydrocortisone group (32%) (P=0.03)".[^1] At day 6-7 and day 14 the two were equal, at P=0.45 and P=1 respectively.[^1] By the end of the second week cure rates were 90% for licochalcone and 92% for hydrocortisone.[^1]

**Effect size:** a 10 percentage point difference in clearing at day 3-4, which disappears by the end of the first week. No confidence interval is given for any comparison in the abstract.

**Generalizes to:** infants under one year with seborrhoeic dermatitis, in a setting where both arms essentially worked. With roughly nine in ten clearing on either side by two weeks, the honest reading is that this condition largely resolves under twice-daily treatment of either kind, and the trial cannot separate that from spontaneous improvement, since there is no untreated arm.

**Key limitation:** the authors name sample size themselves, recording that "The small sample size was a study limitation."[^1] The more consequential gap is the missing third arm: with no vehicle-only or no-treatment side, a 90% two-week cure rate cannot be attributed to either product. PubMed records no funding or conflict-of-interest statement, and the licochalcone product is a commercial formulation that is not named in the abstract.
