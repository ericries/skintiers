A randomized placebo-controlled trial in healthy adults found that 16 weeks of low-dose oral minocycline (DFD-29, the anti-inflammatory-dosed rosacea drug) produced no detectable change in skin, gut, or vaginal microbial abundance or antibiotic resistance versus placebo, addressing a safety question for [[rosacea-papulopustular]] treatment.[^1]

**Design:** Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in healthy adults (not a rosacea-treatment efficacy trial); microbiological samples from skin (forehead), vagina, and stool were collected at baseline and weeks 4, 8, and 16 and assessed via culture and 16S rRNA sequencing.[^1]

**Population:** 60 healthy adults randomized 2:1 to DFD-29 or placebo; the microbiota-evaluable population was 38 on DFD-29 and 19 on placebo.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** DFD-29 (minocycline hydrochloride extended-release, 40 mg) once daily by mouth for 16 weeks versus matching placebo.[^1]

**Primary result:** No significant differences were detected in the abundance of microbial species in skin, stool, or vagina from baseline to week 16 between groups, no significant differences in resistance to minocycline between groups, and no significant differences in presence of opportunistic microbiota at any time point. No significant safety issues were reported.[^1]

**Effect size:** Not applicable; the trial reported an absence of statistically significant microbiome or resistance changes rather than a quantified effect size.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Healthy adults taking the anti-inflammatory 40 mg dose of oral minocycline used for [[rosacea]]; it does not address higher antibacterial-dose minocycline regimens or long-term (beyond 16 week) use.

**Key limitation:** Small microbiota-evaluable sample (38 vs 19) limits power to detect modest microbiome shifts, and the trial was conducted in healthy adults rather than people with rosacea, so it evaluates microbiome safety, not the drug's rosacea efficacy; funding and author-company ties are not stated in the abstract.
