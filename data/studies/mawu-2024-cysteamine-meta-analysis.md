A meta-analysis of 7 RCTs found cysteamine 5% cream significantly outperformed placebo for melasma severity and was statistically indistinguishable from hydroquinone 4%, with a similar adverse-effect profile.[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials, searching Europe PMC, Medline, Scopus, and Cochrane Library through June 22, 2024, using random-effects models to pool odds ratios and standardized mean differences (SMD).[^1]

**Population / n:** 7 RCTs of melasma patients treated with cysteamine 5% cream; the abstract does not state the total number of patients pooled or the individual trial sample sizes.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Cysteamine 5% cream versus placebo, and cysteamine 5% cream versus [[hydroquinone]] 4%.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Cysteamine 5% was more effective than placebo in reducing the Melasma Area and Severity Index (SMD − 0.84; 95% CI − 1.19, − 0.49, p < 0.00001, I² = 0%)" and "showed no significant difference when compared with hydroquinone 4% (SMD 0.16; 95% CI − 0.22, 0.53, p = 0.42, I² = 55%)." On adverse events, "the incidence of adverse events (AEs) like erythema, irritation, burning, itching, and dryness were higher in the cysteamine 5% than placebo. However, the incidence of these AEs was similar between cysteamine 5% and hydroquinone 4%." The abstract gives no numerical (odds ratio or percentage) figures for AE incidence.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** A moderate-to-large, low-heterogeneity effect over placebo (SMD −0.84, I² = 0%) versus a small, non-significant, moderate-heterogeneity difference from hydroquinone 4% (SMD 0.16, I² = 55%, p = 0.42): cysteamine clearly beats placebo and is not shown to differ from the active comparator, though the hydroquinone comparison carries more between-study inconsistency.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to melasma patients as a topical alternative to hydroquinone; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text, so trial-level sample sizes, follow-up durations, and quantitative AE rates could not be verified. The "no significant difference" vs. hydroquinone result also carries moderate heterogeneity (I² = 55%) and an underpowered pooled sample could mask a real difference. Relevant to [[hyperpigmentation]] and a future [[cysteamine]] ingredient page.[^1]
