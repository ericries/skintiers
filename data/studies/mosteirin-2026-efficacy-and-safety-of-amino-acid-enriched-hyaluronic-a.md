A systematic review and meta-analysis of 11 studies found that injectable hyaluronic acid plus amino acid (HA+AA) complexes outperformed conventional HA or placebo on wrinkle severity, aesthetic-improvement, dermal-thickness, and cell-viability measures, with the reported adverse-event comparison not distinguishable from chance.[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis following PRISMA guidelines, searching PubMed, EMBASE, Scopus, and the Cochrane Library; the abstract does not state whether the 11 included studies were restricted to RCTs or were a mixed design.[^1]

**Population / n:** 11 studies met the inclusion criteria, comparing injectable HA + amino acid complexes against conventional HA or placebo in adults undergoing facial rejuvenation; the abstract does not state a pooled total patient count.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** HA + amino acid injectable formulations versus conventional HA or placebo, assessed on wrinkle severity (WSRS), global aesthetic improvement (GAIS), skin thickness, and EGF-positive cell viability, with adverse events assessed for safety.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "A significant reduction in wrinkle severity was observed on WSRS following treatment (MD, 2.15; 95% CI, 2.00-2.30; p < 0.0001)." "Global aesthetic improvement demonstrated a marked enhancement at 3 months (MD = 3.13; 95% CI = 1.94-4.33; p < 0.00001)." "Dermal thickness significantly increased post-treatment (MD = -0.42 mm; 95% CI = -0.55 to -0.30; p < 0.00001)." "Cell viability improved significantly (MD = -24.00; 95% CI = -25.16 to -22.84; p < 0.00001)." On adverse events, the abstract states: "There was a statistically significant difference in adverse events (RR = 5.20; 95% CI = 0.53-50.77; p = 0.16)."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** The four efficacy outcomes (wrinkle severity, aesthetic improvement, dermal thickness, cell viability) all report p < 0.0001, favoring HA + amino acid over comparator. The adverse-event result is internally inconsistent as worded: the 95% CI (0.53-50.77) crosses 1 and p = 0.16 is above the conventional 0.05 threshold, so despite the abstract's wording ("statistically significant difference"), these figures describe a result not distinguishable from no difference in adverse-event risk.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Findings apply to injectable HA + amino acid facial-rejuvenation formulations, not topical [[hyaluronic-acid]] serums covered generally under [[anti-aging]]; only the PubMed abstract was accessed (not the full text), so the identities, designs, and risk-of-bias of the 11 underlying studies, and any pooled n, could not be verified. The authors themselves note "larger prospective studies are needed to confirm these findings."[^1]
