A multicenter RCT in high-risk Japanese neonates found that daily emulsion-moisturizer application from birth through week 32 reduced cumulative incidence of atopic dermatitis/eczema versus standard care.[^1]

**Design:** Prospective, randomized controlled trial (multicenter), testing whether protecting the skin barrier with a moisturizer during the neonatal period prevents development of AD and allergic sensitization; AD/eczema onset assessed by dermatology specialists using modified Hanifin and Rajka criteria, with cumulative incidence at week 32 as the primary outcome and serum allergen-specific IgE for allergic sensitization.[^1]

**Population / n:** 118 neonates at high risk for atopic dermatitis; 59 randomized to the moisturizer arm, 59 to control.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** An emulsion-type moisturizer applied daily during the first 32 weeks of life, versus standard care (no moisturizer) in the control group.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Approximately 32% fewer neonates who received the moisturizer had AD/eczema by week 32 than control subjects (P = .012, log-rank test)."[^1] The abstract does not state the underlying absolute incidence rates or a hazard ratio, only this relative reduction and p-value.

**Effect size / interpretation:** A statistically significant ~32% relative reduction in cumulative AD/eczema incidence by week 32 with early moisturizer use (P = .012). Secondarily, moisturizer use showed no statistically significant effect on allergic sensitization (IgE) itself, though sensitization rates were significantly higher in infants who developed AD/eczema than those who did not (odds ratio 2.86, 95% CI 1.22-6.73). In other words, sensitization tracked with eczematous skin, not with moisturizer exposure.[^1] This is an independent, separate-population (Japan) corroboration of the same primary-prevention finding as Simpson 2014 (US/UK).

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to high-risk neonates (family history of AD/allergic disease) started on daily emulsion moisturizer from birth; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full JACI text, so the specific moisturizer product/formulation, absolute incidence figures, and effect size in absolute terms (vs. the reported ~32% relative reduction) cannot be verified beyond what is quoted above. Relevant to [[atopic-dermatitis]], [[moisturizing]], and [[skin-barrier-strengthening]].
