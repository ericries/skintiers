A single-center randomized controlled trial in facial rejuvenation patients found compound lidocaine cream produced significantly lower pain scores than lidocaine-prilocaine cream, with no significant difference in adverse-reaction rates.[^1]

**Design:** Single-center, prospective randomized controlled trial comparing two topical anesthetic creams before facial rejuvenation procedures; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, so procedural/randomization details beyond what is stated below are not verified.[^1]

**Population / n:** 100 patients undergoing facial rejuvenation, randomized to compound lidocaine cream (n=47) or lidocaine-prilocaine cream (n=53).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Compound lidocaine cream vs. lidocaine-prilocaine cream, both applied topically as anesthesia before the procedure; the abstract does not state the exact formulation concentrations or application duration.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "The mean pain score was significantly lower in the compound lidocaine cream group than in the lidocaine-prilocaine cream group (2.51 ± 1.80 vs. 3.75 ± 1.74; t = -3.48, p < 0.001)." Adverse reactions occurred in 4.3% (2/47) of the compound lidocaine group vs. 11.3% (6/53) of the lidocaine-prilocaine group, a difference the abstract reports as not statistically significant (χ2 = 1.89, p = 0.169).[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** A roughly 1.2-point lower mean pain score (on the scale used in the trial) for compound lidocaine cream, a statistically significant difference (p < 0.001); the adverse-reaction rate was numerically lower with compound lidocaine cream but the between-group difference did not reach significance (p = 0.169), so no safety advantage is established.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Generalizes to adults undergoing facial rejuvenation procedures needing topical anesthesia, relevant to [[lidocaine]] and [[prilocaine]] as procedural numbing-cream ingredients. Key limitation: the abstract states the trial lacked participant blinding, and only the PubMed abstract was accessed (not the full text), so details on procedure type, application protocol, and randomization method cannot be verified here.[^1]
