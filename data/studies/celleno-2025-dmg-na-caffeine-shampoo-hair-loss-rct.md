A manufacturer-involved randomised trial of a shampoo containing dimethylglycine sodium salt and caffeine for male pattern hair loss. The design is strong for a cosmetic product and the primary endpoint is weaker than the title suggests.[^1] The condition hub is [[hair-loss-thinning]] and the active with its own page is [[caffeine]].

**Design:** 24-week, double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trial of a DMG-Na and caffeine-containing shampoo against the corresponding placebo shampoo, applied daily.[^1]

**Population:** 154 men with male pattern hair loss. The abstract gives no age range, no severity staging and no baseline hair counts.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Active shampoo versus matching placebo shampoo. A proper placebo arm, which is more than most cosmetic hair products are tested against.[^1]

**Primary result:** The primary efficacy parameter was the change in the number of hairs pulled in a hair pull test from baseline to six months. The decrease was greater on the active shampoo than placebo, reported as "-2.8 ± 1.6 vs. 0.6 ± 2.2; p < 0.001", with no adverse events reported.[^1]

**Effect size:** Hard to place, because the abstract reports the change without the baseline it is a change from. A fall of 2.8 hairs in a pull test is a shedding measure, not a growth measure. A phototrichogram substudy in 30 of the 154 subjects reported increases in hair number, hair density and the percentage of anagen hairs at six months, all at p < 0.001, but the abstract does not say how those 30 were selected or whether the substudy was randomised.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Men with male pattern hair loss willing to use a specific shampoo daily for six months. Nothing here speaks to women, to other hair loss diagnoses, or to the many caffeine shampoos that do not contain DMG-Na.[^1]

**Key limitation:** The endpoint and the authorship. A hair pull test counts hairs that come away under traction; it is a measure of active shedding rather than of regrowth, and a product can reduce it without changing how much hair is on the head. The conflict statement names three authors as employees of two related manufacturers, "Dr. Kurt Wolff GmbH & Co. KG" and "Dr. August Wolff GmbH & Co. KG Arzneimittel", both of Bielefeld.[^1] Set against that, the listed lead authors are affiliated to contract testing laboratories rather than to those firms, and the disclosure is made per author, which is the better end of the practice ranked on [[manufacturer-authored-studies-by-what-they-disclosed]].
