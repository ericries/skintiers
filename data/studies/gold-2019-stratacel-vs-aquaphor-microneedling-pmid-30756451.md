Tests whether a silicone film-forming dressing beats petrolatum ointment as post-procedure wound care after microneedling. It is the only randomised evidence behind [[stratacel-film-forming-dressing]], and the only trial a brand claim of halving downtime points to.[^1]

**Design:** Randomised, single-blinded, crossover. PubMed indexes it as both a Randomized Controlled Trial and an Equivalence Trial, which sits oddly beside the authors' own stated objective of testing whether the dressing "is superior than standard of care therapy"; publication types are assigned by indexers, not authors, so this is noted and not resolved.[^1]

**Population:** 20 subjects undergoing full-face fractional radiofrequency microneedling with the "Endymed PRO Intensif Handpiece (Endymed, Israel)". The abstract gives no age range, no skin type distribution and no sex breakdown.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Stratacel against Aquaphor, named in the abstract as "treatment (Stratacel® -Stratpharma SG, Switzerland) and control (Aquaphor® -Beiersdorf Inc, USA)", both applied immediately after the procedure and daily thereafter. Assessment at baseline, 2, 3 and 7 days, with digital and Antera 3D imaging.[^1]

**Primary results:**

- **Erythema favoured the dressing at every visit.** "Erythema at each study visit was significantly reduced with the use of the novel wound dressing (P < 0.001)."[^1]
- **So did how it felt to use.** "A statistically significant difference in favor of the innovative wound dressing also emerged with respect to the patient-rated product properties (P = 0.008), such as feel on skin, drying time and stickiness."[^1]
- **Safety was unremarkable in both arms.** "All patients healed properly without reporting adverse reactions to any of the studied products."[^1]

**Effect size:** None reported. The abstract gives P values and no point estimates, no confidence intervals and no erythema scale values, so the magnitude of the difference cannot be stated, only its direction and that the authors called it significant.[^1]

**What it does not measure, which matters commercially.** No downtime duration. The word downtime appears exactly once in the abstract, in a background sentence about rejuvenation technologies generally, and never among the objective, methods, results or conclusions.[^1] Stratacel's marketing cites this trial for the claim that it reduces downtime by half, which is set out on [[stratacel-film-forming-dressing]] and not repeated here.

**Generalizes to:** Erythema in the first week after fractional radiofrequency microneedling, in patients of one US private research site. It does not reach ablative CO2 resurfacing, which is the other procedure the dressing is sold for and which was tested separately in a five-person pilot covered on [[post-ablative-laser-aftercare]].

**Key limitation:** Twenty subjects at a single site, with no funding statement, no conflict-of-interest statement and no registration number in the PubMed record. Read alongside the fact that the product's own website carries a testimonial attributed to a doctor of the first author's surname and city, this is industry-adjacent evidence that has never been independently replicated. That does not make the erythema result wrong; it means one trial is carrying the whole claim.
