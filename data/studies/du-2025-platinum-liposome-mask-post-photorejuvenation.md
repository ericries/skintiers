A split-face trial (30 subjects) of a platinum-liposome facial mask applied after photorejuvenation, paired with laboratory work. Nine of the twelve authors are employed by the company that makes the product, and the clinical comparator is described only as "a control product".[^1]

**Design:** Two parts. First, laboratory work using "a 3D epidermal skin model and normal human epidermal keratinocytes (NHEK)" plus LPS-stimulated THP-1 cells. Then "A randomized, split-face clinical trial involving 30 subjects who underwent photorejuvenation treatment", with each participant applying the test mask to one side of the face and a control product to the other, assessed to 14 days.[^1]

**Population / n:** 30 subjects who had undergone photorejuvenation. Split-face is the design's genuine strength: each participant is their own control, which removes between-person variation.

**Intervention / comparator:** A platinum-liposome mask also containing "panthenol, dipotassium glycyrrhizate, madecassoside, and Portulaca oleracea extract" versus, in the abstract's own words, "a control product".[^1] **What that control contained is not stated**, which is the single biggest limit on reading the result.

**Primary result:** The mask "significantly increased skin hydration and reduced TEWL compared to control (p < 0.001) from Day 1 to Day 14", and subjective plus dermatological evaluation showed "statistically significant improvements in erythema, tightness, dryness, and scaliness on the treated side at all measured time points". "No adverse reactions were reported."[^1] In the laboratory arm, platinum liposomes raised stratum corneum thickness, cholesterol and ceramide chain length (p < 0.01), cut histamine-induced calcium influx in NHEK cells (p < 0.01), and combined with the soothing agents produced a greater drop in IL-8 mRNA than either alone (p < 0.01).[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Not assessable. As with other cosmetic trials of this kind, the abstract reports p-values throughout and **no means, differences or confidence intervals**, so nothing here says whether the hydration or redness changes were large enough to notice. Two further reading cautions. The trial tested the **finished mask**, so it does not isolate platinum: the formula also carries [[panthenol]] and [[madecassoside]], whose own evidence is on their pages, and the synergy claim rests on cell-culture IL-8 rather than on skin. And an unspecified control could be anything from a bland vehicle to plain water, which sets how impressive "compared to control" is.

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies at most to people recovering from photorejuvenation in the two weeks afterwards, a narrow post-procedure window rather than everyday skincare; the barrier context is on [[skin-barrier-repair]] and the procedure context on [[microneedling]]. The governing limitation is **sponsorship**: the conflict statement records nine authors, including the first, as employed by Hua An Tang Biotech Group Co. Ltd., with the remaining authors declaring none.[^1] The conclusion that the mask supports "its potential as a safe and effective option for post-procedural skincare" is therefore the manufacturer's assessment of its own product.
