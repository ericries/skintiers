What clinicians agree to do about topical steroid withdrawal when there is almost no trial evidence to go on. The condition is at [[topical-steroid-withdrawal]] and the state of its evidence base at [[choi-2026-topical-corticosteroid-withdrawal-systematic-review-pmid-42170112]]; neither is retold here.[^1]

**Design:** a modified Delphi consensus, which is structured expert opinion rather than a trial. No patients were treated and no outcome was measured. The authors' stated reason for running it is that "management guidelines for TSW do not exist".[^1]

**Population:** none. The subjects are the panel: "21 physicians and patient advocates".[^1] The abstract gives no split between the two groups and no specialty breakdown.

**Intervention vs comparator:** none. The panel "assessed 72 pharmacologic and supportive management strategies for consensus over 2 survey rounds", with the bar set at "≥70% agreement or disagreement".[^1]

**Primary result:** "Final expert consensus included 12 outcomes, primarily focused on cessation of topical and systemic corticosteroids and psychosocial support."[^1] The panel also "met consensus for dupilumab as a first-line systemic treatment", which is the single most specific recommendation here and concerns [[dupilumab]].[^1]

**Read the denominator.** Twelve of seventy-two strategies reached the threshold, so sixty did not. A Delphi exercise that agrees on one strategy in six is reporting how little settled practice exists, and that is the finding as much as the twelve are.

**One ambiguity this page will not resolve.** The threshold counted "≥70% agreement or disagreement", so a consensus outcome may be agreement that something should be done OR agreement that it should not. The abstract does not say how the twelve divide between the two, and this page does not guess.

**Effect size:** not applicable. Nothing was measured, so no estimate, interval or p-value exists anywhere in this study.

**Generalizes to:** clinicians deciding what to do next, not patients deciding what works. Consensus establishes what a panel of 21 people agreed in 2025, and it is evidence about opinion, not about skin.

**Key limitation:** the authors' own framing is that these are "initial guidelines for the management and treatment of TSW until further therapies can be investigated for future evidence-based care".[^1] Interim by design. Taken together with the systematic review linked above, which found the whole literature to be five case reports, one case series and one survey, the honest summary is that expert opinion is currently filling a space that evidence has not.
