A small add-on randomized controlled trial reported that adding a bentonite-plus-Alcea-sulphurea clay facial mask to oral azithromycin lowered acne lesion counts and severity over 8 weeks; because both arms received the antibiotic, it measures the mask's add-on effect rather than the mask alone, and the between-group advantage held only on unadjusted analysis (see limitations).[^1]

**Design:** Add-on, randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial over 8 weeks, with outcomes assessed at baseline, 4 weeks, and 8 weeks. Recruited across three sites in Sari, Iran (Bu-Ali Hospital, Tooba Clinic, and Tooba Health Center).[^1]

**Population / n:** 60 patients with moderate acne (30 per arm; 46 of 60, or 76.7%, female), recruited October 2022 to November 2023.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** A facial mask combining bentonite clay and Alcea sulphurea extract (a Traditional Persian Medicine botanical) plus oral azithromycin 250 mg, versus a placebo plus oral azithromycin 250 mg. The placebo was a gel, not a mask, so the two arms did not look or feel identical.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim numbers):** "The intervention group exhibited significant reductions in TLC (total lesion count) (55.45% ± 21.74%) and ASI (Acne Severity Index) (66.33% ± 27.15%) from baseline to 8 weeks (p < 0.001), outperforming the placebo group." The abstract also states that total lesion count and Acne Severity Index at 4 and 8 weeks were significantly lower in the mask group than the comparator (p < 0.001), and reports "43.3% being very satisfied compared to 3.3% in the placebo group (p < 0.001)."[^1]

**Effect size:** Roughly a 55% reduction in total lesion count and a 66% reduction in Acne Severity Index from baseline at 8 weeks in the mask-plus-antibiotic arm, greater than the placebo-plus-antibiotic arm on unadjusted analysis (see the age-adjustment caveat below).

**Applicability + key limitation:** Generalizes only to moderate acne, mostly female, over 8 weeks, and only as an ADD-ON to systemic azithromycin rather than a standalone topical. Key limitations stack up: both arms received the antibiotic, so the trial isolates the mask's incremental benefit rather than any effect of the mask alone; the arms differed at baseline in age (about 24 versus 19 years, p = 0.002), and once the between-group comparison was adjusted for age and education it was no longer significant (TLC p = 0.063, ASI p = 0.203), even though the unadjusted between-group comparison was (TLC p = 0.018, ASI p = 0.038) and the within-group changes quoted above were (p < 0.001); participants could not be fully blinded because the mask and placebo gel looked and felt different, which the authors flag as a possible source of bias in the patient-reported satisfaction result; the sample is small (n=60); the active is an obscure Traditional Persian Medicine botanical (Alcea sulphurea) with no independent corroborating trials; and the abstract uses its group labels ("control" versus "placebo") inconsistently.
