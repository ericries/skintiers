The randomised evidence that sunscreen affects photoaging at all. The condition is at [[sun-damage-photoaging]] and the filters at [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; neither is retold here.[^1]

**Design:** a "Randomized, controlled, community-based intervention" in Nambour, Australia, at latitude 26 degrees south, registered as ACTRN12610000086066.[^1] Four groups, crossing daily against discretionary sunscreen use with 30 mg beta-carotene against placebo.

**Population:** "903 adults younger than 55 years out of 1621 adults randomly selected from a community register".[^1] A general community sample, not a clinic population.

**Intervention vs comparator:** daily broad-spectrum sunscreen against **discretionary** sunscreen use. This is the detail most often lost: the control group was not sunscreen-free, it used sunscreen at its own discretion, so the trial measures daily versus occasional rather than sunscreen versus none.

**Primary result:** "The daily sunscreen group showed no detectable increase in skin aging after 4.5 years."[^1] Against the comparator, "Skin aging from baseline to the end of the trial was 24% less in the daily sunscreen group than in the discretionary sunscreen group (relative odds, 0.76 [95% CI, 0.59 to 0.98])".[^1] Beta-carotene "had no overall effect on skin aging".[^1]

**Effect size:** a relative odds of 0.76 with a confidence interval reaching 0.98, so the effect is real at the conventional threshold and its lower bound is close to nothing. The 24% is a relative reduction against discretionary users, not an absolute improvement in anybody's skin.

**The direction matters more than the number.** This is a PREVENTION result measured over four and a half years, between 1992 and 1996. The phrase "no detectable increase" means the daily group held steady; nothing here shows existing photoaging being reversed, and nothing here measures any outcome at 12 weeks or any other short interval.

**Generalizes to:** healthy adults under 55 in a high-UV subtropical setting, assessed by skin microtopography with blinded graders. Latitude 26 south is a stronger UV environment than most of Europe or the northern United States, so the achievable benefit elsewhere may differ in size.

**Key limitation:** the authors' own. "Some outcome data were missing, and power to detect moderate treatment effects was modest."[^1]

## Why this page exists

It was written to check a specific claim rather than to praise sunscreen. A dermatologist video carded on [[sun-damage-photoaging]] states that improvement from SPF alone can be seen "as early as 12 weeks", without naming a study. That phrase is quoted from the transcript of YouTube video YiGNHfJicgY, fetched and cached on 2026-11-18, not from any paper. This trial, the main randomised evidence on sunscreen and photoaging, does not support that: it ran for 4.5 years, its comparator was discretionary rather than no sunscreen, and its finding is that aging did not increase rather than that it improved. The retinoid half of that claim is not addressed here and remains open.
