A Cochrane systematic review of 31 randomized trials (12,579 participants) found combined oral contraceptives (COCs) consistently reduce facial acne lesion counts and severity compared with placebo.[^1]

**Design:** Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials comparing COCs to placebo or other active therapies for facial acne in women; search of CENTRAL, MEDLINE, EMBASE, POPLINE, and LILACS in January 2012, with ClinicalTrials.gov and ICTRP searched August 2011.[^1]

**Population / n:** 31 trials, 12,579 participants total; 9 of the 31 trials were placebo-controlled.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Combined oral contraceptives (six different formulations evaluated in the placebo-controlled trials, varying by progestin) versus placebo; the review also covered COC-vs-other-active-therapy trials.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "All showed COCs reduced acne lesion counts, severity grades and self-assessed acne compared to placebo." By progestin: levonorgestrel gave fewer total lesions (MD -9.98; 95% CI -16.51 to -3.45); norethindrone acetate gave better clinician global assessment (OR 1.86; 95% CI 1.32 to 2.62); norgestimate reduced total lesions (MD -9.32; 95% CI -14.19 to -4.45); drospirenone increased clear/almost clear skin (OR 3.02; 95% CI 1.99 to 4.59); dienogest gave greater percentage decreases in total lesions (MD -15.30; 95% CI -19.98 to -10.62); chlormadinone acetate gave higher odds of ≥50% papule/pustule reduction (OR 2.31; 95% CI 1.50 to 3.55).[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Authors' conclusion, verbatim: "The six COCs evaluated in placebo-controlled trials are effective in reducing inflammatory and non-inflammatory facial acne lesions. Few important and consistent differences were found between COC types in their effectiveness for treating acne." Effect sizes are heterogeneous across progestin types and outcome measures (mean-difference lesion counts vs. odds ratios for global clearance), so no single pooled effect size applies across all six formulations.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to women using COCs for facial acne, across six progestin formulations (levonorgestrel, norethindrone acetate, norgestimate, drospirenone, dienogest, chlormadinone acetate); only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Cochrane review, so trial-level risk-of-bias detail, dropout rates, and adverse-event data could not be verified. The key limitation stated in the abstract itself is that few consistent differences were found *between* COC types, meaning the review supports COCs as a class but does not establish which formulation is superior. Relevant to [[acne]]; pairs with the [[santer-2023-safa-spironolactone-acne]] trial to cover the two major hormonal options for acne.
