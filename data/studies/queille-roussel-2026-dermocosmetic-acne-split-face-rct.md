A small manufacturer-run split-face study reported that a La Roche-Posay dermocosmetic acne cream lowered acne lesion counts on the treated side versus an untreated side within 15 days, but the design (16 subjects, no vehicle control, heavy sponsor involvement) makes it exploratory, not the "strong evidence" its authors claim.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized, intra-individual, split-face study over 15 days. One hemi-face was randomized to the dermocosmetic cream (DC) and the other remained UNTREATED (no vehicle or placebo cream on the control side).[^1]

**Population / n:** 16 subjects (14 women, 2 men; mean age 29.4 plus or minus 7.7 years) with mild-to-moderate acne and fair skin (Fitzpatrick phototype II and III).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** A dermocosmetic cream containing Punica granatum (pomegranate) pericarp extract, [[salicylic-acid]], [[niacinamide]], zinc gluconate, and Aqua Posae Filiformis (a La Roche-Posay proprietary ingredient), versus the untreated contralateral hemi-face.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim significance; no effect sizes reported):** "The inflammatory lesion count significantly (p less than or equal to 0.05) decreased with DC at Day 8 and Day 15; a significant decrease of the non-inflammatory lesion count was observed at Day 15 (p less than 0.01). The total lesion count had significantly (p less than or equal to 0.05) decreased with DC at Day 8 and Day 15. Between-side differences were significant (all p less than or equal to 0.05) for all lesion types at D15." Local tolerance was good and most subjects felt their acne looked improved.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** None can be extracted. The abstract reports only p-values, no lesion-count reductions, percentages, or between-side effect sizes, so the magnitude of benefit cannot be judged. It shows a statistically detectable early reduction on the treated side, not how large or clinically meaningful it is.

**Applicability + key limitation:** Generalizes only to fair-phototype (II to III), mild-to-moderate acne over a very short 15-day window. Key limitations stack up: the sample is tiny (n=16), the comparator is an UNTREATED side rather than a vehicle cream (so simple hydration, washing, or regression cannot be separated from the actives), all authors except two are La Roche-Posay employees and a third is a paid La Roche-Posay consultant, and only the PubMed abstract was accessed. The authors call it "strong evidence," which a 16-subject, 15-day, no-vehicle, sponsor-run exploratory study does not support. For the underlying actives' own evidence, see [[salicylic-acid]], [[niacinamide]], and the [[acne]] hub.
