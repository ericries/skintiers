A randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trial in very elderly nursing-home residents found that topical 0.4% retinol lotion significantly reduced fine wrinkling versus vehicle over 24 weeks, with paired increases in glycosaminoglycan and procollagen I markers.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, left-versus-right arm comparison study.[^1]

**Population / n:** 36 elderly subjects (mean age 87 years) residing in senior facilities; molecular endpoints (glycosaminoglycan, procollagen I) were assessed in smaller biopsy subsets (n=6 and n=4 respectively, per the abstract).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Topical 0.4% retinol lotion applied to one arm versus its vehicle applied to the other arm, by study personnel, up to 3 times per week for 24 weeks.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** At 24 weeks, fine wrinkling scores changed by -1.64 (95% CI, -2.06 to -1.22) on the retinol arm versus -0.08 (95% CI, -0.17 to 0.01) on the vehicle arm (P<.001). Retinol significantly increased glycosaminoglycan expression (P=.02, n=6) and procollagen I immunostaining (P=.049, n=4).[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** The retinol arm's fine-wrinkling improvement was roughly 20-fold larger in magnitude than the vehicle arm's (-1.64 vs -0.08) with non-overlapping 95% confidence intervals, and this clinical change was accompanied by increased dermal glycosaminoglycan and procollagen I, consistent with a collagen-stimulating mechanism. The abstract gives no separate fold-change or percentage figure for the molecular endpoints beyond the p-values.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Generalizes specifically to very elderly (mean age 87), chronologically (not photo-) aged skin under supervised application; the small biopsy subsets (n=6, n=4) for the molecular endpoints limit precision on the mechanistic findings, and only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Archives of Dermatology text, so no additional numbers can be verified. Relevant to [[retinol]] and [[anti-aging]]; supports OTC retinol product claims such as on [[anua-nano-retinol-0-3-niacin-renewing-serum]].[^1]
