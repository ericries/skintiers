A manufacturer-run, split-face study reported that a cream combining [[retinaldehyde]], [[niacinamide]], and haritaki (Terminalia chebula) fruit extract, applied once daily to one half of the face after a rejuvenation procedure, visibly improved wrinkles, firmness, texture, tone, and radiance versus the untreated other half by 1 month; the comparator was an untreated hemiface rather than a plain moisturizer, and the abstract reports no numeric effect sizes.[^1]

**Design:** Monocentric, controlled, randomized, split-face (intra-individual) study over 3 months, run by Pierre Fabre Dermo-Cosmetique (the maker of the Avène brand). Subjects applied the test cream to one randomized hemiface once daily, starting after re-epidermization; the other hemiface was the untreated control. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06942403.[^1]

**Population / n:** 66 subjects who had each undergone one minimally-to-moderately invasive facial rejuvenation procedure, split as chemical peel, hyaluronic acid injection, or fractional laser (n = 22 per procedure group).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** A cream containing three actives, retinaldehyde, niacinamide, and haritaki (Terminalia chebula) fruit extract, on the randomized hemiface, versus the same person's untreated opposite hemiface. There was no vehicle or plain-moisturizer arm, so the design cannot separate the actives from the effect of applying any cream.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim):** "At 1 month (M1), wrinkles were significantly reduced, and the skin was significantly firmer and plumper, with improved texture. Skin tone homogeneity and radiance were significantly enhanced as of M1 (and M2 for skin smoothness) versus the control hemiface." Tolerance was reported as very good.[^1]

**Effect size:** Not quantified. The abstract states the improvements were "significant" versus the untreated side but reports no percentages, mean differences, or p-values, so the magnitude of benefit cannot be judged from the available text.

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults using a post-procedure cream after a peel, HA filler, or fractional laser, over 3 months. The limitations stack up: the study was designed and run by the product's manufacturer (Pierre Fabre), with several authors employed by or paid by the company; the control was an untreated hemiface rather than a plain moisturizer, so any measured benefit could reflect basic moisturization and occlusion rather than the specific actives; the three actives are bundled, so retinaldehyde's contribution cannot be isolated (its own topical evidence is on [[retinaldehyde]]); no numeric effect sizes are reported; and a split-face design with an obviously treated versus untreated side is difficult to blind. Treat this as manufacturer-sponsored, low-quantitative-detail support for a post-procedure adjunct rather than strong evidence that this combination outperforms an ordinary moisturizer. For topical anti-aging generally, see [[anti-aging]].
