A randomized, double-blind, split-face trial found topical 0.75% malassezin performed comparably to 4% [[hydroquinone]] for treating melasma, with no statistically significant difference between the two sides of the face at any visit.[^1] Both are being evaluated as topicals for [[hyperpigmentation]].

**Design:** Randomized, controlled, split-face, double-blind study; each subject's face was randomized 1:1 to determine which side got malassezin vs hydroquinone, treated twice daily for 12 weeks. Published in J Drugs Dermatol.[^1]

**Population:** 20 adult female subjects with symmetrical mild-to-moderate melasma; all 20 completed the study.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Topical 0.75% malassezin formulation (one side of face) versus 4% hydroquinone, the melasma gold standard (other side of face), applied twice daily for 12 weeks.[^1]

**Primary result:** Compared to baseline, both malassezin and hydroquinone-treated areas showed significant improvement at 12 weeks in brightening (1.85 and 1.95, P=0.027 and P=0.008), global improvement (2.2 and 2.3, P=0.004 and P=0.001), and hemi-MASI reduction (2.49 and 2.33, both P<0.001). There was no statistically significant difference between the malassezin side and the hydroquinone side at any visit.[^1]

**Effect size:** Both actives produced significant within-side improvement from baseline on brightening, global improvement, and hemi-MASI scores, but the trial found no significant head-to-head difference between sides at 12 weeks.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Adult women with mild-to-moderate melasma; the trial did not include men or severe melasma.

**Key limitation:** Small sample (n=20) with no data on funding or sponsor ties reported in the abstract; a "no significant difference between sides" result in a study this small does not establish equivalence, only that no difference was detected. Malassezin's general mechanism and hydroquinone's broader safety profile are covered on [[hyperpigmentation]] and [[hydroquinone]].
