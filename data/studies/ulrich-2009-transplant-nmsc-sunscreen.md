Prevention of non-melanoma skin cancer in organ transplant patients by regular use of a sunscreen: a 24 months, prospective, case-control study (British Journal of Dermatology, 2009). A 24-month prospective case-control study in 120 organ transplant recipients finding regular sunscreen use prevented actinic keratoses and invasive squamous-cell carcinoma (zero new invasive SCCs in the sunscreen group vs 8 in controls, p<0.01) in an immunocompromised population at dramatically elevated skin-cancer risk. This is a distinct, clinically important population (transplant patients) not addressed by the Nambour-cohort evidence already cited on sunscreen-uv-filters.md, and it also reports the expected vitamin D trade-off, connecting naturally to the Neale 2019 candidate below.
