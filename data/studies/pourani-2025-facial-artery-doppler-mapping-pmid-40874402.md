A pooled anatomy study, not a treatment study: it asks where the facial artery actually runs, how deep it sits and how wide it is, so that an injector can avoid it. It is indexed here as reference for the injectable side of [[dermal-fillers-for-acne-scars]] and has no bearing on any topical ingredient.[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis following PRISMA 2020, protocol registered on PROSPERO as CRD42024616195. Ten studies met inclusion from 1,087 records, 580 of which were screened on title and abstract after duplicates were removed.[^1]

**Population:** Normal adult populations imaged with Doppler ultrasound. The abstract gives no pooled participant or artery count, no age range and no country breakdown, so who these faces belong to cannot be stated from it.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** None. Doppler ultrasound is the measuring instrument, not a treatment, and there is no control group. The three anatomical levels compared are the lower border of the mandible, the cheilion, and the lateral nasal ala.[^1]

**Primary results:**

- **Ultrasound finds the artery almost every time.** Visualisation rates across the three levels were 100%, 99.9% and 99.8%.[^1]
- **Its course relative to the nasolabial fold varies.** Running medial to the fold (type A) was most common at 46.2%, crossing medial to lateral (type C) 22.5%, and running lateral (type B) rarest at 12.0%.[^1]
- **It gets deeper and narrower as it ascends.** Depth went from 6.27 mm at level 1 to 8.04 mm at level 3; diameter fell from 2.14 mm to 1.46 mm over the same span.[^1]

**Effect size:** Not applicable. These are prevalence and measurement estimates, not a comparison, so there is nothing to size.

**Two arithmetic points to carry, not to smooth over.** The three named course types sum to 80.7%, so roughly a fifth of arteries fall into variants the abstract does not name. And the terminal-branch figures, angular 71.8%, lateral nasal 27.9% and superior labial 5.7%, sum to 105.4%, which cannot be a partition of one artery per face; the abstract does not explain whether arteries contributed more than one terminal branch or whether studies counted differently. Anyone reusing these numbers should resolve that against the full text first.

**Generalizes to:** Pre-procedural vascular mapping before facial injection. The authors' own framing is that ultrasound mapping "can help aestheticians prevent intravascular injections, exerting safer performance during cosmetic procedures".[^1]

**Key limitation:** Ten heterogeneous imaging studies pooled without a stated participant count, and anatomy varies by population, so a pooled prevalence is a prior rather than a map of any individual face. The clinical point survives that caveat intact and is the reason to index it: the artery's position is variable enough that it has to be found in the person in front of you, not assumed.

**Why it is on this site:** It is the evidence behind the claim that ultrasound mapping reduces injection risk, which is a safety argument the site will need whenever it covers injectables. It is deliberately filed as reference beside the procedure pages and is not linked from any ingredient page, because facial vascular anatomy says nothing about what to put on skin.
