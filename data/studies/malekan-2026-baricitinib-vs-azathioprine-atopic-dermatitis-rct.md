A small head-to-head randomized trial compared two systemic treatments for moderate-to-severe [[atopic-dermatitis]], the oral JAK1/2 inhibitor baricitinib and the older immunosuppressant azathioprine, each added to topical corticosteroids, and found baricitinib cleared more patients at 12 weeks.[^1]

**Design:** Single-centre, open-label (unblinded), randomized head-to-head trial (NCT05969730); 12 weeks. The primary endpoint was the proportion reaching EASI-75 (a 75% reduction in the Eczema Area and Severity Index) at week 12.[^1]

**Population:** 40 adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, randomized 1:1 (20 per arm).[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Baricitinib 4 mg daily versus azathioprine 1.5 to 2.5 mg/kg daily, both combined with topical corticosteroids and urea 10% cream.[^1]

**Primary result:** At week 12, 65% (13/20) of the baricitinib group reached EASI-75 versus 15% (3/20) of the azathioprine group (difference 50%, 95% CI 23.9 to 76.1, P=0.002). SCORAD-75 was reached by 55% (11/20) versus 15% (3/20) (difference 40%, 95% CI 13.2 to 66.8, P=0.01).[^1]

**Effect size:** Large. The baseline-adjusted post-treatment mean EASI was 5.60 for baricitinib versus 12.61 for azathioprine (difference -7.01, 95% CI -11.86 to -2.17, P=0.006; Cohen's d = -0.93). Itch, skin-pain, quality-of-life, and mood scores improved in both arms with a non-significant trend favouring baricitinib.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis choosing between a systemic JAK inhibitor and a conventional immunosuppressant as an add-on to topical steroids; both are prescription systemic drugs, not skincare. It does not speak to first-line topical care (see [[atopic-dermatitis]]).[^1]

**Key limitation:** Small (n=40), single-centre, and open-label rather than blinded, so the subjective symptom and quality-of-life endpoints are vulnerable to expectation bias; follow-up was only 12 weeks, and the trial reported no serious adverse events or discontinuations over that short window rather than establishing long-term safety.[^1]
