A 2025 randomised investigator-blinded trial comparing a topical JAK inhibitor, tofacitinib 2% ointment, against the established topical calcineurin inhibitor tacrolimus 0.1% ointment in localized vitiligo, using one patch per side of the same patient as its own control.[^1] Neither of its two headline comparisons reached statistical significance. The condition is on [[vitiligo]]; neither drug has an ingredient page here yet.

**Design:** Prospective randomised investigator-blinded intraindividual single-centre comparative trial over 16 weeks, conducted between January and December 2024. Registered with the Clinical Trial Registry of India, CTRI/2023/12/060431.[^1]

**Population:** 30 patients contributing 60 symmetrical vitiligo patches. Eligibility was slowly spreading nonsegmental vitiligo affecting 5% or less of body surface area.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Patches randomised to topical tofacitinib 2% ointment or topical tacrolimus 0.1% ointment, twice daily for 16 weeks. The unit of randomisation is the patch, not the patient, so each arm holds 30 patches from the same 30 people.[^1]

**Primary result:** Treatment success, defined as a Vitiligo Noticeability Scale score of 4 ("a lot less noticeable") or 5 ("no longer noticeable"), was reached by 47% of tofacitinib patches (14 of 30) against 37% of tacrolimus patches (11 of 30), P = 0.60.[^1]

**Effect size:** A ten-point difference in proportions on a sample of 30 patches per arm, with P = 0.60. Median time to treatment success was 8 weeks (95% CI 4.333 to 11.667) for tofacitinib against 12 weeks (95% CI 8.301 to 15.699) for tacrolimus, P = 0.18, so that gap did not separate either and the two intervals overlap across most of their range. On the secondary repigmentation measure, 33% of tofacitinib patches (n = 10) and 20% of tacrolimus patches (n = 6) exceeded 80% repigmentation. Adverse events were fewer with tofacitinib (n = 2) than tacrolimus (n = 7); no denominator-adjusted comparison or significance test for that difference is given in the abstract. Facial lesions responded better than acral or trunk lesions with both drugs.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** People with slowly spreading nonsegmental vitiligo over no more than 5% of body surface area, treated at a single centre in India, who have symmetrical patches suitable for a split-body design. Both drugs are prescription-only, so nothing here transfers to anything bought over the counter. Segmental vitiligo, stable vitiligo and extensive disease were not studied.[^1]

**Key limitation:** The trial is not powered to show what its framing implies. Its own primary comparison returned P = 0.60 and its time-to-response comparison P = 0.18, which means the data are consistent with the two drugs performing the same, and the authors say so, concluding "comparable efficacy" with "trends toward" earlier response and better tolerability. A reader should treat the 47% versus 37% and the 8 weeks versus 12 weeks as hypothesis-generating, not as a reason to prefer tofacitinib. Two further cautions: the intraindividual design controls elegantly for patient-level confounding but means the 60 patches are not 60 independent observations, and the abstract reports no adjustment for that clustering; and the background frames the question around topical ruxolitinib's FDA approval for vitiligo, which is a different JAK inhibitor that was not tested here.[^1]
