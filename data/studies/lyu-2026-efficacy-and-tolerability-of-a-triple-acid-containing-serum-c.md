An open-label randomized controlled trial in 41 patients with mild-to-moderate acne found that adding a triple acid-containing serum (TAS) to 0.1% [[adapalene]] gel gave significantly greater lesion and IGA improvement than adapalene alone, and that stopping adapalene while continuing the serum alone as maintenance reduced rebound compared to stopping treatment entirely.[^1]

**Design:** 8-week, open-label, randomized controlled trial: a 4-week active-treatment phase followed by a 4-week maintenance phase in which adapalene gel (AG) was discontinued in both arms and the triple acid-containing serum (TAS) was continued only in the experimental group. Lesion counts, Investigator Global Assessment (IGA), microcomedone counts, skin physiological parameters, and VISIA-CR imaging were assessed at baseline and weeks 2, 4, and 8.[^1]

**Population / n:** 41 patients with mild-to-moderate [[acne]] vulgaris; the abstract states the total enrolled but does not give the per-arm n for the treatment vs. control groups.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Treatment group received TAS + 0.1% adapalene gel for 4 weeks, then TAS alone for the 4-week maintenance phase. Control group received AG alone for 4 weeks, then discontinued all treatment for the maintenance phase.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "A significantly greater reduction in noninflammatory, inflammatory, and total lesions, IGA scores, and microcomedone counts was observed in the experimental group. The control group showed rebound worsening after AG withdrawal. Improvements in skin physiological parameters were observed in both groups, although without statistical significance. Mild and transient adverse events occurred." The abstract does not state any percentage reductions, fold-changes, or p-values for these outcomes.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** No quantitative effect size is given in the abstract: only the qualitative direction ("significantly greater reduction") for lesion counts, IGA, and microcomedone counts, and a qualitative description of rebound in the adapalene-only arm after withdrawal versus continued control with TAS maintenance in the experimental arm. Skin physiological parameters improved in both groups but this change was explicitly reported as not statistically significant.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to mild-to-moderate acne patients using adapalene as first-line topical retinoid therapy, supporting a triple-acid serum as an adjunct/maintenance strategy to reduce post-retinoid rebound; it does not identify which three acids comprise the serum or its manufacturer in the abstract. Key limitations: open-label (unblinded) design, small sample (n=41) with no stated per-arm breakdown, and no numeric effect sizes or p-values reported in the abstract. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text, so these figures could not be verified beyond what is stated here.[^1]
