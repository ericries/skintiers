A randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled trial of transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS), an ear-worn nerve-stimulation device, for the facial flushing and redness of erythematotelangiectatic [[rosacea]] (ETR). It is a neuromodulation device therapy, not a topical or over-the-counter skincare treatment.[^1]

**Design:** Single-center, randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled device trial (enrollment February to August 2024, follow-up to February 2025). taVNS delivered "at a frequency of 30 Hz and a pulse width of 200 micro-s for 30 minutes per day" versus sham stimulation, 1:1, for 3 weeks of treatment and 24 weeks of follow-up.[^1]

**Population:** "Seventy-two participants (67 female individuals [93.1%]; median [IQR] age: 29.5 [24.0-36.0] years) with ETR" and a Clinician's Erythema Assessment (CEA) score of at least 2, randomized to taVNS (n=36) or sham (n=36).[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Active taVNS versus sham stimulation (SS).[^1]

**Primary result:** At 3 weeks the mean CEA score was lower with taVNS than sham: "1.56 [0.84] vs 2.47 [0.81]; mean difference, -0.92; 95% CI, -1.3 to -0.53; P < .001." taVNS also reduced anxiety (mean difference -5.42) and depression (mean difference -6.22), and adverse events were uncommon (taVNS 2 of 36 [5.6%], sham 3 of 36 [8.3%]).[^1]

**Effect size:** A roughly one-point CEA reduction versus sham, statistically strong, on a clinician-rated 0 to 4 redness scale, with the benefit said to persist through follow-up and parallel improvements in mood measures.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** The redness and flushing subtype of rosacea (ETR), as a specialized device therapy delivered and studied in a dermatology clinic; it says nothing about topical or skincare-product treatment of rosacea, covered on [[rosacea]].

**Key limitation:** A single-center trial of only 72 mostly young women with a short 3-week treatment period, testing a device not available as routine care; the primary outcome is a clinician's visual redness score rather than an objective measure, and the result needs replication in larger, multi-center trials before taVNS can be considered an established rosacea treatment. (A correction to this article was published in the same issue.)[^1]
