A human clinical study found that petrolatum (Vaseline Petroleum Jelly) accelerated, rather than delayed, stratum corneum barrier recovery after experimental disruption, contradicting the assumption that it acts as an occlusive surface membrane.[^1]

**Design:** Human in-vivo study comparing barrier recovery at petrolatum (VPJ)-treated versus untreated skin sites after acetone-induced barrier disruption, combined with tracer and ruthenium tetroxide staining to localize VPJ within the stratum corneum (SC) and visualize its relationship to intercellular membrane structures.[^1]

**Population / n:** Human volunteers; the abstract does not state the number of subjects.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** VPJ applied to acetone-disrupted skin sites versus untreated (no VPJ) disrupted sites.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "VPJ accelerated, rather than impeded, barrier recovery." "VPJ was present within the interstices at all levels of the SC, where it replaced intercellular bilayers." The abstract gives no transepidermal water loss values, percentages, or p-values.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** No numeric effect size is stated in the abstract; the finding is qualitative. Authors conclude VPJ "neither forms nor acts like an epicutaneous impermeable membrane; instead, it permeates throughout the SC interstices, allowing normal barrier recovery despite its occlusive properties," meaning it works by penetrating between corneocytes rather than sealing the surface, which explains why it speeds rather than blocks repair.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to acute experimental barrier disruption (acetone) in human skin and underlies the general case for [[petrolatum]] as a barrier-repair occlusive, covered on [[skin-barrier-repair]] and [[skin-barrier-strengthening]]. Key limitation: the abstract reports no sample size or quantitative TEWL/statistical figures, only a qualitative summary of direction and mechanism; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology text, so no additional numbers can be verified.
