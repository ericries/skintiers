A narrative review (companion "Part II" to the same authors' evidence review) that lays out ethical dilemmas raised by pediatric cosmeceutical use and gives dermatologists talking points for these patient conversations, without reporting any empirical data.[^1]

**Design:** Narrative review/commentary, not an empirical or clinical study; no methods, measurements, or statistical analysis are reported.[^1]

**Population / n:** Discusses the pediatric population broadly (children and adolescents using cosmeceutical products); the abstract states no subject count or n, consistent with this being a review rather than a study of a cohort.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Not applicable: the article does not test an intervention against a comparator; it discusses categories of product use and marketing practice.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Ethical concerns discussed in this review include the marketing of cosmeceutical products directly to children, the influence of social media and trends related to skincare, the use of anti-aging ingredients by children (who have not yet manifested any aging signs), and the misuse of cosmeceutical products to lighten one's complexion in childhood. We also discuss several related patient talking points for dermatologists to employ with young patients interested in cosmeceutical products." No percentages, counts, or other quantitative figures are given in the abstract.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Not applicable: this is a qualitative/ethical-commentary review, so there is no effect size to report; its contribution is a framework of four ethical concern areas plus physician communication guidance.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to dermatologists and caregivers navigating pediatric [[anti-aging]] ingredient use and cosmetic skin-lightening/[[hyperpigmentation]] product misuse in minors; only the PubMed abstract was accessed (not the full text), and as an opinion/narrative review it carries no empirical weight of its own. It should be read alongside the authors' companion Part I evidence review (PMID 39925031) rather than as a standalone data source.[^1]
