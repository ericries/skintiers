A 12-week randomized, double-blinded, split-body, vehicle-controlled trial found that a proprietary body firming serum (BFS) produced significant improvements in firmness, texture, and crepiness on the distal thighs and back of hands versus vehicle, relevant to the general case for topical [[anti-aging]] care of body skin.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized, double-blinded, split-body, vehicle-controlled trial over 12 weeks; each participant's body served as its own control, with BFS applied to one side and vehicle to the contralateral side.[^1]

**Population / n:** Adults with mild-to-moderate skin crepiness, split into two separately enrolled groups: distal thighs (n=11) and back of hands (n=12).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Body firming serum (BFS) applied twice daily versus vehicle applied twice daily to the contralateral side.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** For the distal thighs, "significant improvements" in firmness, texture, crepiness, and fine lines/wrinkles were reported with BFS versus vehicle and versus baseline at weeks 4 and 12; fine lines/wrinkles improved with vehicle versus baseline at week 8, and texture at week 12. For the back of hands, firmness and texture significantly improved versus baseline at weeks 8 and 12, and crepiness improved significantly at weeks 2 and 12; firmness and crepiness also improved with vehicle versus baseline at week 12. Participants "reported higher satisfaction with BFS than vehicle through week 12." Exploratory histology "showed induction of collagen and elastic fibers." The abstract does not give exact percentage changes or p-values for any of these comparisons.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** The abstract reports statistical significance in qualitative terms only ("significant," "significantly") without numeric effect sizes, percentage changes, or p-values, so the magnitude of improvement cannot be quantified from this source. The histologic finding of collagen and elastic fiber induction is described as exploratory, not a powered outcome.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults with mild-to-moderate body skin crepiness on the thighs and hands specifically; the trial is small (n=11 and n=12 per body site) and industry-sponsored-style split-body design, and BFS's ingredient composition is not disclosed in the abstract. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text of the Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology article, so no quantitative figures beyond what is quoted above could be verified.[^1]
