Two identically-designed phase 3 RCTs found once-daily [[brimonidine]] gel 0.5% significantly more effective than vehicle at reducing facial erythema of [[rosacea-erythematotelangiectatic|rosacea]], with a significant difference detectable within 30 minutes of the first dose.[^1]

**Design:** Two randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled phase 3 studies of identical design.[^1]

**Population / n:** Subjects with moderate to severe facial erythema of rosacea; the abstract does not state the number of subjects enrolled in either study.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Topical brimonidine tartrate gel 0.5% vs. vehicle gel, applied once daily for 4 weeks, randomized 1:1, followed by a 4-week follow-up phase.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** Evaluations used the Clinician's Erythema Assessment and Patient's Self-Assessment. "Topical brimonidine tartrate gel 0.5% was significantly more efficacious than vehicle gel throughout 12 hours on days 1, 15, and 29, with significant difference observed as early as 30 minutes after the first application on day 1 (all P<.001)." The abstract reports no responder percentages or other effect-size figures for either study.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Efficacy is reported only as statistical significance (P<.001) at multiple within-day timepoints on days 1, 15, and 29, not as a quantified magnitude of erythema reduction; safety was favorable, with "slightly higher incidence of adverse events" for brimonidine than vehicle, described as mostly "dermatological, mild, and transient," with no tachyphylaxis, rebound, or aggravation of other disease signs observed.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults with moderate-to-severe facial erythema of rosacea treated for 4 weeks; only the PubMed abstract was accessed (not the full J Drugs Dermatol text), and it discloses no sample size, responder rate, or other quantitative effect-size figures, so the clinical magnitude of benefit cannot be verified from this source alone.
