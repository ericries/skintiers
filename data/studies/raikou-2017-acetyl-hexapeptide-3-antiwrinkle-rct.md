A small four-arm randomized controlled trial in 24 healthy volunteers found scattered statistically significant differences in skin microtopography and transepidermal water loss between groups using acetyl hexapeptide-3, tripeptide-10 citrulline, their combination, or neither, over 60 days, with no effect-size or mean figures reported in the abstract.[^1]

**Design:** Prospective, randomized, controlled trial, 4 parallel arms, 60-day treatment period, with measurements at 20, 40, and 60 days.[^1]

**Population / n:** 24 healthy volunteers total, randomized to Group G1 (acetyl hexapeptide-3 + tripeptide-10 citrulline combination), Group G2 (tripeptide-10 citrulline alone), Group G3 (acetyl hexapeptide-3 alone), or Group G4 (neither peptide); the abstract does not state per-group n.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Combination peptide treatment (G1) vs. each single peptide alone (G2, G3) vs. a no-peptide control (G4); outcomes were skin microtopography parameters cR2 and cR3 (measured by skin visioscan) and transepidermal water loss (TEWL, measured by tewameter).[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "After 20 days, the measurements between G1 and G2 groups (cR2 P=.045, cR3 P=.044), G2 and G3 groups (cR2 P=.017, cR3 P=.017), G3 and G4 groups (CR2 P=.022), and G2 and G4 groups (cR3 P=.028) from baseline were significant. After 60 days, measurements between groups G1 and G3 (cR2 P=.016, cR3 P=.025), groups G2 and G3 (cR2 P=.044, cR3= P=.044), and groups G1 and G4 (cR2 P=.025) were significant. After 20 days, changes in TEWL between groups G1 and G3 (P=.03), groups G2 and G3 (P=.045), and groups G3 and G4 (P=.025) were significant. After 40 days, changes between groups G2 and G3 (P=.028) and groups G3 and G4 (P=.01) from baseline were significant."[^1] The abstract reports only these pairwise p-values; it gives no mean values, percentage changes, or confidence intervals for cR2, cR3, or TEWL.

**Effect size / interpretation:** The authors conclude the results "confirm the antiwrinkle activity of acetyl hexapeptide-3" and report "a significant decrease in TEWL with acetyl hexapeptide-3 treatment," and state they "provided clinical evidence for the antiwrinkle efficacy of tripeptide-10 citrulline." No effect-size, fold-change, or percentage-improvement figure is stated for any outcome; the abstract explicitly notes "the underlying mechanism by which these two peptides can act synergistically was not clear in this study," so no synergy claim between the two peptides is supported.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies narrowly to healthy adult skin and to the specific proprietary peptides tested; generalizes to the case for topical [[peptides]] as a modest cosmetic anti-aging class rather than establishing this specific formulation's real-world effect. Key limitation: only 24 total participants split across 4 groups (~6 per arm by implication, not stated), significance is scattered and inconsistent across timepoints and group-pairs rather than following one clean dose-response pattern, and only p-values (no means, effect sizes, or CIs) are given in the abstract; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology text, so no additional numbers can be verified.[^1]
