A meta-analysis of 25 studies (24 pooled) covering over 1.6 million participants found isotretinoin for [[acne]] was not associated with increased risk of suicide or psychiatric disorders overall, and was associated with lower suicide-attempt risk in the years after treatment.[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials and observational studies, searching PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Scopus from inception through January 24, 2023; risk of bias assessed with the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale and pooled via inverse variance-weighted meta-analyses.[^1]

**Population / n:** 25 studies (24 included in the meta-analysis) totaling 1,625,891 participants; the abstract states an age range of 16-38 years across studies. No further breakdown of individual study sizes is given in the abstract.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Isotretinoin users versus nonusers (and, for the multi-year suicide-attempt analysis, isotretinoin users' risk at successive years post-treatment); exact comparator definitions per outcome are not spelled out beyond this in the abstract.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** One-year absolute risks for completed suicide, suicide attempt, suicidal ideation, and self-harm were each "less than 0.5%." One-year depression risk was "3.83% (95% CI, 2.45-5.93; I2 = 77%)" pooled across 11 studies. "Isotretinoin was not associated with the risk of all psychiatric disorders (RR, 1.08; 95% CI, 0.99-1.19; I2 = 0%)." Suicide-attempt risk in isotretinoin users was lower than comparators at 2 years (RR 0.92), 3 years (RR 0.86), and 4 years (RR 0.85) post-treatment. The abstract does not give a p-value for the all-psychiatric-disorders RR or for the year-by-year suicide-attempt RRs.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** The pooled RR for all psychiatric disorders (1.08, 95% CI 0.99-1.19) is not statistically significant, since the interval crosses 1. The 1-year absolute risks for suicide-related outcomes are small in absolute terms (each under 0.5%), and the declining suicide-attempt RRs over 2-4 years post-treatment (0.92 -> 0.86 -> 0.85) point toward lower attempt risk the further out from treatment, consistent with the authors' conclusion that isotretinoin users do not show increased psychiatric risk and may show reduced suicide-attempt risk post-treatment.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies broadly to isotretinoin users across the pooled trial and observational-study population (average ages 16-38), updating and extending the earlier [[huang-cheng-2017-isotretinoin-depression]] meta-analysis with more recent, larger-scale data. Key limitation: substantial heterogeneity in the depression estimate (I2 = 77%), and the abstract does not report p-values for the headline RRs, so statistical significance beyond CI-based inference cannot be independently confirmed from the abstract alone. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full JAMA Dermatology article, so no additional numbers (e.g., per-study breakdowns, adjusted covariates, or full risk-factor analysis) beyond those stated above could be verified.[^1]
