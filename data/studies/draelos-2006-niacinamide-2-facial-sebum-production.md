Two short clinical trials found that a topical 2% niacinamide moisturizer significantly lowered sebum excretion rate (SER) in Japanese subjects and casual sebum level (CSL) in Caucasian subjects, versus placebo.[^1]

**Design:** Two separate trials reported in one paper: a double-blind, placebo-controlled, two-arm trial in Japan, and a randomized split-face trial in the USA.[^1]

**Population / n:** Japan: 100 subjects (50 niacinamide, 50 placebo), 4 weeks. USA: 30 Caucasian subjects, split-face, 6 weeks.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Topical 2% niacinamide moisturizer vs. a matched placebo moisturizer (between-subjects in the Japanese trial, contralateral half-face in the US trial).[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** Japan: "the SER of the two groups was not significantly different at baseline, but the 2% niacinamide treated group demonstrated significantly lowered SER after 2 and 4 weeks of application." USA: "After 6 weeks of treatment, the CSL was significantly reduced, but the SER was not significantly reduced."[^1] The abstract states significance directionally only; it gives no percentage reduction, fold-change, or p-value for either outcome.

**Effect size / interpretation:** No quantitative effect size is stated in the abstract. Authors' conclusion, verbatim: "Topical 2% niacinamide may be effective in lowering the SER in Japanese individuals and CSL in Caucasian individuals." The two sebum measures (SER = production rate; CSL = surface oil snapshot) did not both reach significance in either population, so the result is a split, endpoint-dependent finding rather than a uniform effect.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Generalizes to short-term (4-6 week) reduction in a sebum-production biomarker, in these two specific populations and endpoints, not to acne lesion counts or any other clinical outcome: no acne endpoint was measured. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Journal of Cosmetic and Laser Therapy text, so no magnitude figures could be verified beyond what the abstract states. Underlies the sebum-regulation claim for [[niacinamide]], relevant to [[oily-skin]] and [[acne]], covered on those pages.
