A systematic review and meta-analysis of 11 studies (57 patients) found a pooled 89.9% response rate to oral JAK inhibitors in refractory [[rosacea]], but all included studies were single-arm (no placebo/standard-of-care comparator).[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis; "a comprehensive search was conducted in five databases from inception to December 1, 2025." Registered with PROSPERO (CRD420251245235).[^1]

**Population / n:** 11 studies, 57 patients total with refractory rosacea treated with oral JAK inhibitors.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Oral JAK inhibitors (agent(s) not specified in the accessed abstract) versus no comparator arm: all 11 included studies were single-arm (individual study designs not specified in the accessed abstract), not randomized or placebo-controlled.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "The overall pooled response rate was 89.9% (95% CI: 74.4%-99.4%), with low heterogeneity (I2 = 16.3%)." Secondary outcomes: "67.8% of patients achieved an IGA score of ≤ 1" and "74.6% achieved a CEA reduction of ≥ 2 points." "Adverse events were reported in 10.5% of patients."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Pooled response rate 89.9% (95% CI 74.4% to 99.4%) across the 57 patients, with low between-study heterogeneity (I² = 16.3%); roughly two-thirds to three-quarters of patients met IGA and CEA response thresholds, and adverse events were reported in about 1 in 10 patients. No comparator arm means this cannot establish effect size versus placebo or versus existing rosacea therapies.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies narrowly to patients with treatment-refractory rosacea considered for off-label oral JAK inhibitor therapy, not first-line or topical rosacea care. The authors' own stated limitation: "given the small sample size and single-arm design, these findings are exploratory," and they call for larger randomized controlled trials; only the PubMed abstract (via PubMed/NCBI E-utilities) was accessed, not the full text, so which specific JAK inhibitor(s), dosing, and duration were used cannot be verified here.[^1]
