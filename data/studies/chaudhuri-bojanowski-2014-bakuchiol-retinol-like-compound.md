An in-vitro gene-expression comparison found bakuchiol produces a highly similar transcriptional and collagen-expression profile to retinol, paired with an uncontrolled 12-week clinical case study reporting qualitative anti-aging improvement without retinol's usual irritation.[^1]

**Design:** Comparative gene-expression profiling of bakuchiol versus retinol in the EpiDerm FT full-thickness skin substitute model, with type I/III/IV collagen and aquaporin 3 expression measured by ELISA and/or histochemistry in human dermal fibroblasts and/or EpiDerm FT substitutes; separately, an uncontrolled clinical case study applying a bakuchiol-containing finished product twice daily for 12 weeks.[^1]

**Population / n:** In-vitro work used the EpiDerm FT skin-substitute model and human dermal fibroblasts (no cell/sample count stated). The abstract does not state the number of subjects in the clinical case study.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** In vitro, bakuchiol versus retinol gene-expression profiles were compared side by side. The clinical portion applied a bakuchiol-formulated product twice daily; the abstract describes no control/vehicle arm or comparator product for this part.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Volcano plots showed great overall similarity of retinol and bakuchiol effects on the gene expression profile." "Retinol-like functionality was further confirmed for the upregulation of types I and IV collagen in DNA microarray study and also show stimulation of type III collagen in the mature fibroblast model." For the clinical case study: "after 12 weeks treatment, significant improvement in lines and wrinkles, pigmentation, elasticity, firmness and overall reduction in photo-damage was observed, without usual retinol therapy-associated undesirable effects." The abstract gives no percentages, p-values, or fold-changes for any of these findings.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** No quantitative effect sizes are reported; similarity between bakuchiol and retinol is described only qualitatively ("great overall similarity"), and the clinical "significant improvement" is not tied to a statistical test or numeric score in the abstract. The authors conclude bakuchiol "can function as an anti-ageing compound through retinol-like regulation of gene expression."[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Supports [[bakuchiol]] as a gene-expression-level analogue to [[retinol]] with a milder tolerability profile, but the clinical evidence here is an uncontrolled case study (no stated n, no control arm, no quantitative outcome data) rather than a randomized trial, and only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full International Journal of Cosmetic Science text.[^1]
