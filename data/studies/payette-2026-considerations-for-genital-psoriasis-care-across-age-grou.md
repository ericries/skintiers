A multidisciplinary expert panel used a modified Delphi consensus process to develop practice considerations for genital psoriasis diagnosis and treatment across pediatric, adult, and geriatric patients.[^1]

**Design:** Modified Delphi consensus initiative, described as using "the modified Delphi process and nominal group technique, informed by a systematic literature review, with consensus defined as ≥ 75% agreement" on a 7-point Likert scale; this is expert-consensus methodology, not a clinical trial or observational study.[^1]

**Population / n:** The panel was convened by the Genital Psoriasis Wellness Consortium and lists 16 named authors/panelists across dermatology and related specialties; the abstract does not state a formal panel size, number of Delphi voting rounds, or a patient sample size (this study produces recommendations, not patient-level data).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** No intervention or comparator in the trial sense; the panel addressed three topic areas by consensus vote: physical exam/diagnosis, pediatric/adolescent treatment, and adult/geriatric treatment of genital psoriasis.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** The abstract reports consensus recommendations, not numeric outcomes: "routine genital assessment within comprehensive skin exams"; "shared decision making among clinicians, caregivers, and patients" for pediatric care; and for adult/geriatric care, consideration of "comorbidities, polypharmacy, and individual goals; prioritizing therapies with established safety" while "minimizing long-term corticosteroid use." The abstract states no percentages, p-values, or effect sizes.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Not applicable. This is a consensus-statement paper with no measured clinical effect; its output is a set of expert-agreed care considerations rather than a quantified treatment effect.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to clinicians managing genital psoriasis in pediatric, adult, and geriatric patients; it is expert consensus informed by a literature review, not primary trial evidence, so its recommendations carry the inherent limitations of Delphi methodology (panel composition and voting-round details are not disclosed in the abstract). Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text. Relevant to [[tazarotene]] (FDA psoriasis indication documented on its own page).
