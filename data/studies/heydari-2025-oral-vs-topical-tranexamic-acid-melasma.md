A single-center head-to-head trial (50 women, Iran) of oral versus topical [[tranexamic-acid]] for [[melasma]], with **no placebo or vehicle arm**. Both routes cut MASI by roughly half and the between-group difference was not significant, which the authors read as the two being interchangeable.[^1]

**Design:** "In this single-center randomized trial, 50 melasma patients received oral TXA (250 mg twice daily) or topical TXA (a 5% cream applied twice daily) for 12 weeks." Randomization was 1:1. "Melasma severity was measured using the Melasma Area and Severity Index (MASI) score at baseline and after 3 months." There is no third arm: no placebo tablet, no vehicle cream, and no sunscreen-only control.[^1]

**Population / n:** 50 patients, all female, mean age about 39.9 years, 25 per arm, at one center in Yazd, Iran. Baseline MASI did not differ between groups (p = 0.28).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Oral tranexamic acid 250 mg twice daily against topical tranexamic acid 5% cream twice daily. The comparator is the other route of the same drug, not a different drug and not placebo.[^1]

**Primary result (MASI reduction at 12 weeks):** "After 12 weeks, both groups demonstrated significant reductions in MASI scores (oral group: 58.86%; topical group: 50.88%; p = 0.001 for each), though the difference was insignificant." Those p values are within-group changes from baseline, not a comparison between the two routes.[^1]

**Safety as reported:** "One patient in the topical group discontinued treatment due to sensitivity, while all in the oral group completed the study." The abstract reports no other adverse events and no thromboembolic screening or outcomes, which is the safety question that matters for a systemic antifibrinolytic. Its conclusion is that "Both forms of TXA are effective with low side effects", a claim the abstract does not support with any adverse-event table.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Read this as a route-comparison, not as evidence that tranexamic acid works. With no placebo or vehicle arm, the 58.86% and 50.88% figures cannot be separated from sun avoidance, seasonal change, regression to the mean, or the moisturizing effect of a cream. And the headline reading is the common inferential error: **a non-significant difference between two arms of 25 patients each is not evidence that the routes are equivalent.** A trial this small has little power to detect a real gap of 8 percentage points, so "insignificant" here means undetermined, not equal. A non-inferiority claim would require a pre-specified margin and a much larger sample, neither of which this trial has.

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adult women with melasma; the condition is on [[melasma]] and the drug's general evidence on [[tranexamic-acid]]. Limits that compound: no placebo or vehicle arm, 25 per arm at a single center, 12 weeks for a relapsing condition, an all-female sample with no reported Fitzpatrick distribution, and no relapse follow-up after treatment stopped, which matters because melasma is "challenging due to frequent relapses and partial responses" by the paper's own account. The published DISCUSSION section also restates the RESULTS text almost word for word rather than interpreting it.[^1]

**Citation note (the author name here is corrected):** A 2025 correction changed the first author's surname and department. It states that "In the originally published version of the manuscript, the name and affiliation of the first author are incorrectly listed as" Behrooz Heidary of the Department of Pharmaceutics, and that "The correct form should be" Behrooz Heydari of the Department of Clinical Pharmacy. The correction changes **no data**, so every figure above stands, but citations copied from the original record carry the wrong surname. This page uses Heydari and keeps Heidary as an alias.[^2]
