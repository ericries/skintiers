A 24-week randomized trial in 349 subjects with facial photodamage found 0.1% tazarotene cream produced the highest global response rate (67%), outperforming both 0.05% tretinoin emollient cream (55%) and vehicle (22%), though the abstract gives no pairwise significance testing between individual arms.[^1]

**Design:** Prospective, multicenter, investigator-masked, randomized, vehicle-controlled, parallel-group trial; once-daily facial application for 24 weeks.[^1]

**Population / n:** 349 subjects with facial photodamage, randomized across six arms (four tazarotene concentrations, tretinoin, and vehicle); only the abstract was accessed, so per-arm baseline characteristics and any dropout/withdrawal counts are not stated.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Tazarotene cream at 0.01%, 0.025%, 0.05%, or 0.1%, each applied once daily, versus 0.05% tretinoin emollient cream once daily, versus vehicle cream once daily, all for 24 weeks. See [[tazarotene]] and [[tretinoin]] for general mechanism/positioning; not re-explained here.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** Week-24 global response rates: "67% (39 of 58 subjects) with 0.1% tazarotene, 52% (30 of 58 subjects) with 0.05% tazarotene, 36% (21 of 58 subjects) with 0.025% tazarotene, 41% (24 of 59 subjects) with 0.01% tazarotene, 55% (32 of 58 subjects) with 0.05% tretinoin, and 22% (13 of 58 subjects) with vehicle." The abstract additionally states "Tazarotene cream and tretinoin cream significantly improved mottled hyperpigmentation and fine wrinkles," but reports this as a combined statement, not as separate percentages for the two endpoints, and gives no P-values or explicit pairwise comparisons (e.g., tazarotene vs vehicle, tazarotene vs tretinoin) in the abstract text.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Ranked by global response rate, 0.1% tazarotene (67%) > 0.05% tretinoin (55%) > 0.05% tazarotene (52%) > 0.01% tazarotene (41%) > 0.025% tazarotene (36%) > vehicle (22%); the 0.025% and 0.01% tazarotene arms did not track dose-response order relative to each other in this ranking. The highest tazarotene dose numerically exceeded the tretinoin comparator, but without abstract-reported significance testing this cannot be called a proven superiority claim. Adverse events were "more frequent with tazarotene at higher concentrations" and described only as "generally mild to moderate," with no per-group irritation/erythema/desquamation rates given.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults with facial photodamage (mottled hyperpigmentation and fine wrinkling) treated once daily for 24 weeks; generalizes the dose-ranging efficacy case for [[tazarotene]] against the [[tretinoin]] benchmark within [[photoaging]]/[[photodamage]] treatment, topics covered on those pages. Key limitation: only the PubMed abstract was accessed (not the full Archives of Dermatology text), so no P-values, confidence intervals, dropout counts, or per-group adverse-event rates could be verified beyond what the abstract states; the "significantly improved" claim for tazarotene/tretinoin vs. presumably vehicle is asserted in the abstract without a reported statistic.[^1]
