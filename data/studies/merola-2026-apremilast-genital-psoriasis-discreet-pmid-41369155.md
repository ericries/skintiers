A phase 3 randomized trial found that oral apremilast, a PDE4 inhibitor, produced sustained improvement in moderate-to-severe genital [[psoriasis]] through 32 weeks, whether started at randomization or after a 16-week placebo delay.[^1]

**Design:** Phase 3, multicentre, randomized, double-blind trial (DISCREET); 16-week placebo-controlled phase (1:1 randomization) followed by a 16-week open apremilast extension through week 32.[^1]

**Population:** 289 patients randomized with moderate to severe genital psoriasis (modified genital PGA score of 3 or higher) inadequately controlled by or intolerant to topical therapy; 229 continued into the extension phase (110 placebo/apremilast, 119 apremilast/apremilast).[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Apremilast 30 mg twice daily versus placebo for weeks 0-16, then all patients received apremilast 30 mg twice daily through week 32.[^1]

**Primary result:** At week 32, 51.8% (95% CI 42.6-60.9) of the placebo/apremilast group and 40.3% (95% CI 32.0-49.3) of the apremilast/apremilast group achieved a modified genital PGA response (score 0/1 with at least a 2-point reduction from baseline); similar improvements in skin, genital signs/symptoms and quality of life were seen regardless of whether apremilast started at week 0 or week 16.[^1]

**Effect size:** Roughly 40-52% genital PGA response rates by week 32 in both start-time groups, with no concurrent placebo arm at that timepoint since all patients had crossed to active drug by week 16.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Adults with moderate-to-severe genital psoriasis whose disease is inadequately controlled by or intolerant to topical therapy, considering an oral systemic option.

**Key limitation:** After week 16 there was no placebo comparator, so the week-32 result compares two apremilast-treated groups rather than apremilast versus placebo; frequent treatment-emergent adverse events during all-apremilast exposure were diarrhoea (25.4%), nausea (19.4%) and headache (17.9%); the trial was funded by Amgen (which markets apremilast), and most authors disclosed consulting, investigator, speaker, or employee/stockholder ties to Amgen and other pharma companies.
