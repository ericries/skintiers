A split-face RCT in Chinese women with epidermal melasma found that adding kojic acid to a hydroquinone/glycolic-acid gel improved clearance on the kojic-acid side versus the same base gel without it.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized, split-face controlled trial; each patient's face received the test formulation on one side and the control formulation on the other, assessed every 4 weeks for 12 weeks.[^1]

**Population / n:** 40 Chinese women with epidermal melasma.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** One side treated with "2% kojic acid in a gel containing 10% glycolic acid and 2% hydroquinone"; the other side treated with the same gel (10% glycolic acid, 2% hydroquinone) without kojic acid.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "More than half of the melasma cleared in 24/40 (60%) patients" on the kojic-acid side, versus "19/40 (47.5%) patients" on the control side; complete clearance occurred in 2 patients, both on the kojic-acid-treated side only. Both sides caused "redness, stinging, and exfoliation," resolving by week three. Wilcoxon's rank sum test was used for analysis; the abstract does not state a p-value for the between-side difference.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** A roughly 12.5-percentage-point higher rate of substantial clearance with kojic acid added (60% vs. 47.5%), plus complete clearance seen only on the kojic-acid side (2 patients). This reads as a modest incremental benefit from adding kojic acid to an already-active hydroquinone/glycolic-acid base, not a standalone efficacy result for kojic acid.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Generalizes to epidermal melasma in Chinese women treated alongside hydroquinone and glycolic acid; it does not establish kojic acid's efficacy as a monotherapy or in other skin types, and the abstract reports no p-value for the primary comparison, so statistical significance of the 60% vs. 47.5% difference cannot be confirmed from this source. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Dermatologic Surgery text. Relevant to [[hyperpigmentation]], [[hydroquinone]], [[glycolic-acid]].[^1]
