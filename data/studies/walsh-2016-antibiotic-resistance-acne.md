A literature review found that in many countries more than 50% of Cutibacterium (Propionibacterium) acnes strains are resistant to topical macrolides, and recommends limiting antibiotic monotherapy in [[acne]] treatment.[^1]

**Design:** Narrative review of the scientific literature (not a quantitative meta-analysis), conducted to propose treatment recommendations; references were identified through PubMed searches for articles published January 1954 to March 7, 2015, using four multiword search terms. The abstract does not describe formal systematic-review methodology (no PRISMA flow, no stated number of included studies).[^1]

**Population / n:** Not a patient cohort; the "population" is the published literature on antibiotic resistance in acne. The abstract does not state how many studies or articles were reviewed.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** No single intervention/comparator pair; the review compares antibiotic-based acne regimens (topical antibiotic alone; oral antibiotic alone or combined) against combinations of [[benzoyl-peroxide]] with a topical retinoid.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Antibiotic resistance is increasing, with many countries reporting that more than 50% of Propionibacterium acnes strains are resistant to topical macrolides, making them less effective." The abstract gives no country-by-country percentages, no p-values, and no fold-change figures beyond this ">50%" resistance figure.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** No quantified effect size for any intervention is given; the paper's output is a set of recommendations rather than a measured outcome: "benzoyl peroxide in combination with a topical retinoid should be used instead of a topical antibiotic to minimise the impact of resistance"; oral antibiotics for moderate-to-severe acne only alongside a topical retinoid, benzoyl peroxide, or their combination, "ideally for no longer than 3 months"; and benzoyl peroxide should always be added when long-term oral antibiotic use is deemed necessary.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies broadly to acne antibiotic prescribing; the recommendations (topical antibiotic avoidance, ≤3-month oral courses, mandatory benzoyl peroxide pairing) are the widely-cited practical takeaway. Key limitation: this is a narrative literature review, not a systematic review with a reproducible search yield or pooled resistance statistics, and only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Lancet Infectious Diseases text, so the underlying per-country resistance data cannot be verified here.
