A combined preclinical (in vitro/ex vivo) and randomized, double-blind clinical study found that a 30% THD Ascorbate antioxidant serum sharply reduced blue-light-induced ROS formation and melanin production and increased collagen production in lab models, while visibly correcting photodamage and hyperpigmentation on imaging in human subjects.[^1]

**Design:** "Using preclinical models and a randomized, double-blind clinical trial, the antioxidant potential, antimelanogenesis, and antiaging properties of the THD-AA serum were evaluated" (i.e., in vitro tissue-model assays (blue-light ROS, tissue co-culture melanin), an ex vivo skin model (collagen, structural architecture), plus a human randomized, double-blind clinical trial (VISIA-CR and Antera 3D photography, Clinical Grader assessment)).[^1]

**Population / n:** Not stated. The abstract does not give the number of clinical-trial participants, nor the sample sizes for the in vitro or ex vivo models.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** A serum containing "30% THD Ascorbate," described as "a patent-pending blend of antioxidants and prebiotics." Comparators varied by assay: blue-light-exposed control (for ROS), in vitro tissue co-culture (24% reduction; comparator baseline not specified in the abstract) (for melanin), a "controlled moisturizer" (for collagen, ex vivo), and (for the clinical trial) baseline photography/grading (no explicit vehicle or active comparator is named for the human arm).[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Using an in vitro tissue model exposed to blue light, there was an 88% reduction in reactive oxygen species (ROS) formation after 30 min, 87% reduction after 60 min, and an 82% reduction after 120 min compared to the blue light-exposed control. Melanin production was reduced by 24% in vitro tissue co-culture. THD-AA serum improved the structural architecture of the skin, including the epidermis, dermal-epidermal junction, and dermis, and upregulated dermal collagen production 4-fold compared to a controlled moisturizer in an ex vivo model. In the clinical trial, existing damage and hyperpigmentation were visibly corrected on VISIA-CR and Antera 3D photographs, as well as in Clinical Grader results. There were no adverse events, and participants tolerated the serum well."[^1] The abstract gives no numeric or statistical (p-value) figures for the clinical-trial imaging/grading results, and no percentage or score improvement is quantified for the human arm.

**Effect size / interpretation:** Large effects in lab models (82 to 88% ROS reduction across timepoints, 24% melanin reduction, and a 4-fold increase in collagen production versus a controlled moisturizer) support an antioxidant, antimelanogenic, and pro-collagen mechanism for THD-AA serum. The human clinical-trial result is reported only qualitatively ("visibly corrected"), so no effect size can be attached to the in-vivo hyperpigmentation/photoaging outcome from this abstract.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Supports [[tetrahexyldecyl-ascorbate]] as a hydroquinone-free option for [[hyperpigmentation]] and photoaging, in contrast with [[hydroquinone]]. Key limitation: only the PubMed abstract was accessed (not the full text), so clinical-trial n, statistical significance, and quantified human-outcome figures could not be verified. The abstract itself omits these numbers, reporting the clinical result only as a qualitative visible correction on imaging/grading.[^1]
