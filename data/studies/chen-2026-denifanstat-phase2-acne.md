A Phase 2 randomized controlled trial of denifanstat, a first-in-class oral fatty-acid-synthase (FASN) inhibitor, in moderate-to-severe [[acne]] found significantly larger reductions in total lesion counts than placebo, but no significant advantage on the stricter Investigator's Global Assessment (IGA) responder endpoint. It is an investigational oral prescription drug, not a topical or over-the-counter product.[^1]

**Design:** Multi-centre, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-escalation Phase 2 trial (NCT05104125). Oral denifanstat at 25, 50, or 75 mg, or placebo, once daily after dinner for 12 weeks.[^1]

**Population:** Patients with moderate-to-severe acne vulgaris, randomized across four arms: denifanstat 25 mg (n=45), 50 mg (n=44), 75 mg (n=45), and placebo (n=45).[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Oral denifanstat (a fatty-acid-synthase inhibitor) at three doses versus matched placebo.[^1]

**Primary result:** At Week 12, median reduction in total lesion counts was significantly greater than placebo at every dose: "25 mg: -53.2%... 50 mg: -61.3%... 75 mg: -53.1%... vs. placebo: -34.2%... all p < 0.05."[^1] However, on the IGA responder measure the drug missed: the proportion achieving a "≥ 2-grade reduction in Investigator's Global Assessment (IGA)" was 31.1%, 31.8%, and 22.2% for the three doses versus 15.6% for placebo, and "the difference among the four groups was not significant (p = 0.336)."[^1]

**Effect size:** Lesion-count reductions ran roughly 19 to 27 median percentage points beyond placebo, with no clean dose-response (50 mg looked best, 75 mg no better than 25 mg). The IGA responder gap, the more clinically stringent measure, was not statistically significant. Safety was acceptable: drug-related adverse events of 47.7% to 62.2% across doses (versus 48.9% on placebo), most commonly dry eye, dry skin, urine protein positive, skin peeling, and conjunctivitis (Grade 1 to 2), with no serious drug-related events.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Adults with moderate-to-severe acne, as an early signal for an investigational oral systemic drug. It says nothing about topical or over-the-counter acne care; denifanstat is not approved and is taken by mouth, not applied to skin.[^1]

**Key limitation:** This is a small (about 45 per arm), 12-week, industry-sponsored Phase 2 trial (multiple authors are employees of the developer, Ascletis BioScience), and it hit the lesion-count endpoint while missing the IGA responder endpoint, so the efficacy signal is mixed. The authors themselves frame denifanstat 50 mg as showing "potential to reduce total lesion counts" and state that "larger and longer term studies are needed."[^1]
