A 12-week randomized, double-blinded pilot trial in 20 women found investigator ratings favored a recombinant topical [[epidermal-growth-factor]] (oligopeptide-1) serum over a human-derived growth factor serum for skin texture and firmness, though the abstract reports no numeric effect sizes.[^1]

**Design:** Prospective, randomized, double-blinded, parallel-group pilot trial, 12 weeks.[^1]

**Population / n:** 20 adult female participants, ages 50-65, predominantly non-Hispanic White, Fitzpatrick Skin Types II and III, with moderate to severe facial photoaging.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Topical recombinant epidermal growth factor (REGF) with oligopeptide-1 serum, applied twice daily, versus a human-derived growth factor (HDEGF) serum, applied twice daily; both groups used a standardized cleanser and sunscreen regimen.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Investigator ratings favored the REGF cohort for improvements in skin texture and firmness at later study visits, while global wrinkle severity ratings changed minimally in both cohorts." "Participants in the REGF cohort reported higher satisfaction across multiple questionnaire items." "In exploratory lay-evaluator assessments, small between-visit differences were observed in select perception domains, including estimated age."[^1] The abstract gives no percentages, p-values, or fold-change figures for any of these outcomes.

**Effect size / interpretation:** The abstract's conclusion states the REGF formulation "was associated with greater improvement in selected investigator- and participant-reported outcomes compared with the HDEGF formulation over 12 weeks," but reports this only qualitatively (ratings "favored" one arm, satisfaction was "higher") with no quantified magnitude of difference between the two serums, and notes the lay-evaluator perception findings are exploratory and limited by a non-validated rating instrument.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Findings apply narrowly to middle-aged-to-older women (50-65) with moderate-to-severe photoaging comparing two specific proprietary serum formulations, not to topical growth factors or [[peptides]] broadly. The authors' own stated limitation is the "small, female-only sample and short follow-up," and only the PubMed abstract was accessed (full text was reported available via Wiley/PMC but not fetched), so no additional numbers beyond those quoted above can be verified.[^1]
