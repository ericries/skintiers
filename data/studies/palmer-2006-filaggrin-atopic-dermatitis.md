A human genetic association study found that two independent loss-of-function variants in the filaggrin gene (FLG), carried by roughly 9% of people of European origin, are strong predisposing factors for atopic dermatitis.[^1]

**Design:** Human genetic association study identifying and testing two independent loss-of-function variants (R510X and 2282del4) in the gene encoding filaggrin (FLG) for association with atopic dermatitis and with asthma occurring in the context of atopic dermatitis.[^1]

**Population / n:** People of European origin; the abstract does not state the number of subjects or cohorts studied.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Observational genetic study, not an intervention; carriers of the R510X and/or 2282del4 FLG loss-of-function variants versus non-carriers.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Two independent loss-of-function genetic variants (R510X and 2282del4) in the gene encoding filaggrin (FLG) are very strong predisposing factors for atopic dermatitis. These variants are carried by approximately 9% of people of European origin. These variants also show highly significant association with asthma occurring in the context of atopic dermatitis."[^1] The abstract gives no odds ratio, relative risk, or p-value; effect strength is described only qualitatively ("very strong," "highly significant").

**Effect size / interpretation:** No numeric effect size (odds ratio, relative risk, or p-value) is stated in the abstract. The authors conclude the findings establish "a key role for impaired skin barrier function in the development of atopic disease," i.e., that a primary epithelial barrier defect, not only immune dysregulation, predisposes to atopic dermatitis.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Findings are established in people of European origin (~9% carrier frequency for the two named variants); applicability to other ancestries is not addressed in the abstract, and FLG variant spectra are known to differ by population. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the Nature Genetics full text, so sample size, cohort structure, and any odds ratios or p-values reported in the paper itself could not be verified here.
