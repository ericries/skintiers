A multicenter US/UK pilot RCT found that daily full-body emollient therapy started within 3 weeks of birth in high-risk neonates cut the cumulative incidence of atopic dermatitis by half versus no emollients.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized controlled trial (multicenter, US and UK), testing full-body emollient therapy from birth as primary prevention of atopic dermatitis; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text.[^1]

**Population / n:** 124 neonates at high risk for atopic dermatitis; the abstract also reports that "forty-two percent of eligible families agreed to be randomized," a feasibility metric rather than an efficacy figure.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Full-body emollient applied at least once daily starting within 3 weeks of birth, versus a no-emollient control group.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "A statistically significant protective effect was found with the use of daily emollient on the cumulative incidence of atopic dermatitis with a relative risk reduction of 50% (relative risk, 0.50; 95% CI, 0.28-0.9; P = .017)." The abstract also states "There were no emollient-related adverse events and no differences in adverse events between groups."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Relative risk 0.50 (95% CI 0.28-0.90, P=.017) for developing atopic dermatitis with emollient use versus control: roughly a halving of cumulative AD incidence in this high-risk cohort. The abstract does not report absolute incidence rates or number-needed-to-treat, so the baseline (control-arm) incidence cannot be verified from this source.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to neonates at high familial risk of atopic dermatitis (not the general population), studied in a feasibility-oriented pilot RCT with a modest sample size (n=124) and a randomization uptake of only 42% among eligible families, which limits generalizability; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, so no additional methodological detail (e.g., blinding, specific emollient products used) could be verified; the abstract does report a 6-month follow-up (cumulative AD incidence assessed at 6 months). Supports the primary-prevention angle for [[atopic-dermatitis]] via [[moisturizing]] and [[skin-barrier-strengthening]] from birth, distinct from the more common evidence base of treating established dry or eczematous skin.[^1]
