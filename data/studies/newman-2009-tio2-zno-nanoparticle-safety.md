A dermatology literature review of studies and position statements from 1980-2008 found no evidence that titanium dioxide or zinc oxide nanoparticles in sunscreens penetrate significantly beyond the skin's stratum corneum.[^1]

**Design:** Narrative literature review characterizing the use, safety, and regulatory state of nanosized titanium dioxide and zinc oxide particles in sunscreens, based on studies and position statements published 1980-2008; not a primary trial. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text.[^1]

**Population / n:** Not applicable (this is a review of prior literature, not a study of human subjects); no n is given.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** None (no intervention was tested); the review synthesizes existing penetration-safety studies of titanium dioxide and zinc oxide nanoparticles in sunscreens.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "[W]e found no evidence of significant penetration of titanium dioxide and zinc oxide nanosized particles beyond the stratum corneum." The abstract states no percentages, fold-changes, or other quantitative figures.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** No effect size is reported or applicable; the finding is a qualitative absence-of-penetration conclusion drawn across the reviewed literature, not a measured quantity.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to the general safety case for nanosized [[titanium-dioxide]] and [[zinc-oxide]] as sunscreen UV filters. Key limitation, stated by the authors: the reviewed studies did not simulate real-world conditions, particularly sunburned skin and concurrent ultraviolet exposure, so further study was called for; this review is also now over 15 years old and does not include post-2008 penetration studies.[^1]
