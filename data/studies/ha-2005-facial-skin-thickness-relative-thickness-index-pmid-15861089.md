The measurement study behind the claim that eyelid skin is the thinnest on the face. It supports the claim, and it is much smaller than the claim's popularity suggests.[^ha]

**Design:** a cadaver study, histometric measurement of skin biopsy specimens. Not ultrasound and not living skin.[^ha]

**Population:** "three fresh adult cadavers".[^ha] That is the whole sample.

**What was measured:** 15 facial sites per subject, named in full: "upper lip vermilion, lower lip vermilion, philtral column, chin, upper eyelid, lower eyelid, brow/forehead, submental crease, right cheek, left cheek, right neck, left neck, malar eminence, nasal dorsum, and nasal tip".[^ha] Measuring every site in the same subject is the design's real strength, because it removes between-person variation from the comparison.

**Primary result:** "In all subjects, the upper eyelid had the thinnest skin and was used as the denominator to calculate relative ratios of skin thicknesses with respect to other sites of the face."[^ha]

**Effect size, expressed as ratios rather than absolute thickness:** "the nasal tip skin thickness was 3.30 times thicker and the brow/forehead was 2.8 times thicker".[^ha] The paper's stated reason for working in ratios is that absolute values "have demonstrated variability among the different modalities", so numbers from different measurement methods cannot be compared directly.[^ha]

**Generalizes to:** the ranking, cautiously. Three cadavers cannot establish a population distribution, and nothing here speaks to how thickness varies with age, sex or ethnicity. A larger in vivo study does exist, Meng 2022, which measured epidermal and dermal thickness by high-frequency ultrasound at eight facial sites including the eyelid in 118 healthy adults; its published abstract reports effects of gender, age and BMI and does NOT report which site is thinnest, so it neither confirms nor contradicts this ranking.[^meng]

**Key limitation:** n = 3, and the tissue is post-mortem. Anyone citing `eyelid skin is the thinnest` as settled fact is resting a very common claim on a very small study.
