A systematic review of published literature on topical treatments for stretch marks found only 11 studies with efficacy data, and concluded no topical formulation is clearly most effective; two prophylactic proprietary creams had level-2 positive evidence, tretinoin's therapeutic results varied, and cocoa butter and olive oil showed no effect. It is the umbrella source behind the "no topical clearly wins" framing on the [[stretch-marks]] tier list.[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review of published literature and manufacturer website information on topicals for striae distensae (SD); not a primary trial or meta-analysis.[^1]

**Population / n:** The unit of analysis is the published literature, not patients; the review states "there are few studies (n = 11) which investigate the efficacy of topicals in management of SD."[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Narrative synthesis across multiple topical products and actives (including Trofolastin, Alphastria, tretinoin, cocoa butter, and olive oil), not a single head-to-head comparison.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Trofolastin and Alphastria creams demonstrated level-2 evidence of positive results for their prophylactic use in SD." "Tretinoin used therapeutically showed varying results whilst cocoa butter and olive oil did not demonstrate any effect." "There is no topical formulation, which is shown to be most effective in eradicating or improving SD."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** None pooled or quantified; this is a qualitative synthesis of which of the 11 identified studies found positive results, not a meta-analysis with an effect size.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** The state of topical-only evidence for striae distensae as of the 2016 search; the review also notes most of the underlying studies failed to distinguish early (striae rubrae) from later (striae albae) stages, or prevention from treatment.[^1]

**Key limitation:** Only 11 studies were found at all, so the review's "no topical works" conclusion reflects a genuinely small evidence base rather than a large body of negative trials; it also draws on "manufacturer website information," a non-independent source, for some product claims.[^1]
