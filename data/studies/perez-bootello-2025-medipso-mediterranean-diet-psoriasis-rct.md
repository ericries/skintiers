A randomized trial testing whether a dietitian-guided Mediterranean diet improves psoriasis severity, as an add-on to stable topical treatment.[^1] Diet is not a topical skincare step, so this informs [[psoriasis]] as adjunctive context only.

**Design:** "an open-label, single-center, single-blinded (evaluator) randomized clinical trial", run February 2024 to March 2025 at a dermatology referral clinic in Madrid, Spain. Registered as NCT06257641.[^1]

**Population:** 38 adults randomized (19 per arm) of 45 screened; 37 (97.4%) completed. Mean age 46.4 years (SD 12.8), 25 male (65.8%). Entry required "mild to moderate psoriasis (Psoriasis Area and Severity Index [PASI] of 2-10, with higher scores indicating maximal disease) receiving stable topical therapy".[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** The intervention arm received "a 16-week, dietitian-guided Mediterranean diet program, including nutritional counseling, educational materials, and weekly provision of extra virgin olive oil". The control arm received "standard low-fat dietary advice without dietitian supervision".[^1]

**Primary result:** Change in PASI from baseline to week 16. Estimated marginal mean PASI change was "-3.4 (95% CI, -4.4 to -2.4) in the intervention group and 0.0 (95% CI, -1.0 to 1.0) in the control group; the between-group EMM difference was -3.4 (95% CI, -4.8 to -2.0; P < .001)".[^1] "Nine of 19 participants in the Mediterranean diet group (47.4%) achieved PASI 75 (a 75% reduction in PASI) compared with none in the control group."[^1]

**Effect size:** Large in relative terms, because the entry PASI ceiling was 10: a 3.4-point absolute drop against a maximum baseline of 10 is a substantial proportion of the measurable range, and the 47.4% versus 0% PASI 75 split is the clearer signal. A secondary metabolic outcome moved too, with a between-group HbA1c difference of "-4.1 mmol/mol [95% CI, -6.9 to -1.3 mmol/mol]; P = .01".[^1] Control-arm PASI did not change at all (0.0), which is itself worth noting in an open-label trial.

**Generalizes to:** Adults with mild-to-moderate plaque psoriasis already on stable topical therapy, in a Mediterranean-diet food culture, with dietitian access. It says nothing about severe psoriasis, about diet replacing treatment, or about topical products, which are covered on [[psoriasis]].

**Key limitation:** The two arms differed by more than diet. The intervention group got dietitian supervision, counseling, educational materials and free extra virgin olive oil every week, while controls got unsupervised low-fat advice.[^1] So the trial compares an intensive supported program against brief advice, and cannot separate the Mediterranean diet itself from the attention, structure and provided food that came with it. Add the open-label design with participants aware of their allocation, 19 per arm, a single center, and 16 weeks, and the honest reading is a promising adjunct signal needing replication against an equally supported control, not established evidence that this diet treats psoriasis. Several authors report extensive pharmaceutical consultancy and the senior authorship includes psoriasis trialists, with full disclosures in the paper.
