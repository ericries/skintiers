A narrative review that organizes the ways skincare active ingredients can be pushed deeper into the skin, because "active ingredients show low transdermal efficiency due to skin barrier and molecular constraints." It is a qualitative summary of delivery technologies, not a trial measuring any skin outcome, and it does not rank the methods against each other.[^1]

**Design:** Narrative review (the authors call it a systematic sorting) of the published literature on dermal-absorption promotion methods, with no quantitative synthesis, no pooled effect estimate, and no head-to-head efficacy ranking.[^1]

**What it covers:** The mechanisms of skin permeation (via epidermal pathways and skin appendages) and the "commonly used permeation methods," split into non-invasive techniques ("chemical enhancers, nanocarriers, ultrasound") and invasive techniques ("microneedles, electroporation, needle-free injectors"), noting that "combinations boost efficacy."[^1]

**Primary takeaway:** The barrier problem is real, "active ingredients show low transdermal efficiency due to skin barrier and molecular constraints," and the review's conclusion is simply that "appropriate dermal technology is crucial to solve the problem of low dermal efficiency of active ingredients." This supports, in general terms, why delivery-system formulations (for example [[encapsulated-retinol]]) and penetration limits (the large-molecule problem noted on [[epidermal-growth-factor]]) matter.[^1]

**Effect size:** None reported; this is a methods review, so it offers no numbers on how much any single technique improves penetration or any skin result.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Formulation and device science for cosmeceutical delivery, not to any specific product or clinical claim. It is useful here as background on why an active's delivery vehicle affects whether it reaches its target, not as evidence that any particular product works.

**Key limitation:** A qualitative narrative review with an explicit commercial framing (it opens on the medical-aesthetics market's "12.8% Compound Annual Growth Rate (projected to reach $35,327.5 million by 2030)"), no risk-of-bias assessment, and no comparative data, so it cannot say which permeation method is most effective or safe for any given ingredient.[^1]
