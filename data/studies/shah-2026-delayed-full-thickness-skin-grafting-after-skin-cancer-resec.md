A systematic review of 24 studies (665 patients) on delaying full-thickness skin grafting after Mohs surgery or wide local excision for skin cancer found an 89.6% overall graft success rate, with a 2-to-3-week delay outperforming grafts placed within one week.[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review. "Databases were searched for studies on delayed FTSG after MMS or WLE for skin cancer removal. Data were extracted on study design, patient and tumor characteristics, surgical details, graft technique, and outcomes including graft success, complications, and aesthetic/functional results." The abstract does not name the databases searched, the search dates, or the risk-of-bias tool used.[^1]

**Population / n:** "Twenty-four studies comprising 665 patients were included. The mean age was 63.8 years, and most patients were male (52.6%)." All patients underwent reconstruction with a delayed full-thickness skin graft (FTSG) following Mohs micrographic surgery (MMS) or wide local excision (WLE) for skin cancer.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Delayed full-thickness skin grafting at varying intervals after tumor resection, compared within the pooled studies by timing of graft placement (≤1 week versus the 2-to-3-week window); this is a review of observational/case-series data, not a randomized comparison.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "The average delay to grafting was 16.5 days, with an overall graft success rate of 89.6% at an average follow-up period of 13 months. The optimal time to delay was 2 to 3 weeks with grafts placed in ≤1 week demonstrating significantly lower success. Outcomes were overall assessed as favorable, and patients were satisfied with their results." The abstract states early grafts showed "significantly lower success" but does not give a p-value, an effect size, or the success rate specifically for the ≤1-week subgroup.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** No pooled effect size (e.g., odds ratio, risk ratio) is reported in the abstract; the 89.6% figure is a pooled overall success rate across 24 heterogeneous studies, not a single trial's outcome, and the timing comparison is stated qualitatively ("significantly lower success") without the underlying numbers.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to surgical reconstruction decisions after skin cancer removal (timing of grafting relative to margin clearance), which sits downstream of the skin-cancer risk that [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] use aims to reduce; it does not address any topical skincare product or ingredient. Key limitation: this is a systematic review pooling 24 studies of unspecified design (likely mostly retrospective case series) rather than a randomized trial, so the "significantly lower success" claim for early grafting cannot be checked for confounding, and no database list, date range, or risk-of-bias assessment is given in the abstract. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text in Dermatologic Surgery.[^1]
