A randomized, single-blind, 6-month trial testing an over-the-counter "dermocosmetic" serum (vitamin B3, that is [[niacinamide]], plus 2-mercaptonicotinyl glycine) against the prescription Kligman triple formula, the standard topical for [[melasma]].[^1] It is an industry-sponsored comparison of a cosmetic routine versus the Rx benchmark, not a test of a single ingredient.

**Design:** Randomized, single-blind, 6-month study in women with melasma. Group A used the dermocosmetic (DC) serum in the morning and a cream at night for 6 months; Group B used a hydrating gel in the morning and the Kligman triple (KT) at night for the first 3 months, then switched to the DC routine for 3 months. All subjects used an SPF 50+ sunscreen twice daily.[^1]

**Population:** "Ninety-one women aged 44 ± 6 years of Phototypes III (30%) and IV (36%)."[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Dermocosmetic serum (vitamin B3 + 2-mercaptonicotinyl glycine) versus the Kligman triple formula (the hydroquinone, tretinoin, and corticosteroid combination), with sunscreen in both arms.[^1]

**Primary result:** Both regimens "significantly (p < 0.05) improved MASI after 3 months. A clinically relevant difference in favor of KT was observed." After Group B switched from KT to the DC routine, both arms reached a similar reduction: "Group A (38.1%) and Group B (41.2%) provided a similar significant (p < 0.05) percentage reduction of MASI score, with no between-group difference." Quality of life (MELASQOL) and skin hydration improved in both; "Global tolerance was better with the DC routine."[^1]

**Effect size:** About a 38% to 41% MASI reduction by 6 months in both arms; at the 3-month mark the prescription KT was meaningfully more effective than the cosmetic serum.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Women with melasma of phototypes III to IV; the general condition and its treatments are covered on [[melasma]].

**Key limitation:** Single-blind (not double-blind) and funded by the serum's maker (Vichy/L'Oréal authors and honoraria), with the prescription KT outperforming the dermocosmetic serum at 3 months, so the honest read is a gentler, better-tolerated cosmetic option that works more slowly than the Rx standard rather than an equal substitute.[^1]
