A split-face RCT in 30 women found that a soothing-complex cream applied after intense pulsed light (IPL) therapy significantly reduced erythema index, erythema area, and transepidermal water loss by day 28 versus a control moisturizer.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind, split-face controlled trial. One facial side received the test cream ("TTS cream," containing 4-tert-butylcyclohexanol, Tephrosia purpurea seed extract, and Schisandra sphenanthera extract), the other a control moisturizing toner, following IPL therapy. Assessments were taken at baseline, immediately post-IPL, 30 minutes post-application, and at days 3, 7, 14, and 28.[^1]

**Population / n:** 30 healthy female subjects receiving IPL therapy.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** TTS soothing cream (test side) vs. control moisturizing toner (control side), split-face on the same subjects.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** At 30 minutes, the test cream improved skin hydration significantly (Δ = 12.79 vs. 0.32 corneometer units [C.U.] for control). "At D28, TTS cream significantly reduced erythema index (∆ = -54.61, p < 0.01), erythema area (∆ = -7.02%, p < 0.01), and TEWL (∆ = -4.41 g/m2 h, p < 0.01)." Subjects also reported significant relief of stinging, burning, and redness (p < 0.01), with satisfaction rates of 100% for gentleness and 96.67% for symptom relief; no adverse effects were reported.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Magnitudes are given as raw deltas versus control rather than percent-change or fold-change (erythema index change of -54.61 units, erythema area change of -7.02 percentage points, TEWL change of -4.41 g/m²h, all p < 0.01), so the practical/clinical significance of these unit changes cannot be judged from the abstract alone. The consistent direction and significance across hydration, erythema, and TEWL measures support a genuine barrier-recovery and anti-erythema effect over the 28-day follow-up.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies specifically to post-IPL skin recovery in healthy women using this particular three-ingredient proprietary complex; it does not establish efficacy for erythema or barrier repair outside the post-procedure setting, and none of the three actives (4-tert-butylcyclohexanol, Tephrosia purpurea seed extract, Schisandra sphenanthera extract) has an independent evidence page on this site yet. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text, so methodological details (randomization method, blinding procedure, statistical model) and any additional secondary outcomes could not be verified.[^1]
