A cross-sectional study of 300 Caucasian women found that objective skin-glow parameters correlate with age mainly at nonfacial sites (especially the neck), while facial skin glow showed no measurable age correlation.[^1]

**Design:** Cross-sectional observational study establishing objective reference ranges for the "skin glow" emergent perceptual category by correlating age against three instrument-measured parameters, using melanin index (MI), erythema index (EI), and gloss diffuse scattering correction (DSC) value, across five body sites.[^1]

**Population / n:** 300 Caucasian women, aged 20-69 years, divided into five age groups.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** No intervention; this is a correlational comparison of age against MI, EI, and gloss DSC, measured with a Mexameter and a Skin-Glossymeter at five sites (forehead, cheek, neck, décolleté, and the back of the hand).[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "A moderate positive correlation was observed between age and EI for the neck. Low correlations were identified for the neck (MI), décolleté (EI, gloss DSC), and hand (MI, EI, and gloss DSC). No correlations were found between age and the EI, MI, and gloss DSC parameters in the facial areas, nor for gloss DSC in the neck and MI in the décolleté."[^1] The abstract does not report numeric correlation coefficients (r) or p-values for any of these correlations.

**Effect size / interpretation:** The authors characterize correlation strength only in qualitative terms ("moderate," "low," "no correlation"), not with r or p statistics. The neck erythema index was the standout age-correlated parameter; facial skin (forehead, cheek) showed no age correlation on any of the three parameters measured. The study frames this as support for using the neck, rather than the face, as a reference site for age-related skin-glow assessment.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Findings apply only to Caucasian women aged 20-69 measured with this specific instrument pair (Mexameter, Skin-Glossymeter); generalizability to other skin tones/ethnicities or to self-reported/perceived glow (rather than instrument-measured glow) is not established. It is cross-sectional, so it shows age-association, not causation or change over time within individuals. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text of the *Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology* article, so no additional numbers (r/p values, per-age-group means) beyond the qualitative correlation descriptions above can be verified. Relevant to the objective measurement of [[hyperpigmentation]] (melanin index) and [[rosacea-erythematotelangiectatic]] (erythema index) as "detractors" of skin glow, covered on those pages.[^1]
