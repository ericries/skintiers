A murine and human skin barrier-repair study found that topical mixtures of ceramides, cholesterol, and free fatty acids must be applied in an equimolar ratio to restore normal barrier recovery, and that increasing any one of the three up to 3-fold accelerates repair further.[^1]

**Design:** Experimental study applying various topical mixtures of the three key stratum corneum lipids (ceramides, cholesterol, and free fatty acids, i.e. linoleic, palmitic, or stearic acid) to barrier-perturbed skin, then measuring recovery via transepidermal water loss; acylceramides, glycosylceramides, sphingomyelin, and triglycerides were also tested as substitutes.[^1]

**Population / n:** Murine (mouse) skin and human skin were both used as models; the abstract does not state a number of animals or human subjects/sites.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Application of one or two of the three lipids alone versus all three combined at an equimolar ratio versus mixtures with one lipid increased up to 3-fold relative to the others.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "application of one or two of these lipids to perturbed skin delays barrier recovery; only equimolar mixtures allow normal recovery." Increasing any one of ceramides, cholesterol, or free fatty acids "up to 3-fold" relative to the others produced "further acceleration of barrier repair." The abstract does not give a quantified delay duration for the incomplete (one- or two-lipid) mixtures, nor a percentage or fold-change for the acceleration seen with the 3-fold-increased mixtures.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Establishes that barrier repair depends on lipid ratio, not only lipid presence: an equimolar 1:1:1 mix of ceramides, cholesterol, and free fatty acids is the minimum needed for normal recovery, with supraequimolar (up to 3x) amounts of any one component speeding recovery further. This is the mechanistic basis for the claim that single-lipid "barrier" products can underperform full-spectrum, correctly-ratioed formulas. No effect sizes (fold-change, %, p-values) are stated in the abstract.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** A foundational mechanistic study for [[ceramides]], [[cholesterol]], and [[phytosphingosine]] formulation ratios and for the [[skin-barrier-repair]] / [[skin-barrier-strengthening]] pages; findings come from acutely barrier-perturbed murine (mouse) and human skin measured by TEWL, not from a clinical trial of finished cosmetic products or chronic dermatoses, and only the PubMed abstract was accessed (not the full J Invest Dermatol text), so exact sample sizes and quantitative effect sizes cannot be verified beyond what is quoted here.
