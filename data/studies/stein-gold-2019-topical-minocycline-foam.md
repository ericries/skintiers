Two identical phase 3 RCTs of a topical minocycline foam (FMX101 4%) found significantly greater reduction in inflammatory lesions versus vehicle in both trials, and superior Investigator's Global Assessment (IGA) success in one of the two.[^1]

**Design:** "Two identical phase 3" randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled studies (referred to as study 04 and study 05), 12 weeks in duration, with subjects "randomized 2:1 to once-daily FMX101 4% or foam vehicle."[^1]

**Population / n:** "A total of 961 subjects were enrolled (study 04, N = 466; study 05, N = 495)," with moderate-to-severe acne vulgaris; the abstract does not break n down further by treatment vs. vehicle arm within each study.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Once-daily topical FMX101 4% (minocycline foam) versus foam vehicle, applied for 12 weeks.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** Two co-primary endpoints at week 12: "FMX101 4% demonstrated a significantly greater reduction in inflammatory lesions in both studies (P < .05)" and "a greater rate of treatment success in study 05 according to the Investigator's Global Assessment (P < .05)": the IGA co-primary endpoint reached significance in study 05, but the abstract does not state that it reached significance in study 04. Noninflammatory lesion count (a secondary endpoint) "was also significantly reduced" with FMX101 vs. vehicle in both trials. "Pooled analyses of the 2 studies demonstrated statistical significance for both coprimary end points (all P < .05)." "Skin-related adverse events were reported in less than 1% of subjects treated with FMX101 4%." The abstract gives no percentage lesion-reduction figures, no exact IGA success rates, and no exact p-values beyond "P < .05" for either study.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Direction and statistical significance are reported (P < .05) for inflammatory-lesion reduction in both trials and for IGA success in one of the two trials, but no magnitude (percent reduction, responder rate, or between-arm difference) is given in the abstract, so the clinical effect size cannot be quantified from this source alone.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults/adolescents with moderate-to-severe [[acne]] treated for 12 weeks with a topical antibiotic vehicle-controlled design; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full JAAD text, so exact lesion-count percentages, IGA responder rates, and full adverse-event breakdowns could not be verified. The key limitation from the abstract alone: IGA success was individually significant only in study 05 (study 04 not stated as significant), though pooled analysis of the two studies met both co-primary endpoints (all P < .05).
