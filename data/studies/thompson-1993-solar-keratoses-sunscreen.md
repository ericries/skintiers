An Australian randomized controlled trial found that daily application of an SPF-17 sunscreen, versus a base-cream control, reduced solar (actinic) keratosis counts over one summer, with a clear dose-response relationship: independent RCT evidence for [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] beyond the single Nambour cohort already cited there.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized controlled trial, one Australian summer (September 1991 to March 1992).[^1]

**Population / n:** 588 participants aged 40 and older in Australia.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Daily application of broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 17) to the head, neck, forearms, and hands versus a base-cream control applied the same way.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "The mean number of solar keratoses increased by 1.0 per subject in the base-cream group and decreased by 0.6 in the sunscreen group (difference, 1.53; 95 percent confidence interval, 0.81 to 2.25)." "The sunscreen group had fewer new lesions (rate ratio, 0.62; 95 percent confidence interval, 0.54 to 0.71) and more remissions (odds ratio, 1.53; 95 percent confidence interval, 1.29 to 1.80)." "There was a dose-response relation: the amount of sunscreen cream used was related to both the development of new lesions and the remission of existing ones."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Sunscreen users saw existing lesion counts fall (net swing of 1.53 lesions/subject vs. base cream) while also developing new lesions at about 62% the rate of controls (rate ratio 0.62, a ~38% reduction) and clearing existing lesions at about 1.5x the odds (odds ratio 1.53); more cream used tracked with both outcomes, supporting a causal dose-response reading (not a chance finding).[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to older adults (40+) in a high-UV Australian setting using daily SPF-17 sunscreen over a single summer; it demonstrates prevention/regression of solar keratoses (a precancerous lesion), not a directly measured skin-cancer endpoint; the authors state the skin-cancer risk reduction is "by implication" only. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full NEJM text, so methodological details (e.g., blinding, adherence measurement) beyond the abstract are unverified here.[^1]
