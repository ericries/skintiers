A randomized study of 90 subjects with facial hyperpigmentation found that a [[thiamidol]] serum and a fuller Thiamidol-based day/night regimen both produced visible reductions in [[hyperpigmentation]] by colorimeter and skin-tone measures as early as week 2, continuing through 12 weeks of use.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized study measuring facial pigmentation by colorimeter and individual typology angle (ITA), comparing two Thiamidol-containing formulations over 12 weeks with a 6-week regression period after stopping treatment.[^1]

**Population:** 90 subjects presenting with facial hyperpigmentation (melasma, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, and/or solar lentigines): n=43 in the Thiamidol serum arm, n=47 in the Thiamidol regimen arm.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Thiamidol-based serum applied twice daily (morning and night) versus a fuller Thiamidol-based regimen (day lotion with SPF 30 plus serum in the morning; night cream plus serum at night); the abstract does not describe a placebo or untreated control arm.[^1]

**Primary result:** Both groups showed a significant visible reduction in facial hyperpigmentation, measured as increases in L* and ITA° values, plus increased skin radiance and shine, starting as early as week 2 and continuing to improve through week 12 relative to baseline. At week 12, radiance and shine improvements trended higher in the regimen group than the serum group.[^1]

**Effect size:** The abstract reports directional and statistical-significance language ("significant visible reduction") but does not give numeric magnitudes (percent change, mean L*/ITA° values, or p-values) for either arm.

**Generalizes to:** Adults with visible facial hyperpigmentation from melasma, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, or solar lentigines, comparing a simpler serum-only routine against a fuller sunscreen-plus-serum-plus-night-cream regimen.

**Key limitation:** No placebo or vehicle-control arm is described, so the reduction cannot be separated from regression to the mean, sun-behavior changes during the study, or a general moisturizing/vehicle effect; the abstract also omits actual effect-size numbers, and the trial's funding and any author ties to Thiamidol's manufacturer are not disclosed in the abstract.
