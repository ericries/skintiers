A post hoc pooled analysis of two phase III trials found abrocitinib produced numerically higher and faster itch responses than dupilumab in moderate-to-severe [[atopic-dermatitis]], though confidence intervals for the two primary itch measures overlapped.[^1]

**Design:** Post hoc analysis of pooled data from two phase III randomized controlled trials, JADE COMPARE and JADE DARE, evaluating itch relief and quality-of-life outcomes over 16 weeks of treatment alongside topical therapy. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text.[^1]

**Population / n:** 1,196 patients with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (abrocitinib, n = 588; dupilumab, n = 608).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Abrocitinib 200 mg/day (oral JAK inhibitor) versus dupilumab 300 mg every 2 weeks (injectable IL-4/IL-13 blocker), both alongside topical therapy for 16 weeks.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "At week 16, numerically more patients achieved PP-NRS4 (67% vs 63%) and PP-NRS 0/1 (36% vs 27%) with abrocitinib versus dupilumab, respectively, regardless of baseline itch severity, although 95% confidence intervals overlapped for both measures. Median time to achieve PP-NRS4 (11 vs 29 days) and PP-NRS 0/1 (57 vs 139 days) was shorter with abrocitinib versus dupilumab, respectively." The abstract states no p-values for these comparisons.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Abrocitinib showed a numerical advantage over dupilumab on both the proportion reaching a ≥4-point Peak Pruritus NRS improvement (67% vs 63%) and the proportion reaching an itch-free state, PP-NRS 0/1 (36% vs 27%), and reached both thresholds roughly 2-3x faster by median days (11 vs 29 days; 57 vs 139 days). The authors report these as numerical differences with overlapping 95% CIs, not as statistically significant superiority. Patients who reached the itch-free state had greater quality-of-life and sleep improvements than those with residual itch (PP-NRS ≥2), per pooled DLQI, POEM, and SCORAD sleep-loss measures.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis being considered for systemic abrocitinib or dupilumab therapy alongside topicals; it does not address mild disease or other systemic agents. Key limitation: this is a post hoc, non-randomized comparison across pooled trial arms (not a single head-to-head randomized comparison with a pre-specified primary endpoint for abrocitinib vs. dupilumab), the reported itch differences are described as "numerical" with overlapping 95% CIs rather than statistically tested, and only the abstract was accessed so no additional figures (e.g., exact CI bounds, adverse events) can be verified.[^1]
