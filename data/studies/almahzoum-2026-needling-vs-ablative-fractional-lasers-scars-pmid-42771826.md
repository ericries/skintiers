The independent review behind the downtime-versus-efficacy trade-off this site documents in several places. The procedures themselves are at [[microneedling]], [[fractional-co2-laser-resurfacing]] and [[rf-microneedling-acne-scars]]; none is retold here.[^1]

**Design:** a systematic review following "PRISMA 2020 and the Cochrane Handbook", searching PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science and Cochrane "from inception to 9 April 2026 without restrictions", with a protocol registered in PROSPERO as "CRD420261383840".[^1] Risk of bias used RoB 2, ROBINS-I and the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale.[^1]

**Population:** "Twenty-seven studies involving 1137 participants were included, comprising 17 RCTs, 2 non-randomized comparative trials, and 8 cohort studies."[^1] Most of it is acne scarring: 18 studies and 870 patients on atrophic or hypertrophic acne scars, against 4 studies and 87 patients on striae, 3 and 105 on burn or postburn scars, and 2 and 75 on traumatic scars.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** a needling-based therapy against an ablative fractional laser, in studies enrolling "patients with scars of any etiology".[^1]

**Primary results, and the reason there are no pooled numbers here:** the synthesis is narrative. "Because of heterogeneity, findings were synthesized narratively, with exploratory random-effects meta-analysis where data were sufficiently comparable."[^1] So the direction of each finding is reported and the magnitude is not.

What it reports: "Both treatment categories improved scar appearance." For atrophic acne scars, ablative fractional lasers, "particularly fractional CO2 and, in some studies, Er:YAG systems, suggested greater improvement than microneedling". Against that, "needling-based therapies were associated with shorter downtime, better tolerability, and fewer pigmentary adverse effects", and microneedling radiofrequency "appeared to provide efficacy closer to that of ablative lasers in several individual studies and demonstrated a more favorable safety profile".[^1]

**Effect size:** none usable. No pooled estimate, confidence interval or p-value for any comparison appears in the abstract, and the authors warn that what exploratory pooling they did was sensitive to individual studies.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** atrophic acne scarring most of all, since that is two-thirds of the evidence by study count and three-quarters by patients. The striae, burn and traumatic scar groups are small enough that this page would not carry a claim about any of them.

**Key limitation:** the heterogeneity that forced the narrative synthesis is the finding as much as a caveat. The authors' own framing is that the conclusions "should be interpreted cautiously because of clinical heterogeneity and the sensitivity of the exploratory pooled estimate to individual studies", and that "Treatment selection should remain individualized according to scar type, patient priorities, and acceptable downtime."[^1] A reader wanting to know how much better a CO2 laser is cannot learn it here. A reader choosing between more improvement and less downtime can.
