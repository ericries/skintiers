A systematic review and meta-analysis of 31 studies found no significant increase in depression scores from isotretinoin treatment for [[acne]] versus alternative treatment, and found that depression prevalence and severity significantly decreased after isotretinoin treatment.[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of literature from inception through September 30, 2016, pooling controlled and prospective non-controlled trials of isotretinoin for acne; 31 studies met inclusion criteria.[^1]

**Population / n:** Controlled or prospective non-controlled trials enrolling 15 or more acne patients receiving isotretinoin treatment. The abstract does not state a total pooled patient count across the 31 studies.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Isotretinoin treatment for acne, compared against an alternative acne treatment in the controlled-study subgroup, and against patients' own pre-treatment (baseline) depression scores in the pre/post subgroup.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** In the controlled comparison, depression score change was "not significantly different between patients receiving isotretinoin treatment and those receiving an alternative treatment" (SMD -0.334, 95% CI -0.680 to 0.011). "The prevalence of depression after isotretinoin treatment significantly declined" (RR 0.588, 95% CI 0.382-0.904). Mean depression scores "significantly decreased from baseline" (SMD -0.335, 95% CI -0.498 to -0.172). Conclusion: "Isotretinoin treatment for acne does not appear to be associated with an increased risk for depression. Moreover, the treatment of acne appears to ameliorate depressive symptoms."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** The controlled-trial comparison of depression-score change versus alternative acne treatment is a small, non-significant effect (95% CI crosses zero). The single-arm findings show a moderate, statistically significant reduction in both depression prevalence (RR 0.588) and depression score severity (SMD -0.335) from baseline to after isotretinoin treatment, i.e., improvement rather than worsening.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to acne patients treated with isotretinoin under the trial conditions pooled here (mostly observational/prospective, not randomized). Key limitation, stated in the abstract: "No randomized controlled trials were reviewed; a large inter-study variation was observed," so the pooled effect estimates rest on non-randomized designs with substantial heterogeneity between studies. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full JAAD article, so no additional numbers (e.g., total pooled n, I² heterogeneity statistic) beyond those stated above could be verified.
