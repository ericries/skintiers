A systematic review and meta-analysis of eight RCTs found topical tretinoin produced statistically significant improvements in both fine and coarse facial wrinkles versus vehicle, strengthening the aggregate evidence base for [[tretinoin]] in [[anti-aging]].[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.[^1]

**Population / n:** Eight trials, 1,361 patients; "median age range 29-76 years; average follow-up duration, 16 weeks to 2 years." The abstract does not state how many trials/patients were screened before inclusion, and reports no I² or other heterogeneity statistics.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Topical tretinoin versus vehicle (placebo).[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** Fine wrinkles: "mean difference [MD]: 0.412; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.233-0.590; P<0.001." Coarse wrinkles: "MD: 0.245; 95% CI: 0.119-0.370; P<0.001." Conclusion as stated by the authors: "Topical tretinoin is a safe and effective treatment for fine and coarse facial wrinkles resulting from photodamage."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Both fine and coarse wrinkle scores show statistically significant mean differences favoring tretinoin over vehicle (P<0.001 for each), with the effect somewhat larger for fine wrinkles (MD 0.412) than coarse wrinkles (MD 0.245); the abstract does not state the underlying wrinkle-grading scale, so the clinical magnitude of these mean-difference units cannot be judged from the abstract alone.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Pools RCT data across a wide age range (29-76) and long follow-up spread (16 weeks to 2 years), supporting tretinoin's efficacy for photodamage-related wrinkles generally; however, the authors' "safe" conclusion is not backed by any adverse-event or tolerability data in the abstract, no risk-of-bias or heterogeneity statistics are reported, and only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text, so the wrinkle-grading instrument, per-trial doses/vehicles, and safety findings could not be verified.[^1]
