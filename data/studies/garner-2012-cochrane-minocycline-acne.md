A Cochrane systematic review of 39 RCTs (6,013 participants) found oral minocycline effective for moderate to moderately-severe inflammatory [[acne]], but with no evidence it outperforms other commonly-used acne treatments, and with safety concerns versus other tetracyclines.[^1]

**Design:** Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis; databases (Cochrane Skin Group Specialised Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE from 1946, EMBASE from 1974, LILACS from 1982) searched through November 2011, plus trials registers and reference lists, for randomized controlled trials of minocycline for acne.[^1]

**Population / n:** 39 RCTs totaling 6,013 participants (an update adding 12 new RCTs to the prior review); 16 additional studies were selected specifically for adverse-effects analysis.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** [[minocycline-topical-oral|minocycline]], at any dose, versus an active or a placebo control, in participants with inflammatory acne vulgaris; the abstract names doxycycline specifically in its safety comparison. (A fuller list of active comparators may appear in the full-text review, not the abstract.)[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Although minocycline was shown to be an effective treatment for moderate to moderately-severe acne vulgaris, there was no evidence that it is better than any of the other commonly-used acne treatments." Meta-analysis comparisons were "rarely possible because of the lack of data and different outcome measures and trial durations." On safety: "minocycline is associated with more severe adverse effects than doxycycline," and "Minocycline, but not other tetracyclines, is associated with lupus erythematosus, but the risk is small: 8.8 cases per 100,000 person-years." "The risk of autoimmune reactions increases with duration of use." The abstract states no pooled effect-size numbers (e.g., RR, mean difference, or p-value) for the head-to-head efficacy comparisons.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** No quantitative effect size is stated for minocycline's efficacy versus comparators: the review explicitly could not pool most comparisons due to heterogeneous outcome measures and trial durations, and instead reports a qualitative finding of "effective, but not superior." The one quantified figure in the abstract is a safety statistic: an incidence of 8.8 lupus erythematosus cases per 100,000 person-years attributable to minocycline specifically (not other tetracyclines).[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to people with moderate to moderately-severe inflammatory acne being considered for oral minocycline versus other standard acne therapies. Key limitation, per the authors: the included RCTs were "generally small and poor quality," no trials tested minocycline in acne resistant to other therapies, there is no evidence to guide dosing, and "extended-release preparation" was not shown to be safer than standard preparations despite higher cost. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Cochrane review text, so the underlying per-trial data and any subgroup or pooled statistics beyond what is quoted above could not be verified.[^1]
