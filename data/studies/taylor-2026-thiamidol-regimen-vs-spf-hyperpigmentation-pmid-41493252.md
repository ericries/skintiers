A randomized study found that a thiamidol-containing skincare regimen reduced facial hyperpigmentation more than a standard SPF 30 lotion alone by weeks 8 and 12, supporting thiamidol (a topical tyrosinase inhibitor) as an add-on to photoprotection for [[hyperpigmentation]].[^1]

**Design:** Randomized, active-controlled trial (comparator was SPF 30 lotion, not a vehicle placebo); 12-week treatment plus a 6-week regression phase. Published in J Drugs Dermatol.[^1]

**Population:** 95 subjects aged 18 to 65 with facial hyperpigmentation, quantified by colorimeter and individual typology angle (ITA°): n=47 thiamidol regimen, n=48 standard SPF 30 lotion.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** A thiamidol regimen (Day Lotion with SPF 30 plus Serum in the morning; Night Cream plus Serum in the evening) versus a standard SPF 30 lotion alone.[^1]

**Primary result:** Facial hyperpigmentation (skin lightness, ITA° values, radiance, shine) was significantly reduced from baseline in both groups as early as week 2, and was significantly greater for the thiamidol regimen than for SPF 30 alone at weeks 8 and 12.[^1]

**Effect size:** Reported as statistically significant superiority for the thiamidol regimen at weeks 8 and 12; the abstract gives no numeric effect sizes (ITA°/lightness deltas).[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Adults with facial hyperpigmentation using a daily sunscreen-based regimen.

**Key limitation:** The comparator is SPF alone, not a matched vehicle, so the thiamidol effect is measured against sunscreen rather than an identical placebo cream; the abstract reports no effect-size numbers; and thiamidol (isobutylamido thiazolyl resorcinol) is Beiersdorf's proprietary active, so this cosmeto-clinical study is industry-relevant. It does confirm the well-established point that SPF alone reduces hyperpigmentation, covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] and [[hyperpigmentation]].
