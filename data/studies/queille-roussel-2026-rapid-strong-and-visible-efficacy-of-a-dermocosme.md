A 57-day split-face trial in 16 adults with darker skin phototypes found a twice-daily dermocosmetic (DC) cream significantly outperformed an untreated hemiface on total lesion count and acne-induced hyperpigmentation.[^1]

**Design:** Intra-individual, randomized, split-face, single-centre, 57-day study; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text.[^1]

**Population / n:** 16 adults with dark skin phototypes (IV, V, VI) and mild-to-moderate [[acne]]; 75% women, mean age 26.8 ± 6.7 years; 50% phototype IV, 31.2% phototype V, 18.8% phototype VI; 68.8% mild (GEA 2), 31.2% moderate (GEA 3) acne.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** DC cream applied twice daily to one randomized hemiface versus the untreated hemiface of the same subject; the abstract does not name the cream's ingredients.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "The total lesion count significantly decreased from Day 5 (-17.1%; p < 0.05) until Day 57 (-44.9%; p < 0.01) on the DC-treated side versus - 28.4% on the untreated side at Day 57. Inflammatory and non-inflammatory lesion counts significantly (p < 0.01) decreased from Day 11 to Day 57 with DC cream. AIH marks intensity significantly (p < 0.01) decreased starting Day 11 until Day 57; as did the PAHPI score (-22.7%) after 57 days with hemi-face differences being significantly (p < 0.05) in favor of DC cream."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Total lesion reduction was roughly 17 points larger on the treated side than the untreated side by Day 57 (-44.9% vs -28.4%), and acne-induced hyperpigmentation (AIH) intensity and PAHPI score (-22.7%) improved significantly only on the treated side; the abstract does not report a between-side p-value or confidence interval for the Day-57 total-lesion-count comparison itself, only that inflammatory/non-inflammatory counts and AIH measures were significantly in favor of DC cream.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies narrowly to adults with phototypes IV-VI and mild-to-moderate acne accompanied by [[hyperpigmentation]]; n = 16 at a single centre is small, the DC cream's composition is not disclosed in the abstract, and only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology article, so additional statistical detail (confidence intervals, exact between-group p-values for total lesion count) could not be verified.[^1]
