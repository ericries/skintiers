A post-hoc analysis of a one-year dupilumab trial in adults with [[atopic-dermatitis]] found that EASI-75 (75% improvement in Eczema Area and Severity Index from baseline) most closely tracked clinically meaningful and optimal patient-reported outcomes, with little added patient-perceived benefit beyond that threshold.[^1]

**Design:** Post-hoc repeated-measures regression analysis of a randomized controlled trial, examining relationships between percentage EASI improvement from baseline and patient-reported outcomes (PROMs) across six different measures.[^1]

**Population / n:** 106 adult patients with atopic dermatitis.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** All patients received dupilumab 300 mg every 2 weeks plus topical corticosteroids for one year; the analysis compares patient-reported-outcome responses across EASI improvement bands (e.g., EASI-50, EASI-75, EASI-90) rather than against a separate control arm.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Most clinically meaningful responses in PROMs were associated with 50-75% EASI improvement from baseline with the greatest impact on achieving clinically meaningful and optimal PROM responses when transitioning from EASI-50 to EASI-75 and minimal additional benefit when transitioning from EASI-75 to EASI-90." The abstract gives no p-values, confidence intervals, or per-PROM percentage figures.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** The authors conclude "EASI-75 aligns closely with clinically meaningful and optimal treatment responses from the patient perspective, providing a treatment goal that is both meaningful to patients and visually quantifiable for physicians." This is a threshold-identification finding (which EASI cutoff best matches patient-perceived benefit), not a treatment-effect size; no fold-change or magnitude-of-improvement statistic is given for the PROMs themselves.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults with atopic dermatitis treated with dupilumab plus topical corticosteroids for one year; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full American Journal of Clinical Dermatology text, so no additional numbers, PROM instrument details, or statistical significance figures can be verified. Single-arm/no-comparator-drug design (all patients on dupilumab) means the finding is about outcome-threshold interpretation, not comparative drug efficacy.
