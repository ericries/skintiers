A small double-blind, vehicle-controlled randomized trial found that daily 0.1% tretinoin cream improved the clinical appearance of early, active stretch marks (striae rubrae) versus vehicle, the positive-but-narrow evidence behind the "early red striae only" caveat on the [[stretch-marks]] tier list; the active is covered on [[tretinoin]] and [[retinoids]].[^1]

**Design:** Double-blind, randomized, vehicle-controlled trial; daily application for 6 months, with monthly physical exam and before/after biopsy.[^1]

**Population / n:** 22 patients with early, clinically active stretch marks; 0.1% tretinoin (n = 10) versus vehicle (n = 12).[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** 0.1% tretinoin (retinoic acid) cream versus vehicle cream, once daily to affected areas.[^1]

**Primary result:** After 2 months, tretinoin gave significant improvement in severity scores versus vehicle (P < .05). After 6 months, "eight (80%) of the 10 tretinoin-treated patients had definite or marked improvement compared with one (8%) of the 12 vehicle-treated patients (P = .002)." Targeted marks under tretinoin decreased in mean length and width by 14% and 8%, versus an increase of 10% (P < .001) and 24% (P = .008) under vehicle.[^1]

**Effect size:** Large on the clinical global measure (80% vs 8% definite/marked improvement); the dimensional changes were modest.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Early, clinically active (red/purple) stretch marks, i.e. striae rubrae, not old white striae albae. Small, single-study sample.[^1]

**Key limitation:** Tiny sample (n = 22), short duration, and only early active striae; biopsies showed "no significant differences" in dermal collagen or elastic fibers between groups, so the mechanism of the clinical improvement is unexplained.[^1]
