A systematic review and meta-analysis of 13 randomized controlled trials found that weight-loss interventions produced a small but statistically significant reduction in psoriasis severity and improved quality of life compared to usual care, relevant to overweight or obese people managing [[psoriasis]].[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis, systematically searching five databases and two trial registries from inception to September 3, 2025, including RCTs in adults with psoriasis comparing any weight-loss intervention versus usual care or a lower-intensity weight-loss intervention; random effects meta-analyses were used. Published in J Eur Acad Dermatol Venereol.[^1]

**Population:** 13 RCTs with 1145 participants across 14 comparisons; 11 interventions advised dietary changes (four of which also included physical activity), and three used weight-loss medications.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Weight-loss interventions (diet, diet plus physical activity, or weight-loss medications) versus usual care or a lower-intensity weight-loss intervention.[^1]

**Primary result:** Across 14 comparisons (n=1145, mean weight change difference -6.7 kg), weight-loss interventions produced a greater reduction in PASI than control: mean difference -2.5 (95% CI -3.8 to -1.1, I2=85.2%). Interventions significantly increased the likelihood of achieving PASI75 (RR=1.6, 95% CI 1.1-2.2, I2=22.6%, six comparisons, n=681, MD in weight change -7.3 kg), but not PASI50 (RR=1.5, 95% CI 0.9-2.4, I2=72.8%, four comparisons, n=509, MD in weight change -4.0 kg) or PASI100 (RR=1.6, 95% CI 0.3-9.7, I2=0.0%, two comparisons, n=334, MD in weight change -5.2 kg). Across seven comparisons (n=364, MD in weight change -7.8 kg), interventions significantly improved DLQI versus control: MD -5.0 (95% CI -9.7 to -0.3, I2=96.0%).[^1]

**Effect size:** A roughly 6.7 kg greater weight loss produced about a 2.5-point greater PASI reduction and a 60% relative increase in the likelihood of reaching PASI75, but the PASI50 and PASI100 comparisons involved too few studies to reach significance; heterogeneity across analyses was mostly high to very high (I2 up to 96%).[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Adults with psoriasis who are overweight or obese, since the abstract notes 80% of people with psoriasis also live with overweight or obesity; the source studies mostly used dietary or combined diet-and-activity interventions, with a minority using weight-loss medications.[^1]

**Key limitation:** Heterogeneity across comparisons was high to very high (I2 up to 96.0% for the DLQI outcome), and the PASI50 and PASI100 subgroup analyses were limited by few included studies; several authors reported industry ties, including one author (the senior psoriasis clinician-author) with institutional funding, consulting fees and speaking fees from multiple pharmaceutical companies (including Almirall, Boehringer Ingelheim, Bristol Myers Squibb, Johnson & Johnson, Novartis and UCB), all disclosed as outside the submitted work; the review itself was supported by non-industry grants (British Skin Foundation, NIHR). This is a systemic/behavioral intervention, not a topical; the condition itself is covered on [[psoriasis]].
