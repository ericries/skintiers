A phase IIb dose-finding trial (139 adults, China) of pumecitinib 3% gel, an investigational topical selective JAK1/2 inhibitor, in mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis. It is not an approved drug and this is not a registration trial.[^1]

**Design:** Multicentre randomized, double-blind, parallel, placebo-controlled phase IIb trial over 8 weeks of treatment. Participants were randomized 1:1:1 to three arms. The stated objectives were to assess efficacy and safety "and to determine the optimal treatment regimen", which is what a phase IIb is for: choosing a dose, not confirming a benefit.[^1]

**Population / n:** 139 adults with mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis. Arms were pumecitinib 3% gel twice daily (n = 47), pumecitinib 3% gel once daily (n = 46), and placebo (n = 46). All listed sites are in China.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Pumecitinib 3% gel (development code PG-011), described as "a novel selective Janus kinase (JAK)1/2 inhibitor, applied topically", at two dosing frequencies versus placebo gel.[^1]

**Primary result (percentage change in EASI from baseline to week 8):** "the percentage change from baseline in EASI score in the pumecitinib 3% gel twice daily, pumecitinib 3% gel once daily and placebo groups was -83.6%, -44.0% and -22.0%, respectively". Both active regimens beat placebo (P < 0.006), and twice daily beat once daily (P < 0.001).[^1]

**Secondary outcomes:** Investigator's Global Assessment of 0 or 1 with at least a 2-point improvement, EASI 50, EASI 75, EASI 90, and quality of life were all evaluated. The abstract reports only that these "were also improved in participants in the pumecitinib groups vs. those in the placebo group", without figures, so no responder rates can be quoted here.[^1]

**Safety:** "the rate of adverse events in the pumecitinib and placebo groups was 48% and 48%, respectively", and safety profiles were "generally similar" between groups. Mean plasma drug concentrations were low, "range 38-104 pg mL-1", over the 8 weeks, which is the relevant measurement for a topical JAK inhibitor because it speaks to how little reaches the bloodstream.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** The dose separation is the most useful finding: twice-daily dosing nearly doubled the EASI reduction of once-daily (-83.6% versus -44.0%), so frequency mattered more than the presence of the drug alone. Read the -83.6% with care. It is a percentage change in a continuous severity score in a **mild-to-moderate** population over 8 weeks, not a clearance rate, and the placebo arm also improved 22%.

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults with mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis; the general disease context is on [[atopic-dermatitis]]. Three limits matter together. This is **phase IIb**, so the drug is investigational and unapproved, and phase IIb results routinely shrink in larger confirmatory trials. The trial is **small** at roughly 46 per arm and **short** at 8 weeks, for a chronic relapsing disease. And one listed author is affiliated with Prime Gene Therapeutics Co., Ltd, the drug's developer, which the paper discloses by affiliation. For a topical JAK inhibitor that is actually approved and carries a full label, see [[delgocitinib]].
