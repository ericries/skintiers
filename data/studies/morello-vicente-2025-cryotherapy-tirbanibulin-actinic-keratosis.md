A 2025 randomised trial asking whether following cryotherapy with a month-later course of topical tirbanibulin 1% clears more actinic keratoses than cryotherapy alone, in solid organ transplant recipients.[^1] It does, substantially, over four months. The condition is on [[actinic-keratosis]]; neither tirbanibulin nor cryotherapy has a page here yet.

**Design:** single-centre, randomised, intraindividual comparative trial with blinded evaluation. Each patient supplied two comparable 25 cm2 areas; one received cryotherapy followed by tirbanibulin after one month, the other cryotherapy alone.[^1]

**Population:** 37 patients completed the study, all solid organ transplant recipients. The abstract gives no age range, sex distribution, transplanted organ, immunosuppressive regimen or baseline lesion count.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** cryotherapy then 1% topical tirbanibulin against cryotherapy alone. Note what this does not test: tirbanibulin on its own. Every area in the trial was frozen first, so the result is an increment on top of cryotherapy, not a head-to-head between the two.[^1]

**Primary result:** at four months, actinic keratosis count fell by 81.6% (SD 26.9) in the sequential area against 53.4% (SD 32.3) with cryotherapy alone, P < .001.[^1]

**Effect size:** a 28 percentage point difference in lesion reduction, which is large, and the standard deviations are wide on both arms (26.9 and 32.3), so individual responses varied a great deal around those means. The abstract states that complete and partial responses, lesion persistence and new actinic keratosis appearance "also favored sequential therapy (P < .05)" and gives no figure for any of them, so the direction is supported for those four outcomes and the magnitude is not. Adverse effects were reported as mostly mild, with a mean local skin response of 4.57 on a scale the abstract reports only as a range of 1 to 11.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** solid organ transplant recipients at one centre. That is the point of the trial and also its boundary: this group is immunosuppressed and carries a far higher actinic keratosis and squamous cell carcinoma burden than the general population, so neither the 81.6% figure nor the 28 point gap should be read across to an ordinary patient with sun damage. Nothing here speaks to [[fluorouracil-topical]] or [[imiquimod]], which were not comparators.

**Key limitation:** the authors name two, a four-month follow-up and unverifiable adherence to home-applied tirbanibulin.[^1] The follow-up matters most, because the reason to treat actinic keratoses at all is squamous cell carcinoma risk over years, and four months cannot speak to it. Add a third the abstract does not raise: the intraindividual design controls elegantly for patient-level confounding, but the treated areas come in pairs from 37 people, so the 74 that two-per-patient implies are not 74 independent observations, and no adjustment for that clustering is reported. The abstract never states a total area count; 74 is arithmetic on its own design. Add a fourth, that "blinded evaluation" is stated without saying whether patients could tell which side had received a cream for a month; they almost certainly could.
