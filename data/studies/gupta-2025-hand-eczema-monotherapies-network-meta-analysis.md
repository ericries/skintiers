A Bayesian network meta-analysis pooled randomized trials of prescription monotherapies for chronic hand eczema (CHE), a common condition distinct from [[atopic-dermatitis]], and ranked topical delgocitinib as the most efficacious of the treatments compared.[^1]

**Design:** Bayesian network meta-analysis under a fixed-effects model, drawing randomized trials from PubMed and Scopus; the outcome was mean change in the Hand Eczema Severity Index (HECSI) at 12 and 16 weeks, with treatments ranked by Surface Under the Cumulative Ranking Curve (SUCRA) values and all models adjusted for baseline severity plus separate sex- and age-adjusted sensitivity analyses. The authors declare no conflicts of interest.[^1]

**Population:** 5 randomized trials contributing 10 active comparators for chronic hand eczema, among them two regulator-approved treatments, oral alitretinoin 30 mg once daily and topical delgocitinib 20 mg/g twice daily (the 20 mg/g cream was FDA-approved for CHE in July 2025).[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** The 10 comparators, compared indirectly through the network, were alitretinoin 30 mg (oral); ARQ-252 cream at 0.1% once daily, 0.3% once daily, and 0.3% twice daily (topical); delgocitinib at 1, 3, 8, and 20 mg/g (topical); dupilumab 300 mg every 2 weeks (subcutaneous); and PUVA (systemic).[^1]

**Primary result:** Topical delgocitinib 8 mg/g twice daily was ranked the most efficacious across all three networks, in both the base and the sensitivity analyses. It was significantly more efficacious than oral alitretinoin 30 mg on 12-week HECSI reduction (mean difference 24.65, 95% CI 9.05 to 40.07, p<0.05) and on 16-week HECSI reduction (mean difference 21.56, 95% CI 2.16 to 41.09, p<0.05).[^1]

**Effect size:** The delgocitinib-over-alitretinoin advantage was roughly 22 to 25 HECSI points and held after both sex and age adjustment. Dupilumab beat alitretinoin at 16 weeks in the base analysis (mean difference 23.06, 95% CI 0.66 to 45.35, p<0.05) but the difference lost significance after sex or age adjustment.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Adults with chronic hand eczema choosing among newer systemic and topical prescription monotherapies; these are physician-prescribed drugs, not skincare products. The study notes CHE is a distinct diagnosis from [[atopic-dermatitis]] and a highly prevalent occupational skin problem.

**Key limitation:** Only 5 trials fed the network, and the top-ranked delgocitinib 8 mg/g is not the strength regulators approved (20 mg/g); the model was fixed-effects with covariate adjustment done ecologically (aggregate-level rather than individual-patient data), and common everyday options such as topical corticosteroids, calcineurin inhibitors, and moisturizers were excluded for lack of shared comparators.[^1]
