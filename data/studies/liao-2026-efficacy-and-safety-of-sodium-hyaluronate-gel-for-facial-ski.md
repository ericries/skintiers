A multicenter randomized controlled trial of injectable non-cross-linked sodium hyaluronate gel found large, sustained improvements in global aesthetic rating and skin dryness/dullness versus no treatment.[^1]

**Design:** Multicenter randomized controlled trial in a Chinese population, with blinded Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale (GAIS) evaluations; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text.[^1]

**Population / n:** 420 participants enrolled, randomized 2:1 (treatment:control); n≈280 vs n≈140 (derived from the stated 2:1 ratio; per-arm counts not given in the abstract).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Three full-face intradermal injections of non-cross-linked [[hyaluronic-acid]] (sodium hyaluronate) gel, spaced three weeks apart, versus a no-intervention control (not a sham/vehicle injection).[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "90.63% vs 3.65%" improvement on the Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale, and "83.20% vs 3.65%" composite improvement rate for skin dryness and dullness, both treatment vs control (p < .0001); effects were "sustained" through six months. "Peak efficacy rates for fine lines and roughness reached 51.59% and 50.00%, respectively." Adverse events were reported as mild and transient, with declining incidence over the study; the abstract gives no specific adverse-event percentage.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** A roughly 87-percentage-point absolute difference in GAIS responder rate and a roughly 80-point difference in dryness/dullness composite response, both favoring the HA gel injections over no treatment, with lower (but still substantial) response rates (~50%) for fine lines and roughness specifically. No relative-risk, odds-ratio, or fold-change figure is given in the abstract.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to intradermal injection of this specific non-cross-linked HA gel in a Chinese trial population seeking facial rejuvenation, not to topical [[hyaluronic-acid]] serums, and the comparator was untreated controls rather than a sham-injection/vehicle arm, so the very large effect sizes are not isolated from placebo/expectation and injection-procedure effects; evaluator blinding (GAIS) is stated but participant/injector blinding is not. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text, so methodological detail on randomization, blinding of participants, and full adverse-event data could not be verified.[^1]
