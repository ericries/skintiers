A phase III randomized trial of oral apremilast, a PDE4 inhibitor taken by mouth rather than applied to skin, in patients aged 6 to 17 with moderate-to-severe plaque [[psoriasis]]; the 52-week results report continued improvement after the placebo-controlled phase ended at week 16.[^1]

**Design:** Phase III, multicentre, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial (SPROUT, NCT03701763). Randomization was 2:1. Placebo-controlled for 16 weeks, after which all patients received apremilast through week 52.[^1]

**Population:** 245 patients randomized, aged 6-17, with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis "inadequately controlled by or intolerant to topical therapy".[^1] 221 entered the apremilast extension phase (apremilast/apremilast n=149; placebo/apremilast n=72) and 186 completed 52 weeks (n=125 and n=61).[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Apremilast 20 mg twice daily for patients weighing 20 to under 50 kg, or 30 mg twice daily for those at or above 50 kg, versus placebo for weeks 0-16; apremilast for all from week 16 to week 52.[^1]

**Primary result:** Static Physician Global Assessment response (score 0 or 1 with at least a 2-point reduction from baseline) rose from 30.1% at week 16 to 47.7% at week 52 in the apremilast/apremilast group, and from 9.8% to 44.4% in the placebo/apremilast group. PASI-75 rose from 42.3% to 60.4% and from 13.4% to 63.9% in those two groups respectively.[^1]

**Effect size:** At the one timepoint with a comparator, week 16, sPGA response was 30.1% on apremilast against 9.8% on placebo, a difference of about 20 percentage points. The week-52 figures of roughly 45-48% sPGA response and 60-64% PASI-75 have no comparator at all.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Children and adolescents aged 6 to 17 with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis that topical treatment has not controlled, considering an oral systemic drug. It says nothing about topical skincare, and nothing about adults, for whom the apremilast evidence is separate; see [[merola-2026-apremilast-genital-psoriasis-discreet-pmid-41369155]] for the adult genital-psoriasis trial with the same crossover design.

**Key limitation:** Every patient was on apremilast after week 16, so the headline 52-week numbers are uncontrolled and cannot be read as apremilast versus placebo. The two groups converge by week 52 (47.7% and 44.4%), which is what happens once both arms are taking the same drug, and says nothing about whether the effect keeps growing with time. The abstract reports "No new safety signals were observed" without giving adverse-event rates. Five of the ten authors are employees of and stockholders in Amgen, which markets apremilast, and the other five disclose investigator, consultant, speaker or advisory-board ties to Amgen or other pharmaceutical companies.[^1]
