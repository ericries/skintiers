The main human study behind topical salicin, and it has no control arm of any kind. Every result in it is a change from the participant's own baseline after twelve weeks of using a serum twice daily. Willow bark's general story is at [[willow-bark-extract]] and is not retold here.[^1]

**Design:** "This single-center study enrolled 30 female subjects", who "used the study serum product containing 0.5% salicin on their face twice daily for 12 weeks".[^1] Searched in full, the abstract contains no occurrence of random, placebo, vehicle, control, blind or split-face, so there is no comparator and no stated blinding.

**Population:** 30 women "showing mild to moderate signs of aging, between the ages of 35 and 70 having Fitzpatrick skin types ranging between I and IV". "Twenty-nine of 30 subjects successfully completed the study."[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** a serum **containing** 0.5% salicin, against nothing. This is the distinction the conclusion skips: what was tested is a finished formulation, so any result belongs to the whole serum, not to the salicin in it. A twelve-week twice-daily moisturising routine would be expected to improve several of these measures on its own.

**Primary results:** investigator grading on "a nine-point scale (0 = none, 1-3 = mild, 4-6 = moderate, 7-9 = severe)" at baseline and weeks 1, 4, 8 and 12.[^1] The abstract reports "statistically significant improvements in wrinkles, tactile roughness, pore size, radiance, and overall appearance at week 1 time point (P ≤ 0.05) against baseline", then "mottled pigmentation, global firmness, and jaw-line contour at week 4", with "Cutometry, corneometry, and ultrasound measurements" significant "at week 12 time point (P ≤ 0.05) against baseline".[^1] "No tolerability issues were reported."[^1]

**Effect size:** not reportable. The abstract gives no mean change, no standard deviation and no confidence interval for any outcome, only the phrase significant against baseline attached to a list of graded attributes. Significant improvement in wrinkles at **week 1** is the figure to look at hardest: one week is short for a wrinkle change and easy for hydration to produce.

**Generalizes to:** at most, women aged 35 to 70 with Fitzpatrick I to IV and mild to moderate ageing, using this serum. It does not establish anything about salicin as an ingredient, because no arm of this study omitted it.

**Key limitation, and it is structural:** this is a company study of its own product, with no control, concluding that the ingredient works. The author affiliation is "Nu Skin Enterprises, Global Research and Development, Provo, UT, USA", and the corresponding address is an @nuskin.com one.[^1] PubMed records no conflict-of-interest or funding statement. The stated premise is unpublished: "Based on unpublished in-house comprehensive consumer clinical studies, it is believed salicin may have anti-aging capabilities when applied topically to human skin."[^1] And the mechanism it borrows is from a different route entirely: "Salicin, an extract from white willow bark, has been researched as a potent anti-inflammatory agent when taken orally."[^1] The conclusion, "it can be concluded that salicin has the ability to reduce the visible signs of skin aging when applied topically", is a claim about an ingredient drawn from an uncontrolled trial of a formulation.[^1]
