A pre-specified subgroup analysis of two pooled phase III trials found that the injectable biologic dupilumab reduced itch and cleared skin lesions in adults with moderate-to-severe prurigo nodularis, and that it worked about equally well in patients with and without a history of atopic conditions (such as [[atopic-dermatitis]]).[^1]

**Design:** Pre-specified subgroup analysis of pooled data from two independent randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 24-week phase III trials (LIBERTY-PN PRIME and PRIME2), run in 16 countries. Patients were randomized 1:1 to dupilumab or matched placebo and stratified by presence or absence of atopic comorbidity history.[^1]

**Population:** 311 adults with moderate-to-severe prurigo nodularis inadequately controlled by topical prescription therapy: dupilumab N=153 (atopic/non-atopic 67/86), placebo N=158 (atopic/non-atopic 68/90).[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Dupilumab 300 mg every 2 weeks versus matched placebo.[^1]

**Primary result:** At week 24, dupilumab beat placebo in both subgroups. Clinically meaningful itch improvement (Worst Itch NRS): atopic 58.2% vs 20.6% (P<0.0001); non-atopic 59.3% vs 17.8% (P<0.0001). Clear or almost-clear skin (IGA PN-Stage): atopic 52.2% vs 16.2% (P<0.0001); non-atopic 41.9% vs 17.8% (P=0.0005). Concomitant itch-and-lesion improvement: atopic 37.3% vs 7.4% (P=0.0057); non-atopic 33.7% vs 10.0% (P=0.007).[^1]

**Effect size:** Large and consistent across subgroups: placebo-adjusted itch-response differences of roughly 38 to 42 percentage points, with similar onset and magnitude regardless of atopic history. Skin pain, quality of life (DLQI), anxiety/depression (HADS), and sleep also improved versus placebo in both subgroups.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Adults with moderate-to-severe prurigo nodularis not controlled by topical prescriptions, with or without atopic comorbidities. Dupilumab is an injectable anti-IL-4Rα prescription biologic, not a skincare product; this study does not speak to topical or over-the-counter care.[^1]

**Key limitation:** This is a subgroup analysis, not a trial powered to compare atopic against non-atopic patients, so the cross-subgroup similarity is descriptive rather than a formally tested equivalence; the trials were industry-funded (Sanofi/Regeneron) and ran 24 weeks, so longer-term durability is not established here.[^1]
