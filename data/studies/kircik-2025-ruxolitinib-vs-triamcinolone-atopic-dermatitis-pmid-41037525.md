A phase 2 dose-ranging trial compared twice-daily 1.5% ruxolitinib cream against a midpotency topical steroid (0.1% triamcinolone) in adults with mild to moderate [[atopic-dermatitis]], finding ruxolitinib matched or exceeded the steroid on clearance and beat it on itch relief.[^1]

**Design:** Phase 2, randomized, dose-ranging trial; triamcinolone was used only during the first 4 continuous weeks of an 8-week vehicle-controlled period for safety reasons, so results here cover only through week 4.[^1]

**Population:** Adults with mild to moderate atopic dermatitis for 2 or more years, comparing 1.5% ruxolitinib cream twice daily to 0.1% triamcinolone cream twice daily (a midpotency topical corticosteroid).[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** [[opzelura-ruxolitinib-cream]] (1.5%, a JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor) twice daily versus 0.1% triamcinolone cream twice daily.[^1]

**Primary result:** At week 4, more ruxolitinib than triamcinolone patients reached EASI-75 (56.0% vs 47.1%) and EASI-90 (26.0% vs 13.7%), and more reached IGA 0/1 with a 2-grade improvement (38.0% vs 25.5%). On itch, significantly more ruxolitinib patients hit a 2-point NRS improvement by day 2 (42.5% vs 20.5%, P=0.0412) and a 4-point NRS improvement by week 4 (62.5% vs 32.3%, P=0.0128).[^1]

**Effect size:** EASI and IGA differences favored ruxolitinib by roughly 9 to 13 percentage points; the itch-relief gap was larger and statistically significant, with ruxolitinib roughly doubling the triamcinolone response rate at both itch checkpoints.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Adults with mild to moderate atopic dermatitis considering a nonsteroidal topical versus a midpotency steroid for short-term (up to 4 week) use; does not establish long-term head-to-head safety or efficacy since triamcinolone was capped at 4 weeks.

**Key limitation:** Triamcinolone was deliberately limited to 4 weeks for safety, so this is not a full 8-week head-to-head; the trial only reports comparative data through week 4. It is also a small phase 2 substudy evaluating the manufacturer's own drug: ruxolitinib cream is marketed by Incyte as Opzelura, and this is a sponsor dose-ranging trial (the abstract itself carries no funding statement).
