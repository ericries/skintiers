The trial behind home radiofrequency body-contouring claims, and the one that names cellulite improvement as an objective and then never reports it. That absence is set out on [[cellulite]] and is not repeated here. This page covers the study's own structure and numbers.[^1]

**Design:** Two separate pieces reported together. "An ex-vivo human skin model was used for histological and biochemical evaluations", and separately twenty four subjects treated the abdomen and thigh.[^1] Reading the whole abstract, it contains no occurrence of randomised, randomized, placebo or blind, and no follow-up interval after the treatment course is given.[^1]

**Population:** "twenty four subjects used the new device on the abdomen and thigh areas". No age range, sex breakdown, BMI or country appears in the abstract. The authors are at the Pitie Salpetriere Hospital and the GREDECO Research Association in Paris.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** A home-use TriPollar radiofrequency device against an untreated contralateral thigh. This matters and is easy to miss: the thigh outcome HAS a within-subject control, because the abstract reports that "On the control thighs a lesser, non-significant reduction was found".[^1] The abdominal outcomes have no comparator, and the ex-vivo measurements have none described.

**Primary results:**

- **Thigh circumference.** "A significant average reduction of 2.4 cm was measured on the treated thighs", against the lesser non-significant change on control thighs.[^1]
- **Abdomen.** "Average abdominal laxity was reduced from 1.4 at baseline to 0.8 following treatments", on a scale the abstract does not name, and "A certain reduction was measured in the abdomen circumferences, although it was not significant".[^1]
- **Ex-vivo model, not people.** "a significant increase of 82% in hypodermal glycerol release", "a 34% alteration in adipocyte appearance" and collagen synthesis "increased by 31%".[^1] These are laboratory measurements on an ex-vivo skin model and are not outcomes in the 24 subjects.

**Effect size:** Not reportable. The abstract gives no standard deviations, no confidence intervals and no p-values, only the word significant attached to some results and not to others.

**The statistical move to watch.** The thigh comparison is reported as a treated side that reached significance beside a control side that did not. That is not a test of the difference between them, and no between-side comparison is reported anywhere in the abstract.[^1] A result can be significant on one side and not the other while the two sides do not differ significantly from each other, which is why this design needs the between-side test it does not publish.

**Generalizes to:** Abdomen and thigh treatment with a home radiofrequency device over an unstated number of sessions. Nothing in it speaks to the face, and nothing speaks to durability, since no post-course follow-up interval is reported.

**Key limitation:** Twenty four subjects, no randomisation or blinding described, no between-side statistical comparison, and a conclusion, that the results "demonstrate the safety and efficacy of the new TriPollar home-use device for body contouring and skin tightening", that is broader than the one outcome with a control behind it.

**Why it is indexed here:** It is the evidence cited for home body radiofrequency on [[home-body-devices-by-whether-body-evidence-exists]], and it shares the TriPollar technology name with [[pollogen-stop-u-tripollar]] WITHOUT being evidence for it: that device is cleared for facial wrinkles, while this study treated the abdomen and thigh and measured circumference and laxity. That page now records the distinction so a reader who finds this study does not read it across. [[cellulite]] carries the absence finding.
