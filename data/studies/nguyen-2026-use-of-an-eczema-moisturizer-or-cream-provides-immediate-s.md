A randomized split-body trial of two products from Galderma's own Cetaphil Restoraderm eczema line (a structural conflict of interest, and the abstract discloses no funding or conflict-of-interest statement) found single applications produced itch relief within under a minute and near-complete itch resolution by 24 hours.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized, blinded, split-body (within-subject) trial with 33 participants; the abstract describes a split-body design (inferred here as each participant applying one product per side, not stated verbatim) and reports no vehicle or untreated-skin control arm.[^1]

**Population / n:** 33 participants with bilateral eczema-prone skin and mild-to-moderate itch.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Cetaphil® Restoraderm Eczema Soothing Moisturizer vs. Cetaphil® Restoraderm Eczema Rapid Relief Cream, a single application of each, split-body.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "The eczema moisturizer and the eczema cream provided rapid itch relief after a single application, with mean times to itch relief of 38 seconds and 43 seconds, respectively. Both treatments significantly reduced itch severity from baseline by 98% to 99% at 24 hours (eczema moisturizer mean NRS: 0.03; P<0.0001; eczema cream mean NRS: 0.09; P<0.0001)."[^1] Baseline NRS values are not stated in the abstract.

**Effect size / interpretation:** On an 11-point itch-severity NRS, mean scores fell to near 0 (0.03 and 0.09) at 24 hours, a 98-99% reduction from baseline for both products, each significant at P<0.0001; the abstract does not report a head-to-head statistical comparison between the two products.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies narrowly to adults with mild-to-moderate itch from eczema-prone skin using these two specific proprietary Cetaphil Restoraderm formulations; both products belong to manufacturer Galderma's own line, a structural conflict of interest, and the abstract discloses no funding or conflict-of-interest statement. There was no vehicle or untreated-skin control arm, so the itch relief cannot be separated from a general moisturizing/vehicle effect, and n=33 with only a 24-hour follow-up is small and short. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Journal of Drugs in Dermatology article. Supports the general case for emollients/moisturizers in [[atopic-dermatitis]] itch management, covered on that page; see also the [[cetaphil]] brand page for the product line.
