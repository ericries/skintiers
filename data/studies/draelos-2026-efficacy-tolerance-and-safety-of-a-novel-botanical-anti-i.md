A double-blinded randomized controlled trial in 60 adults with mild-to-severe [[rosacea]] found a topical anti-inflammatory moisturizer containing [[oat]] and [[aloe]] produced significantly greater reduction in erythema and lesion counts than 0.75% [[metronidazole]] cream over 12 weeks.[^1]

**Design:** Double-blinded, randomized, controlled trial comparing a topical anti-inflammatory moisturizer to a topical prescription comparator, with efficacy and tolerability assessed at baseline and Weeks 4, 8, and 12.[^1]

**Population / n:** 60 adults with mild-to-severe rosacea, randomized between the two arms; the abstract does not state the per-arm n.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Study arm applied a topical anti-inflammatory moisturizer containing oat and aloe daily for 12 weeks; comparator arm applied 0.75% metronidazole cream daily for 12 weeks. Efficacy measured by investigator global assessment (IGA) of erythema and inflammatory lesion counts.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Both study products demonstrated similar IGA scores at Week 4. By Week 8, the study oat and aloe anti-inflammatory moisturizer demonstrated significantly greater reduction in both erythema IGA (50% vs. 22%, p = 0.001) and lesion counts (74% vs. 6%, p = 0.015). At the conclusion of the 12-week study, the anti-inflammatory moisturizer produced a reduction in IGA erythema of 54% vs. 23% (p < 0.001) and a reduction in inflammatory lesion counts of 74% vs. 17% (p = 0.044). No tolerability issues were noted."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** At Week 12 the oat/aloe moisturizer more than doubled the erythema-IGA reduction seen with metronidazole (54% vs. 23%) and produced a substantially larger reduction in inflammatory lesion counts (74% vs. 17%), both differences reaching statistical significance; the authors conclude the botanical moisturizer "was superior and outperformed 0.75% metronidazole cream in both erythema and lesion reduction in rosacea subjects after 8 and 12 weeks of treatment."[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults with mild-to-severe rosacea over a 12-week window against an active prescription comparator (not vehicle/placebo), so it speaks to relative, not absolute, efficacy; the abstract does not report per-arm n, dropout, or how many subjects reported no tolerability issues beyond the blanket statement, and only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text of the paper.[^1]
