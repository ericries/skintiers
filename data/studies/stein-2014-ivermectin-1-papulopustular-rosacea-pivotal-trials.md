Two identically designed phase 3 trials found once-daily [[ivermectin-topical|ivermectin 1% cream]] significantly outperformed vehicle for treatment success and inflammatory lesion reduction in [[rosacea-papulopustular|papulopustular rosacea]].[^1]

**Design:** Two identically designed, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled studies of once-daily ivermectin 1% cream (IVM 1%) versus vehicle for 12 weeks; the abstract does not state the number of study centers.[^1]

**Population / n:** Subjects with moderate to severe papulopustular rosacea (PPR); the abstract does not state the number of subjects enrolled in either trial.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Ivermectin 1% cream applied once daily versus vehicle cream applied once daily, each for 12 weeks.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "In both studies, a greater proportion of subjects in the IVM 1% group achieved treatment success (IGA 'clear' or 'almost clear'): 38.4% and 40.1% vs 11.6% and 18.8% for vehicle (both P<.001), respectively." Secondary lesion-count result: "Ivermectin was superior to vehicle in terms of reduction from baseline in inflammatory lesion counts (76.0% and 75.0% vs 50.0% for both vehicle groups, respectively)." "For all endpoints, starting at week 4 and continuing through week 12, IVM 1% was statistically significantly superior (P<.001)." Safety: "Fewer subjects treated by IVM 1% reported dermatologic AEs, and a higher proportion of subjects were observed to have no skin dryness or itching compared to vehicle." No adverse-event incidence percentages or exact n per arm are given in the abstract.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Roughly 2-3x the vehicle response rate for treatment success (e.g., 38.4% vs 11.6% and 40.1% vs 18.8%), both trials significant at P<.001, plus a larger absolute reduction in inflammatory lesion counts (76.0%/75.0% vs 50.0% vehicle).[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults with moderate to severe papulopustular rosacea treated for up to 12 weeks; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full J Drugs Dermatol text, so sample sizes per arm, exact adverse-event rates, and any longer-term or maintenance data cannot be verified here. The abstract does not disclose vehicle-formulation details or dropout/discontinuation numbers.[^1]
