A randomized open-label trial in Indian women with facial melasma found 5% cysteamine and 2% kojic acid creams produced similar improvement at 16 weeks, with no statistically significant difference between them.[^1]

**Design:** Prospective, randomized, open-label, comparative clinical study; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text.[^1]

**Population / n:** 72 adult females with facial melasma, randomized 1:1 into two groups (abstract does not state per-group n).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** 5% cysteamine cream vs. 2% kojic acid cream, each applied once daily at night for 16 weeks.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** At week 16, the cysteamine group showed a "numerically greater mean percentage reduction in mMASI scores (12.25% vs. 10%)" than kojic acid, and melanin content by Mexameter fell "5.13% vs. 4.81%"; the abstract states these differences "were not statistically significant." No p-values are given in the abstract. "No abnormal clinical or functional signs were reported during the study."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Both creams reduced mMASI and Mexameter-measured melanin content from baseline, with cysteamine numerically ahead on both measures, but the between-group difference did not reach statistical significance, so the trial does not establish superiority of either agent over the other.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to Indian adult women with facial melasma treated for 16 weeks; open-label design (no blinding) and no reported statistical test or p-value for the between-group comparison are the key limitations, and only the abstract was accessed so exact per-group n and any additional endpoints (cosmetic acceptability, subject questionnaire results) cannot be verified.
