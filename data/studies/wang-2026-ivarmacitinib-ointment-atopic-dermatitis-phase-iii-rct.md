A phase III vehicle-controlled trial reported that ivarmacitinib ointment, a topical JAK1 inhibitor, cleared more mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis than its vehicle at 8 weeks (EASI 75 in about 43 to 45 percent versus 18 percent), with itch relief within 48 hours and no skin atrophy, positioning it as a potential steroid-sparing topical.[^1]

**Design:** Multicentre, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled adaptive phase II/III trial at 27 sites in China; this is the phase III evaluation. An 8-week vehicle-controlled period, with vehicle patients re-randomized to active treatment in a blinded extension through week 52.[^1]

**Population / n:** Adults aged 18 to 75 with Hanifin-Rajka-defined mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis, randomized 1:1:1 to ivarmacitinib ointment 0.5%, ivarmacitinib ointment 1%, or vehicle (roughly 122 patients per group).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Ivarmacitinib ointment 0.5% or 1%, described as a highly selective topical Janus kinase 1 (JAK1) inhibitor, applied twice daily, versus vehicle, for 8 weeks.[^1]

**Primary results (co-primary endpoints at week 8, verbatim):** "significantly more patients achieved an IGA response with ivarmacitinib than with vehicle [ivarmacitinib 0.5%: 21.3% vs. 10.6% (P = 0.02); ivarmacitinib 1%: 26.2% vs. 10.6% (P = 0.001)]." For EASI 75, "ivarmacitinib 0.5%: 42.6% vs. 17.9%; ivarmacitinib 1%: 45.1% vs. 17.9% (both P < 0.001)." Relief from pruritus "was seen within 48 h and maintained through week 52."[^1]

**Effect size:** At the 1% dose, EASI 75 was about 45% versus 18% on vehicle (roughly a 27 percentage-point absolute difference), and IGA clear or almost-clear about 26% versus 11%; the 0.5% dose was slightly lower. Most patients did not reach EASI 75, and the absolute IGA response rate stayed near a quarter.

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults with mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis, and the trial ran entirely in China across 27 sites. Key limitations stack up: the absolute response rates are modest (about a quarter reached IGA clear or almost-clear at 8 weeks), it is a single-country trial, and ivarmacitinib ointment is an investigational drug not yet broadly marketed, so this is early regulatory-stage evidence rather than a settled treatment. Safety over the 8-week controlled period looked clean (no skin atrophy, telangiectasia, or application-site irritation), with mostly mild treatment-emergent adverse events (42.6%, 56.6%, and 52.0% in the 0.5%, 1%, and vehicle groups).
