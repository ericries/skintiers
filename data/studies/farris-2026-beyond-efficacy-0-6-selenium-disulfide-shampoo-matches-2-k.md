A randomized, double-blind trial in moderate-to-severe scalp seborrheic dermatitis/dandruff found a 0.6% micronized selenium disulfide shampoo statistically matched 2% ketoconazole shampoo on efficacy while beating it on cosmesis (hair feel/appearance).[^1]

**Design:** Single-center, 6-week, randomized, double-blind, head-to-head comparison of two antifungal shampoos.[^1]

**Population / n:** 87 male and female subjects with moderate to severe dandruff.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** 0.6% micronized [[selenium-disulfide]] shampoo (SeS2) vs. 2% [[ketoconazole]] shampoo, used for 6 weeks.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "After 6 weeks of use, Total Scale Score (TSS) decreased by 69.5% in the ketoconazole group, and 77.9% in the SeS2 group, and Symptomatic Scale of Seborrheic Dermatitis (SSSD) decreased by 68.0% in the ketoconazole group and 74.5% in the SeS2 group (all P<0.001). Pruritus severity score decreased by 76.2% and 75.9% in the ketoconazole and SeS2 groups, respectively, and global efficacy and satisfaction, as assessed by blinded dermatologic evaluation, showed significant improvement with both products (all P<0.001). There was no significant difference between the two groups for these outcomes at any time point. Tolerability was excellent for both products. In terms of cosmesis, subjects across diverse curl types favored the SeS2 shampoo with significantly more agreeing that the SeS2 shampoo leaves hair soft, supple, glossy, natural appearing, and was suitable for their hair type when compared to ketoconazole (P<0.001)."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Both shampoos produced large within-group reductions in scaling, symptom score, and pruritus (all P<0.001 vs. baseline), and the between-group P<0.001 figures reported apply to improvement-from-baseline for each product, not to a SeS2-vs-ketoconazole efficacy gap: the abstract states explicitly that the two groups did not differ significantly from each other on TSS, SSSD, pruritus, or global efficacy/satisfaction at any time point. The differentiator was cosmesis, where SeS2 was preferred by significantly more subjects (P<0.001) for softness, suppleness, glossiness, natural appearance, and suitability across curl types; no absolute percentage or count is given for the cosmesis preference margin.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to adults with moderate-to-severe [[seborrheic-dermatitis]] (dandruff) choosing between two established antifungal shampoo actives, with cosmetic/hair-texture tolerability as a differentiator relevant to curly/textured hair. Single-center design, sponsor/formulation-specific (a particular micronized SeS2 formulation vs. a particular ketoconazole formulation), 6-week duration only; and only the abstract was accessed, so no full-text methods, randomization details, or dropout/adverse-event counts could be verified.[^1]
