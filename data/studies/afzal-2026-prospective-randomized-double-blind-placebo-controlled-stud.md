An 8-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial found that an oral antioxidant-rich synbiotic supplement significantly reduced wrinkle severity and increased cheek elasticity, firmness, and viscoelasticity versus placebo.[^1]

**Design:** Prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial; noninvasive facial measurements (hydration, elasticity, erythema, pigmentation) and facial photography at baseline, week 4, and week 8; tolerability also assessed.[^1]

**Population / n:** 36 participants from the Sacramento region; the abstract does not state the per-arm split between treatment and placebo.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Oral antioxidant-rich synbiotic supplement vs. placebo.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "At week 8, the treatment group showed a significant reduction in wrinkle severity (-5.28%, p < 0.01), whereas the placebo group did not. Cheek elasticity increased significantly at week 4 (22.0%, p < 0.01) and week 8 (13.8%, p < 0.05), along with firmness at week 8 (12.2%, p < 0.01). The placebo group showed no significant changes in these parameters. Additionally, the viscoelasticity of the cheek increased at week 4 (30.2%, p < 0.01) in the treatment group, with no change in the placebo group."[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Modest but statistically significant improvements versus placebo across multiple instrumental measures over 8 weeks: -5.28% wrinkle severity, +22.0%/+13.8% cheek elasticity (week 4/week 8), +12.2% firmness, +30.2% viscoelasticity (week 4). The abstract reports no hydration, erythema, or pigmentation figures despite listing them as outcomes, and gives no effect size for tolerability.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Small trial (n=36, single region) of an oral antioxidant-rich synbiotic on instrumentally-measured photoaging markers; supports the general case for dietary/oral antioxidant approaches discussed on [[anti-aging]] and [[probiotics]], but the abstract does not name the specific synbiotic strains/antioxidants, does not report per-arm n, and only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full text, so composition and additional outcome data (hydration, erythema, pigmentation) could not be verified.[^1]
