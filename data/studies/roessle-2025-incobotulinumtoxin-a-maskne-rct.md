A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of intradermal botulinum toxin for mask-associated acneiform eruption. Logged with a scope note: this is an injectable procedure, not a topical, and the site brackets procedures as later-phase context. Topical acne evidence is on [[acne]].

**What it tested:** intradermal incobotulinumtoxinA, 20 units, into the mid and lower face of women with "maskne", the mask-induced acneiform eruption that became common during the COVID-19 pandemic.[^study]

**Design:** "a prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial", which is a stronger design than most cosmetic-dermatology reports this site logs.[^study] Published in a supplement issue of the Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology; the record declares no conflicts of interest and carries no funding statement.[^study]

**Population / n:** "36 women with maskne" randomised 2:1 to toxin or placebo, so roughly 24 against 12, with "Thirty-three participants completed the study".[^study] No age range, skin-type distribution or baseline severity appears in the abstract.

**Intervention vs comparator:** incobotulinumtoxinA 20 U intradermally against placebo, over 112 days.[^study]

**Primary results (verbatim):** "The INCO group showed significantly greater SEr improvement than placebo on days 28, 56, 84, and 112."[^study] Sebum fell "significantly by day 28 (-19.27 μg/cm2 vs. +1.27 μg/cm2 in placebo; p < 0.001)", pore size "differed significantly at days 28 and 56", and erythema fell further in the toxin group ("-43.81 vs. -3.27; p = 0.041").[^study] Patients and investigators both reported improved skin quality on a Global Impression of Change Scale.[^study]

**Effect size:** partially reportable, and the units are the problem. The sebum difference is given in μg/cm2 and is the one figure a reader can interpret. The erythema figures carry no unit in the abstract, and the roughness and pore-size results are reported as significance without a magnitude. No confidence interval appears anywhere.[^study]

**Who it generalises to:** women with mask-associated skin changes, over about four months. It does not generalise to acne treatment decisions, for the reason below, and it says nothing about ordinary facial acne.

**The one key limitation:** the trial is named for acne and does not measure acne. Every endpoint is a biophysical skin-quality surrogate, skin roughness, sebum level, pore size and erythema index, plus a global impression scale.[^study] Checked by string search of the abstract, the words lesion, inflammatory, comedone, papule, pustule and count do not appear in it at all, and neither does any acne severity scale.[^study] A sebum reduction is a plausible mechanism for fewer acneiform papules; it is not a measurement of them. The authors' own wording stays hedged, that the approach is "promising" and that "further investigations are necessary to fully establish its benefits".[^study] No adverse-event or safety reporting appears in the abstract either.
