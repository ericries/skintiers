A split-face randomized trial of an unnamed experimental moisturizing cream against an unnamed control cream in [[sensitive-skin]], notable mainly because its abstract reports no numbers at all.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind, self-controlled split-face trial. One side of the face received the experimental cream, the other the control cream, twice daily for 28 days, with assessments at Day 0, 7, 14 and 28 and 28 days of post-trial safety monitoring.[^1]

**Population:** 35 participants with sensitive skin. No age range, sex distribution, country or severity threshold appears in the abstract.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Experimental cream versus "control cream". The control is not described as a vehicle or a placebo and its composition is not stated, so this is an active-versus-unknown comparison rather than a product-versus-nothing one.[^1]

**Primary result:** Both creams improved symptoms and signs, raised stratum corneum hydration, and lowered transepidermal water loss, lactic acid stinging test scores and Dermatology Life Quality Index scores. The experimental cream "outperforms the control cream in improving pruritus, erythema, and TWEL" and had what the authors call "the unique advantage of reducing CAT scores and increasing CPT", the capsaicin pain test and current perception threshold.[^1]

**Effect size:** Not reportable. The abstract gives no means, no differences, no confidence intervals and no p-values for any outcome, only the direction of change.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** People who already consider their skin sensitive and are choosing between moisturisers. It says nothing about whether either cream beats no treatment, since both arms improved and neither arm was untreated.

**Key limitation:** Two problems compound. The comparator is an undescribed cream rather than a vehicle, so the headline is one unnamed product beating another unnamed product; and the abstract quantifies nothing, so even that comparison cannot be sized. The instruments used, the lactic acid stinging test and capsaicin provocation, are themselves the ones whose validation is thin; see [[sensitive-skin-tests-by-what-validation-shows]]. The authors "declare no conflicts of interest", and no funder is stated in the record.[^1]
