A U.K. multicenter derivation study that used blinded dual-observer assessment and multiple logistic regression to reduce 31 candidate diagnostic features for [[atopic-dermatitis]] down to a six-item minimum criteria set.[^1]

**Design:** Prospective, multicenter derivation study. A working party of 13 dermatologists, two family practitioners, and a paediatrician each selected 10 consecutive cases and 10 controls; two independent observers, blind to the clinical diagnosis and study aim, examined each subject for 31 clinically useful diagnostic features. The key physician's clinical diagnosis served as the gold standard against which the sensitivity and specificity of each of the 31 criteria were tested; multiple logistic regression was then used to derive a minimum diagnostic set, which was checked against a separate community sample.[^1]

**Population / n:** 224 patients studied (120 cases with atopic dermatitis, 102 controls with other inflammatory dermatoses), plus a further validation sample of 150 community-dwelling patients who did not have skin disease.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Not a treatment trial. The "intervention" was structured clinical examination for 31 candidate diagnostic features versus the key physician's independent clinical diagnosis (gold standard); the derived criteria set was then checked (comparator) against the 150-person community sample without skin disease.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Two hundred and twenty-four patients were studied (120 cases and 102 controls)." The derived minimum criteria were: "history of flexural involvement, history of a dry skin, onset under the age of 2, personal history of asthma, history of a pruritic skin condition, and visible flexural dermatitis." "Adjustment for age, sex, region, social class and ethnic group did not alter the choice of final criteria." "The discriminatory value of these criteria was also satisfactory when tested against a further sample of 150 patients drawn from the community, who did not have skin disease." The abstract does not state numeric sensitivity, specificity, odds-ratio, or p-value figures for the final six-item set.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** No quantitative effect-size figures (sensitivity/specificity percentages, ORs, or p-values) are given in the abstract. The result is reported qualitatively: the six-item criteria set was stable to adjustment for demographic covariates and retained "satisfactory" discriminatory value in an independent community sample, supporting it as a minimum, reproducible clinical definition of AD rather than a scored/weighted instrument.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** These are the six discriminators cited verbatim on the [[atopic-dermatitis]] condition page for recognizing the condition; the derivation population was mild-to-moderate, physician-selected cases and controls with other inflammatory dermatoses (not severe or diagnostically ambiguous cases), plus a general community sample. Key limitation: only the PubMed abstract was accessed (not the full Br J Dermatol text), and the abstract itself reports no numeric sensitivity/specificity, PPV, or statistical significance values for the final criteria (only that discrimination was "satisfactory"), so those figures cannot be verified or cited from this source.[^1]
