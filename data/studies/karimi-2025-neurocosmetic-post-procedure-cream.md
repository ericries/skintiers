A self-described pilot study of an unnamed "neurocosmetic Post Procedure Cream" applied after fully ablative erbium:YAG resurfacing of the lower eyelid.[^1] **Record this as unusable for grading.** The abstract reports no sample size, no randomization procedure, and no statistic of any kind, so none of its comparative claims can be weighed. It is logged here so a later pass does not mistake its wording for evidence.

**What it tested:** a post-procedure cream applied after "Contour Tunable Resurfacing Laser (TRL)", described as "an erbium: yttrium aluminum garnet (YAG) fully ablative laser commonly used to treat the delicate lower eyelid skin for undereye rejuvenation".[^1] Ablative resurfacing context is on [[fractional-co2-laser-resurfacing]].

**Design:** the abstract calls it a "pilot study". PubMed indexes the publication type as **Randomized Controlled Trial**, but the abstract itself never states that participants were randomized, nor how they were allocated, nor whether anyone was blinded.[^1] That gap between the index term and the text is the single most important thing on this page.

**Population / n:** NOT STATED. The abstract gives no number of participants, no age range, no skin-type distribution and no completion rate.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** the cream, referred to only as "PPC", against "the commonly used anhydrous topical".[^1] Neither product is named and no composition is given for either, so the comparison cannot be reproduced or even identified. An anhydrous topical after ablative laser is in practice usually a plain occlusive; see [[petrolatum]] for that category.

**Primary result:** there is no primary endpoint and no numeric result in the abstract. Its claims are stated in prose: the cream "ameliorated discomfort by reducing erythema, edema, stinging, and itch post-treatment", "reduced erythema in the treatment area better than the comparator after 3 days", "restored skin tone evenness to pre-procedure condition as early as 7 days post-procedure and maintained skin health for 31 days".[^1] No effect size, confidence interval or p-value accompanies any of these.

**Effect size:** not computable from the abstract.

**Who it generalizes to:** unclear. Without a stated population it cannot be mapped to a reader group, and the setting is in-office post-ablative care rather than routine skincare.

**Key limitation, and why this page says do not use it:** every comparative claim rests on prose with no denominator. Three further cautions compound it. The abstract states an aim of "improving patient retention", which is a clinic business outcome rather than a clinical endpoint. The term "neurocosmetic" is marketing vocabulary, not an established pharmacological or regulatory class. And PubMed records no author affiliations and no conflict-of-interest statement for this article, so sponsorship cannot be assessed either way; no claim is made here about who funded it.[^1] There is no PMC full text, so the numbers could not be recovered from the paper.
