A split-face comparative trial in melasma patients found glycolic-acid vehicles containing either hydroquinone or kojic acid produced roughly comparable pigment reduction, with a minority of patients favoring one side or the other.[^1]

**Design:** Split-face comparison; one side of each patient's face treated with a glycolic acid/kojic acid formulation, the other with a glycolic acid/hydroquinone formulation, assessed by clinical exam and Wood's-light exam with ultraviolet photography.[^1]

**Population / n:** 39 melasma patients.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Glycolic acid + kojic acid (one facial side) versus glycolic acid + hydroquinone (other facial side), within-patient.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Fifty-one percent of the patients responded equally to hydroquinone and kojic acid. Twenty-eight percent had a more dramatic reduction in pigment on the kojic acid side; whereas 21% had a more dramatic improvement with the hydroquinone formulation." These results were not statistically different (i.e., neither formulation was statistically superior for pigment reduction). The kojic acid preparation was more irritating (the abstract reports no significance test for this irritation difference).[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** No quantitative pigment-reduction magnitude (e.g., percent lightening or scale score) is given, only the distribution of which side responded better; both formulations were described by the authors as "highly effective in reducing the pigment in melasma patients," supporting kojic acid as a tyrosinase-inhibiting alternative to hydroquinone rather than a superior one.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Small (n=39), unblinded, split-face design in melasma patients only; the between-side pigment-response difference was not statistically different, and greater kojic-acid-side irritation is reported without any significance test in the abstract; no numeric lightening outcome is stated in the abstract. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the Dermatologic Surgery full text. Underlies the tyrosinase-inhibitor case for [[kojic-acid]] as a [[hydroquinone]] alternative within a [[glycolic-acid]] vehicle for [[hyperpigmentation]]/melasma, covered on those pages.
