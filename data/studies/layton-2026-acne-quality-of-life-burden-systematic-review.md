A systematic review synthesizing 101 studies found that [[acne]] vulgaris adversely affects health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and psychosocial well-being, with the impact concentrated in the emotional and psychological domains and heaviest in adults, females, and people with more severe acne. It is evidence about the burden of the condition, not a test of any treatment.[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42024539174), with structured searches of PubMed and Web of Science and risk of bias assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal tools. No pooled meta-analytic estimate; the synthesis is narrative.[^1]

**Population:** Eligible studies measured any HRQoL or psychosocial outcome as a primary endpoint in "≥ 50 patients with acne" of all ages, "conducted in Europe and North America," published in English from "1 January 2014 to 30 April 2024." In total, "101 studies were deemed eligible for inclusion."[^1]

**What it measured:** Observational HRQoL and psychosocial outcomes in people with acne, not an intervention versus a comparator.[^1]

**Primary result:** The included studies "overall demonstrated the adverse impacts of acne on HRQoL, mental health outcomes, and the lived experiences of people with acne," and "frequently found that acne predominantly affected the emotional and psychological domains of HRQoL, and was particularly burdensome to adults, females, and those with more severe acne."[^1]

**Effect size:** Not quantifiable from this review; the 101 studies "varied widely in terms of study design, population, outcomes, and quality," so the finding is a consistent direction of effect (acne worsens HRQoL and mental health) rather than a single pooled magnitude.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Adolescents and adults with acne in Europe and North America (the review's geographic and language scope); it is most relevant to the emotional and psychological burden of acne, and supports treating acne as more than a cosmetic concern.

**Key limitation:** A narrative synthesis of highly heterogeneous observational studies with no pooled estimate, restricted to English-language European and North American studies from 2014 to 2024 (limiting generalizability), and two of the authors are employees of Sanofi. It maps the burden of acne; it does not show that any specific treatment improves these outcomes.[^1]
