An expert-panel safety assessment concluded that synthetically manufactured amorphous [[silica]] and hydrated silica are safe for use in cosmetics when formulated to be non-irritating.[^1]

**Design:** Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) Expert Panel safety assessment: a panel review of existing safety, use-frequency, and concentration-of-use data, not a clinical trial or experimental study.[^1]

**Population / n:** Not applicable / not stated. This is a panel review of the ingredient literature and reported use data, not a study on human or animal subjects; the abstract states no n.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** None; the Panel reviewed relevant data, "including frequency and concentration of uses," for synthetically manufactured amorphous Silica and Hydrated Silica specifically (the assessment explicitly excludes naturally derived or non-synthetic manufacturing routes). There is no treatment-vs-comparator design.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "The Panel considered the method of manufacture of these ingredients to be of significant importance when reviewing safety. Thus, the current assessment is exclusive to amorphous Silica and Hydrated Silica when manufactured via synthetic methods." Both ingredients "are reported to function as abrasives, absorbents, anticaking agents, bulking agents, and opacifying agents in cosmetic products." The Panel's conclusion: "synthetically manufactured amorphous Silica and Hydrated Silica are safe in the present practices of use and concentration when formulated to be non-irritating."[^1] The abstract gives no quantitative safety figures (no percentages, dose thresholds, or p-values); it is a qualitative panel conclusion.

**Effect size / interpretation:** Not applicable; this is a regulatory-style safety determination, not a measured effect. The finding is a conditional safety conclusion (safe "when formulated to be non-irritating"), scoped only to the synthetic manufacturing route.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to cosmetic formulators' and consumers' safety assessment of synthetically manufactured amorphous [[silica]]/hydrated silica specifically; it does not cover naturally derived or crystalline silica, and it is a literature/use-data review rather than new experimental evidence, so it cannot speak to individual-product formulation quality or irritation risk beyond the panel's general conclusion. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Int J Toxicol supplement text, so the underlying data tables and irritation studies the Panel relied on could not be independently verified here.
