This Cochrane systematic review of 31 RCTs found low-quality, largely inconclusive evidence on whether oral isotretinoin outperforms standard oral-antibiotic-plus-topical treatment for acne, though it may modestly improve physician-rated severity.[^1]

**Design:** Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials, searching the Cochrane Skin Group Specialised Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase, PsycINFO, and LILACS up to July 2017 (updated March 2018).[^1]

**Population / n:** 31 RCTs, 3,836 participants aged 12-55 years with mild to severe acne.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Oral isotretinoin versus standard oral antibiotic plus topical treatment; also isotretinoin dosing-regimen comparisons within the review (continuous low dose vs. intermittent dosing; conventional dose vs. intermittent dosing). Outcomes across the review were measured 8-32 weeks, mean 19.7 weeks.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** For isotretinoin vs. oral antibiotic plus topical treatment: inflammatory lesion count "RR 1.01, 95% CI 0.96 to 1.06" (very low-quality evidence, described as "isotretinoin makes no difference"); physician's global evaluation "RR 1.15, 95% CI 1.00 to 1.32; 351 participants; 2 studies" (low-quality evidence, described as a possible 15% improvement); less serious adverse effects (e.g., dry lips/skin) "RR 1.67, 95% CI 1.42 to 1.98; 351 participants; 2 studies" (low-quality evidence); one serious adverse effect (Stevens-Johnson syndrome) occurred in the isotretinoin group, "RR 3.00, 95% CI 0.12 to 72.98." For dosing-regimen comparisons: continuous low dose vs. intermittent, "MD 3.72 lesions; 95% CI 2.13 to 5.31"; conventional dose vs. intermittent, "MD 3.87 lesions; 95% CI 2.31 to 5.43" (both labeled low-quality evidence).[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** The review's authors conclude they "are unsure if isotretinoin improves acne severity compared with standard oral antibiotic and topical treatment when assessed by a decrease in total inflammatory lesion count, but it may slightly improve physician-assessed acne severity," while carrying a higher (67%) relative risk of minor adverse effects; "evidence was low-quality for most assessed outcomes." The two dosing-regimen results favor continuous/conventional over intermittent dosing by several lesions on a mean-difference scale, and the abstract labels both as low-quality evidence.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies broadly to adolescents and adults (12-55 years) with mild-to-severe acne treated with oral isotretinoin under RCT conditions; it is the clinical-efficacy and adverse-event counterpart to the site's mechanistic sebum-suppression evidence for [[isotretinoin-sebum-suppression]] in [[acne]]. Key limitation: the authors rate evidence "low-quality" (very low-quality for the lesion-count outcome) across most comparisons, chiefly from small trials and reporting inconsistencies, so effect estimates (including the null lesion-count result and the wide, inconclusive serious-adverse-effect confidence interval) should be treated as uncertain; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full Cochrane review, so additional outcomes (e.g., birth-defect surveillance, which the review states none of the included studies reported) could not be verified beyond what is quoted here.[^1]
