The randomised trial behind [[strataderm-silicone-scar-gel]], and the one that tests silicone against an active alternative rather than against nothing. The class evidence is at [[silicone-gel-and-sheeting-for-scars]] and is not retold here.[^1]

**Design:** a "Multicentre, parallel-group, randomized controlled trial" in hospital outpatient clinics, a colocated research centre, or the participant's home.[^1] The outcome assessor and data analyst were blinded for scar thickness.

**Population:** 153 children aged 0 to 18 referred for burn scar management, allocated silicone n = 51, pressure n = 49, combined n = 53.[^1] Median age 4.9 years, median total body surface area burn 1%, 65% male. Small burns in young children.

**Intervention vs comparator:** three arms, "(1) topical silicone gel only, (2) pressure garment therapy only, or (3) combined topical silicone gel and pressure garment therapy".[^1] **There is no untreated arm**, so the trial compares active treatments against each other and cannot show that any of them beats leaving a scar alone.

**Primary result:** largely null. One between-group difference reached significance, thinner scars on silicone alone than on the combined arm, "mean difference (95% confidence interval) = -0.04 cm (-0.07, -0.00), P = 0.05".[^1] Everything else: "No other between-group differences were identified for scar thickness or itch intensity at six months post-burn."[^1]

**Effect size:** 0.04 cm, four tenths of a millimetre, with a confidence interval whose upper bound touches zero and a p value sitting exactly on the conventional threshold. Treat it as a boundary finding.

**The authors' conclusion is the honest summary:** "No difference was identified in the effectiveness of silicone and pressure interventions alone. No benefit to a combined silicone and pressure intervention was identified for the prevention and management of abnormal scarring in children at six months post-burn injury, compared to the silicone or pressure interventions alone."[^1]

**Generalizes to:** children with small burn scars, assessed at six months. Not to adult surgical scars, keloids, or acne scarring, which is what most readers of [[acne-scars]] want to know about.

**Key limitation:** the missing untreated arm. Every child received an active treatment, so the trial establishes equivalence between two options rather than efficacy for either.

**Funding worth noting, because it runs the other way.** Stratpharma, which sells a silicone gel, paid only the publication fee: "Financial support was provided by Stratpharma for the open access publication of this manuscript", while "Stratpharma had no part in the final study design, data collection, or analysis of results", "The funders did not have access to the results prior to them being released publicly", and "The investigators have no financial interest in Strataderm or the Stratpharma Company."[^1] A manufacturer paying to make a null result freely readable is close to the opposite of the pattern at [[ingredient-evidence-by-who-ran-the-only-trial]].
