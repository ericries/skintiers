A four-arm single-blind RCT in 80 melasma patients found kojic acid combined with hydroquinone produced the highest clinical efficacy, ahead of the triple combination and kojic acid alone, with kojic acid plus betamethasone valerate lowest.[^1]

**Design:** Single-blind, randomized, comparative trial at a single tertiary care center; patients applied their assigned cream once daily at night, were assessed every 2 weeks, and the fall in melasma area severity index (MASI) score was calculated at the end of a 12-week study period.[^1]

**Population / n:** 80 melasma patients, randomized into four parallel groups of 20 each (Groups A through D).[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Group A: kojic acid 1% cream. Group B: kojic acid 1% + hydroquinone 2% cream. Group C: kojic acid 1% + betamethasone valerate 0.1% cream. Group D: kojic acid 1% + hydroquinone 2% + betamethasone valerate 0.1% cream. Response was compared by percentage decrease in MASI score.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "The clinical efficacy of group B was the highest followed closely by group D and group A, that of group C being the lowest." "Kojic acid in synergy with hydroquinone is a superior depigmenting agent as compared with other combinations."[^1] The abstract does not state the numeric MASI score values, percentage MASI reduction per group, or any p-values for between-group comparisons; only the efficacy ranking is given.

**Effect size / interpretation:** No quantitative effect size is stated in the abstract; the result is reported only as a rank ordering of efficacy (B > D ≈ A > C), indicating hydroquinone adds more benefit to kojic acid than betamethasone valerate does, and that adding betamethasone valerate does not improve (and may modestly reduce) efficacy relative to kojic acid alone.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Applies to a single-blind, single-center Indian melasma population treated for 12 weeks; only the PubMed abstract was accessed (not the full text), so no numeric MASI scores, percentage reductions, statistical significance, or the safety/adverse-effect findings implied by the title could be verified: the abstract itself reports no side-effect data despite "Safety" in the title. Supplements the two older split-face kojic acid trials for [[kojic-acid]] in [[hyperpigmentation]] and [[melasma]]; see [[hydroquinone]] for the comparator ingredient.
