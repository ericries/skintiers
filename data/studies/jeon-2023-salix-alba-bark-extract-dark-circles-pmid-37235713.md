Mostly a laboratory paper with a short human section attached, and the human section reports no numbers at all. The ingredient's general case is at [[willow-bark-extract]], graded `tier: weak`, and the condition is at [[dark-circles]]; neither is retold here.[^1]

**Design:** two in vitro arms plus one uncontrolled human arm. The lab work used "ELISA and real-time PCR" on human dermal fibroblasts for hyaluronic acid synthesis, then treated human dermal microvascular endothelial cells with conditioned medium from those fibroblasts to test endothelial permeability.[^1] The human arm is described in one sentence: "we conducted a clinical study on 29 subjects by having them apply SABE containing cream for 8 weeks."[^1]

**Population:** 29 subjects. No age range, sex, skin type, country or dark-circle subtype appears in the abstract.

**Intervention vs comparator:** a cream containing 2% Salix alba bark extract, against nothing stated. Searched in full, the English abstract contains no occurrence of random, placebo, vehicle, control, blind or split, so no comparator is described and no blinding is claimed.[^1]

**Primary results:** for the human arm, one sentence with no figures: "Treatment with the cream containing 2% SABE for 8 weeks improved the parameters measuring dark circles, skin microcirculation and elasticity."[^1] The lab results are reported the same way: SABE "increased HA synthesis and regulated HMW-HA-related gene expressions in HDF", and the conditioned medium "alleviated endothelial permeability and led to improved vascular integrity in HMEC-1 cells".[^1]

**Effect size:** none given. The only numeral attached to any outcome in the entire English abstract is the 2% concentration of the extract itself. There is no mean change, no percentage improvement, no spread and no p-value for any measure, in the lab arms or the human one.

**Generalizes to:** nothing that can be stated with confidence. An eight-week open application in 29 people, with no comparator and no reported magnitude, cannot distinguish the extract from the cream base, from eight weeks of moisturising the under-eye area, or from measurement drift.

**Key limitation, beyond the missing numbers:** this is the ingredient maker's own study. All eight authors give a single affiliation, "BioSpectrum Life Science Institute, Yongin, Korea", and the paper's closing sentence is a supplier conclusion rather than a scientific one: "Therefore, SABE can be used as an active ingredient for improving dark circles."[^1] PubMed records no conflict-of-interest or funding statement. A second caution for anyone re-checking this paper: the PubMed record carries a full French translation of the abstract after the English, so a naive text search will match the French and appear to confirm quotations that were never checked against the English.
