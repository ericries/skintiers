A split-face trial, by [[zoe-draelos|Zoe Draelos]], of an unnamed "AGE inhibitory moisturizer" against a bland moisturizer on the other half of the same face, used after one in-office resurfacing procedure, reporting better investigator-scored appearance on the treated side at week 8.[^study]

**Design:** "single-site, randomized, split-face, double-blind, controlled study".[^study] Indexed by PubMed as a Randomized Controlled Trial. Split-face is the strength here: each subject is her own control.

**Population / n:** "42 female subjects, including Fitzpatrick skin types I through VI".[^study] All female, one site. Splitting by phototype leaves roughly 21 per procedure stratum.

**Intervention / comparator:** The AGE inhibitory moisturizer on one half-face against "bland moisturizer" on the other, "twice daily application for 10 weeks", which the abstract describes as 14 days of pre-treatment use plus the post-procedure period.[^study] At baseline each subject then received a single session of either radiofrequency microneedling or a glycolic acid peel. Assessment used "Investigator clinical efficacy was assessed using the modified Griffiths scale at baseline and weeks 1, 2, 4, and 8", with photography by "VISIA Imaging (Canfield Scientific)".[^study]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** For the glycolic acid peel group, the treated side showed "a greater statistically significant improvement at week 8 in 5 attributes: skin clarity, evenness of skin tone, fine lines, elasticity, and overall appearance (all P&lt;0.05)".[^study] For the radiofrequency microneedling group, the treated side "resulted in a greater statistically significant improvement in laxity, clarity, fine lines, elasticity, and overall facial appearance at week 8 versus the control moisturizer-treated side of the face (all P&lt;0.05)".[^study] A second, weaker claim appears alongside these: "the following 10 attributes were statistically significantly improved compared to baseline".[^study]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Unreportable. The abstract gives no means, no point differences and no confidence intervals on the modified Griffiths scale, only that differences cleared P<0.05. Two cautions follow. First, the 10-attribute list is a within-arm change against baseline, not a comparison against the control side, and should not be read as a bigger version of the 5-attribute result. Second, procedure was assigned by phototype rather than at random: Fitzpatrick I to II received radiofrequency microneedling and III to VI received the glycolic acid peel, so a benefit after microneedling cannot be separated from a benefit in lighter skin in this design.

**Applicability + key limitation:** Generalises to adult women in the eight weeks after a single [[microneedling|radiofrequency microneedling]] session or a single [[glycolic-acid|glycolic acid]] peel, across phototypes I to VI, with the phototype confound above. The key limitation for anyone trying to act on it: the abstract never names the product or its composition, so this evidence cannot be attached to anything a reader can buy. The authors' own conclusion is broader than that, calling it "The AGE inhibitory moisturizer is an effective and well-tolerated option for women of all skin tones", which is a claim about a category with one unnamed member. PubMed records no author affiliations, no conflict-of-interest statement and no grant support for this paper.
