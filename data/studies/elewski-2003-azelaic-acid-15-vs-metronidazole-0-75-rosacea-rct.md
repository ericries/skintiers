A multicenter randomized trial in papulopustular rosacea found 15% [[azelaic-acid]] gel significantly outperformed 0.75% metronidazole gel on inflammatory lesion count and erythema over 15 weeks.[^1]

**Design:** Multicenter, double-blind, randomized, parallel-group trial conducted at 13 US centers, comparing azelaic acid gel 15% vs metronidazole gel 0.75%, each applied twice daily for 15 weeks.[^1]

**Population / n:** 251 patients with papulopustular [[rosacea]] presenting with persistent erythema and telangiectasia; the abstract does not state the per-arm n.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Azelaic acid gel 15% twice daily vs metronidazole gel 0.75% twice daily, both for 15 weeks.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "Azelaic acid gel was superior to metronidazole gel in reduction of mean nominal lesion count (-12.9 vs -10.7, respectively) (P=.003) and mean percent decrease in inflammatory lesions (-72.7% vs -55.8%, respectively) (P<.001)." "With respect to erythema severity, 56% of azelaic acid gel-treated patients were rated improved vs 42% of metronidazole gel-treated patients (P=.02)." "Both the investigator's global assessment (P=.02) and overall assessment of improvement (P=.005) showed a significant therapeutic advantage for azelaic acid gel." Neither treatment had a clinically appreciable effect on telangiectasia.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** Azelaic acid's advantage was consistent across every reported endpoint (lesion count, percent lesion reduction, erythema, investigator global assessment, overall improvement), with azelaic acid showing progressive improvement through week 15 while metronidazole's effect plateaued after week 8; no serious or systemic treatment-related adverse events were reported in either group, and both treatments were rated as having high cosmetic acceptability.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Generalizes to adults with papulopustular rosacea being considered for topical azelaic acid vs metronidazole therapy; only the PubMed abstract was accessed (not the full Archives of Dermatology text), so per-arm n, adverse-event rates, and dropout figures cited only in the full paper cannot be verified here.
