A randomized, double-blind, split-face, vehicle-controlled trial in 40 rosacea patients found topical metronidazole 0.75% gel produced substantially greater reductions in papules and pustules than vehicle over 9 weeks.[^1]

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind, split-face, paired-comparison, vehicle-controlled trial of topical metronidazole 0.75% gel for rosacea.[^1]

**Population / n:** 40 patients with rosacea; each patient's face was split, with metronidazole gel applied to one side and vehicle gel applied to the other side, so each patient served as their own control. Only 2 of 40 patients failed to complete the trial.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Metronidazole 0.75% gel applied twice daily to one side of the face vs. vehicle gel alone applied twice daily to the other side, for 9 weeks.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** On the metronidazole side, "36.7, 48.5, and 65.1 mean percent reductions in total papules and pustules were noted over baseline at 3, 6, and 9 weeks, respectively," vs. a "maximum mean percent reduction of 14.9 at nine weeks" on the vehicle side. Erythema "responded but was less improved by the end of nine weeks" [than papules/pustules]. Telangiectasia showed a "mild increase...noted on both actively treated and placebo-treated sides." The gel "was locally well tolerated," with "no systemic symptoms" and "no consistent abnormalities...in the results of laboratory studies." The abstract does not report a p-value or statistical test for the papule/pustule comparison.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** By week 9, metronidazole produced roughly 4.4x the mean percent reduction in papules/pustules seen with vehicle (65.1% vs. 14.9%); no confidence interval or p-value is given in the abstract. Telangiectasia was not improved by treatment and increased mildly on both sides, so the trial's benefit was specific to inflammatory lesions (papules/pustules) and, to a lesser extent, erythema, not to vascular signs.[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Supports topical [[metronidazole]] as an effective, well-tolerated treatment for the papule/pustule component of [[rosacea]]; one of the earliest RCTs establishing this. Only the PubMed abstract was accessed (no full text), so exact statistical methods, p-values, and detailed inclusion/exclusion criteria beyond a rosacea diagnosis are not verifiable; the split-face design controls for between-patient variability but cannot fully rule out a treatment effect crossing over between the two facial halves.[^1]
