A meta-analysis pooling controlled studies of recombinant human [[epidermal-growth-factor]] (rhEGF) added to standard debridement and suturing of maxillofacial (facial) trauma wounds found the combination healed wounds faster and left better scar scores than debridement and suture alone. This is rhEGF applied to open, freshly sutured wounds as a surgical adjunct, not a topical cosmetic EGF serum on intact skin.[^1]

**Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis (RevMan 5.4.1), searching CNKI, Wanfang, VIP, China Biomedicine, PubMed, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library, with heterogeneity assessed by the Q test and publication bias by funnel plots.[^1]

**Population:** Patients treated for maxillofacial trauma. The abstract does not report the number of included studies or the total pooled patient count.[^1]

**Intervention vs comparator:** Cosmetic (aesthetic) debridement and suture combined with rhEGF, versus debridement and suture without rhEGF.[^1]

**Primary result:** The combined therapy showed higher odds of favorable clinical efficacy, "OR = 6.62, 95% confidence interval (95% CI) (3.14-13.92), p < 0.00001," and a shorter wound-healing time, "mean difference (MD) = -2.69, 95% CI (-3.10 to -2.29), p < 0.00001" (the time unit is not stated in the abstract). At 6 months it reported "improved scar outcomes (lower Vancouver Scar Scale (VSS) and Patient and Observer Scar Assessment Scale (POSAS) scores)," with higher serum EGF and lower IL-6 and TNF-alpha in the combined group (all p < 0.05).[^1]

**Effect size:** Large on the pooled efficacy odds ratio (6.62) and statistically strong on healing time, but the "efficacy" outcome is a composite the abstract does not define, and the confidence intervals are wide.[^1]

**Generalizes to:** Surgical management of acute facial trauma wounds, where rhEGF is applied to open, sutured tissue. It says nothing about topical EGF cosmetic serums on intact skin, the large-protein-penetration problem for which is set out on [[epidermal-growth-factor]].[^1]

**Key limitation:** A meta-analysis is only as good as its inputs, and the abstract does not disclose how many studies or patients were pooled, nor define the composite "efficacy" endpoint; the literature base is drawn heavily from Chinese-language databases, which raises language and publication-bias concerns even though funnel plots were run. The result supports rhEGF as a wound-care adjunct, not the intact-skin cosmetic use.[^1]
