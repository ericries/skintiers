A 16-week randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trial found that topical tretinoin produced statistically significant clinical and histologic improvement in photoaged skin on both the forearm and the face compared with vehicle cream.[^1]

**Design:** 16-week randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled study; each patient applied tretinoin to one forearm and vehicle cream to the other (within-patient comparison), and separately either tretinoin or vehicle cream to the face (between-patient comparison). Clinical and histologic endpoints were assessed.[^1]

**Population / n:** 30 patients completed the study; the abstract does not state how many were enrolled at the start (i.e., the number who dropped out, if any, is not given). Of these 30, 15 received tretinoin on the face and the other half (implicitly 15) received vehicle cream on the face.[^1]

**Intervention / comparator:** Topical tretinoin versus vehicle cream, applied to one forearm each (within-patient) and to the face (between-patient, tretinoin group vs vehicle group). The abstract does not state the tretinoin concentration or application frequency.[^1]

**Primary result (verbatim figures):** "All 30 patients who completed the study showed statistically significant improvement in photoaging on the tretinoin-treated forearms, but not on the vehicle-treated forearms." On the face, "Fourteen of the 15 patients who received tretinoin to the face had improvement...whereas none of the vehicle-treated patients' faces improved, a statistically significant difference." "Statistically significant histologic changes were seen in forearm skin treated with tretinoin, but not with vehicle cream." Side effects were "limited to irritation of tretinoin-exposed skin." The abstract states significance qualitatively; it does not give exact p-values.[^1]

**Effect size / interpretation:** 30/30 (100%) of completers improved on the tretinoin-treated forearm versus the vehicle-treated forearm (no improvement stated); 14/15 (93%) improved on the tretinoin-treated face versus 0/15 (0%) on the vehicle-treated face. This is one of the original clinical trials establishing topical tretinoin as an evidence-based treatment for photoaging beyond its original acne indication, underlying the general case for [[tretinoin]] in [[anti-aging]].[^1]

**Applicability + key limitation:** Small trial (n=30 completers, single site) with results reported only as significant/not significant rather than with exact effect sizes or p-values in the abstract, and no stated tretinoin concentration or dosing regimen; only the PubMed abstract was accessed, not the full JAMA text, so exact statistics, concentration, and dropout details cannot be verified here. Applies to adults with visibly photoaged skin treated for 16 weeks.
