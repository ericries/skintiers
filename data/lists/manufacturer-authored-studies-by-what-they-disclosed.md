Most cosmetic efficacy research is written by the people selling the product. That is neither a scandal nor grounds for dismissal, because a sponsor-run trial can still be blinded, vehicle-controlled and honestly reported. What separates a readable sponsor study from an unreadable one is whether the paper tells you who paid for it and who signed it. This ranks the manufacturer-authored studies summarised on this site by that question alone.

## How to read one

Check the affiliation line, not the funding line. Employment is usually declared next to the authors' names, and a funding statement can be silent while every byline sits at the same corporate address. Read it sceptically in both directions: an older record may carry an affiliation for the first author only.

Check what the product was measured against. A sponsor study with a vehicle arm has answered a question. One measured against an untreated patch of skin has mostly established that something was applied.

Check who wrote the conclusion. A phrase like "a promising cosmeceutical agent" in an abstract by the maker's own staff is positioning, and the result it rests on usually sits one sentence above it, smaller.
