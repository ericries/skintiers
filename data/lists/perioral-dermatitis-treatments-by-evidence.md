Perioral dermatitis, covered in full on [[perioral-dermatitis]], is a facial rash of small red papules around the mouth, eyes, or nose that is closely tied to topical corticosteroid use. This ranks the treatment options by the strength of their own trial evidence, which is uniformly thinner than for acne or rosacea.

The honest framing: stopping topical corticosteroids is the one step with strong causal evidence behind it,[^searle][^statpearls] and every drug option below is an adjunct layered on top of that, not a substitute for it. A 2022 GRADE systematic review of 11 randomized trials found only low- or very-low-certainty evidence for every intervention it assessed, including a null result for azelaic acid gel and a confidence interval that crosses zero for pimecrolimus.[^gray] Oral tetracyclines (doxycycline, minocycline) come out ahead of the topicals, both in the 2022 review and in an earlier 2014 review that rated them as having "the best valid evidence."[^tempark] Topical metronidazole, clindamycin, sulfur, pimecrolimus, and tacrolimus all rest on thinner or unassessed footing.
