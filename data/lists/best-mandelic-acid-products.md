Mandelic acid is the largest, slowest-penetrating alpha-hydroxy acid the site covers, which is the mechanistic reason it is often reached for as a gentler option for sensitive or darker skin, detailed on [[mandelic-acid]]. But its isolated clinical evidence is thin: almost every controlled trial is a small in-office peel at 30 to 45 percent, mostly testing acne or hyperpigmentation, not the low-percent leave-on serums sold for daily home use. The one trial that pitted mandelic acid alone against another gentle AHA, lactic acid, found lactic acid produced better pigmentation results, higher satisfaction, and less irritation. Only two dedicated mandelic acid products are on-site right now; this list ranks them by labeled strength and transparency rather than by any head-to-head data, because none exists.

## The tier list

Both are unmedicated leave-on serums at concentrations well below the in-office peels the trial evidence is built on, so treat either as a mild, cosmetic exfoliant rather than a treatment for melasma or acne, both covered in depth on [[hyperpigmentation]] and [[acne]].

**[[naturium-mandelic-topical-acid-12|Naturium Mandelic Topical Acid 12%]].** Labeled 12% mandelic acid paired with niacinamide, the higher of the two labeled strengths here, though still far below the 30-45% peel concentrations the trial evidence is drawn from.

**[[the-ordinary-mandelic-acid-10-ha|The Ordinary Mandelic Acid 10% + HA]].** Labeled 10% mandelic acid at a manufacturer-stated pH of 3.5-4.5 with hyaluronic acid for hydration; a lower-cost, lower-strength option with the same evidence gap.

Neither formula has been tested in a published trial, and the general mandelic acid evidence page's honest caveat applies to both: the "gentler than glycolic, better for sensitive or dark skin" claim is mostly plausible from the molecule's size, not from head-to-head proof, and the one direct comparison available found lactic acid outperformed it. For a broader alpha-hydroxy-acid comparison, see [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]]; for pigment-focused products with stronger individual evidence, see [[best-products-dark-spots-hyperpigmentation]].
