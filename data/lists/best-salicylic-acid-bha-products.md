This ranks finished over-the-counter products built on salicylic acid, the lipophilic beta-hydroxy acid (BHA) that can get into oil-clogged pores in a way water-soluble AHAs cannot. The full mechanism and trial evidence live on [[salicylic-acid]]. Be honest about where it sits: the FDA's own OTC acne monograph caps it at 0.5-2%,[^cfr] and the American Academy of Dermatology's 2024 acne guideline places salicylic acid and other alternative topicals below benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids as first-line therapy.[^aad] It is a genuine, guideline-recognized adjunct for [[acne]] and [[oily-skin]], and a reasonable body-acne and texture option, not a replacement for retinoids or benzoyl peroxide.

## The tier list

Ranking favors disclosed concentration at or near the 2% OTC ceiling, leave-on contact time over rinse-off, and an independent evidence base for the specific use (face acne, body acne, or rough/bumpy texture) over undisclosed-percentage combination formulas.

**[[paulas-choice-skin-perfecting-2-bha|Paula's Choice Skin Perfecting 2% BHA Liquid Exfoliant]].** The reference leave-on: labeled 2% salicylic acid, the top of the FDA's OTC range, and the product most other BHA leave-ons on this list get compared against.

**[[naturium-bha-liquid-exfoliant-2|Naturium BHA Liquid Exfoliant 2%]].** Same 2% leave-on format and concentration as the Paula's Choice reference, a lower-cost equivalent.

**[[paulas-choice-weightless-body-treatment-2-bha|Paula's Choice Weightless Body Treatment 2% BHA]].** The same 2% leave-on active reformulated for body acne and keratosis-pilaris-prone texture, where a rinse-off cleanser has too little contact time to work as well.

**[[paulas-choice-clear-back-body-acne-spray|Paula's Choice Clear Back and Body Acne Spray]].** A leave-on spray format for body acne, useful for hard-to-reach areas like the back.

**[[cerave-sa-smoothing-cleanser|CeraVe SA Smoothing Cleanser]].** A genuine, guideline-recognized salicylic acid acne active, but as a rinse-off cleanser it has far less skin contact time than a leave-on exfoliant.

**[[la-roche-posay-effaclar-duo|La Roche-Posay Effaclar Duo+]].** Salicylic acid at the low end of the OTC range (0.5%) combined with niacinamide; gentler but weaker-dosed than the 2% leave-ons above.

**[[cerave-acne-control-gel|CeraVe Acne Control Gel]].** Combines salicylic acid with glycolic and lactic acid on the Drug Facts panel without a disclosed salicylic acid percentage, so it cannot be placed against the 2% leave-ons on strength alone.

**[[cosrx-aha-bha-clarifying-treatment-toner|COSRX AHA/BHA Clarifying Treatment Toner]].** Combines glycolic acid with betaine salicylate, a salicylic-acid ester, at undisclosed concentrations; evidence for this exact combination is preliminary.

**[[la-roche-posay-effaclar-micro-peeling-purifying-gel-cleanser|La Roche-Posay Effaclar Micro-Peeling Purifying Gel Cleanser]].** A rinse-off cleanser pairing salicylic acid with capryloyl salicylic acid (LHA), another BHA derivative, at undisclosed strength.

**[[cerave-sa-smoothing-cream|CeraVe SA Smoothing Cream]].** A body cream where 10% urea, not the salicylic acid, is the one active declared at a stated concentration; salicylic acid here is a supporting, undisclosed-strength ingredient.

**[[cerave-sa-body-lotion-for-rough-and-bumpy-skin|CeraVe SA Lotion for Rough & Bumpy Skin]].** Combines an AHA (ammonium lactate) and a BHA (salicylic acid) for texture and keratosis pilaris, but discloses no percentage for either acid and no independent trial of this exact formula was found.

**[[gold-bond-rough-and-bumpy-daily-skin-therapy|Gold Bond Rough & Bumpy Daily Skin Therapy Cream]].** Another undisclosed-strength salicylic acid plus lactic acid and PHA body cream for rough, bumpy texture.

**[[the-ordinary-aha-30-bha-2-peeling-solution|The Ordinary AHA 30% + BHA 2% Peeling Solution]].** A short-contact peel where a high 30% AHA load, not the 2% BHA, does most of the work and most of the irritation risk; a harsher, less targeted way to get salicylic acid than the leave-on exfoliants above.

Nothing here treats moderate or severe acne on its own. For most people with active acne, benzoyl peroxide or a topical retinoid should come first; salicylic acid is the adjunct layered on top, per [[acne]]. Nothing here is medical advice.
