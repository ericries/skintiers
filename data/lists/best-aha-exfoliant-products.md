This ranks leave-on alpha hydroxy acid (AHA) products, glycolic, lactic, and mandelic acid exfoliants, by how well their labeled concentration and pH line up with what controlled trials actually tested. AHAs exfoliate the skin surface and smooth texture, and glycolic and lactic acid have vehicle-controlled trial evidence for modest photoaging improvement, with 12% lactic acid showing firming changes that reach the dermis, deeper than the epidermis, but the evidence is mostly cosmetic-appearance, not disease-modifying, and higher concentration is not automatically better: the trial anchor for glycolic and lactic acid is an 8% concentration, not 20% or 30%. Concentration and pH detail live on [[glycolic-acid]], [[lactic-acid]], and [[mandelic-acid]]; the broader photoaging case is on [[anti-aging]].

## The tier list

Products that match the studied 8% glycolic/lactic strength rank highest; lactic and glycolic acid both trace to the same vehicle-controlled trial, so a genuine 7-10% product in that family outranks a mandelic acid product even at a higher labeled percentage, because mandelic acid's own evidence is thinner and comes mostly from high-strength in-office peels, not leave-on use.

**[[paulas-choice-skin-perfecting-8-aha-gel|Paula's Choice Skin Perfecting 8% AHA Gel]].** A genuine 8% glycolic acid gel at pH 3.5-3.9, matching the exact concentration used in the controlled trial behind glycolic acid's photoaging evidence, even though the trial tested a cream vehicle rather than a gel.[^gly8]

**[[the-ordinary-glycolic-acid-7-toning-solution|The Ordinary Glycolic Acid 7% Toning Solution]].** Labeled 7% glycolic acid, one point below the studied 8% cream, in a leave-on toner rather than the tested vehicle.[^gly8]

**[[amlactin-daily-moisturizing-lotion-12-lactic-acid|AmLactin Daily Moisturizing Lotion (12% Lactic Acid)]].** A 12% ammonium lactate lotion; a separate 3-month trial at this product's own 12% concentration measured a dermal, not merely epidermal, skin change that 5% did not produce, giving it its own concentration-matched data point rather than a read-across.[^amlactin]

**[[the-ordinary-lactic-acid-10-ha|The Ordinary Lactic Acid 10% + HA]].** A genuine 10% lactic acid at pH 3.60-3.80; no trial has tested this exact formula, so the closest evidence is the 8% lactic acid cream from the glycolic/lactic trial, read across rather than direct.[^gly8]

**[[the-ordinary-aha-30-bha-2-peeling-solution|The Ordinary AHA 30% + BHA 2% Peeling Solution]].** A once-or-twice-weekly rinse-off mask combining glycolic, lactic, tartaric, and citric acid with salicylic acid at a brand-stated total of 32% acids; no product-specific trial, and the brand itself restricts it to experienced users and warns against use on sensitive or compromised skin, so it sits below the daily leave-on products despite the higher acid load.

**[[the-ordinary-mandelic-acid-10-ha|The Ordinary Mandelic Acid 10% + HA]].** A labeled 10% mandelic acid at pH 3.5-4.5; mandelic acid's controlled evidence comes mostly from small in-office peel trials at 30-45%, and the one trial pitting mandelic acid against lactic acid found lactic acid worked better, so this ranks below the glycolic and lactic products above.

**[[naturium-mandelic-topical-acid-12|Naturium Mandelic Topical Acid 12%]].** A labeled 12% mandelic acid serum, higher percentage than The Ordinary's version but drawing on the same thin, mostly in-office-peel evidence base for mandelic acid, with no leave-on trial at any concentration.

**[[cosrx-aha-bha-clarifying-treatment-toner|COSRX AHA/BHA Clarifying Treatment Toner]].** Combines glycolic acid with a salicylic-acid ester, but neither active's concentration is disclosed and both sit well down the ingredient list after water and several other ingredients, so it ranks last on transparency and likely dose alone.

Redness, stinging, or peeling means the concentration or frequency is too high for that skin; AHAs also increase sun sensitivity, so daily sunscreen matters more, not less, while using them. Nothing here is medical advice.
