A cleanser's health-relevant job is narrow: lift dirt, oil, and makeup without stripping the skin barrier, and every fragrance-free, low-surfactant wash on the site earns roughly the same grade for that job, a modest effect on mixed evidence versus a harsher, high-foaming or high-pH cleanser. The better-evidenced work for dry, sensitive, and barrier-impaired skin happens after rinsing, in a leave-on moisturizer; that case is set out on [[moisturizing]] and, for atopic dermatitis specifically, on [[atopic-dermatitis]], where a Cochrane review of 77 trials found moisturizing reduces flares and spares topical corticosteroids and no single moisturizer beats another. Two of the four cleansers below also carry [[ceramides]], the barrier lipids eczema-prone skin runs short of, but a rinse-off wash can only leave a trace behind; the closest controlled test of a ceramide wash improved a barrier measurement without reducing eczema severity more than a plain vehicle.

> Any of the four cleansers below is a sound, gentle choice; none is shown to clean or protect the barrier better than the others. The ranking here turns on formulation and fit rather than hype: fragrance-free status, foaming versus non-foaming, what barrier ingredients are left behind, and price. Three are non-foaming or low-foaming creams built for dry and sensitive skin; the fourth foams and is built for oily skin.

## Non-foaming and low-foaming cream cleansers for dry, sensitive, and barrier-impaired skin

[[cerave-hydrating-facial-cleanser|CeraVe Hydrating Facial Cleanser]] and [[cetaphil-gentle-skin-cleanser|Cetaphil Gentle Skin Cleanser]] are effectively tied. Both are fragrance-free, non-foaming or low-foaming cream washes made for normal to dry skin, and both carry the same modest-effect, mixed-evidence grade for gentle, non-stripping cleansing. CeraVe Hydrating leaves behind three ceramides, hyaluronic acid, and glycerin, and it holds the National Eczema Association's Seal of Acceptance; quoted in NewBeauty, dermatologist Valerie Callender says, "I recommend this for my patients with normal or dry sensitive skin on their face." No trial isolates this exact cleanser. Cetaphil is the cheaper of the two, about $0.81 per fluid ounce at Target, and it is the only one of the four with a published study of the exact product: a two-week trial in 30 rosacea patients found no rise in water loss through the skin and no irritation, with a post hoc drop in rosacea severity, though the study had no control arm. Wirecutter, which credits Cetaphil with decades of dermatologist recommendation for sensitive and acne-prone skin, also reports its own testers with sensitive skin saw breakouts and sensitivity, and that the formula is gentle enough to leave heavy makeup and oily skin not fully cleaned, a cosmetic limit rather than a barrier one.

[[la-roche-posay-toleriane-hydrating-gentle-cleanser|La Roche-Posay Toleriane Hydrating Gentle Cleanser]] carries the fullest formula of the three, both ceramide-3 and niacinamide alongside glycerin and panthenol, where CeraVe Hydrating has ceramides without niacinamide and Cetaphil has niacinamide without ceramides. Wirecutter names it "Best for dry skin" among the cleansers it tested and reports it "efficiently cleans without robbing skin of precious moisture," sharing eight of the same moisture-holding ingredients it identifies in its CeraVe pick. Like the CeraVe and Cetaphil washes, no controlled trial isolates this cleanser, and Wirecutter also calls the plain formula "more dutiful than beautiful," a cosmetic verdict rather than a health one. It is the most expensive of the four, $19.99 for the 400 ml bottle against $15.99 for CeraVe Hydrating and about $12.89 for a 16 fl oz Cetaphil bottle.

## A foaming cleanser for oily and combination skin

[[cerave-foaming-facial-cleanser|CeraVe Foaming Facial Cleanser]] carries the same core grade for gentle, non-stripping cleansing, and the same niacinamide-plus-ceramide-and-hyaluronic-acid formula as its Hydrating sibling, but it foams and is built for normal to oily rather than dry or barrier-impaired skin. Quoted in NewBeauty, New York dermatologist Julia Tzu recommends it: "I love this cleanser because it's gentle to my skin but effectively removes the dirt and oils so that my face feels clean." At $15.99 it is priced the same as CeraVe Hydrating. It belongs on this list as the sulfate-free, fragrance-free option for reactive skin that runs oily rather than dry, not as a substitute for the three non-foaming washes above on classically dry or barrier-impaired skin.
