Every device here is FDA-cleared for the same thing, and clearance is not the question this page asks. The question is whose study the file rests on: the device in front of you, a different model, a prescription machine, a pig, or nothing at all. Whether home radiofrequency does anything is on [[home-radiofrequency-skin-tightening]]; the cross-category device ranking is on [[home-use-skincare-devices-by-evidence]].

> **The whole category is twelve filings, and one of them is why the category exists.** openFDA records 12 entries under product code PAY, the over-the-counter radiofrequency classification: the NEWA De Novo that created it, plus 11 subsequent 510(k) clearances.[^pay] The De Novo defined a new generic type, "Electrosurgical device for over-the-counter aesthetic use", for "a device using radiofrequency energy to produce localized heating within tissues for over-the-counter non-invasive aesthetic use".[^denovo] Every home RF device sold since has been cleared by arguing it is equivalent to something already in that class.

## Rung 1: FDA reviewed a study of this device

**NEWA, and it is alone here.** [[newa-skin-therapy-system]] came in by De Novo, which means there was no predicate to point at and the agency reviewed EndyMed's own clinical evidence. The decision summary reports effectiveness on 62 completers, with success defined as at least two of three independent dermatologists scoring at least one grade of improvement: 95.16% (59/62) at the end of four weeks of treatment and 100% (62/62) at three months, against a pre-specified bar of "at least 75% of patients being a success".[^denovo]

Two things keep this from being a strong effect claim. There is no control group, and the measured win is one grade on the five-point Fitzpatrick Wrinkle Severity Scale. The summary itself says the gains held but that "additional improvement from the end of treatment are unlikely once treatment is discontinued".[^denovo]

## Rung 2: ran its own study, under equivalence

**Silk'n Titan AllWays.** [[silkn-titan-allways]] took the 510(k) route but still submitted a clinical study, which almost nothing else in this product code did: one "conducted at a certified clinic in the US in 32 eligible adult women", reporting "a statistically significant average reduction of 1.39 Fitzpatrick scores comparing baseline to 3-month follow-up (p < 0.001), as determined by 3 blinded evaluators".[^k230013] Three blinded evaluators is a real methodological choice and it controls for who reads the photographs. It does not control for what untreated skin would have done over the same five months.

## Rung 3: the clinical evidence belongs to a different device

**Pollogen STOP U, sold as TriPollar.** [[pollogen-stop-u-tripollar]] is the filing much of this category points back to, and its clinical section does not describe the over-the-counter product. It offers predecessor-model and human-factors work, then states that "a clinical study was conducted demonstrating the effect of the TriPollar technology for mild to moderate facial wrinkles and rhytides while using the FDA-cleared STOP U device for prescription use (K140255)".[^k220322] The study that carries the efficacy argument was run with a prescription machine. Its own predicate is the previous model, "Pollogen Ltd., STOP U Model UXV (K203665)".[^k220322]

## Rung 4: no clinical testing, and the file says so

**CurrentBody Skin RF.** [[currentbody-skin-rf]] is admirably direct: "No clinical testing was performed with the subject device, as it is substantially equivalent to the predicate device, and thus requires no additional clinical data to prove its safety and efficacy."[^k232424] What stands in for it is bench work on pig skin, where "Measurements showed comparable (near identical) spatio-temporal tissue heating between the subject and predicate devices."[^k232424] It is an equivalence argument about heat, and heat is what it measures. And the predicate is the "Pollogen Stop U Model UXV device, cleared under 510(k) number K220322 on May 4th, 2023", which is rung 3.[^k232424] Follow the chain to its end and the human efficacy data sits with a prescription device two steps away.

## Rung 5: nothing to read

**FOREO's RF-coded masks.** Two FAQ masks sit in this product code rather than under an LED code: FAQ 101 (K222012, April 2023) and FAQ 102 (K240616, July 2024), both filed by Foreo, Inc. and both published as 510(k) **Statements**, which disclose nothing.[^pay] A Statement is legal and it ends the inquiry: there is no predicate, no testing and no indication text to weigh. The devices are at [[foreo-faq-led-masks]], and what a Statement does and does not publish is set out on [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]].

## What this ranking does not buy you

**No rung here is a controlled trial.** Rung 1 is the top of a category whose best evidence is a single-arm study of 62 people. Nothing in product code PAY was tested against a sham device, so the whole ladder measures provenance and candour, not effect size. The effect question belongs to [[home-radiofrequency-skin-tightening]].

**Every readable file in this category stops at Fitzpatrick skin type IV.** All four indications that can be read carry the same phototype ceiling: NEWA's is for "adult women who have Fitzpatrick Skin Types I-IV", Silk'n's for "adult women who have Fitzpatrick skin types I-IV", the Pollogen STOP U's for "adult users who have Fitzpatrick Skin Types I-IV", and the CurrentBody's for "adults with Fitzpatrick skin types I-IV".[^denovo][^k230013][^k220322][^k232424] Phototypes V and VI sit outside all four. Two of the four also restrict to women, where Pollogen and CurrentBody say adults; none of that limitation survives into the retail copy, as each product page shows. The FOREO masks cannot be checked at all, which is rung 5.

**One applicant, several brand names, again.** The CurrentBody Skin RF is filed by El Global Trade, Ltd., which also holds the sensiLift (K170499) and Sensilift Pro (K250341) clearances in this code; Pollogen holds five of the eleven 510(k)s.[^pay] Five applicants account for the entire category, which is worth knowing before treating two shelf-neighbours as independent competitors. The same pattern is documented for LED at [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]] and for [[silkn]].
