The live answer to the question [[us-sunscreen-filters-by-what-fda-proposed-in-2019]] answers historically. Of everything FDA proposed, three filters have been settled by final order and the rest are explicitly still pending.

## Settled: one filter added

**[[bemotrizinol]], GRASE at up to 6 percent.** Final order OTC000039, announced 10 June 2026, amends M020 "to add bemotrizinol at concentrations up to 6 percent as a sunscreen active ingredient".[^039] It came through a Tier 1 monograph order request from DSM Nutritional Products LLC in September 2024 rather than from the 2019 rulemaking, and FDA made no changes between the proposed order and the final one.[^039] The order also sets dosage forms, and the spray condition is unusually specific: a spray must have "no propellant" or isolate all propellant from the formulation "within the container closure system", with "no contact between the propellant and the drug product formulation".[^039] What bemotrizinol does on skin is on its own page and is not repeated here.

## Settled: two filters removed

**PABA and trolamine salicylate are out.** Final order OTC000008-1, announced 11 September 2026, amends M020 "to remove PABA and trolamine salicylate as sunscreen active ingredients", and states that a product containing either "is not generally recognized as safe and effective (GRASE)".[^paba] FDA's stated reason is the one it proposed in 2019 and again in 2021: "the risks associated with use of these active ingredients in sunscreen drug products outweigh the benefits".[^paba] Comments were considered and the determination was finalised "without change".[^paba] Neither filter has a page here, because neither is something a reader will find to buy.

## Not settled: everything else

Every other filter is where it was. The 2021 proposed order OTC000008 covered much more than these two, and the September 2026 final order closes only the PABA and trolamine salicylate part of it, saying so in one sentence: "FDA will address the other aspects of proposed order OTC000008 in a future order or orders."[^paba]

So nothing has been finalised about the twelve filters the 2019 rule put in Category III, including [[avobenzone]], [[octocrylene]], [[homosalate]], [[octisalate]], [[octinoxate]], [[oxybenzone]] and [[ensulizole]], and nothing has been finalised about the proposed GRASE status of [[zinc-oxide]] and [[titanium-dioxide]]. Those filters remain legal to sell under M020 as deemed in 2020; what has not happened is the resolution of the data gaps FDA described.

## How the orders fit together

M020 is not one document, which is why the per-filter status takes three orders to read. It was deemed final by statute on 27 March 2020 under the CARES Act, as order OTC000006.[^039] OTC000039 amended that version in June 2026. OTC000008-1 then amended the version as set out by OTC000039 in September 2026.[^paba] Each order amends the last, so the current monograph is the stack, not any single file.

## What this list is not

It is not an evidence ranking and carries no tier list; a filter's regulatory status and how well it blocks UV are different axes, and [[uv-filters-by-uva-protection]] covers the second. It also reports only the Federal Register announcements of these orders. The orders themselves live on FDA's OTC Monographs portal and were not retrieved, so any condition not quoted above should be checked there before being relied on.
