This ranks over-the-counter body products built on the two actives with real anti-aging trial evidence, [[retinoids|retinol]] and [[lactic-acid|lactic acid]], by how their disclosed concentration lines up with that evidence, not by a head-to-head test of the finished formulas. Almost none of the underlying trials were run on trunk or leg skin: most retinol evidence is facial or inner-arm, and the lactic acid evidence is a cream, not a body lotion. See [[neck-chest-decolletage-care]] for the fuller routine (sunscreen comes first there) and [[retinoids]] and [[lactic-acid]] for the ingredient-level grading.

> Retinol targets collagen and fine lines, beyond surface texture, so a disclosed, in-range retinol tops the list. But that edge holds only when the product discloses a real concentration: an undisclosed "stabilized retinol" or a dose below the tested range is a weaker bet than a disclosed, matched-dose AHA, which is why two lactic acid products outrank the retinol lotions that hide or undershoot their dose.

## The tier list

**[[necessaire-the-body-retinol|Necessaire The Body Retinol]].** Discloses 0.1% retinol, the same dose used in a 52-week facial trial behind retinol's evidence; it earns the top spot because it is the only product here pairing retinol's collagen-level mechanism with a disclosed, in-range dose, though the product itself has only an unpublished, uncontrolled brand panel study, not an independent trial.[^necessaire]

**[[amlactin-daily-moisturizing-lotion-12-lactic-acid|AmLactin Daily Moisturizing Body Lotion 12% Lactic Acid]].** Its 12% ammonium lactate matches the dose that produced a measurable dermal effect, deeper than surface, in a lactic-acid trial, and sits above the 10% shown effective for keratosis pilaris in a separate trial; a disclosed, matched-dose AHA outranks the retinol products below it, which either hide their dose or fall short of it.[^lactic]

**[[the-ordinary-lactic-acid-10-ha|The Ordinary Lactic Acid 10% + HA]].** Labeled 10% lactic acid, above the 8% cream used in the controlled AHA trial for texture and photodamage, though no trial has tested this specific leave-on serum.[^ordinary]

**[[cocokind-retinol-body-cream-0-05|Cocokind Retinol Body Cream 0.05%]].** Discloses 0.05% retinol, below both tested doses (0.1% and 0.4%), so its grade rests on facial and inner-arm evidence extrapolated down in dose and out to broader body skin; a sub-range retinol dose is a weaker bet than the disclosed, matched-dose AHAs above it.[^cocokind]

**[[gold-bond-age-renew-retinol-overnight-body-lotion|Gold Bond Age Renew Retinol Overnight Body & Face Lotion]].** Names "stabilized Retinol" but discloses no percentage, so it cannot be checked against the 0.1%-0.4% doses the trials used; that uncheckable dose lands it last, even though part of retinol's trial evidence was generated on body (inner-arm) skin, a closer match to this lotion's format.[^goldbond]

None of these substitute for daily sunscreen on exposed body skin, and none has an independent trial of the exact finished formula; every grade above rests on reading the disclosed concentration against ingredient-level trial evidence. Nothing here is medical advice.
