This ranks finished hyaluronic-acid serums and moisturizers, not the ingredient itself: the general case for hyaluronic acid, including its molecular-weight limits, is on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. The honest framing up front: hyaluronic acid is a humectant that binds water at or near the skin's surface and reliably raises a measured hydration reading, but it does not rebuild collagen or restructure skin, and high-molecular-weight hyaluronic acid (the form in most serums) does not cross the stratum corneum at all.[^essendoubi] Only lower-molecular-weight forms (roughly 20 to 300 kDa) penetrate, and even the placebo-controlled trial behind the ingredient's wrinkle claim found that benefit only for the lowest weights tested (50 and 130 kDa), not the higher ones.[^pavicic] "Multi-molecular-weight" blends and specific kilodalton counts on a label are mostly marketing; few brands disclose actual weights, so most such claims cannot be verified against the trial data.

## The tier list

The order below favors formulas where hyaluronic acid is genuinely the headline active and sits in a sensible base, glycerin or another humectant for support, an appropriate occlusive for the format, rather than formulas that lean on multi-form or "nano" marketing without disclosing weights. A plain gel-cream with a well-placed glycerin backup ranks above a pricier serum whose real workhorse ingredient turns out to be something else on the label.

Two products deliberately left off this list despite containing hyaluronic acid: [[the-ordinary-multi-peptide-ha-serum|The Ordinary Multi-Peptide + HA Serum]] is built around peptides with HA as a secondary add-in, and several other The Ordinary acid serums (lactic, mandelic, azelaic) pair an exfoliating or brightening acid with HA as a support ingredient, not the headline active. Those belong on their own actives' pages, not here.
