One 2026 scoping review graded every major home-use device category on a single scale, which makes it the closest thing to a straight answer about which gadget is worth buying.[^home] This page is that ladder. What each modality does, and the countervailing evidence, is on the individual pages rather than repeated here.

> **The short version: LED is the only home device category with both decent evidence and a clean safety record.** For acne, "blue light-emitting diodes (LED) and combination blue-red LED devices demonstrated moderate quality evidence (Level B) with favorable safety profiles and Grade I recommendations".[^home] At the other end, home microneedling was "supported by low-quality evidence (Level D) and associated with infectious complications", and home intense pulsed light and heat-based devices "lacked supportive clinical data (Level D) and were not recommended".[^home] Read that Level D strictly: it is a verdict on home IPL FOR ACNE, from a review covering acne and rejuvenation only, and it does not apply to hair removal, which is ranked separately on [[hair-removal-devices-by-evidence]]. The highest grade of all went to home fractional nonablative lasers, which reached "high-quality evidence (Level A)".[^home] That modality now has a page, and it carries a catch: the one cleared OTC device this site could trace claims only crow's feet, not facial rejuvenation. See [[home-fractional-nonablative-laser]].

## The Full Ladder

Quoting the review's own grades, best to worst, with the indication each grade was given for.

- **Level A, facial rejuvenation: home-use fractional nonablative lasers**, with "consistent clinical improvement".[^home] Now covered on [[home-fractional-nonablative-laser]], where the gap between the modality grade and the cleared device indication is set out. Note the word nonablative: this is NOT the in-office ablative procedure on [[fractional-co2-laser-resurfacing]], which is a different wavelength class, power and setting, and the Level A grade does not transfer to it.
- **Level B, acne: blue and combination blue-red LED**, with favourable safety and a Grade I recommendation.[^home] The best-evidenced thing you can buy. See [[home-led-light-therapy]].
- **Level B, facial rejuvenation: LED and radiofrequency devices**, moderate evidence.[^home] Radiofrequency has no page here.
- **Level C, acne: red LED alone and light-heat energy devices**, low-quality evidence but "minimal adverse events".[^home] Red-only is a step down from blue-red, which is the practical reason to check a mask's wavelengths rather than its marketing.
- **Level D, facial rejuvenation: home microneedling**, and the review pairs that grade with "infectious complications".[^home] The procedure case is on [[microneedling]].
- **Level D, acne: home intense pulsed light and heat-based devices**, "not recommended".[^home]

## Two Traps

**The IPL on this site is not the IPL in that review.** The Level D verdict above is for IPL used against **acne**. The home IPL devices this site covers, [[braun-silk-expert-pro-5-ipl]] and [[philips-lumea-ipl-9000-series]], are **hair removal** devices, judged on a different question with its own evidence on [[laser-hair-removal]]. A poor grade for IPL against pimples says nothing about IPL against hair.

**A clearance is not a grade.** The one home LED device profiled here, [[revive-light-therapy-wrinkle-and-acne-led-device]], is FDA-cleared, and its clearance file contains no efficacy study at all. The grades on this page come from the clinical literature, not from any regulator.

**What the review is and is not.** It is a scoping review that grades bodies of evidence; it pools no effect sizes and reports none, so nothing here says how much improvement to expect.[^home] It also never tested a specific product.
