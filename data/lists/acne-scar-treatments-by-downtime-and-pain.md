Most rankings of acne scar treatments compare how well they work. This one compares what they cost you in pain and recovery days, because for the laser comparison the cost is the better-measured half.[^song] For the efficacy ranking see [[acne-scar-procedures-by-evidence]]; for the scar types themselves see [[acne-scars]].

## Why this axis is worth a separate list

**For CO2 versus erbium, the penalty is firmer than the payoff.** A 2026 meta-analysis of five studies and 130 analyzed participants found erbium-based lasers had "a lower probability of > 50% clinical response than fractional CO₂ laser (RR, 0.69; 95% CI, 0.49-0.97; I²=45.9%)", which favours CO2. But in "the randomized-only sensitivity analysis, the direction was maintained but the CI included the null (RR, 0.76; 95% CI, 0.54-1.08)".[^song] The tolerability findings did not weaken that way: CO2 showed "greater treatment-related pain (MD, 1.86; 95% CI, 1.33-2.39) and longer downtime (MD, 3.67 days; 95% CI, 1.90-5.44)".[^song] So the more aggressive laser reliably costs more and only probably delivers more.

**Neither laser was worse for pigmentation in that comparison.** "No statistically significant differences were observed for post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (OR, 1.75; 95% CI, 0.67-4.57) or acneiform events".[^song] Read that as undetermined on 130 participants rather than as reassurance, and note that the needling review reached a different conclusion on pigment.

**Needling trades potency for comfort, by the authors' own description.** A 2026 systematic review of 27 studies and 1,137 participants reports that "ablative fractional lasers, particularly fractional CO2 and, in some studies, Er:YAG systems, suggested greater improvement than microneedling", while "needling-based therapies were associated with shorter downtime, better tolerability, and fewer pigmentary adverse effects".[^needling] That review synthesized its findings **narratively** because of heterogeneity, so those tolerability statements are directional and carry no pooled numbers.[^needling] It also notes microneedling radiofrequency "appeared to provide efficacy closer to that of ablative lasers in several individual studies and demonstrated a more favorable safety profile", which is the most interesting unresolved thread here.[^needling]

**What is not ranked, and why.** [[chemical-peels-acne-scars]], [[subcision]] and [[platelet-rich-plasma-acne-scars]] all have pages on this site, and none of the three sources consulted reports a pain or downtime finding for them. They are left out of the grading rather than given a guessed position. The 2025 network meta-analysis ranked pain for only one option, microneedling, and reported no treatment reduced erythema or post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation risk relative to the others.[^nma]

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. Downtime figures are averages from small comparative studies; what you personally experience depends on settings, passes, skin tone and aftercare, which is a conversation with the person holding the device.
