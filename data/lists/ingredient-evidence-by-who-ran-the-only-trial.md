Most cosmetic ingredients have exactly one human trial behind them, and it was usually run by whoever sells the ingredient. That is not automatically disqualifying, and pretending otherwise would empty the category. What matters is whether you can see it. This page sorts four cases from this site's own study pages by how legible the arrangement is.

> **The counterintuitive part: a declared company study is the best of this set.** When every author lists the manufacturer as their affiliation, you know precisely what you are reading. The lower rungs are not more independent, they are less checkable, and one of them claims independence it does not evidence.

## Rung 1: the seller ran it and says so

[[jeon-2023-salix-alba-bark-extract-dark-circles-pmid-37235713]] has all eight authors at a single company institute, and its closing sentence is a supplier conclusion rather than a scientific one.[^jeon] [[gopaul-2010-topical-salicin-skin-aging-pmid-20883292]] is a Nu Skin author studying a Nu Skin ingredient, with no control arm.[^gopaul]

Both are weak evidence. Both are honest about why. A reader can discount them accurately, which is more than the rungs below allow.

## Rung 2: the disclosures contradict each other

[[zhang-2025-forearm-tape-strip-capsaicin-model-pmid-39381993]] states that "The authors received no specific funding for this work" while its acknowledgments thank a named party "for financial support", one author's listed contribution is "funding acquisition", and the paper declares no conflicts of interest.[^zhang] Those four statements cannot all be right.

This is worse than rung 1, not better. A disclosed conflict can be weighed. A set of mutually inconsistent disclosures leaves a reader unable to weigh anything, including the parts of the paper that may be perfectly sound.

## Rung 3: the study is cited but not citeable

[[kravebeauty-great-barrier-relief]] is sold partly on "an independent clinical study on 20 participants", with no reference given.[^krave] The word doing the work is independent, and it is the one thing an uncited study cannot demonstrate. To the brand's credit it states the sample size rather than hiding it, and twenty is small enough that the number is not flattering.

## What this page is not saying

None of these four ingredients is being graded here as effective or ineffective; each page carries its own verdict. Nor is a company-run trial presented as worthless. Industry funds most cosmetic science, and the alternative to a disclosed company study is usually no study.

The claim is narrower: when the only trial comes from the seller, transparency is the one thing left to assess, and it varies a great deal.
