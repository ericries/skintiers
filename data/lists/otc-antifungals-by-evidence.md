One Cochrane review of 67 randomized trials is the only source that pools these agents on a single question, so it is the axis this ranking uses.[^cochrane] **Bottom line:** just one comparison in it is a genuine head-to-head, and it put allylamines ahead of azoles. Everything else is an agent measured against placebo, which tells you the agent works and tells you nothing about how it stacks up against the tube next to it.

> **The most tempting number in this review is the one you must not rank on.** The placebo-controlled pools are "allylamines RR 0.33 (95% CI 0.24 to 0.44); azoles RR 0.30 (95% CI 0.20 to 0.45); ciclopiroxolamine RR 0.27 (95% CI 0.11 to 0.66); tolnaftate RR 0.19 (95% CI 0.08 to 0.44); butenafine RR 0.33 (95% CI 0.24 to 0.45); undecanoates RR 0.29 (95% CI 0.12 - 0.70)".[^cochrane] Read that list and tolnaftate looks best and allylamines look worst, which is the reverse of the head-to-head result. These are separate pools against separate placebo groups, and [[tolnaftate]] works through why that comparison fails.

## The one direct comparison

"Meta-analysis of 11 trials comparing allylamines and azoles showed a risk ratio of treatment failure RR 0.63 (95% CI 0.42 to 0.94) in favour of allylamines."[^cochrane] That is the whole comparative basis for the ranking above. The review's own summary keeps it modest: "Allylamines cure slightly more infections than azoles and are now available OTC."[^cochrane] Slightly, not decisively, and the confidence interval reaches 0.94, close to no difference at all.

## Nails are a different question with worse answers

The review treats toenail infection separately and is blunt that the evidence is thinner: "Evidence for the management of topical treatments for infections of the toenails is sparser."[^cochrane] For nails it reports that ciclopiroxolamine and butenafine are both effective but "need to be applied daily for prolonged periods (at least 1 year)", that topical [[ciclopirox]] "has poor cure rates", and that amorolfine "might be substantially more effective but more research is required".[^cochrane] Anyone treating [[onychomycosis]] should read that before expecting a cream to work, and the condition page covers the oral options this review does not.

## What this ranking does not cover

**The foot, and only the foot.** Every figure above comes from trials in "fungal infections of the skin and nails of the foot".[^cochrane] It is directly relevant to [[athletes-foot]]. It is not evidence about [[tinea-corporis-ringworm]] on the body, [[tinea-capitis]] on the scalp, or [[pityriasis-versicolor]], even though several of the same agents are used for them.

**Yeast and Malassezia agents are deliberately absent.** [[selenium-sulfide]], [[zinc-pyrithione]] and [[sulfur]] are not in this ranking, because the review pooled trials of dermatophyte foot infection and none of those three appears in it. They are used against *Malassezia*, which is a different organism and a different question, covered at [[seborrheic-dermatitis]] and [[fungal-acne]]. Placing them in a tier here would imply this evidence rates them, and it does not.

**The searches stopped in January 2005.** The review was published in 2007 and searched to January 2005.[^cochrane] Nothing here reflects trials since. It remains the best pooled comparison available on this question, which is itself worth knowing.
