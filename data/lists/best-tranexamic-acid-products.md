This ranks over-the-counter products built around topical tranexamic acid (TXA) for melasma and hyperpigmentation. Be honest about the ceiling first: TXA's strongest evidence is oral, prescribed by a doctor, where a placebo-controlled trial cut melasma severity scores 49% versus 18% for placebo at 3 months.[^rct] Topical TXA has only a systematic-review-level recommendation and thinner, more mixed trial data, and no marketed serum here has been tested in an independent, isolated, placebo-controlled trial of its own formula.[^austin] The full case, including relapse after stopping and safety limits, is on [[tranexamic-acid]]; melasma and general hyperpigmentation context is on [[melasma]] and [[hyperpigmentation]].

## The tier list

Ranked by how closely the labeled TXA dose tracks the nearest topical study (3% TXA with kojic acid and niacinamide over 12 weeks[^desai]) and by how much of the formula's brightening case rests on TXA itself versus other actives.

**[[skinceuticals-discoloration-defense|SkinCeuticals Discoloration Defense]].** Declares 3% tranexamic acid, matching the dose in the nearest topical clinical evaluation, stacked with kojic acid and niacinamide, two other evidence-backed brighteners.[^desai]

**[[anua-niacinamide-10-tranexamic-acid-4-serum|Anua Niacinamide 10% + TXA 4% Serum]].** Declares 4% tranexamic acid, above the 3% studied dose, paired with niacinamide.

**[[the-inkey-list-tranexamic-acid-night-treatment|The INKEY List Tranexamic Acid Night Treatment]].** A dedicated, low-cost tranexamic acid product at a declared 2%, below the 3% studied dose but without other actives diluting the case.

**[[skinmedica-lytera-2-pigment-correcting-serum|SkinMedica Lytera 2.0 Pigment Correcting Serum]].** Combines tranexamic acid with phenylethyl resorcinol and niacinamide, each separately evidenced for pigmentation, but with no disclosed percentage of any active and no trial of this exact combination.

**[[good-molecules-discoloration-correcting-serum|Good Molecules Discoloration Correcting Serum]].** Pairs tranexamic acid with niacinamide at no disclosed percentage; an inexpensive way into the same two-active idea as the products above.

**[[murad-rapid-dark-spot-correcting-serum|Murad Rapid Dark Spot Correcting Serum]].** Adds tranexamic acid to glycolic acid and 4-ethylresorcinol at no disclosed percentages; glycolic acid's one direct pigment trial found it added nothing to hydroquinone in a split-face study, which weakens the case for that second active.

**[[topicals-faded-brightening-clearing-serum|Topicals Faded Brightening + Clearing Serum]].** Lists tranexamic acid as one of five actives (with niacinamide, azelaic acid, kojic acid, and melatonin) with no disclosed percentages, so tranexamic acid's own contribution to this formula is the least isolable of the group.

None of these substitute for the oral prescription route, which has the strongest evidence and is a medical decision made with a dermatologist because of tranexamic acid's clotting-risk profile.[^label] Nothing here is medical advice.
