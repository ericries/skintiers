A binary question asked of eighteen cleared light devices: does the filing name a photobiological safety standard, or does it not? This ranks **what is on the record**, not which device is safer for your eyes. A filing that names the standard can be checked; one that does not simply cannot be, and that is the whole claim here. Whether LED works is on [[home-led-light-therapy]], what the files publish about dose is on [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]], and the eye question itself is at [[blue-light-eye-safety-home-devices]].

> **Two standards do this job, and either counts.** IEC 62471 is named in these filings as the standard for the photobiological safety of lamps and lamp systems; IEC 60601-2-57 is named as the standard for non-laser light source equipment.[^iec] Naming either means someone tested the emitter against a published optical-exposure limit. Naming neither does not mean nobody looked. It means the summary does not say they did.

## Rung 1: names a photobiological standard by number

Eight devices, and the standard is named outright in each filing's testing section.

- **IEC 62471.** [[illumask-acne-light-therapy-mask]] (K123999), which lists conformity in a single line of its Non-Clinical Testing section;[^k123999] [[dpl-spectralite]] (K171386), at the 2006 edition;[^k171386] [[nuface-trinity-wrinkle-remover]] (K120560), at IEC 62471:2006;[^k120560] [[pro-x-otc-5-la-lumiere-wrinkle-mask]] (K140471); and both [[pulsaderm-acne-mask-28]] and [[pulsaderm-wrinkle-mask-28]].[^pages]
- **IEC 60601-2-57,** the non-laser light source equipment standard. [[theraface-led-light-ring]] and [[foreo-faq-302]], each naming it inside a wider IEC 60601 series list.[^pages]

## Rung 2: names optical safety as a test, but no standard

One device, and it is the most recent file on this page.

[[nuface-trinity-plus-wrinkle-reducer]] (K212947, cleared 2022) concludes that "The safety and performance testing results for Optical Safety, Electrical Safety, EMC, Wireless Coexistence and bench testing concluded that the TWR Plus device meets and complies with the safety and performance of the applicable standards and bench testing requirements."[^k212947] Optical safety was tested. Which applicable standard it was tested against is not stated, because **the string IEC does not occur anywhere in that document**, nor does 62471.[^k212947]

## Rung 3: names no photobiological standard at all

Nine devices. In each case the filing's testing section is recorded in full on its own page, and it lists electrical safety, electromagnetic compatibility, biocompatibility, software or some combination, with nothing optical among them.[^pages]

[[dpl-ii-panel]], [[dpl-iia-panel]], [[dpl-faceware]], [[dpl-spotlite]], [[dpl-nuve]], [[dpl-therapy-system]], [[foreo-faq-led-masks]], [[tanda-mini-skincare-system]] and [[revive-light-therapy-led-cleansing-system]].

One of those deserves a note. [[dpl-nuve]]'s file names EN 60601-2-22, which is the particular standard for **laser** equipment, on a device that contains no laser.[^pages] It is the only optical-adjacent standard in Rung 3 and it is the wrong one.

## What the split actually shows

**One company, six files, one mention.** Six LED Technologies filings are covered here, and exactly one names a photobiological standard: the dpl SpectraLite, the handheld meant for use around the eyes. Its own page already noted that the sibling panel's file does not list it.[^pages] The pattern is not that this manufacturer ignores the standard; it is that the standard appears on the file where the eye exposure is most obvious and nowhere else.

**The same company got less specific over ten years.** NuFACE's two light heads sit on either side of this line. The 2012 clearance names IEC 62471:2006 in its compliance section.[^k120560] Its 2022 replacement, a brighter head with an extra wavelength and an app, names optical safety as a category and no standard at all.[^k212947] Both are legitimate filings and the newer device is not thereby less safe. The later public record just says less.

**Scope and what was left out.** Eighteen devices here, all of them light-emitting and all with a complete testing section recorded from the filing. Microcurrent and radiofrequency devices are excluded as not light-emitting. So are light devices whose pages do not yet record a full standards list, including [[dpl-nuve-for-wrinkles]], [[omnilux-new-u]], [[neutrogena-light-therapy-acne-mask-plus]] and the reVive Lux range: absence of a standard from a page that never recorded one is not evidence, and those stay off until their filings are read.
