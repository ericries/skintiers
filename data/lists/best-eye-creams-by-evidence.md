Eye creams are a small, crowded category and most of the marketing outruns the evidence. Per [[dark-circles]], topicals mainly help the pigmentary type of dark circle, and only modestly; retinoids are the best-studied active for that, brighteners are adjuncts, and caffeine gives a real but transient de-puffing effect rather than fixing pigment or hollowing. Nothing here reaches vascular or structural dark circles, which respond better to lasers or fillers, outside topical skincare.

Only a handful of dedicated eye-area products exist on the site so far; this list ranks those, not the wider category.

## The tier list

Ranked by how well-evidenced the headline active is for the eye area, most-evidenced first.

**[[beauty-of-joseon-revive-eye-serum-ginseng-retinal|Beauty of Joseon Revive Eye Serum: Ginseng + Retinal]]** leads on retinaldehyde, the class [[dark-circles]] calls the best-studied topical for periorbital hyperpigmentation because it both fades pigment and thickens the thin under-eye skin that lets vessels show through; full retinoid evidence is on [[retinoids]].

**[[the-ordinary-caffeine-solution-5-egcg|The Ordinary Caffeine Solution 5% + EGCG]]** is a disclosed 5% caffeine serum for temporary puffiness and brightness. Caffeine's vasoconstrictive mechanism is plausible and it shows up across small studies, but no isolated-ingredient controlled trial exists for dark circles or puffiness, and this is a transient cosmetic effect, not a fix for the underlying darkness; detail on [[caffeine]].

**[[anua-pdrn-caffeine-rejuvenating-eye-cream|Anua PDRN Caffeine Rejuvenating Eye Cream]]** sits last. Its other headline active, PDRN, has real regenerative evidence only from injected or wound-applied use, not a leave-on cream, and a board-certified dermatologist who reviewed this specific product on video called the PDRN "a gimmick," crediting any visible benefit to the base moisturizers and niacinamide instead.

Retinoids stay the best-evidenced route into the eye area; caffeine is a reasonable, cheap add-on for temporary puffiness; trend ingredients like PDRN in an eye cream are unproven for that use even when they have other evidence elsewhere.
