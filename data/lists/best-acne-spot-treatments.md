This ranks on-site leave-on acne spot treatments by the strength of evidence behind their labeled active. The honest framing up front: [[benzoyl-peroxide|benzoyl peroxide]] is the best-evidenced OTC spot active and the American Academy of Dermatology ranks it above [[salicylic-acid|salicylic acid]], but the site does not yet have a page for a plain leave-on benzoyl peroxide spot gel; the benzoyl peroxide products covered so far are either rinse-off washes, a rosacea-only prescription, or combination retinoid-plus-BPO prescriptions, none of which fit a "dab it on a pimple" spot treatment. Sulfur and hydrocolloid pimple patches also have no dedicated product page yet. So every item below is a salicylic acid leave-on treatment; see [[acne]] for the full active-by-active picture and [[salicylic-acid]] for the trial evidence behind this tier.

## The tier list

Salicylic acid is a real, guideline-recognized OTC acne active, but a second-tier one: it penetrates oil-filled pores and has solid if modest trial support, while the AAD's current guideline gives it only a conditional recommendation and ranks it below benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids. Within salicylic acid products, concentration matters: the FDA's OTC acne monograph allows 0.5% to 2%, and the two products at the top of this list sit at the top of that range.

**[[cerave-acne-control-gel|CeraVe Acne Control Gel]].** Labeled 2% salicylic acid, the top of the OTC monograph range, in a leave-on facial gel with added ceramides for barrier support alongside the drying active.

**[[paulas-choice-clear-back-body-acne-spray|Paula's Choice Clear Back and Body Acne Spray]].** Also 2% salicylic acid, same top-of-range dose, but in a spray format built for spot-treating the back and chest, where applying a gel by hand is awkward.

**[[la-roche-posay-effaclar-duo|La Roche-Posay Effaclar Duo+]].** Labeled 0.5% salicylic acid, the low end of the OTC range, so a gentler but weaker version of the same active; it also carries niacinamide for post-acne marks, a separate and lower-priority claim.

A hydrocolloid patch does nothing chemically: it works by covering an already-open or draining lesion, absorbing fluid and blocking picking, and it has no effect on a closed, deep, or not-yet-surfaced pimple. Since no hydrocolloid patch has a page here yet, that gap stays unranked rather than guessed at. Nothing here is medical advice; persistent or scarring acne is a reason to see a dermatologist rather than rely on spot treatments alone.
