Brands sell home LED for back and chest acne. The clearances behind those devices do not mention either.

Every device ranked here has its cleared indication recorded on its own page, and across all of them the pattern is the same: either the indication says "on the face" outright, or it names acne and no body site at all. None affirmatively covers the trunk. That is an absence in the clearance records collected on this site, not proof that body use is unsafe or useless, and the distinction matters because the marketing fills the gap confidently.[^pages]

The practical consequence is that a reader choosing an LED device for back acne is choosing on form factor, not evidence. A panel can be aimed at a torso; a contoured face mask cannot. Nothing in the regulatory record tells you whether aiming it there works.

For what the light itself has behind it, see [[led-colours-for-acne-by-evidence]] and [[home-led-light-therapy]]. For body acne treatments that are not devices, [[best-body-acne-products]] ranks the topicals, and the condition is on [[acne]].

Ranked above, in frontmatter, on cleared indication and form. Four devices are graded and four more are recorded as considered and excluded, two of them because their indications name the face explicitly and two because their form decides the question regardless of what the indication omits.
