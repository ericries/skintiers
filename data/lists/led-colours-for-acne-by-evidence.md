Masks are sold by colour, so this ranks the colours. Whether home LED is worth buying at all is on [[home-led-light-therapy]], and what the specification numbers mean is on [[led-dose-j-per-cm2-and-wavelength]]; neither is repeated here.

> **The ranking has two rungs, not three.** The 2026 GRADE scoping review grades blue and blue-plus-red **together**, and red alone one step below: "For acne, blue light-emitting diodes (LED) and combination blue-red LED devices demonstrated moderate quality evidence (Level B) with favorable safety profiles and Grade I recommendations", while "Red LED and light-heat energy devices showed low-quality evidence (Level C) but minimal adverse events."[^grade] So blue versus blue-plus-red is a question this evidence does not answer.

## Blue, and blue-plus-red: Level B

Graded together, at moderate quality, with Grade I recommendations and favourable safety.[^grade] Because the review pools them, nothing here supports paying more for a dual-colour device over a blue-only one on acne grounds. A mechanism review puts both in the same pathway, describing blue and red LED as providing "gradual anti-inflammatory effects suitable for mild-to-moderate acne by reducing C. acnes colonization and modulating cytokines such as TGF-β1, IL-10, and TNF-α".[^mechanism] Note the population in that sentence: mild-to-moderate.

On this site: [[foreo-espada-blue-light-pen]] and [[revive-light-therapy-lux-spot]] are the blue-light spot devices with pages; [[dpl-spotlite]] is the multi-wavelength one.

## Red alone: Level C

One rung down, on low-quality evidence, and grouped by the review with light-heat energy devices rather than kept separate.[^grade] Red LED is the colour most heavily marketed for ageing rather than acne, and for facial rejuvenation the same review does put LED at Level B.[^grade] If acne is the target, the evidence prefers blue in the mix; if ageing is the target, that is a different page, [[anti-aging]].

## What the ranking does not settle

**The ceiling on all of it is low.** A Cochrane review of light therapies for acne, covering 71 studies and 4,211 participants, concluded that high-quality evidence is lacking, judged most studies small at a median of 31 participants, and found two thirds industry-sponsored with conflicts either declared or undeclared.[^cochrane] A Level B grade means moderate confidence, not a settled answer, and the full reading of how those two reviews fit together is on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

**Colour is not dose.** A wavelength in nanometres tells you which pathway is being aimed at and nothing about whether enough light arrived. Most consumer devices publish no irradiance at all, which is documented with worked numbers on [[led-dose-j-per-cm2-and-wavelength]].

**Nothing here beats a topical.** None of the sources above compares a home LED device of any colour against [[benzoyl-peroxide]] or [[adapalene]]. Treat a mask as a possible addition to a routine that already works, which is the same conclusion [[acne]] reaches by a different route.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The grades above describe mild-to-moderate acne; nodular, scarring or severe acne is a reason to see a dermatologist rather than change the colour of a lamp.
