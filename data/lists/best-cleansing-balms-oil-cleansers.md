An oil cleanser or cleansing balm is the first step of a [[double-cleansing|double cleanse]]: its job is to dissolve makeup and water-resistant sunscreen that a foaming water-based cleanser leaves largely behind. That removal job is the one thing here backed by controlled evidence, and it is a property of the format (emollient oils to dissolve oil-based grime, plus emulsifiers so it rinses clean), not of any single brand; the trial evidence is set out on [[double-cleansing]]. No published trial separates these three products on how well they cleanse, so the ranking below turns on fragrance, format, and price rather than a proven efficacy gap.

> If you wear water-resistant sunscreen or long-wear makeup, an oil-based first cleanse genuinely removes what a water wash misses. Beyond that, pick on tolerance and feel: fragrance-free formulas are the safer default for reactive or easily-irritated skin, and all three below are faithful, well-built examples of the class. None is a treatment; a second, water-based cleanse follows (see [[double-cleansing]]).

## The most sensitive-skin-friendly pick, and the cheapest

[[the-inkey-list-oat-cleansing-balm|The INKEY List Oat Cleansing Balm]] is a fragrance-free balm built on colloidal oat, at about $17 for 150 mL, the lowest price per volume here. Fragrance-free is the reason to reach for it first if your skin is reactive: it removes the same makeup and sunscreen as the others without added fragrance, the one common irritant in this category. INKEY reformulated the oat content from an earlier version, a change noted on its product page.

## The classic fragrance-free oil

[[dhc-deep-cleansing-oil|DHC Deep Cleansing Oil]] is the long-selling Japanese olive-oil cleansing oil, at about $31.50 for 200 mL. It is a liquid oil rather than a solid balm, fragrance-free apart from a little rosemary oil, and the brand positions it as a one-step cleanse; whether one oil step is enough depends on how much you are washing off (see [[double-cleansing]]). It is the priciest per bottle but the largest size, and a sound fragrance-free option for those who prefer an oil to a balm.

## The bestseller, with one caveat

[[banila-co-clean-it-zero-cleansing-balm-original|Banila Co Clean It Zero Cleansing Balm Original]] is the widely sold K-beauty oil-to-milk balm, at about $21 for 100 mL. It cleanses as competently as the others, but unlike them it contains added fragrance, which is worth knowing if your skin is easily irritated; its "vitamin C" is a trace acerola extract low on the ingredient list, not a treatment (detail on its product page). A pleasant, effective balm whose only real drawback here is the added fragrance.
