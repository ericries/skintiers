A stripped-down acne routine assembled only from products already graded on the site, each carrying its own trial-level evidence: a gentle cleanser, an over-the-counter retinoid at night, azelaic acid at a studied strength in the morning, a plain barrier moisturizer, and a daily sunscreen to cover the sun sensitivity retinoids bring.

## What the routine is built from

The two actives are the parts with the strongest acne evidence, and they are split across the day so their irritation does not compound.

- [[differin-adapalene-0-1-gel|Differin Adapalene 0.1% Gel]] is the anchor. Adapalene is the first topical retinoid the FDA cleared for nonprescription acne treatment, and in head-to-head trials it lands level with benzoyl peroxide and tretinoin while being better tolerated than tretinoin; the class evidence sits on [[adapalene]] and [[retinoids]]. Retinoids are typically kept to the night because light can degrade them.
- [[cos-de-baha-az20-azelaic-acid-20-serum|Cos De BAHA AZ20 Azelaic Acid 20% Cream]] carries azelaic acid at 20%, the strength azelaic acid's acne and pigmentation trials used, above the 10% most over-the-counter serums list; the ingredient case is on [[azelaic-acid]]. Keeping it in the morning holds it off the same clock as the nightly retinoid, which lowers the combined irritation.

The supporting products are chosen to keep the routine gentle rather than to add more actives.

- [[cerave-foaming-facial-cleanser|CeraVe Foaming Facial Cleanser]] is a plain, ceramide-containing cleanser for both washes; the barrier case for [[ceramides]] is linked there.
- [[cerave-moisturizing-cream|CeraVe Moisturizing Cream]] is a plain barrier moisturizer that offsets the dryness retinoids and azelaic acid can cause, without competing actives.
- [[eltamd-uv-clear-spf-46|EltaMD UV Clear SPF 46]] is the daytime sunscreen. Sun protection carries the most weight in any retinoid routine because retinoids raise photosensitivity; the filter evidence sits on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].
