Korean sunscreens are popular for a real reason: many use modern UVA filters, like Tinosorb M, Uvinul A Plus, and Mexoryl, that are not in the current US OTC sunscreen monograph (bemotrizinol, Tinosorb S, was added only in June 2026), and the textures tend to be lighter than US chemical or mineral formulas. The honest caveat applies to every product on this list: SPF and PA ratings here are set by Korea's MFDS testing standard, not the FDA's, so the labeled number is a regulatory claim from a different testing system, not an FDA-verified one. The category case for broad-spectrum protection itself, and how PA and the various filters compare, is on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## The tier list

Ranked first by whether an independent lab, rather than the brand alone, has tested the SPF/UVA claim, then by how modern and broad the UV filter set is. Multi-filter modern-organic formulas (Tinosorb, Mexoryl, Uvinul A Plus) rank above single-filter mineral (zinc oxide only) formulas, which rank above formulas leaning on older filters like avobenzone alone.

**[[beauty-of-joseon-relief-sun-rice-probiotics-spf50|Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics]]** is the only product here with an independent (non-brand) lab result: Consumer Reports scored it 5 of 5 for both UVB and UVA, with a tested SPF of 36 against a 50+ label.[^cr]

**[[beauty-of-joseon-aqua-fresh-sun-serum|Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun: Aqua-fresh Rice + B5]]** pairs four modern UVA filters, ecamsule (Mexoryl SX), drometrizole trisiloxane (Mexoryl XL), bemotrizinol (Tinosorb S), and Uvinul A Plus, the broadest UVA filter stack on this list, though its filter identities rest on an ingredient-database listing rather than an independent assay.

**[[isntree-hyaluronic-acid-watery-sun-gel-spf-50|Isntree Hyaluronic Acid Watery Sun Gel]]** also skips avobenzone entirely, running on Tinosorb S, Tinosorb M, and Uvinul A Plus for a strong/strong grade on both UVB and UVA.

**[[skin1004-hyalu-cica-water-fit-sun-serum-uv|SKIN1004 Hyalu-Cica Water-Fit Sun Serum]]** uses an FDA-monograph-recognized four-filter set (avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, octocrylene), which makes its filters more familiar to US shoppers, but its only UVA-contributing filter is avobenzone, the older, less stable UVA filter, so it grades below the Mexoryl/Tinosorb formulas above it.

**[[round-lab-birch-juice-moisturizing-sun-spf-50|Round Lab Birch Juice Moisturizing Sun]]** also relies on avobenzone plus homosalate and octisalate, the same older US-style filters, for a notable/modest grade.

**[[dr-g-green-mild-up-sun|Dr.G Green Mild Up Sun+]]** and **[[dr-g-baby-mild-up-sun|Dr.G Baby Mild Up Sun]]** are single-filter, zinc-oxide-only mineral sunscreens; zinc oxide is a genuine, photostable broad-spectrum filter, but one mineral filter alone typically trails the top multi-filter organic formulas above, and the Baby version is only rated SPF35 versus SPF50+ for Green.

**[[beauty-of-joseon-matte-sun-stick|Beauty of Joseon Matte Sun Stick]]** carries a preliminary evidence grade rather than solid; no independent test of the stick format was located, unlike its Relief Sun sibling.

**[[skin1004-madagascar-centella-air-fit-suncream|SKIN1004 Madagascar Centella Air-Fit Suncream]]** is a tinted mineral (zinc oxide + titanium dioxide) tone-up formula with undisclosed filter concentrations, and its cosmetic brightening claim is unsupported by a product-specific trial.

**[[aestura-derma-uv365-barrier-hydro-mineral-sunscreen|Aestura Derma UV365 Barrier Hydro Mineral Sunscreen]]** sits last: every grade on it is preliminary, its zinc oxide concentration is undisclosed, and its barrier-improvement claim rests on unpublished brand testing.

None of this changes the core advice: any broad-spectrum SPF30+ sunscreen, Korean or not, applied daily and at an adequate amount, beats a "better" one used sparingly or skipped. [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] covers filter chemistry and the reapplication evidence in full.
