Compares the site's daily-wear facial sunscreens by the UVB and UVA grades already established on each product's own page. Broad-spectrum protection is the best-evidenced way to slow photoaging and lower skin-cancer risk, the case set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. The strongest entries here pair a high UVB rating with genuine UVA coverage, from either a modern organic filter set or a high mineral load, and each entry says plainly whether that rating is FDA-tested or a foreign label claim.

> The two Korean sunscreens built on modern organic filters, [[beauty-of-joseon-relief-sun-rice-probiotics-spf50]] and [[isntree-hyaluronic-acid-watery-sun-gel-spf-50]], carry the strongest UVA grades on this list, and Beauty of Joseon is the only product here with an independent lab test (Consumer Reports) behind those numbers. Among the FDA-tested US options, the high-load mineral formulas and Neutrogena's heavily stabilized avobenzone outscore the plainer avobenzone-only sets on UVA. Two Dr.G- and Supergoop-branded pages are kept as discontinued reference entries rather than ranked, since neither formula is currently sold.

## Modern-filter Korean sunscreens with the strongest UVA grades

[[beauty-of-joseon-relief-sun-rice-probiotics-spf50]] pairs a Korean SPF50+ PA++++ label with two modern UVA filters, bisoctrizole (Tinosorb M) and Uvinul A Plus. It is the only product on this list with an independent lab test behind those numbers: Consumer Reports scored its UVA and UVB protection 5 of 5 each and measured an average SPF of 36, above the older-filter US sunscreens it competes with as an import, though the SPF50+ figure itself is a Korean label claim, not FDA-tested.

[[isntree-hyaluronic-acid-watery-sun-gel-spf-50]] carries the same strong UVB and UVA grades on an even broader modern-filter set, Tinosorb S, Tinosorb M, and Uvinul A Plus together, also rated under Korean rather than FDA testing. No independent lab test of this formula was found, and the brand's own US store says this exact SKU "is not available in the U.S. due to OTC regulations," pointing to a separate reformulation instead.

## FDA-tested US mineral sunscreens

[[prequel-sun-barrier-mineral-sunscreen]] is FDA-tested Broad Spectrum SPF 50 from an 18% zinc oxide load, roughly double [[eltamd-uv-clear-spf-46]]'s 9%, earning the same strong UVB and UVA grades as the two Korean imports above under actual FDA testing rather than a foreign label claim.

[[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50]] is FDA-tested Broad Spectrum SPF 50 from titanium dioxide 9% and zinc oxide 7%, grading notable rather than strong on both axes. It is the most affordable FDA-tested mineral option on this list and, like Prequel, avoids the photostability trade-offs of an avobenzone-based formula.

## FDA-tested chemical sunscreens on the older, avobenzone-based US filter set

[[neutrogena-ultra-sheer-dry-touch-spf-55]] is FDA-tested Broad Spectrum SPF 55 on avobenzone stabilized by a relatively high 10% octocrylene load, grading notable for UVA, a step above the other three products in this group.

[[eltamd-uv-clear-spf-46]] is FDA-tested Broad Spectrum SPF 46 from zinc oxide 9% and octinoxate rather than avobenzone. Its UVA protection still grades modest, because that 9% zinc load sits well below Prequel's 18%.

[[la-roche-posay-anthelios-melt-in-milk-spf-60]] is FDA-tested Broad Spectrum SPF 60, the highest UVB number on this list, but its UVA protection also grades modest, resting on avobenzone stabilized by a lower 7% octocrylene load than Neutrogena's.

[[round-lab-birch-juice-moisturizing-sun-spf-50]] is sold in the US at SPF45+ PA++++ on the same class of older filters as the three products above, avobenzone, homosalate, and octisalate, grading notable for UVB and modest for UVA, the weakest UVB grade in this group.

## Korean- and EU-tested options, not FDA-tested

[[avene-mineral-ultra-light-spf-50]] is an all-mineral titanium dioxide 4%/zinc oxide 6% formula rated SPF50+ under the EU testing standard, grading strong for UVB and notable for UVA, with a lower combined 10% mineral load than Prequel or CeraVe.

[[dr-g-green-mild-up-sun]] relies on zinc oxide alone, at an undisclosed concentration, for its Korean SPF50+ PA++++ rating, grading notable for both UVB and UVA.

[[skin1004-madagascar-centella-air-fit-suncream]] discloses no zinc oxide or titanium dioxide concentration at all, so its Korean SPF50+ PA++++ rating carries a mixed rather than solid evidence grade, the weakest evidence quality on this list. The 50 ml size is also currently listed sold out on the brand's own store.

## Discontinued reference entries

[[supergoop-unseen-sunscreen-spf-40]] and [[dr-g-baby-mild-up-sun]] remain on the site as reference pages only, not ranked above. Supergoop's own site no longer sells the SPF 40 formula, replacing it with a reformulated SPF 50 version, and Dr.G's current sunscreen lineup no longer includes Baby Mild Up Sun.
