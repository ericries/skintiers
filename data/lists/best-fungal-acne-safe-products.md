[[fungal-acne]] (Malassezia folliculitis) is caused by a yeast, not bacteria or clogged pores, so the products that actually clear it are antifungals, not the salicylic acid or benzoyl peroxide aimed at ordinary acne. This list ranks two different things honestly, and keeps them separate: antifungal washes and creams with real anti-Malassezia evidence first, then a couple of "fungal-acne-safe" leave-on humectants that simply avoid the fatty-acid esters, plant oils, and polysorbates the yeast is thought to feed on. The second group does not treat anything; it just stays out of the way.

## The tier list

Ketoconazole and ciclopirox lead because they have the deepest independent trial base against Malassezia-driven scaling, ketoconazole as the azole named first-line for fungal acne itself, ciclopirox with a large Cochrane meta-analysis behind it.[^saunte][^cochrane] Selenium sulfide is next, explicitly named as an off-label topical option for fungal acne, though less directly studied for it than the azoles.[^saunte] Ducray's combination cream pairs two genuine anti-Malassezia actives for leave-on facial use, but its own cream-specific numbers are unpublished brand data, so it sits below the single-ingredient shampoos with independent trials. Pyrithione zinc products are real antifungals too, just a tier below on comparative evidence. The two hyaluronic-acid products at the bottom are not antifungals at all: they are included because their ingredient lists are unusually clean of plant oils, fatty-acid esters, and polysorbates, the ingredients most often blamed for feeding Malassezia, though neither product was tested for that specifically.

None of these off-label uses (shampoos or bar soap used as a short-contact body/face wash) have been trialed in fungal acne patients directly; the evidence behind each active is for dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis, the same Malassezia-driven process. Widespread or recurrent fungal acne, or anything not improving after a few weeks of consistent antifungal use, is a reason to see a dermatologist for oral itraconazole or fluconazole rather than escalating over-the-counter products.[^saunte]
