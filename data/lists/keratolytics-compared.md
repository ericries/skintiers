Keratolytics loosen and shed built-up keratin, which is why they are the fast route for rough, scaly skin. **Bottom line:** the head-to-head trials that exist point at strength and formulation rather than at which acid you choose. Picking between urea, lactic acid and salicylic acid matters less than picking an adequate concentration of any of them, and each agent's own page carries its grade.

> **Two old trials are the only direct comparisons here, and they disagree about what matters.** Against a weaker version of the same acid, strength won: "12% ammonium lactate lotion was significantly more effective than 5% lactic acid lotion in reducing the severity of xerosis" in both a three-week treatment period and a three-week no-treatment period afterwards.[^rogers] Against a different agent at a sensible strength, nothing won. A paired trial applying 5% salicylic acid with 10% urea to one foot and 12% ammonium lactate to the other found "there was no statistically significant difference between treatment groups".[^jennings]

## What each one is at what strength

**Urea is the clearest case of concentration deciding the action.** It is formulated from roughly 5% up to 40%, behaving as a humectant at the low end and a keratolytic at the high end, and the two uses rest on different evidence. The detail, including why its high-strength keratolytic use is best documented as a step that helps antifungal drugs reach thickened nails rather than as a stand-alone treatment, is on [[urea]].

**Salicylic acid changes job entirely with strength.** At 0.5% to 2% it is a nonprescription acne active; its best-evidenced keratolytic use is wart removal at 12% to 40%, which is not what anyone means by smoothing texture. See [[salicylic-acid]], and [[capryloyl-salicylic-acid-lha]] for the modified version sold on more precise exfoliation.

**Lactic acid is tested in leave-on products from 5% to 12%, and the neutralised 12% form is the one with drug approval.** [[ammonium-lactate]] is 12% lactic acid neutralised with ammonium hydroxide, approved as a prescription drug for dry scaly skin, which is why it carries both the head-to-head wins above and the best-documented evidence in this category. The acid itself is on [[lactic-acid]].

## What those comparisons do not establish

**They are about xerosis, mostly on feet.** The salicylic-acid-plus-urea comparison was in "mild-to-moderate xerosis" of the foot, and the lactic acid comparison in "moderate to severe xerosis".[^jennings][^rogers] Neither tested facial texture, so nothing here ranks these agents for the face. The texture goal is at [[smoothing-skin-texture]].

**The strength comparison does not isolate strength.** 12% ammonium lactate differs from 5% lactic acid in two ways at once, concentration and whether the acid is neutralised, so the trial shows the higher-strength neutralised product winning rather than proving concentration alone did it.[^rogers]

**The agent comparison tested a combination against a single agent.** The salicylic acid arm was 5% salicylic acid *with* 10% urea, so the honest reading is that this combination and 12% ammonium lactate performed alike, not that salicylic acid alone equals ammonium lactate.[^jennings] That trial also shrank as it ran, from 70 enrolled to 54 evaluated at two weeks and 39 at four.[^jennings]

**Both trials are old.** 1989 and 1998. They remain the direct comparisons available, which is itself worth knowing about a category sold on constant reformulation.
