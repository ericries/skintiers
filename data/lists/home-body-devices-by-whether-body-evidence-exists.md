The site covers three devices sold for use below the neck. This page exists to say how much published evidence stands behind that positioning, which is less than the category implies and in one case is none.

> **One study, one modality, one device nobody here sells.** The only home-use body trial found is a 2010 evaluation of a TriPollar radiofrequency device in 24 people.[^tripollar] For home microcurrent on the body there is no trial at all, and for home LED on the trunk the evidence the site relies on covers the face only.

## What the one body trial found

Boisnic and colleagues tested a home-use TriPollar radiofrequency device on the abdomen and thighs of 24 subjects, alongside laboratory work on an ex-vivo human skin model.[^tripollar] Two things in it are worth separating.

**The human result is modest and split.** The abstract reports that "A significant average reduction of 2.4 cm was measured on the treated thighs", while on the control thighs "a lesser, non-significant reduction was found".[^tripollar] On the abdomen, "A certain reduction was measured in the abdomen circumferences, although it was not significant."[^tripollar] Abdominal laxity went from 1.4 to 0.8 on the paper's own scale. The authors' own summary is appropriately hedged: treatment "may lead to discrete circumference reduction and moderate laxity improvement".[^tripollar]

**The striking numbers are not from the people.** The 82% increase in hypodermal glycerol release, the 34% alteration in adipocyte appearance and the 31% rise in collagen synthesis all come from the ex-vivo skin model, not from the 24 subjects.[^tripollar] Those are the figures most likely to be quoted at you, and they describe excised tissue in a laboratory.

That is the entire home-use body-device literature this page could find.

## Why the other two inherit nothing

**Microcurrent.** Searching the published literature for microcurrent body outcomes returns burn wound healing, chronic wound care, animal studies and musculoskeletal electrotherapy, and no trial of body contouring or toning in people.[^search] [[nubody-skin-toning-device]] was cleared by showing equivalence to a facial microcurrent device, and that filing contains no body outcome either; its page sets this out.

**LED on the trunk.** The GRADE review the site uses for home LED grades facial acne and facial rejuvenation, which is the basis for [[home-use-skincare-devices-by-evidence]]. A chest-and-back panel such as [[revive-light-therapy-lux-panel]] is being sold for an application that review does not assess. [[home-led-devices-for-body-acne]] makes the narrower version of this point for acne specifically.

## What this means in practice

- **Facial evidence does not transfer downward.** Skin on the trunk and thighs differs in thickness and in what is underneath it, and no amount of facial data speaks to circumference.
- **Ask which part of a study was done in people.** The TriPollar paper is a fair example of a real human result sitting beside much larger laboratory percentages.
- **For microcurrent specifically, there is nothing to weigh.** Nobody has published the test, which is a different situation from a test that came out badly.
