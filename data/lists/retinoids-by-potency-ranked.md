Compares the topical [[retinoids]] the site has profiled by potency and evidence, from prescription drugs to the mildest over-the-counter esters. Most of these compounds sit on one vitamin-A conversion pathway, retinyl ester to [[retinol]] to [[retinaldehyde]] to retinoic acid ([[tretinoin]]), and each step is an oxidation the skin must perform before the molecule can bind a retinoic acid receptor. The fewer conversion steps a retinoid needs, the closer its evidence sits to prescription-grade; more steps generally means a milder, slower, better-tolerated product with a thinner trial record.

> [[tretinoin]] and [[tazarotene]] are receptor-active on application and carry the deepest trial record. [[adapalene]] is also receptor-active and prescription-grade in its evidence, but it sits off the conversion pathway entirely and is now sold over the counter. [[retinaldehyde]] is one oxidation step from the active acid and is the best-evidenced over-the-counter precursor; [[retinol]] is two steps away and is the most-studied over-the-counter option. [[hydroxypinacolone-retinoate]] and [[retinyl-retinoate]] are lab-engineered esters marketed as alternatives to that pathway, and the [[retinyl-esters|plain retinyl esters]] (palmitate, acetate, propionate, linoleate) need the most conversion of all and carry the thinnest, least favorable evidence.

## Prescription retinoids: receptor-active, no conversion needed

[[tretinoin]] is all-trans retinoic acid itself, the molecule that binds retinoic acid receptors directly rather than requiring conversion, and it is the trial-validated benchmark against which milder retinoids are measured, with replicated vehicle-controlled RCTs and an 8-RCT meta-analysis behind its photoaging effect and FDA approval for acne. [[tretinoin-0-025-cream]] is the entry prescription strength, and in its own product-specific trial matched the stronger 0.1% cream on photoaging with less irritation.

[[tazarotene]] is a prescription pro-drug hydrolyzed by skin enzymes into tazarotenic acid, which favors two of the three retinoic acid receptor subtypes, the same selective design used by adapalene. It carries FDA approval and vehicle-controlled registration trials across three separate uses, acne, plaque psoriasis, and photodamaged skin, the broadest indication set of any topical retinoid on the site.

## Adapalene: prescription-grade data, now over the counter

[[adapalene]] is a synthetic retinoid that acts on the receptors directly, off the retinol-conversion pathway, and its acne evidence is solid: a Cochrane review and a pooled analysis of comparative RCTs put it roughly level with benzoyl peroxide and tretinoin on efficacy while better tolerated than tretinoin. Originally prescription-only, it is now also sold as an FDA over-the-counter acne treatment at the same 0.1% strength, as in [[la-roche-posay-effaclar-adapalene-gel-0-1]].

## Retinaldehyde: the best-studied over-the-counter precursor

[[retinaldehyde]] sits one oxidation step below the active acid, and Saurat and colleagues found it induces the retinoic-acid marker CRABP-2 more strongly than retinol does. It has the strongest over-the-counter trial record on the site: a vehicle-controlled trial found 0.05% retinaldehyde performed comparably to 0.05% retinoic acid on wrinkles and roughness at 18 weeks, evidence ahead of retinol's and well ahead of the retinyl esters'. [[avene-retrinal-0-1-intensive-cream]] is a labeled 0.1% retinaldehyde cream in this tier.

## Retinol: the most-studied over-the-counter retinoid

[[retinol]] sits two oxidation steps from the active acid, and while that extra distance makes it milder and slower than tretinoin, it has the deepest over-the-counter evidence base by volume: two independent vehicle-controlled RCTs in different populations, plus mechanistic studies showing it raises collagen and lowers collagen-degrading enzymes. [[cerave-resurfacing-retinol-serum]] is a genuine (non-ester) retinol product in this tier, though its own label does not disclose a percentage.

## Novel synthetic esters and retinyl esters: the weakest, least direct data

[[retinyl-retinoate]], an ester joining retinol and retinoic acid, has the least-thin evidence of this bottom group: small split-face trials from its originating patent holder found it outperformed retinol on roughness, though none of that work has been independently replicated. [[hydroxypinacolone-retinoate]] (marketed as Granactive Retinoid) has no controlled human trial isolating it; its supporting data are in-vitro cell work and combination-product studies that cannot attribute an effect to HPR alone. [[the-inkey-list-starter-retinol-serum]] pairs HPR with a low, 0.01% dose of retinaldehyde, well below the 0.05-0.1% tested in retinaldehyde's own controlled trials.

Plain [[retinyl-esters]] (retinyl palmitate, acetate, propionate, and linoleate) sit at the far end of the pathway, needing hydrolysis to retinol and then two further oxidations before they do anything, and they carry the weakest evidence on the site: the one clinical trial of a retinyl ester cream, 0.15% retinyl propionate, found no statistically significant improvement over placebo on any measured endpoint.
