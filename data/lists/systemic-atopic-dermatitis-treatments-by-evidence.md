Systemic drugs for atopic dermatitis that other treatments have not controlled, ranked on how well their own clearance evidence holds up. These are prescription pills and injections managed by a physician, not skincare. The barrier-repair and trigger-avoidance side of eczema, which is where this site's usual advice lives, is on [[atopic-dermatitis]].

> **Bottom line.** The ranking most people want, which drug clears skin best, has a clear answer: upadacitinib 30 mg came top across every endpoint in a 2026 network meta-analysis of 33 randomized trials, and it led the real-world data too.[^nma][^ronnstad] The ranking that matters in a clinic is close to the reverse. Both oral JAK inhibitors graded here are approved only for disease **not** controlled by other systemic drugs **including biologics**, and both carry a boxed warning for serious infections, mortality, malignancy, major adverse cardiovascular events and thrombosis.[^fdaupa][^fdaabro] So the drugs at the top of this list are the ones a dermatologist reaches for last. Read the tiers as clearance evidence, not as a running order.

## How This Is Graded

Two 2026 evidence syntheses do most of the work, and they answer different questions.

The first is a Bayesian network meta-analysis of phase 3 and 4 randomized trials of approved targeted systemics, covering "27 reports encompassing 33 trials and 16,334 participants".[^nma] It ranks drugs that have mostly never been tested against each other, by routing through their shared placebo comparisons. Its finding was that "Upadacitinib 30 mg consistently exhibited the highest probability of achieving each clinical endpoint", while "no significant differences were observed between dupilumab 300 mg and stapokibart 300 mg, or between ivarmacitinib 8 mg and upadacitinib 15 mg".[^nma] The authors are explicit about the limit: these are "derived primarily from indirect comparisons, and head-to-head randomized trials are needed".[^nma]

The second pools real-world use of the three oral JAK inhibitors across 63 observational studies, and found clearance at 16 weeks of 75% and 38% (EASI-75 and EASI-90) for abrocitinib, 51% and 24% for baricitinib, and 83% and 55% for upadacitinib.[^ronnstad] EASI-75 means a 75% improvement in eczema severity.

These two should not be stacked on one axis. One pools randomized trials, the other pools everyday practice, and a percentage from a real-world cohort is not comparable with a percentage from a trial population. What makes the upadacitinib result credible is that the two agree on direction while measuring different things.

## What the Ranking Leaves Out

Three limits are worth stating plainly, because a tier list invites being read as more complete than it is.

**Only two drugs are graded.** The site has pages for upadacitinib and abrocitinib, so only those carry tiers. Baricitinib is the third oral JAK inhibitor and the weakest of them on clearance, at EASI-75 51% and EASI-90 24%.[^ronnstad] More importantly, **dupilumab**, the biologic that both JAK labels treat as the therapy to try first, has no page here yet and therefore no tier.[^fdaupa][^fdaabro] Its absence from the ladder is a gap in this site's coverage, not a judgement about the drug.

**Clearance is not the only outcome.** The boxed warning on the JAK class covers risks that an EASI score cannot capture, and the real-world data is thin exactly where those risks live: the pooled review notes that "few studies reported serious AEs".[^ronnstad] What it did capture were the common, treatment-limiting problems, acne and herpes simplex reactivation, which the authors call "the most common reasons for discontinuation".[^ronnstad]

**Indirect comparison is not head-to-head.** Both syntheses say so, and it is the single biggest caveat on any ordering of these drugs.[^nma][^ronnstad]
