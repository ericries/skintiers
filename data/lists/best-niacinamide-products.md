Topical niacinamide has modest evidence, most of it from small or manufacturer-run trials at 2% to 5%, for supporting the skin barrier, evening facial hyperpigmentation, and mild inflammatory acne, the case set out on [[niacinamide]]. Every serum below carries niacinamide as its headline or co-headline active. Three use it plain at 10%, twice the top of that tested range; two pair it with a retinoid, where part of the case for using the product rests on the retinoid rather than the niacinamide.

> None of the five products below has been tested in a trial of its own. They are ranked by how their disclosed niacinamide concentration and formula line up against the evidence on [[niacinamide]], with health-oriented uses (barrier, pigment, acne) weighed ahead of cosmetic pore- and glow-positioning, and a combination product's case for its retinoid kept separate from its case for niacinamide.

## A 10% niacinamide serum with an added, trial-backed oil-reduction case

[[some-by-mi-yuja-niacin-serum|Some By Mi Yuja Niacin 30 Days Blemish Care Serum]] pairs 10% niacinamide with an 83% yuja (citron) extract base. It carries the same health-oriented grades as the plain 10% serums below (barrier support, facial hyperpigmentation, mild acne), plus one they lack: a 2% niacinamide moisturizer significantly lowered facial sebum output in two dedicated trials, a 4-week, 100-subject placebo-controlled study and a 6-week, 30-subject split-face study, reported on [[niacinamide-sebum-studies]] and graded a health-adjacent use on this product's page, versus the plain serums' oil-control claims below, which grade only as minimal-effect cosmetic positioning. Like the plain serums, its 10% dose sits above the 2% to 5% actually tested, and its citron peel oil and the fragrance components limonene and linalool add a tolerability consideration the fragrance-free serums below do not carry. About $19.70 for 50 ml, several times the plain serums' price.

## Plain, low-cost 10% niacinamide serums

[[the-ordinary-niacinamide-10-zinc-1|The Ordinary Niacinamide 10% + Zinc 1%]] and [[good-molecules-niacinamide-serum|Good Molecules Niacinamide Serum]] carry an identical set of grades: modest effect on preliminary-to-mixed evidence for barrier support, hyperpigmentation, and mild acne, and a minimal-effect, preliminary cosmetic grade for their own pore- and oil-control marketing. Both are fragrance-free, water-based 10% niacinamide serums priced at about $6 for 30 ml, above the 2% to 5% concentration actually used in the trials behind that evidence, a gap the cosmetic chemists at Chemist Confessions tie in part to this exact Ordinary product's popularity. The Ordinary adds 1% zinc PCA for oil control, an addition with no established independent contribution; Good Molecules omits it, using a shorter nine-ingredient list instead. Neither formula is shown to outperform the other.

## Niacinamide paired with a retinoid

[[youth-to-the-people-retinal-niacinamide-serum|Youth To The People Retinal + Niacinamide Youth Serum]] discloses niacinamide at 5%, within the range actually tested in the trials on [[niacinamide]], the only product on this list dosed inside that range. Its primary anti-aging case, though, rests on retinal (retinaldehyde), the best-evidenced over-the-counter retinoid form, whose own concentration this product's retailers do not disclose; that evidence lives on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]], not on the niacinamide. At about £66 to EUR75.90 for 30 ml, it is by far the most expensive product on this list.

[[anua-nano-retinol-0-3-niacin-renewing-serum|Anua Nano Retinol 0.3% + Niacin Renewing Serum]] lists niacinamide third on the ingredient list without disclosing a concentration, paired with retinol at 0.3%, a weaker-evidenced over-the-counter retinoid form than the retinaldehyde in the Youth To The People serum above, because retinol needs an extra enzymatic conversion step to reach retinoic acid; that comparison is set out on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]. Its niacinamide-specific grade rests on a single manufacturer trial of a similar concentration reporting improved tone and fine lines, a cosmetic endpoint.
