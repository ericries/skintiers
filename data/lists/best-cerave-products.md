[[cerave|CeraVe]] built its whole line around one formulation idea, developed with dermatologists: pair three ceramides with a humectant (usually hyaluronic acid or glycerin) in a fragrance-free base, a positioning explained on [[ceramides]] and [[moisturizing]]. That base is a genuinely solid, well-evidenced barrier moisturizer category. It is not, by itself, a reason to rank a product high here; what moves a CeraVe product up or down this list is whether it also carries a real, dosed active with its own trial evidence for the job on the label, an FDA-regulated OTC drug ingredient, or a tested mineral sunscreen filter.

## The tier list

The mineral sunscreens and the OTC drug products (pramoxine for itch, salicylic acid for psoriasis scale) lead, because their actives are FDA-tested or monograph-recognized at a stated percentage. The plain ceramide-and-humectant moisturizers follow: the barrier-moisturizer category has solid trial support, even though no single CeraVe formula has been trial-tested on its own and the ceramide addition specifically has not been shown to beat a plain vehicle on eczema severity. Lower down sit the products whose named active is real (retinol, or the salicylic acid the "SA" line advertises) but undisclosed in strength, so its dose cannot be compared to the concentrations tested in the underlying trials. The plain cleansers rank last: gentle, low-surfactant washing has category-level support, but a rinse-off product leaves little of any active behind, and no trial isolates these two formulas.

**[[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50|CeraVe Hydrating Mineral Sunscreen SPF 50]]** and **[[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-30-face|SPF 30 Face]]**. FDA-tested Broad Spectrum, mineral-only zinc oxide and titanium dioxide formulas; the SPF 50 carries higher filter loads (9%/7% vs 6%/5%) for stronger UVA reach. Sunscreen-filter evidence lives on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

**[[cerave-healing-ointment|CeraVe Healing Ointment]]**. Mostly petrolatum (46.5% to 49.5%), the reference occlusive for cutting water loss on chafed or cracked skin; ceramides and hyaluronic acid are add-ons at undisclosed strength.

**[[cerave-itch-relief-moisturizing-lotion|CeraVe Itch Relief Moisturizing Lotion]]**. An FDA-labeled OTC drug, pramoxine hydrochloride 1% as an external analgesic, in the ceramide-and-glycerin base; it numbs itch, it does not treat what is causing it.

**[[cerave-psoriasis-moisturizing-cream|CeraVe Psoriasis Moisturizing Cream]]**. Salicylic acid in a keratolytic, monograph-recognized role for lifting psoriasis scale, plus the standard ceramide base; it manages scale and moisturizes but is not disease-modifying.

**[[cerave-daily-moisturizing-lotion|Daily Moisturizing Lotion]]**, **[[cerave-moisturizing-cream|Moisturizing Cream]]**, and **[[cerave-pm-facial-moisturizing-lotion|PM Facial Moisturizing Lotion]]**. The core ceramide-plus-hyaluronic-acid line; solid category-level evidence for barrier support and hydration, no trial of these exact formulas.

**[[cerave-skin-renewing-night-cream|Skin Renewing Night Cream]]**. Same barrier-moisturizer case, marketed toward nighttime skin renewal without a disclosed active driving that specific claim beyond the standard ceramide-and-HA base.

**[[cerave-acne-control-gel|Acne Control Gel]]**. Salicylic acid 2%, the top of the OTC acne monograph range, in a leave-on gel; a real, guideline-recognized acne active, though a second-tier one behind benzoyl peroxide and prescription retinoids.

**[[cerave-resurfacing-retinol-serum|Resurfacing Retinol Serum]]** and **[[cerave-skin-renewing-retinol-serum|Skin Renewing Retinol Serum]]**. Genuine retinol, not a weaker retinyl ester, but CeraVe does not disclose the percentage on either, so there is no way to compare the dose to the concentrations tested in the photoaging trials behind [[retinol]].

**[[cerave-sa-smoothing-cream|SA Smoothing Cream]]**. Urea, declared at 10%, is the best-evidenced active in this UK body cream, with controlled-trial support for dry, scaling skin; the salicylic acid the "SA" name promises is present without a stated concentration.

**[[cerave-sa-body-lotion-for-rough-and-bumpy-skin|SA Body Lotion for Rough and Bumpy Skin]]**. Two exfoliating acids, salicylic acid and ammonium lactate (the salt form of lactic acid), neither disclosed at a stated concentration; the acid-class evidence for rough, bumpy skin lives on [[keratosis-pilaris]].

**[[cerave-sa-smoothing-cleanser|SA Smoothing Cleanser]]**. A rinse-off salicylic acid wash; short contact time limits how much exfoliating acid the skin actually sees.

**[[cerave-foaming-facial-cleanser|Foaming Facial Cleanser]]** and **[[cerave-hydrating-facial-cleanser|Hydrating Facial Cleanser]]**. Gentle, low-surfactant washing has category-level support for not stripping the barrier, but as rinse-off products they leave little of their ceramide or niacinamide content behind, and no trial isolates either formula.

Nothing here treats acne, eczema, or psoriasis on its own; CeraVe positions these as dermatologist-developed maintenance products, and that is the brand's own claim, not an independent trial finding. For the disease-specific evidence, see [[acne]], [[atopic-dermatitis]], and [[psoriasis]].
