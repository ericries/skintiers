Four procedure pages on this site each carried a note explaining why they could not host a ranking of their own. This is that ranking, drawn from one 2025 Bayesian network meta-analysis of 68 randomized trials in 4,480 patients.[^nma] **Bottom line:** the winners are all laser-plus-something combinations, nothing beat the others on side-effect risk, and the procedure that wins on comfort is not the one that wins on scar reduction.

## What the ranking does and does not tell you

**It ranks combinations, not single procedures.** The three severity and satisfaction winners are laser plus platelet-rich plasma, laser plus filler injection, and laser plus chemical peels.[^nma] If you are choosing one thing to do, this analysis does not name it. The peel pairing now has its own page, where a third analysis disputes whether any of these rankings is dependable: [[chemical-peels-acne-scars]].

**The confidence behind each rank varies a lot.** Laser plus PRP led the ECCA scale at a SUCRA of 98.4%, which is close to always-best across the simulated rankings. Laser plus filler led the Goodman and Baron Scale at 72.1%, which is a much weaker separation from the field; a rank that high is compatible with several other options being nearly as good.[^nma] Treating both as "best" flattens a real difference in certainty.

**Nothing on this list protects your skin from the known risks.** The analysis found "no strong evidence suggesting a treatment reduced the risk of erythema nor post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation compared to the other treatments".[^nma] Those two outcomes matter most in deeper skin tones, and they are not a basis for choosing between these procedures.

**Scope.** These are treatments for scars left by acne that has already happened. Treating active [[acne]] early is still the only thing that prevents new scars, and the flat brown or red marks that get called scars are handled on [[acne-scars]] and [[hyperpigmentation]].
