Compares the vitamin C forms the site has profiled by how much controlled human evidence stands behind each, not by how it is marketed. Every form shares one active endpoint, L-ascorbic acid, but they differ in how many conversion steps separate them from it and in how much of that conversion is actually demonstrated in skin. The full mechanism, the conversion pathway, and the per-form citations live on the hub, [[vitamin-c]]; this page ranks the family that hub describes.

> [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]] is the reference form and the only one biologically active without conversion, with the deepest human record. Among the stabilized derivatives, [[magnesium-ascorbyl-phosphate]] is the one with a published clinical pigment study, and even that is a single open cohort. The remaining derivatives, [[sodium-ascorbyl-phosphate]], [[ascorbyl-glucoside]], [[3-o-ethyl-ascorbic-acid]], [[tetrahexyldecyl-ascorbate|tetrahexyldecyl ascorbate]], and [[ascorbyl-palmitate]], are more stable than L-ascorbic acid by design but carry little controlled human efficacy data, and their benefit rests on conversion that is variously slow, poorly absorbed, or unquantified.

## The reference form: L-ascorbic acid

[[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]] (LAA, pure vitamin C) is the unmodified vitamin and the only form the skin can use without first converting it, which makes it the benchmark the derivatives are measured against. It is also the least stable, oxidizing readily in water and air, and it must be formulated at low pH and high concentration to penetrate, the formulation problem the derivatives were built to avoid. Its trial record and the penetration work behind it are on its own page.

## The one derivative with a clinical study: magnesium ascorbyl phosphate

[[magnesium-ascorbyl-phosphate]] (MAP) is a phosphate salt of ascorbic acid, more stable than LAA and usable nearer neutral pH, but converted to ascorbic acid slowly and poorly absorbed through skin. It is the single derivative here with a published clinical pigment result, and that result is a modest, preliminary depigmenting signal from one open cohort rather than a vehicle-controlled trial. That is enough to place it above the other derivatives, and well below LAA.

## Stable but thinly evidenced derivatives

[[sodium-ascorbyl-phosphate]], [[ascorbyl-glucoside]], [[3-o-ethyl-ascorbic-acid]], [[tetrahexyldecyl-ascorbate|tetrahexyldecyl ascorbate]], and [[ascorbyl-palmitate]] share one problem: their skin benefit depends on conversion to ascorbic acid that is slow and poorly absorbed for the phosphates, unknown in rate for the glucoside, and in doubt for the lipid-soluble esters. Their effect versus L-ascorbic acid is unresolvable in isolation, and much of the data behind them is in-vitro stability, penetration, and conversion work rather than controlled human efficacy. They sit together at the bottom because the evidence does not support ranking them against each other.
