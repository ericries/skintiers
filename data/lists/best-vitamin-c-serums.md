Topical vitamin C's evidence splits into a health-oriented antioxidant and photoprotection-support role, plus a collagen-synthesis role, covered on [[vitamin-c]] and, for the reference form, [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]], and a separate cosmetic brightening and [[hyperpigmentation|tone-evening]] effect that builds more gradually. The strongest health evidence belongs to one specific combination: 15% L-ascorbic acid paired with 1% [[tocopherol|vitamin E]] and 0.5% [[ferulic-acid]], the concentrations Lin 2005 and Murray 2008 tested.[^1][^2]

> Only one of the six serums here, SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic, has been tested in a published trial of its own. The other five are ranked by how completely their disclosed formula matches that studied combination and concentration, not by a head-to-head trial of each product.

## The studied formula: 15% L-ascorbic acid, vitamin E, and ferulic acid

**[[skinceuticals-c-e-ferulic|SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic]].** The reference formula itself, 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% alpha-tocopherol, and 0.5% ferulic acid, the exact combination and concentrations Lin 2005 and Murray 2008 tested. Adding ferulic acid to the vitamin C and vitamin E solution roughly doubled measured photoprotection in pig skin, and the same formula reduced UV-damage markers (erythema, sunburn cells, thymine dimers) in a small human study.[^1][^2] Both studies came from the formula's inventors, who disclosed a commercial tie to SkinCeuticals. At about $185 for 30 ml, it is by far the most expensive of the six.

## Low-cost matches to the same three-antioxidant combination

No product in this tier has been tested in a trial of its own. Each is ranked by how completely its disclosed formula matches the concentrations above and by what independent, product-specific checks exist.

**[[maelove-the-glow-maker|Maelove The Glow Maker]].** States 15% L-ascorbic acid, the concentration the SkinCeuticals studies tested, though its vitamin E and ferulic acid percentages go undisclosed. An independent, formulator-run accelerated stability test found it aged normally over three weeks, grouping it with the better-behaved dupes tested. About $33.

**[[timeless-20-vitamin-c-e-ferulic-serum|Timeless 20% Vitamin C + E Ferulic Acid Serum]].** States 20% L-ascorbic acid, above the concentration the reference studies used and at the point formulation research identifies as maximal skin absorption; an independent blogger reports its vitamin E and ferulic acid at about 1% each, though the manufacturer does not disclose those figures. The same stability test that passed Maelove found this formula degraded and sputtered faster than several peers, though the testers still passed it, and the manufacturer's own product page recommends refrigeration and use within 3 to 6 months as it oxidizes. NYT Wirecutter names it its budget pick, "an affordable option with proven ingredients." About $28.

**[[trader-joes-nourish-vitamins-c-e-facial-serum|Trader Joe's Vitamin C Serum]].** Its own front label states all three of the studied concentrations, 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% alpha-tocopherol, 0.5% ferulic acid, a fuller disclosed match than either Maelove or Timeless carries, though no independent stability test or clinical check of this product has been published, and reviewers note its base swaps in sunflower seed oil and a different solubilizer. At $9.99 for 1 fl oz, it is the least expensive of the six.

## Pure L-ascorbic acid, without the vitamin E pairing

**[[prequel-lucent-c-vitamin-c-serum|Prequel Lucent-C Brightening Vitamin C Serum]].** Also 15% L-ascorbic acid at a low, penetration-appropriate pH (3.2), but formulated without vitamin E, so the ferulic-acid photoprotection studies, which tested vitamin C together with vitamin E, do not describe this formula; it substitutes ergothioneine and niacinamide. Wirecutter tested it and did not select it, citing a "thin, adhesive feel and a musty-meets-chem-lab aroma." About $23.

## A vitamin C derivative, not L-ascorbic acid

**[[beauty-of-joseon-light-on-serum-centella-vita-c|Beauty of Joseon Light On Serum: Centella + Vita C]].** Its vitamin C is 3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid, a derivative rather than the L-ascorbic acid the penetration and photoprotection literature is built on, at a concentration the manufacturer does not state. Centella asiatica extract, listed high on the panel, adds a separate cosmetic soothing effect ([[centella-asiatica]]). About $17.
