This ranks the chronic hand eczema treatments that have pages here. The condition itself, including how to recognise it and why it is a distinct diagnosis from atopic dermatitis, is on [[chronic-hand-eczema]].

> **Read the gaps before the ranking.** The one network meta-analysis of CHE monotherapies deliberately left out the everyday first-line options. Its authors state that "Topical corticosteroids are a therapeutic option for CHE; however, we did not include studies that investigated them in our NMA due to a lack of common comparators", and that calcineurin inhibitors and moisturizers were omitted "for the same reason".[^gupta] So that paper's silence on them is a gap in the comparative literature rather than evidence against them, and a list sorted purely by it would rank them last for the wrong reason. Two further catches: the strength it ranked top, delgocitinib **8 mg/g**, is not the strength regulators approved (**20 mg/g**), and the whole network rests on **five trials**.[^gupta]

## The tier list

**[[delgocitinib|Delgocitinib (Anzupgo, 20 mg/g cream)]].** The strongest CHE-specific case on this site: approved for moderate-to-severe chronic hand eczema after corticosteroids have failed or been ruled out, backed by two vehicle-controlled 16-week trials in 960 adults, and ranked most efficacious across all three networks in the Gupta analysis.[^gupta] Temper it with its own numbers, which are on that page: clearance reached 20% and 29% against 10% and 7% on vehicle, so symptom relief is the stronger result than clear skin.

**[[moisturizing|Emollients and moisturizers]].** The barrier-first foundation, and the only rung here you can buy without a prescription. Its evidence is strong for eczema generally rather than head-to-head against CHE drugs, which is exactly why it was left out of the network. Reducing irritant and wet-work exposure belongs with it, and for an occupational condition that is often the part that decides whether anything else holds.

**[[topical-corticosteroids|Topical corticosteroids]].** What most people with hand eczema are actually given first. The moderate grade reflects a hole in the comparative evidence, not a judgement that they work less well: no CHE-specific ranking includes them. Treat it as first-line in practice but unranked in the CHE literature.

**[[tacrolimus-topical|Tacrolimus (topical calcineurin inhibitor)]].** The steroid-sparing option, useful where repeated steroid use is a problem. Same evidentiary position as corticosteroids here, resting on general eczema trials rather than hand-specific ones.

**Not ranked, and worth knowing about.** Oral alitretinoin 30 mg daily is the other treatment approved for CHE by the FDA and EMA, and subcutaneous dupilumab and PUVA also appeared in the network.[^gupta] None has a page here yet, so none is graded; they are systemic, physician-managed treatments rather than skincare.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. Every graded option except moisturizers is prescription-only, and persistent hand eczema with cracking, pain, or a workplace cause behind it needs a real assessment.
