Eleven plant-derived ingredients this site grades, sorted by what has actually been tested in people. The useful thing this sorting exposes is that a low grade means two opposite things, and the difference matters more than the grade.

> **Untested and tested-and-failed look identical on a tier list.** [[allium-cepa-onion-extract]] and [[witch-hazel]] both grade `tier: weak` here. Onion extract is weak because two independent randomised trials against petrolatum and a 13-trial meta-analysis all came out negative. Witch hazel is weak because the most relevant study was run in a keratinocyte cell line and its own authors say acne data are lacking. One has been answered and the answer was no. The other has not been asked.

## Rung 1: trial-backed and codified, with one caveat

**[[colloidal-oatmeal]]**, `tier: mid`. Two randomised controlled trials plus the FDA final monograph, which codifies the itch and irritation indication in the Code of Federal Regulations. The caveat its own page records is that both efficacy trials were funded and conducted by the same manufacturer. That is the strongest position any botanical on this site occupies, and it still carries an asterisk.

## Rung 2: tested properly in people, and it did not work

**[[allium-cepa-onion-extract]]**, `tier: weak`. Its page describes the evidence as "reasonably good but consistently negative for the isolated product": two independent randomised trials against petrolatum, plus a 13-RCT meta-analysis, all pointing the same way. This rung has one member and it is the most informative entry on the page. A negative answer from good trials is worth more than silence.

## Rung 3: one small human trial, or several that cannot be pooled

Each of these has real human data and not enough of it.

- **[[bakuchiol]]**, `tier: weak`. One small independent RCT anchors the claim; the supporting studies are open-label, uncontrolled, or test it inside a combination product.
- **[[licorice-root-extract]]**, `tier: weak`. One double-blind RCT with a base-gel control and a two-week endpoint, plus cell-culture and animal work.
- **[[green-tea-egcg]]**, `tier: mid`. A well-designed but small and non-blinded within-subject mechanistic study, and a separate trial that combined oral and topical green tea so the topical contribution cannot be isolated.
- **[[aloe-vera]]**, `tier: mid`. One small vehicle-controlled instrumental study underpins the hydration claim; the soothing use rests largely on tradition.
- **[[centella-asiatica]]**, `tier: weak`. Consistent preclinical signals, few and small human trials, no pooled clinical estimate.
- **[[tea-tree-oil]]**, `tier: weak`. A 2015 Cochrane review of complementary acne therapies included 35 studies and 3,227 participants but could pool only four of them.

## Rung 4: no human trial for the thing it is sold for

- **[[witch-hazel]]**, `tier: weak`. The most directly relevant paper tested a glycolic bark extract against induced inflammation in HaCaT keratinocytes, a cell line.
- **[[willow-bark-extract]]**, `tier: weak`. Every study found for that page measured something else: visible ageing, dark circles, or cytokines in a keratinocyte line. Two of them are uncontrolled before-and-after studies, and both were run by companies selling the ingredient, as set out on [[gopaul-2010-topical-salicin-skin-aging-pmid-20883292]] and [[jeon-2023-salix-alba-bark-extract-dark-circles-pmid-37235713]].

## What the category-level evidence says

One systematic review and meta-analysis covers the whole question for three common conditions. Jones and colleagues screened 417 articles, included 40, and pooled complementary and alternative treatments for acne, atopic dermatitis and psoriasis.[^jones] Their conclusion: "Based on our quantitative findings we conclude that there is insufficient evidence to support the efficacy and the recommendation of CAM for acne, AD, and psoriasis."[^jones]

**One of their six pooled estimates was significant, and flattening that would misrepresent the paper.** Five of the six standardised mean differences have confidence intervals crossing zero, including both acne lesion-count estimates and both psoriasis PASI estimates.[^jones] The exception is the eczema area and severity index, at a SMD of "-0.70 (95% CI [-1.19, -0.21])", which does not cross zero.[^jones] The authors still conclude insufficient evidence overall, and they name the reason: "Differences between the study designs, sample sizes, outcome measures, and treatment durations limit the generalizability of data."[^jones]

## Scope

Two plant-derived pages are deliberately absent. [[squalane]] is graded on a category-level emollient classification rather than on botanical efficacy claims, so this axis does not apply to it. [[feverfew-extract]] carries no `tier:` yet and is not placed rather than guessed at. Every grade and every characterisation above is taken from the ingredient page it names; no new literature search was run for this page.
