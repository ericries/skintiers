This ranks finished humectant-forward serums, hyaluronic acid and glycerin leading, sometimes with panthenol or a barrier-support add-on, for hydrating dry skin. The active-level evidence lives on [[hyaluronic-acid]] and [[moisturizing]]; this list only judges how well each product carries that evidence into its formula.

> A serum is a humectant delivery step, not the whole routine. On genuinely dry skin, a humectant applied alone, especially in low humidity, can pull water from the deeper skin layers toward the surface and make things worse if nothing seals it in; the full case for layering a moisturizer or occlusive on top is on [[dry-skin]] and [[moisturizing]]. None of these substitute for that step.

## The tier list

Ranked by how much of the declared formula is actually humectant, how transparent the brand is about concentration and hyaluronate molecular weight (lower-weight forms penetrate the stratum corneum; higher-weight forms sit on the surface and still help via a surface film, per [[hyaluronic-acid]]), and how much of the marketing claim rests on independent evidence versus the brand's own small internal tests.

**[[torriden-dive-in-low-molecular-hyaluronic-acid-serum|Torriden Dive-In Low Molecular Hyaluronic Acid Serum]].** Five declared hyaluronate forms skewed toward the lower-molecular-weight tier, with glycerin third on the ingredient list, ahead of all five. The most formula-transparent entry here.

**[[la-roche-posay-hyalu-b5-hyaluronic-acid-serum|La Roche-Posay Hyalu B5 Pure Hyaluronic Acid Serum]].** Glycerin sits second on the list, ahead of both declared hyaluronate forms (a standard higher-weight sodium hyaluronate and a hydrolyzed lower-weight form), so its well-evidenced humectant effect is doing at least as much work as the headline actives.

**[[niod-multi-molecular-hyaluronic-complex|NIOD Multi-Molecular Hyaluronic Complex]].** Five hyaluronate forms spanning high to low molecular weight, plus glycerin third on the list. No independent trial of this specific 15-technology complex exists, so the case rests on the category-level hyaluronic acid and glycerin evidence, not this formula in particular.

**[[chemist-confessions-aquafix|Chemist Confessions Aquafix Hydrating Water Gel]].** Leads with a declared "9.5% Smart Glycerin Complex," well ahead of 2% sodium PCA and 0.2% hyaluronic acid, making glycerin, not the headline hyaluronic acid, the main hydrating active. The most concentration-transparent formula on this list.

**[[danessa-myricks-barrier-boost-plumping-serum|Danessa Myricks Beauty Yummy Skin 10/10 Barrier Boost Hyaluronic Acid + Ectoin]].** Six declared hyaluronate forms split evenly between unmodified and hydrolyzed types, so it plausibly covers both penetrating and surface-film humectant effects, but no independent source classifies each form's actual molecular weight and the brand's own percentage claims are unpublished.

**[[vichy-mineral-89-hydrating-plumping-daily-booster|Vichy Minéral 89 Hydrating & Plumping Daily Booster]].** Pairs hyaluronic acid with polyglutamic acid, a newer humectant whose evidence so far is preliminary, cell-culture and lab-grown-skin-model only, not tested in living human skin (see [[polyglutamic-acid]]). The brand's 93%/19% hydration figures are small, uncontrolled, company-run tests, not independently published.

None of these are barrier repair on their own; for that, pair with a moisturizer per [[moisturizing]] or see [[dry-skin]] for the full routine. Nothing here is medical advice.
