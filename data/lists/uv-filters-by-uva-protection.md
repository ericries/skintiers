Compares the ultraviolet filters the site profiles by the factor that actually separates them: how much ultraviolet-A protection each one delivers. Almost every filter covers ultraviolet-B, the sunburn band that SPF measures, so UVB is not where they differ; the meaningful gap is in ultraviolet-A, and especially the long-wave UVA1 (about 340 to 400 nm) that penetrates deepest, drives photoaging, and that many filters leave largely uncovered. The mechanism, the country-by-country regulatory landscape, and the randomized evidence that regular broad-spectrum use slows photoaging and lowers skin-cancer burden all live on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; this page maps the individual filters against each other on the UVA axis.

## Why UVA is the axis that separates filters

Ultraviolet light splits into two bands that do different damage, and filters are easy to tell apart on one of them and not the other.

- **UVB (about 290 to 320 nm)** causes sunburn and most of the DNA photodamage behind keratinocyte skin cancers, and it is the band SPF measures. Nearly every filter on this page covers UVB to some degree, so it is not where they meaningfully differ; the [[octinoxate|cinnamate]] and [[octisalate|salicylate]] filters are UVB-only, and even the dedicated UVA filters are simply paired with a UVB filter to raise SPF.
- **UVA (about 320 to 400 nm)** penetrates deeper and is the principal driver of photoaging, and the hard, valuable part is the long-wave **UVA1 (about 340 to 400 nm)** at the far end. This is where filters diverge sharply: a few cover the whole UVA range, most cover only part of it, and some cover none. The US "Broad Spectrum" label is a single low pass/fail bar (a critical wavelength of 370 nm or greater), not a graded UVA strength, so two products can both say "broad spectrum" and still differ a lot in real UVA protection, as [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] explains.

A second axis rides alongside coverage: **photostability**. A filter that breaks down in sunlight loses the protection it started the day with unless the rest of the formula holds it stable. That is why [[avobenzone]], which has genuinely broad UVA coverage, sits below the photostable modern filters here, and why [[octinoxate]] sits at the bottom despite being an efficient UVB absorber. The tiers above fold both together: how far into UVA a filter reaches, and whether it stays intact under light.

## How to read the tiers

- **Modern broad-spectrum organics ([[bemotrizinol]], [[bisoctrizole]], [[drometrizole-trisiloxane]], [[mexoryl-400-mce]], [[diethylamino-hydroxybenzoyl-hexyl-benzoate|DHHB]], [[ecamsule]]).** These reach furthest into long-wave UVA and stay photostable, which is why the highest-UVA products tend to be built on them; most are standard in Europe and Asia but absent or restricted in the US. Products built on them include [[isntree-hyaluronic-acid-watery-sun-gel-spf-50]] (Tinosorb S/M plus DHHB), [[beauty-of-joseon-relief-sun-rice-probiotics-spf50]] (Tinosorb M plus DHHB and ethylhexyl triazone), and [[la-roche-posay-anthelios-uvmune-400-spf-50]] (Mexoryl 400 with Mexoryl XL and DHHB).
- **Avobenzone, the US UVA workhorse.** [[avobenzone]] covers long-wave UVA well but degrades in sunlight, so the protection it actually delivers depends on the formula stabilizing it; examples are [[neutrogena-ultra-sheer-dry-touch-spf-55]] and [[la-roche-posay-anthelios-melt-in-milk-spf-60]].
- **Mineral filters ([[zinc-oxide]], [[titanium-dioxide]]).** Photostable and broad, but broad-but-weak per unit, so their protection scales with how heavily they are loaded; zinc oxide reaches further into long-wave UVA than titanium dioxide. Seen in [[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50]], [[avene-mineral-ultra-light-spf-50]], [[prequel-sun-barrier-mineral-sunscreen]], and the zinc-oxide-based [[eltamd-uv-clear-spf-46]].
- **UVB-only filters ([[ethylhexyl-triazone]], [[iscotrizinol]], [[octinoxate]], [[homosalate]], [[octisalate]]).** These are efficient sunburn-and-SPF filters with essentially no UVA, so they are never the reason a product is broad-spectrum; they are always paired with one of the UVA filters above. Placing them at the bottom of a UVA ladder is not a mark against them, it reflects that UVA is a different job than the one they do.
