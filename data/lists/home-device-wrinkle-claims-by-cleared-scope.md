Every device below is sold to people who want fewer wrinkles. The sentence each one is actually cleared for differs enormously, and three kinds of narrowing hide inside it: the part of the face, the severity, and who the user is allowed to be.

## The three ways a wrinkle claim gets narrowed

**By site.** The clearest case here is one brand's own two panels. The dpl II Panel is cleared for "treating wrinkles" with no qualifier; the dpl SpectraLite, cleared four days apart, is cleared for "treating wrinkles within the periorbital region", and that word periorbital means around the eyes. Same brand, same technology family, radically different permission.

**By severity.** Every radiofrequency device on this list is cleared for "mild to moderate" wrinkles. None of them is cleared for severe ones, which is the group most likely to go looking for a device.

**By who you are.** Four devices here restrict the user, and not identically. NEWA and Silk'n both say "adult women who have Fitzpatrick Skin Types I-IV". Pollogen says "adult users". CurrentBody says "adults". Those are different populations written into otherwise near-identical sentences, and the skin-type ceiling of IV means the clearances stop short of the darkest skin in all four cases.

## What the ranking does not buy you

A wider clearance is not a better device, and on this list the correlation runs backwards. The device with the broadest wrinkle claim, the dpl II Panel, has no clinical study in its file at all. The device with a population-restricted claim, NEWA, is the only one whose own clinical data FDA reviewed. Reading these sentences tells you what a company is permitted to say, not what the device will do; the efficacy questions are on [[home-led-light-therapy]] and [[home-radiofrequency-skin-tightening]], and the provenance of the radiofrequency files is ranked separately on [[home-rf-devices-by-whose-evidence-the-file-rests-on]].
