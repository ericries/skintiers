This ranks finished over-the-counter and prescription products for back, chest, and shoulder acne by the strength of evidence behind their active ingredient, not by marketing. Truncal acne is the same disease as facial acne, just on skin with a larger surface area and thicker follicles, so the same evidence hierarchy applies: topical retinoids and benzoyl peroxide lead, salicylic acid is an alternative for people who cannot tolerate them, and antibacterial washes sit lowest because they treat a different mechanism with thinner direct evidence.[^statpearls]

## The tier list

A leave-on adapalene-plus-benzoyl-peroxide combination tops the list because it is FDA-labeled for acne vulgaris and pairs two first-line actives in one product. Adapalene alone follows as the OTC first-line retinoid. The two PanOxyl benzoyl peroxide washes come next, ranked by strength, with the honest caveat that a rinse-off wash contacts skin more briefly than a leave-on gel. Paula's Choice's salicylic acid spray is the adjunct option StatPearls describes for people who cannot tolerate retinoids.[^statpearls] CLn's sodium hypochlorite body wash closes the list: its antibacterial mechanism has a sound rationale but the least direct trial support of anything here, and it is not one of the guideline-listed acne actives.

Large surface area favors wash-off benzoyl peroxide for convenience, but leave-on actives generally carry the stronger evidence. Widespread, scarring, or severe truncal acne warrants a dermatologist and possible oral therapy, not merely an over-the-counter swap.[^statpearls] Nothing here is medical advice.
