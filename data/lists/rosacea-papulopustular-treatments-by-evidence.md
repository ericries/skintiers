This ranks the treatments on the site for [[rosacea-papulopustular|papulopustular rosacea]], the inflammatory-bump-and-pustule form of rosacea, by how well their own randomized trials hold up. All of them work by calming inflammation or reducing bacterial/Demodex load; none reverses the underlying vascular disease, so rosacea is managed in ongoing courses rather than cured.

The honest framing: azelaic acid and ivermectin are first-line by more than convention: each has beaten the older standard, metronidazole, in its own head-to-head randomized trial.[^elewski][^taieb] Metronidazole still works and is still FDA-approved, it is just the weaker of the three on direct comparison. Microencapsulated benzoyl peroxide (Epsolay) and sub-antimicrobial oral doxycycline (Oracea) are solid, trial-backed options generally reached for when a topical alone falls short or a systemic approach is wanted, though the doxycycline dose showed no proven edge over other active drugs in a 2021 meta-analysis.[^husein] The two newer minocycline products, Zilxi (topical foam) and Emrosi (oral extended-release), have real FDA trial data but a thinner track record and, for Zilxi, no head-to-head data against azelaic acid or ivermectin.
