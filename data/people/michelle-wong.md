Michelle Wong is a chemist and science communicator who publishes beauty and skincare science content as Lab Muffin Beauty Science.[^1]

## Credentials and education

Wong holds a Bachelor of Advanced Science with first-class honours and a university medal, majoring in chemistry, and a PhD in chemistry from the University of Sydney, with a doctoral project in medicinal, supramolecular, and synthetic organic chemistry centered on cyclic peptides.[^1][^2] She also holds a Diploma of Personal Care Formulation from the Institute of Personal Care Science.[^1] An interview with IFLScience describes a teaching fellowship she held at the University of Sydney during her doctoral studies, consistent with that PhD.[^4]

Wong has taught chemistry at high school and university level for more than two decades and spent five years as chemistry coordinator, later head of chemistry, at Matrix Education, where she helped develop teaching materials and train teachers.[^1][^4]

## Lab Muffin Beauty Science

Wong founded Lab Muffin Beauty Science in December 2011, after finding little easy-to-understand, science-based information online about beauty products.[^1] She describes the site's purpose as helping readers "figure out what beauty products are worth buying, and which ones aren't, using science."[^1] The publisher of her book describes the platform as debunking "beauty myths and the science behind the biggest skincare and haircare trends."[^3]

Her content explains the chemistry behind skincare and haircare ingredients and formulations and addresses misinformation in the category, including sunscreen: an interview with The Proof covers sunscreen safety questions such as claims that chemical UV filters are harmful, and an episode of The Eco Well's podcast covers misconceptions about skin absorption and the environmental impact of UV filters including zinc oxide and titanium dioxide.[^5][^6] The mechanics of UV filters and how sunscreen protection is measured are covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

Wong is a co-founder of BeautySciComm, which describes itself as founded "by those who care about the science behind beauty."[^7]

## The Science of Beauty

Wong is the author of "The Science of Beauty: Debunk the Myths and Discover What Goes into Your Beauty Routine," published by DK in 2024.[^3] The publisher's author bio states she "has collaborated with L'Oreal, P&G, and Johnson & Johnson as part of her science education work, and has been profiled by The New York Times, Vogue Business, and Harper's Bazaar."[^3]
