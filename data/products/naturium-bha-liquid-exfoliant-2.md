Naturium BHA Liquid Exfoliant 2% is a leave-on liquid exfoliant built on 2% salicylic acid, sold in a 120 ml bottle in a fragrance-free, alcohol-free and oil-free formula the manufacturer lists at pH 3.50 to 4.00.[^1]

> A watery, leave-on salicylic acid exfoliant for clogged pores and rough texture, priced at the budget end of its category. Salicylic acid is an over-the-counter acne active recognized by US drug regulation, and this is a plain 2% version at an acidic pH, though the American Academy of Dermatology recommends salicylic acid only conditionally and ranks benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids above it (see [[salicylic-acid]]). No independent trial tests this product; the brand's "clinically proven" pore and tone claims trace to one 8-week company study described only in marketing copy. The manufacturer lists it at $19 for 120 ml / 4.0 fl oz.[^1]

## Summary

BHA Liquid Exfoliant 2% is Naturium's leave-on salicylic acid toner: one drug-monograph active, [[salicylic-acid|salicylic acid]] at the product's stated 2%, in a short water-based formula with humectants, three citrus fruit extracts, and a preservative system.[^1] Its health-oriented job is the one salicylic acid is known for: as the oil-soluble hydroxy acid it penetrates the sebum-filled pore, shedding built-up dead skin and clearing the clogged follicle. That makes it relevant to blackheads and mild-to-moderate acne, where salicylic acid is a real but second-tier active: the 2024 American Academy of Dermatology guideline recommends it only conditionally and places benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids ahead of it. That category evidence, graded skeptically, is set out on [[salicylic-acid]].

The formula carries the active at the top over-the-counter acne strength (2%) and at a manufacturer-stated pH of 3.50 to 4.00; the list is fragrance-free, alcohol-free and oil-free.[^1] The brand's distinctive additions, "encapsulated" salicylic acid and "bioactive fruit acids" from three citrus extracts, are marketing positions without published product data and are weighed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

Its most direct comparator is [[paulas-choice-skin-perfecting-2-bha|Paula's Choice Skin Perfecting 2% BHA Liquid Exfoliant]], the same active at the same strength in the same leave-on format at a higher list price. Naturium's is listed at $19 for the 120 ml / 4.0 fl oz bottle on the brand's own site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-19).[^1]

## The Evidence

Salicylic acid is a well-established nonprescription acne active: it sits on the US over-the-counter acne monograph at 0.5% to 2%, landed level with the prescription retinoid tretinoin in the one head-to-head trial a Cochrane review could pool, and carries only a conditional recommendation from the American Academy of Dermatology, which ranks benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids above it; that evidence, with its limits, is on [[salicylic-acid]]. What is specific to this product is the delivery: 2% salicylic acid, the top monograph strength, in a leave-on vehicle the manufacturer states sits at pH 3.50 to 4.00.[^1]

Naturium's own clinical support for the product is a single 8-week study on pore-appearance and tone-evening endpoints, described only in marketing copy with no sample size, control arm or publication (see [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)); no published independent trial tests the formula itself.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its health grade rests on salicylic acid's general, guideline-recognized but second-tier acne evidence; the product delivers that active faithfully at 2% and a stated acidic pH, and its cosmetic pore and tone claims rest on a single company study described only in marketing copy.[^1]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer: Water, Butylene Glycol, Propanediol, Salicylic Acid, Sodium Polyacrylate, Citric Acid, Glycerin, Diglycerin, Citrus Glauca Fruit Extract, Microcitrus Australasica Fruit Extract, Microcitrus Australis Fruit Extract, Hydroxyacetophenone, Carrageenan, Phenoxyethanol, Sodium Phytate, Caprylyl Glycol, Phenylpropanol, Tocopherol.[^1]

Active:

- [[salicylic-acid|salicylic acid]], at the product's stated 2%, the oil-soluble beta hydroxy acid at the top over-the-counter acne strength[^1]

Other functional ingredients:

- three citrus fruit extracts (Citrus glauca, Microcitrus australasica, Microcitrus australis), the "bioactive fruit acids" of the marketing copy, with no declared concentrations[^1]
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant, last on the list

Base, texture and preservative system: water, butylene glycol and propanediol (solvents and humectants), glycerin and diglycerin (humectants), sodium polyacrylate and carrageenan (thickeners), citric acid (pH), sodium phytate (a chelator), and hydroxyacetophenone, phenoxyethanol, caprylyl glycol and phenylpropanol (the preservative system).[^1]

Two composition facts matter. The formula is acidic by design: the manufacturer states "Formula pH Level: 3.50-4.00."[^1] And it is fragrance-free, alcohol-free and oil-free, with no other exfoliating acids or actives competing with the salicylic acid; the fruit extracts appear after glycerin in the list.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Clinically proven to reduce the appearance of pores, smooth and even skin tone, revealing more radiant, brighter looking skin."** The manufacturer's footnote attributes this to "an 8-week clinical study on subjects, representation across all skin types & self-perceived sensitive skin," with no sample size, control arm, methodology or publication.[^1] A company study on cosmetic endpoints.
- **"Encapsulated salicylic acid for optimal stability."** A vehicle claim; the INCI lists plain salicylic acid, and no published data comparing the encapsulated form to a standard 2% formula is cited.[^1]
- **"Bioactive fruit acids."** Marketing shorthand for the three citrus fruit extracts, which carry no declared concentrations and no product-specific evidence.[^1]
- **"Dermatologist Tested," "Cruelty Free," "Paraben Free," "Vegan," "Gluten Free," "Fragrance Free."** Label badges; the testing claims carry no published methodology or results.[^1]
