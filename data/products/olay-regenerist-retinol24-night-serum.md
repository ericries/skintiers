Olay Regenerist Retinol24 Night Moisturizer is a leave-on retinoid treatment from [[olay]], sold in a 1.7 fl oz size for $39.99 direct from Olay and $29.99 at Target, combining retinol and retinyl propionate with niacinamide at undisclosed concentrations.[^olay][^target]

> Topical retinol has category-level, vehicle-controlled trial evidence for improving fine wrinkles and pigment, covered on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]], but no published trial tests this exact product, and Olay does not disclose a retinol percentage on its own product page.[^olay] Asked about it, Olay told Target shoppers that ingredient percentages are "proprietary information" it is "unable to provide."[^target] The formula pairs retinol with retinyl propionate, a weaker retinoid ester whose own 0.15% clinical trial found no significant benefit over placebo, [see the evidence](#the-evidence).[^olay] Like every over-the-counter retinol it remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin-0-025-cream|tretinoin]].

## Summary

Olay Regenerist Retinol24 Night Moisturizer is sold on Olay's own site as "Retinol24 Night Moisturizer," a 1.7 fl oz size priced at $39.99, and listed at Target as "Olay Regenerist Retinol 24 Night Face Cream Moisturizer" for $29.99, the same 1.7 oz size.[^olay][^target] Its headline actives are retinol and retinyl propionate, backed by niacinamide and a "Triple Collagen Peptide" complex, but none of the three carries a disclosed concentration. Asked about that, Olay told Target shoppers that "specific product formulas and the ingredient percentages are considered proprietary information which we're unable to provide."[^target]

The evidence that retinol, as an ingredient, improves aging skin is real: vehicle-controlled trials show measurable gains in fine wrinkles and pigment, and increases in collagen-building activity, [set out in full on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]](#the-evidence). None of that trial evidence was generated on this specific product, and because its concentration is not disclosed, there is no way to compare its dose to the concentrations those trials actually tested. The formula also carries retinyl propionate, a weaker ester precursor; the one clinical trial of a 0.15% retinyl propionate cream cited on [[retinyl-esters]] found it did not significantly outperform placebo on any photoaging measure, [detailed below](#the-evidence).

Niacinamide sits eighth on a 23-ingredient list, ahead of the peptide complex, but its own concentration is likewise undisclosed. Niacinamide has modest topical evidence for skin tone and barrier support, covered on [[niacinamide]]. Olay's own directions call for nightly application after cleansing, with SPF the following morning, and describe results building over 28 days of consistent use.[^olay]

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinol improves aging skin, and how it compares with prescription retinoids, is covered on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]: vehicle-controlled trials support gradual improvement in fine wrinkles and pigment and increased collagen synthesis, with the primary studies and the concentrations they tested set out on those pages. No published trial tests this exact product, and its own retinol percentage is not disclosed on either Olay's product page or Target's retailer listing.[^olay][^target]

> **Bottom line.** The retinol case for this product rests on the category-level evidence for the ingredient, not on a trial of the product itself, and because Olay does not disclose a retinol percentage, there is no way to know how its dose compares to the concentrations those category trials tested.[^olay][^target]

The formula lists retinyl propionate immediately after retinol on the ingredient panel.[^olay] Retinyl propionate is a retinyl ester, the mildest and least-potent tier of over-the-counter retinoids because it must first be converted to retinol before it does anything; the one cited clinical trial of a 0.15% retinyl propionate cream "did not demonstrate any statistically significant improvement over placebo in any of the evaluated histopathological or clinical symptoms of photoaging," set out on [[retinyl-esters]].

> **Bottom line.** The product combines a retinoid with category-level evidence for photoaging (retinol) with a weaker ester (retinyl propionate) whose own tested concentration showed no significant benefit over placebo; neither ingredient's concentration in this specific product is disclosed.[^olay]
