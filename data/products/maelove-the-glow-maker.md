Maelove The Glow Maker is a leave-on vitamin C serum built around 15% L-ascorbic acid with vitamin E and ferulic acid, sold at a fraction of the price of SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic, the formula it is widely described as an alternative to.[^1][^2]

> A daily antioxidant serum positioned as a budget match for SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic. It states 15% L-ascorbic acid, the same vitamin C concentration the pricier formula was clinically tested at, plus vitamin E and ferulic acid at amounts the manufacturer does not disclose. No clinical trial has tested this product itself, so the case for it rests on that partial formula match rather than product-specific data; the only product-specific check found is a short formulator-run stability test. Reviewers and cosmetic chemists nonetheless name it among the most popular C E Ferulic dupes. The antioxidant evidence behind the category sits on [[vitamin-c]], and the premium original it is matched against is [[skinceuticals-c-e-ferulic]].[^1][^2][^3][^4]

## Summary

The Glow Maker is a morning antioxidant serum aimed at the same job as SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic: sitting under sunscreen to help neutralize everyday UV and pollution damage, alongside a gradual brightening effect. The active ingredient doing that work is [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]], the pure and best-studied form of vitamin C, mapped along with the other forms on [[vitamin-c]]. What distinguishes this product is not the active but the price: it is sold as a lower-cost way into the same three-antioxidant combination, at roughly a sixth of what the reference formula costs ([Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)).

Maelove states its formula is "the same clinical formula luxury brands charge $185 for" and that "the industry 'gold standard' is Vitamin C, Vitamin E, and Ferulic Acid at a pH of 3.5 or lower," which "Glow Maker uses."[^1] The product page and an independent dupe-list teardown agree the serum is formulated at 15% L-ascorbic acid, the concentration [[skinceuticals-c-e-ferulic|SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic]] was clinically tested at.[^1][^2] Neither source states a percentage for the vitamin E or ferulic acid, so the exact match to the studied 1% vitamin E, 0.5% ferulic acid ratio cannot be confirmed independently; that comparison, and [the human and pig-skin studies behind it](#the-evidence), belong to the original formula, not to this product.[^1][^2]

On reputation, HuffPost describes it as "considered by many skin care enthusiasts to be an affordable alternative to SkinCeuticals' cult-fave serum."[^4] Melissa Jane Lee names it "one of the best-selling options" among C E Ferulic dupes and, after testing it, writes "Maelove is a contender for the top spot for sure."[^2] The cosmetic-chemist blog Chemist Confessions bought and put it through an accelerated stability test alongside two other budget vitamin C serums; [the result is reported in The Evidence](#the-evidence).[^3]

Maelove lists the 30 mL (1 fl oz) bottle at $32.95 (accessed 2026-07-30, see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)),[^1] and it sells at around $39 on Amazon.[^4]

## The Evidence

Topical L-ascorbic acid is a well-studied antioxidant with characterized roles in defending skin against oxidative UV damage and supporting collagen synthesis; the vitamin C forms and their evidence are mapped on [[vitamin-c]], and the reference form's profile is [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]]. The fixed-ratio combination of L-ascorbic acid, vitamin E, and ferulic acid this product is compared against was developed and clinically tested by the inventors of [[skinceuticals-c-e-ferulic|SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic]], whose page carries that evidence and its limits. No clinical trial of Maelove The Glow Maker itself has been published.

Chemist Confessions, a blog run by two cosmetic formulators, bought Glow Maker along with four other SkinCeuticals-style dupes and ran a three-week accelerated stability test, storing the serums in a heated oven and tracking weekly color change, gas buildup, odor, and packaging condition to, in their words, "see if there were any tips we could provide in sussing out and finding the 'better' dupe."[^3] Glow Maker started "pretty much clear," and the authors concluded "we would pass all three of these ascorbic acid serums," grouping it with two other drugstore-priced dupes on the basis of normal color progression, no packaging issues, and expected odor development over the three weeks.[^3]

> **Bottom line.** A three-week accelerated heat-stability test by two cosmetic formulators found Glow Maker aged normally alongside two other budget vitamin C dupes. This measures whether the formula holds up in the bottle, not whether it protects or brightens skin; it is not a clinical trial.[^3]

On composition, the manufacturer's own page and an independent dupe-list teardown agree on 15% L-ascorbic acid, the concentration used in the studies behind the reference formula, but neither discloses the vitamin E or ferulic acid percentage.[^1][^2] That leaves the vitamin C concentration as the one figure independently confirmed to match the studied reference formula; the supporting antioxidants are present but at undisclosed amounts.

## What's In It

The active antioxidants and base as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the solvent, humectant, and preservative system. [INCIDecoder](https://incidecoder.com/products/maelove-the-glow-maker) lists the same ingredients.

Antioxidant actives:

- [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]] 15%, pure vitamin C[^1][^2]
- [[ferulic-acid]], a plant antioxidant that stabilizes vitamin C and vitamin E in the reference formula; concentration not disclosed[^1][^2]
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E); concentration not disclosed[^1]
- [[hyaluronic-acid|sodium hyaluronate]], a humectant[^1]

Base, solvent, and preservative system (per the manufacturer's current declared INCI): water, ethoxydiglycol (a penetration solvent), aloe barbadensis leaf juice, glycerin, lecithin, citrus aurantium dulcis callus culture extract, magnolia officinalis bark extract, grape seed extract, xanthan gum, disodium EDTA, sodium metabisulfite, sodium hydroxide (pH adjuster), gluconolactone, calcium gluconate, phenoxyethanol, sodium benzoate, ethylhexylglycerin, and maltodextrin.[^1]

Label and marketing language not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"The industry 'gold standard' ... Glow Maker uses this exact formula."** A manufacturer characterization of its own product against the reference combination; see the composition discussion in [The Evidence](#the-evidence).[^1]
