## Summary

SkinCeuticals Retinol 0.5 Refining Night Cream is the lower-strength entry in [[skinceuticals|SkinCeuticals]]'s retinol night-cream line, sold in a 1 oz jar. Target's listing gives the title "SkinCeuticals Retinol 0.5 Refining Night Cream, 1 oz" and describes it as "a nighttime treatment that promotes cell turnover and reduces signs of aging," stating "With 0.5% pure retinol, it smooths fine lines and refines pores."[^target] Target separately labels it "Moderate strength retinol for resurfacing" and recommends it for "Mature" skin.[^target]

The evidence that retinol, as an ingredient, improves aging skin is real: vehicle-controlled trials show measurable, if gradual, gains in fine wrinkles and skin pigment, [set out in full on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]](#the-evidence). None of that trial evidence was generated on this specific cream. What sets it apart from a comparator like [[skinmedica-retinol-complex-1-0|SkinMedica Retinol Complex 1.0]] is that Target's listing states its concentration outright, "0.5% pure retinol," rather than only a tier name.[^target] Like all over-the-counter retinol, it remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin]], which delivers retinoic acid directly rather than requiring the skin to convert a precursor.

Target's listing does not publish a full declared ingredient (INCI) list for this cream, so [beyond the named 0.5% retinol, the rest of the formula cannot be independently confirmed from the source cited here](#whats-in-it).[^target] Target's own directions call for it to be used "only at night."[^target]

It sits at the premium end for a 1 oz face cream: Target lists it at $78.59, discounted from a regular price of $117.58 (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07), and the listing shows the item out of stock.[^target]

## The Evidence

No independently published trial of SkinCeuticals Retinol 0.5 Refining Night Cream itself is cited here. Its case rests on the general, vehicle-controlled trial evidence that topical retinol improves fine wrinkles and pigment over months, set out with its study designs and numbers on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]. Target's listing names the cream's 0.5% retinol concentration but does not disclose a full ingredient list, order, or the concentration of anything else in the formula, so this cream's specific composition beyond that headline figure cannot be weighed against the general evidence.[^target]

> **Bottom line.** The retinol case for this cream rests on the category-level evidence for the ingredient, not on a trial of the product itself. Target's listing is the only source confirming its 0.5% concentration, and it does not disclose the rest of the formula.[^target]

Against [[skinmedica-retinol-complex-1-0|SkinMedica Retinol Complex 1.0]], the comparator's manufacturer and retailer listings name only a "1.0" tier with no disclosed percentage, while this cream's Target listing states "0.5% pure retinol" directly, making its labeled concentration the more transparent of the two even though SkinMedica's tier name implies a higher dose.[^target]
