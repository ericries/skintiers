CeraVe PM Facial Moisturizing Lotion is a lightweight, fragrance-free nighttime facial moisturizer whose ingredient list includes niacinamide, three ceramides (ceramide NP, AP, EOP), and hyaluronic acid (as sodium hyaluronate), sold inexpensively in a pump bottle.[^1]

> A cheap, fragrance-free nighttime moisturizer for normal to dry skin. Keeping dry or barrier-impaired skin moisturized is one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines, and this lotion is a faithful example of that kind of formula, layering a niacinamide-ceramide-hyaluronic acid combination on top of the category case (see [[moisturizing]]). A dermatologist quoted by NewBeauty credits its ceramides for nourishing her own dry winter skin. No trial has tested this exact lotion; the ceramide-specific and niacinamide-specific angles rest on general ingredient evidence rather than a product-specific outcome.[^1][^4]

## Summary

CeraVe PM Facial Moisturizing Lotion is a thin, fragrance-free lotion marketed for nighttime use on normal to dry skin. Its main job is a health one: slowing the water skin loses through its surface and supporting a dry or damaged barrier. It does that with a familiar combination of humectants ([[hyaluronic-acid]] and glycerin), an occlusive ([[dimethicone]]), and three [[ceramides]], the fatty molecules that make up part of the skin's own barrier. Keeping dry or barrier-impaired skin moisturized this way is one of the best-evidenced routines in dermatology; that general case is set out on [[moisturizing]]. Unlike CeraVe's plainer [[cerave-moisturizing-cream|Moisturizing Cream]], this formula also adds [[niacinamide]], a form of vitamin B3 with its own modest evidence for barrier support.[^1]

What sets it apart from a plain lotion is the ceramide-and-niacinamide combination and the dermatologist backing, not a unique active. It is marketed as "Developed with dermatologists," and a Maryland dermatologist, Valerie Callender, MD, quoted by NewBeauty, said of the product: "My skin is extremely dry during the winter months, and the ceramides help to nourish my skin and protect it from the cold weather."[^1][^4]

The thinner part of the case is the ceramide- and niacinamide-specific angles. No published trial tests this exact lotion. The closest controlled test of a ceramide-dominant cream ([studied on the ceramides page](#the-evidence)) improved a barrier measurement but did not reduce eczema severity more than a plain vehicle, and the niacinamide barrier evidence comes from a single manufacturer research group (this product does not disclose its niacinamide percentage). Those product-specific limits are in [The Evidence](#the-evidence), and the general case for each active is on [[ceramides]], [[niacinamide]], and [[hyaluronic-acid]]. It is inexpensive: the manufacturer's page lists it at $19.99, and Target lists the 3 fl oz size at $14.99 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-04).

## The Evidence

Keeping dry or barrier-impaired skin moisturized reduces flares and supports the skin barrier, one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines; that general case, including the roles of occlusives, humectants, and barrier lipids like [[ceramides]], is set out on [[moisturizing]]. No published trial tests this exact lotion. Its case rests on the general evidence for each of its three notable actives, applied to this formulation.

For the ceramides, the closest controlled test is a manufacturer-sponsored randomized trial of a *different* brand's ceramide-dominant cream, which improved a barrier measure (transepidermal water loss) but did not reduce eczema severity more than a plain vehicle; that trial, its industry funding, and its limits are set out on [[ceramides]].

For the niacinamide, the barrier evidence (raised stratum-corneum lipids and lower water loss in dry skin) comes from a single manufacturer research group; that work and its limits are on [[niacinamide]]. This product does not disclose its niacinamide percentage.

For the hyaluronic acid, a placebo-controlled trial found topical hyaluronic acid raised skin-hydration readings across the molecular weights tested; that trial and the penetration data behind it are set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. This product's ingredient list does not specify which molecular weight it uses.

> **Bottom line.** No study has isolated this lotion's own effect. Its case rests on general evidence for niacinamide, ceramides, and hyaluronic acid individually, plus a faithful, dermatologist-developed formulation of all three together.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

Barrier and hydration ingredients:

- [[niacinamide]], a form of vitamin B3
- three [[ceramides]] (ceramide NP, AP, EOP), plus cholesterol and [[phytosphingosine]], the formula's barrier lipids
- sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]) and [[glycerin]], humectants
- [[dimethicone]], an occlusive that slows water loss

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, caprylic/capric triglyceride (emollient), cetearyl alcohol (emulsifier), potassium phosphate and dipotassium phosphate (pH buffers), carbomer and xanthan gum (thickeners), ceteareth-20, behentrimonium methosulfate and sodium lauroyl lactylate (emulsifiers), polyglyceryl-3 diisostearate (emulsifier), phenoxyethanol and caprylyl glycol (preservatives), disodium EDTA (chelator), and ethylhexylglycerin.[^1]

The formula contains no petrolatum (unlike CeraVe's thicker [[cerave-moisturizing-cream|Moisturizing Cream]]), no added fragrance, and no drying alcohol (the cetearyl alcohol is an emollient fatty alcohol, not the drying kind).[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"MVE Technology" for controlled overnight release.**[^1][^2] A delivery-system claim; no independent trial reports its clinical effect for this lotion.

- **Niacinamide "may help calm skin."**[^1] Niacinamide's better-evidenced topical use is barrier support, covered on [[niacinamide]]; this product's own concentration is not disclosed.
