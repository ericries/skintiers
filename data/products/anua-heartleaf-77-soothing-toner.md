Anua Heartleaf 77% Soothing Toner is a Korean toner built on a brand-labeled 77% Houttuynia Cordata (heartleaf) water base, carrying panthenol, centella asiatica, and several hyaluronic-acid forms, sold in 250 ml and 500 ml sizes for $23.00 on the brand's US site (size-specific price not disambiguated on the page).[^anua]

> The brand markets the toner for redness-calming and an immediate cooling sensation on contact; the only cited support for those claims is the manufacturer's own device-measured figures, not an independent trial, and [[houttuynia-cordata|heartleaf]] itself has no established controlled-trial case here. Supporting ingredients further down the list, panthenol and hyaluronic acid, carry their own general evidence for barrier recovery and hydration, but neither is declared at a stated concentration in this formula.[^anua]

## Summary

Anua Heartleaf 77% Soothing Toner is a fragrance-free Korean toner positioned around [[houttuynia-cordata|heartleaf]] (Houttuynia cordata), a plant water the brand labels at 77% and lists first on the ingredient list, ahead of plain water.[^anua] The brand markets it for "Low-Irritation Daily Redness Care" and an "Instant Cooling Temperature Drop," describing a "skin cooling effect (temperature decrease) on contact."[^anua] Heartleaf's evidence for that soothing effect rests here on the brand's own testing rather than an independent or published trial (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)).

What distinguishes the formula from a plain water-based toner is less any single dosed active and more the combination: the heartleaf base is followed on the ingredient list by panthenol, centella asiatica extract, several other botanical extracts (burdock root, Phellinus linteus, purslane, chamomile, chaste tree), and a cluster of seven hyaluronic-acid forms toward the end of the list.[^anua] None of these supporting ingredients carries a declared concentration. Panthenol has controlled-trial evidence for accelerating barrier recovery and reducing irritation at tested doses, covered on [[panthenol]], and topical hyaluronic acid reliably raises skin-hydration readings, covered on [[hyaluronic-acid]]; both are relevant to general [[moisturizing|moisturizing]] and barrier support, but neither ingredient is dosed or tested in this specific formula.

The manufacturer's own product page reports that skin temperature dropped 14.52% after one use, sebum decreased 49.55% after two weeks, and hydration rose 97.52% after one use, alongside a claim of primary irritation testing.[^anua] These are manufacturer-run figures with no disclosed sample size, design, or independent publication, and are quarantined under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims) rather than treated as established evidence.

It is priced at $23.00 on the brand's US site (size-specific price not disambiguated on the page), which lists 250 ml and 500 ml sizes, as of 2026-08-08.[^anua]

## The Evidence

No published, independently reported trial of this toner, or of Houttuynia cordata as a topical soothing agent generally, is cited here. The product's marketed cooling and redness-calming claims rest on the manufacturer's own device-measured figures (temperature and sebum readings) reported on its product page, without a disclosed sample size, comparator arm, or independent publication.[^anua] The supporting ingredients further down the list, panthenol and hyaluronic acid, each have their own general controlled-trial evidence for barrier recovery, irritation reduction, and hydration, set out on [[panthenol]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]], but this toner does not declare a concentration for either, so that ingredient-level evidence cannot be read across to a specific expected effect from this formula.

> **Bottom line.** The toner's marketed cooling and calming effect is supported only by the brand's own unpublished figures. Its supporting humectant and barrier ingredients, panthenol and hyaluronic acid, are individually well-evidenced for hydration and barrier recovery, but this formula does not state how much of either it contains.[^anua]
