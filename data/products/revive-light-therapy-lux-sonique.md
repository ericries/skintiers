A $199 sonic facial cleansing brush with LED built in, from reVive Light Therapy, SKU LXSONIQUE; the $129 Lux Soniqué Mini shares its three settings and its claims.[^brand] **Bottom line:** the cleansing half is a convenience, the LED half carries the treatment claims, and the device on sale cannot be matched to a file in its maker's own FDA record by name. The category evidence is on [[home-led-light-therapy]] and the maker on [[revive-light-therapy]].

> **The light is specified; the device is not findable.** The brand gives a primary wavelength range of 415 to 880 nm and names each setting: Setting 1 is "Red · Amber · Infrared" for anti-aging, Setting 2 is "Blue · Red" for anti-acne, Setting 3 combines all four.[^brand] That is more disclosure than most home LED devices offer. But LED Technologies holds 15 FDA clearances and not one of them is named Lux or Soniqué, so a buyer reading the public record cannot tie this product to the file behind it.[^openfda]

## Summary

What the maker does hold is closer than the naming suggests. Two of its clearances are titled "reVive Light Therapy LED Cleansing System": K180447 from June 2018 under product code OLP, which FDA defines as "Over-The-Counter Powered Light Based Laser For Acne", and K221430 from July 2022 under code OHS, "Light Based Over The Counter Wrinkle Reduction".[^openfda] Those two codes map onto exactly the two treatment claims this device makes. Whether either filing is this product is not something the public record answers, and this page does not assume it.

Note which half of the device the clearances cover. Both codes are about light. Nothing in them concerns sonic cleansing, and nothing needs to, because cleansing is not a treatment claim. The "3-in-1" framing bundles one regulated function with two unregulated ones.

The two percentages on the page deserve the same treatment they got on this maker's other devices. The brand publishes "97.4% saw visible improvement in fine lines, wrinkles, or skin tone" and "100% saw visible improvement in pores, skin tone, texture, or overall skin condition", with an asterisk resolving to "Consumer study of participants ages 39-66."[^brand] No sample size, duration, comparison arm or publication is given, and the identical pair of figures appears beside [[revive-light-therapy-lux-glo]], a different device at $79. Numbers that travel between products are claims about a brand, not measurements of a device.

## The Rubric

**Anti-Acne setting (blue plus red)**
Graded modest on preliminary evidence, inherited from the modality rather than earned here. Blue combined with red is the best-supported home LED approach for acne on [[home-led-light-therapy]], and this device's Setting 2 is that combination. No clinical study of this device appears in the public record.

**Anti-Aging setting (red, amber, infrared)**
Graded minimal on preliminary evidence. Home LED for wrinkles rests on a thinner base than home LED for acne, which is argued on the category page rather than re-argued here.

**Sonic cleansing**
Graded unknown. Nothing read for this page tests it, and no clearance covers it.

**The brand's two percentages**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. See the quarantined section below.

## Common Marketing Claims

Quarantined: these are reVive's own promotional lines, not findings, and nothing above rests on them.

"This 3-in-1 at-home device pairs sonic facial cleansing with clinically tested, multi-wavelength LED light therapy."[^brand] Clinically tested names no test. The wavelengths are disclosed, which is to the brand's credit; the testing is not.

"97.4% saw visible improvement in fine lines, wrinkles, or skin tone" and "100% saw visible improvement in pores, skin tone, texture, or overall skin condition".[^brand] A consumer study in people aged 39 to 66, otherwise undescribed, and the same two numbers the brand puts on a different device.

"See results in as little as 2 weeks!"[^brand] A timeline with nothing behind it in the record.
