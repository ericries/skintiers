A $16 gentle cleanser whose surfactant system is genuinely well chosen and whose marketing rests on a 22-person study nobody can read. **Bottom line:** buy it as a mild wash, not for the matcha or the 48-hour hydration claim.

> **A cleanser's job is mostly to not do harm, and this one is built for that.** The cleansing agents are coco-betaine, coco-glucoside, decyl glucoside and sodium cocoyl isethionate, a mild amphoteric and glucoside blend, with no SLS or SLES anywhere in 34 ingredients. That is the part worth paying for. Everything the name promises sits on top of it and gets rinsed off.

## Summary

Price is $16.00 for one 120 mL tube, or $27.00 for a two-pack, from the brand's own storefront.[^brand] The declared ingredient list runs to 34 items, led by Camellia Sinensis Leaf Water, Water, Glycerin and Coco-Betaine, with Camellia Sinensis Leaf Powder at position 14, Cannabis Sativa (Hemp) Seed Oil at 17, and Panthenol, Avena Sativa (Oat) Kernel Extract and Sodium PCA further down, as listed by incidecoder, whose page for this product was read on 2026-10-26. incidecoder aggregates label text rather than publishing it, so it is credited for the list and the photograph and is not evidence for any claim.

**Green tea water is listed before water.** Camellia Sinensis Leaf Water heads the list, ahead of Water itself. That is a legitimate way to build a formula and it is also the oldest trick in natural-beauty labelling, because a leaf water is mostly water. It tells you the brand wanted matcha at the top of the list more than it tells you how much matcha is in the tube.

## The Rubric

**Washing the face without stripping it**
Graded notable on anecdotal evidence, and the grade comes from the formula rather than from a study. Coco-betaine, coco-glucoside, decyl glucoside and sodium cocoyl isethionate are mild cleansing agents, and the absence of SLS and SLES from a 34-item list is a deliberate choice. Glycerin in third position and panthenol further down are sensible additions to a wash. No study cited here measures stripping, so this is a formulation judgement stated as one.

**48 hours of increased skin moisture after a single use**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. The brand's claim is specific: "Clinically Proven: 48 hours of increased skin moisture capacity after one use. Based on an independent clinical study of 22 subjects".[^brand] Three things are missing. The study is not named, published or linked, so its design cannot be checked. No comparator is stated, so there is no way to know what the increase was measured against. And the product is rinsed off within a minute, which makes sustained 48-hour hydration a strong result for an unpublished study of 22 people to carry. "Clinically Proven" is a marketing phrase, not a regulatory status.

**Strengthening the skin barrier**
Graded minimal on anecdotal evidence. The brand pairs matcha "with Hemp Seed Oil to deeply moisturize and strengthen the skin barrier".[^brand] Hemp seed oil is at position 17 of 34 in a product designed to be washed away. Hemp seed oil is linoleic-rich, which on the ratio argument set out at [[olive-oil]] puts it on the better side of the plant-oil question, and that argument has not been tested on this cleanser or on any cleanser.

**Antioxidant protection from matcha**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. Green tea polyphenols have a genuine antioxidant literature. None of it concerns a rinse-off cleanser, where contact time is the few seconds before the water hits.

## The Evidence

**Contact time is the whole argument about cleansers.** Anything in a wash has seconds on the skin, so the actives that matter in a leave-on product mostly cannot act here. So the surfactants earn a high grade above and the matcha, hemp and barrier claims do not. The objection is not to the ingredients, it is to their position in a product that leaves the skin in seconds. The brand's own strongest line is the one that respects this, that the product helps by "leaving the natural lipids and oils your skin needs" rather than by adding anything.[^brand]

**The one claim with a number behind it is the one you cannot check.** An independent study of 22 subjects is the entire evidence base the brand offers, and nothing about it is published.[^brand] A cleanser that does not dry the skin out is a real and useful product. A cleanser clinically proven to raise skin moisture for two days after one wash would be a different and more surprising product, and the evidence offered does not distinguish between the two.

**Who it suits.** The brand says it is for "All skin types but especially dry, sensitive skin".[^brand] That matches the formula: a mild, sulfate-free wash with no fragrance or parfum in its declared list is a reasonable pick for reactive or dry skin, and [[dry-skin]] and [[skin-barrier-repair]] cover what else those cases need. If you are cleansing twice daily for oily or congested skin, [[oily-skin]] is the better starting point, and nothing here is a treatment active.

## Common Marketing Claims

**"Clinically Proven: 48 hours of increased skin moisture capacity after one use."** Assessed in the rubric above. The qualifier the brand itself prints, "Based on an independent clinical study of 22 subjects", is the part to read.[^brand]

**"Matcha, rich in free radical-fighting antioxidants, paired with Hemp Seed Oil to deeply moisturize and strengthen the skin barrier."** Accurate about what the ingredients are and silent about contact time in a product that is rinsed off.[^brand]

**"Gently cleanses without leaving skin stripped."** The best-supported claim on the page, and the surfactant list backs it.[^brand] Note the brand also describes the same cleanser as one that "Deeply cleanses the skin without leaving a dry, tight after-feel" in its duo listings, so both gentle and deep appear in its own copy.[^brand]
