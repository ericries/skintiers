Prequel Gleanser (Glycerin Cleanser) is a fragrance-free, sulfate-free rinse-off cleanser for face and body built around 50 percent glycerin, made by Prequel, a skincare brand whose product page identifies its founder, Dr. Samantha Ellis, as a dermatologist.[^pdp]

> A sulfate-free, fragrance-free glycerin cleanser for face and body, one of several Prequel products carrying the National Rosacea Society's Seal of Acceptance, a designation independently confirmed on the Society's own brand listing rather than resting on Prequel's word alone.[^nrs-brand] The Seal means an independent panel of dermatologists judged the formula free of ingredients tied to damaged barrier function, flushing, and burning or stinging in rosacea-prone skin; the Society states plainly that the Seal "does not guarantee that the product will improve an individual's rosacea" and "does not represent an endorsement" by the NRS.[^nrs-info] Prequel's own site separately cites brand-run figures for redness and flushing reduction and a 24-hour hydration claim; those rest on unpublished, brand-commissioned testing with no independent source confirming them, so [they are reported here as marketing claims, not established findings](#common-marketing-claims).[^pdp][^blog] It is inexpensive for the category: the manufacturer lists it at $18 and Target at $17.99 for the same 13.5 fl oz size (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-05).[^pdp][^target]

## Summary

Prequel Gleanser is a rinse-off wash for face and body built around 50 percent glycerin, the single largest ingredient in the formula, alongside inulin, arginine (as dihydroxypropyl arginine HCl), oat extract (avena sativa kernel flour), and aloe.[^pdp] It is fragrance-free, sulfate-free, and formulated to a mildly acidic pH of about 4.5.[^pdp] Prequel markets it as a "non-stripping" cleanser suitable for sensitive, eczema-prone, and rosacea-prone skin.[^pdp]

The cleanser's most distinguishing, independently checkable feature is the National Rosacea Society (NRS) Seal of Acceptance. Prequel's product page lists the Seal among the cleanser's certifications, and the NRS's own site independently lists "Gleanser Non-Drying Glycerin Cleanser" among the Prequel products carrying it, describing it as "a non-stripping glycerin cleanser for face and body formulated with 50% glycerin that cleanses while maintaining skin's natural moisture barrier and pH."[^pdp][^nrs-brand] The NRS also spells out what the Seal does and does not mean: products are reviewed by "an independent panel of dermatologists" and must be free of ingredients tied to damaged skin-barrier function, vasomotor instability (flushing), and unwanted neurosensory stimulation (burning, itching), but the Seal "does not guarantee that the product will improve an individual's rosacea," is not an NRS "endorsement," and does not substitute for a clinician's guidance.[^nrs-info] In plain terms, the Seal is evidence this formula was screened for ingredients that commonly aggravate rosacea-prone skin, not evidence that washing with it treats rosacea; the broader picture of [[rosacea]] and of keeping reactive skin moisturized is covered on [[moisturizing]].

The formula's lead ingredient, glycerin, is a well-evidenced humectant for raising skin's water content and supporting barrier function, covered in depth on [[glycerin]]. That evidence base comes from leave-on formulations; whether a rinse-off wash deposits enough of its glycerin to matter on skin after rinsing is not something [a product-specific trial has measured](#the-evidence).

Prequel's own site also cites brand-commissioned figures for hydration and for reductions in redness, flushing, and acne; because they rest on unpublished, internal testing with no outside corroboration, they are catalogued as [marketing claims](#common-marketing-claims) rather than folded into the grades above ([the evidence](#the-evidence) sets out the details and an inconsistency between Prequel's own pages).[^blog]

The product is inexpensive for the category. The manufacturer lists a 13.5 fl oz pump bottle at $18, and Target lists the same size at $17.99 (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-05).[^pdp][^target]

## The Evidence

Glycerin is a well-evidenced humectant: a systematic review and a placebo-controlled human trial both found it raises stratum corneum water content and supports barrier repair, evidence set out in full on [[glycerin]]. That evidence base is built on leave-on moisturizers rather than rinse-off washes. Whether this cleanser's rinse-off glycerin meaningfully hydrates or protects rosacea-prone skin is not something a product-specific trial has tested; the closest thing to product-specific data is the brand-commissioned testing described below.

Prequel's own site reports two internal tests. The first is a hydration study: 33 participants were tested at what the brand describes as "an independent testing facility," measuring forearm moisture by corneometer at baseline, immediately after application, and 24 hours later, with the site reporting that "91% of subjects showed immediate improvement in skin hydration (p <0.001) and 94% of subjects showed hydration after 24 hours post-application."[^blog] The second is described as a rosacea study: after "1 week twice daily use," participants with diagnosed rosacea were reported to show "26% reduction in redness, 27% reduction in flushing severity, and 38% reduction in facial acne (p<0.01)."[^blog] Prequel's product page cites only the flushing figure, and attributes it to "a 5-week clinical study on 30 subjects with dermatologist-diagnosed rosacea," a different duration than the "1 week" the blog post describes for the same result.[^pdp] Neither test names an outside investigator, an independent testing facility, or a publication, and no second source corroborates either set of figures.

> Bottom line: the brand's own hydration and rosacea-symptom figures are internally generated and inconsistently described between Prequel's own pages, with no independent corroboration located. The separately verified fact is the NRS Seal of Acceptance itself, confirmed on the Society's own site.[^nrs-brand]

## What's In It

The ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^pdp] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages where one exists; the rest are cleansing agents, texture, and preservative system.

Humectant and soothing ingredients:

- [[glycerin]], 50 percent of the formula and its predominant ingredient, a humectant
- Inulin, a chicory-root-derived polysaccharide the manufacturer describes as forming a film on skin after rinsing
- Dihydroxypropyl arginine HCl, an arginine derivative marketed as a moisture-binding, cationic ingredient
- Aloe barbadensis leaf juice (aloe vera) and avena sativa kernel flour (oat), both soothing agents
- Glyceryl glucoside, a humectant

Cleansing, texture, and preservative system: water, cocamidopropyl betaine and disodium cocoamphodiacetate (mild amphoteric surfactants), polyquaternium-10 (conditioning polymer), hexylene glycol and pentylene glycol (solvents/humectants), citric acid, disodium phosphate, sodium phosphate, and sodium hydroxide (pH adjusters and buffers), ethylhexylglycerin and phenoxyethanol (preservative system), hydroxyethylcellulose and xanthan gum (thickeners), polysorbate 60 (emulsifier), tetrasodium glutamate diacetate (chelator), and lecithin (emollient).[^pdp]

Not in the formula: no added fragrance and no sulfate detergents such as sodium lauryl or laureth sulfate; Prequel states the formula is sulfate-free and fragrance-free.[^pdp]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Clinically tested 24 hour moisture" / "clinically proven to provide immediate moisturization that lasts for up to 24 hours."**[^pdp] This rests on a brand-commissioned corneometer test of 33 people at an unnamed "independent testing facility," reported only on Prequel's own blog, with figures of 91% showing immediate improved hydration (p<0.001) and 94% showing hydration at 24 hours.[^blog] No independent source or published methodology was located.
- **"27% reduction in flushing severity"** (product page) and, on the brand's blog, **"26% reduction in redness, 27% reduction in flushing severity, and 38% reduction in facial acne (p<0.01)"** in people with diagnosed rosacea after "1 week twice daily use."[^blog] The product page instead attributes the flushing figure to "a 5-week clinical study on 30 subjects," a different duration than the blog's own description of the same result.[^pdp] Both accounts are brand-generated, appear only on Prequel's own site, and are not corroborated by an outside source; the NRS Seal the product carries explicitly "does not guarantee that the product will improve an individual's rosacea."[^nrs-info]
- **"A unique aquaporin-stimulating active."**[^pdp] The product page does not name which ingredient this refers to or cite supporting data; inulin is described elsewhere on the same page only as a rinse-resistant film-former, not by an aquaporin mechanism.[^pdp]
- **Non-comedogenic, hypoallergenic, allergy-tested, "no eye irritation," "dermatologist-developed," "dermatologist-tested."**[^pdp] Label and brand positioning claims, not independently tested outcomes.
