The first FDA clearance in the dpl line, and it is not a skincare device. **Bottom line:** in 2008 dpl was an infrared lamp cleared for muscle aches and arthritis under the Physical Medicine panel, with no wrinkle or acne claim anywhere in its file. The brand page is [[dpl]]; what the later skin devices claim is on [[dpl-nuve]], [[dpl-ii-panel]] and [[dpl-faceware]].

> **Read the cleared indication and the skincare brand disappears.** In full: "Intended for the relaxation of muscles and relief of muscle spasms; temporary relief of minor muscle and joint aches, pains, and stiffness; temporary relief of minor pain and stiffness associated with arthritis; and to temporarily increase local blood circulation."[^k081570] That is the standard heating-lamp indication. Checked by string search of the whole Summary, it contains no occurrence of wrinkle, acne, collagen, wavelength or nm.[^k081570]

## Summary

Cleared 2008-11-07 as K081570, applicant recorded by openFDA as Led Technologies, LLC, product code **ILY**, which FDA classifies as "Lamp, Infrared, Therapeutic Heating" under 21 CFR 890.5500, reviewed by the Physical Medicine panel rather than the General and Plastic Surgery panel every later dpl filing went to.[^openfda][^ily] Over-the-counter use is the box ticked.[^k081570]

**What the device was.** "The DPLTM Therapy System is a compact infrared lamp for therapeutic heating. It delivers infrared light to the skin resulting in a safe elevation of the skin temperature for therapeutic effects."[^k081570] Two detachable panels on a desk stand, usable singly or as a pair, with "an automatic 17-minute timed cycle which may be repeated by activating the ON cycle".[^k081570] The file gives the warm-up plainly: "Device takes 8 minutes to reach target temperature on skin. Total time for treatment is 17 minutes."[^k081570]

**Equivalence rests on one outside device.** "The DPLTM Therapy System is substantially equivalent to the Dermillume Red HR 1000 device, 510(k) K051681."[^k081570] Unlike the later dpl filings, which chain to the firm's own earlier products, this one points at another company's lamp.

**No price is quoted here.** This is a 2008 clearance and no current listing was fetched.

## The Rubric

**Relaxing muscles and relieving minor aches, the cleared indication (health)**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence, and the honest reason is scope rather than scepticism: this is a physical-medicine heating claim and this site does not grade pain outcomes. What the file offers is a temperature measurement, not a pain result, which the next section sets out.

**Anything for skin**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence, because there is nothing to grade. The words wrinkle, acne and collagen appear nowhere in the document.[^k081570] A device that heats skin is not thereby a treatment for skin, and the dpl products that do make skin claims are separate clearances under different product codes.

## The Evidence

**This is the only dpl filing covered here that mentions clinical studies, and the endpoint is temperature.** Its Test Data section reads: "Clinical studies demonstrate that therapeutic heat is attained for male and female subjects. Safe temperatures were maintained for the duration of the treatment time. No adverse effects at complications were encountered."[^k081570] That measures whether the lamp warms skin safely. It is not a measurement of pain, stiffness or circulation, and no sample size, duration or result figure appears anywhere.[^k081570] Compare the later panels at [[dpl-ii-panel]] and [[dpl-iia-panel]], whose only human testing is a usability study.

**No dose is published, as usual for this firm.** Checked by string search, the Summary contains no occurrence of mW/cm, J/cm, irradiance, wavelength or nm.[^k081570] Infrared is named as the output and never quantified. The disclosure ranking is on [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]].

**The entity and the address both moved.** This filing is submitted by LED Technologies, LLC of Elizabeth, Colorado, with a contact at a consultancy in Boulder; openFDA records the applicant city as Boulder, which is the consultant's address rather than the submitter's.[^k081570][^openfda] Later filings are by Led Technologies, Inc. of Greenwood Village. Worth knowing if you try to trace the firm's history through the database.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. An infrared heating lamp cleared for aches and arthritis is a physical-medicine product; whether heat helps a given pain problem is outside what this site covers. Skin claims for this brand belong to its other clearances, not this one.

## Common Marketing Claims

**No brand marketing copy is quoted here.** This is a 2008 clearance and no current product page for it was found, so the only claims assessed are the regulated ones.

**The lineage is the thing worth carrying forward.** A brand now sold for wrinkles and acne began with a clearance for muscle spasms and arthritis, under a different FDA panel and against another company's heating lamp.[^k081570][^openfda] That is not improper, and new indications require new clearances, which the firm obtained. It is simply a useful corrective to the idea that a long FDA history means a long history of skin evidence.
