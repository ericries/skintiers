This is the device at the root of the Neutrogena acne-mask lineage, and reading its file overturns what the rest of that lineage suggested. **Bottom line:** the chain does NOT bottom out in an absence of clinical data. It bottoms out here, in a real 12-week IRB-approved study with large numbers and a design that cannot support them.[^k123999] Category evidence is on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

> **Three generations, one study, and it is at the bottom.** [[neutrogena-light-therapy-acne-mask-plus]] rests on [[pulsaderm-acne-mask-28]], which names this device as its predicate. Neither of those later files contains a clinical study. This one has a section headed "9. Clinical Summary" describing an "IRB (Institutional Review Board) approved 12-week clinical study".[^k123999] So the clinical evidence in this lineage was generated once, in 2013, by the smallest company in it, and the two better-known masks inherited the clearance without repeating it.

## Summary

Cleared 2013-08-28 as K123999 to La Lumiere, LLC, filed as a 510(k) Summary, product code OLP.[^k123999] The indication: the mask "is intended to emit energy in the red and blue region of the spectrum, specifically indicated to treat mild to moderate acne on the face."[^k123999] It emits "A combination of red light (630nm) and blue light (445nm)".[^k123999] Its predicate is the "Omnilux Clear-U Acne Treatment System" from PhotoTherapeutics Ltd.[^k123999] No price is quoted; no brand page was fetched and no metered lookup was spent.

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate facial acne**
- **Effect size: modest, and smaller than the headline.** The investigator's in-person counts at 8 weeks were "a 82.8% decrease in the number of inflammatory acne lesions, 71.2% decrease in the number of non-inflammatory acne lesions, and a 50% improvement in acne severity in accordance with the FDA Global Acne Severity Scale, which corresponds to a median movement of 2 points".[^k123999] But the blinded assessment of the same patients, by "two independent doctors" grading photographs "in a blinded and randomized manner", returned "a reduction of total acne lesions by 36%" at week 4 and "a reduction by 65.8%" at week 8.[^k123999] When the unblinded and blinded figures for the same trial differ that much, the blinded one is the one to carry.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary, and the best in its lineage.** This is a real study with a real IRB, a defined population and blinded photographic grading, which is more than either successor device offers. It is still not a controlled trial. See below.

**What the design can and cannot show**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a methods point.** The comparator was the same people earlier in time: "The same set of patients were followed for total of 12 weeks with the first 4 weeks being the Washout Period and the last 9 weeks being the Treatment Period."[^k123999] No sham mask, no second arm, no randomisation of order.
- **Evidence quality: the washout helps but does not fix it.** During washout, with cleanser only, lesion counts rose slightly: "The Washout Period showed a 3.13% increase in total acne lesion count", which the file calls "not clinically significant".[^k123999] That is useful, because it shows the group was not already improving on its own. It still cannot separate the device from eight further weeks of attention, routine and seasonal change, and no p-value or confidence interval appears anywhere in the file.

**Who it was tested in, which is better than usual**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a generalisability point.** "Inclusion criteria for the study stated that subjects be aged 14-40 and have 10 or more acne lesions with mild to moderate acne. There were no exclusion criteria for skin type - all skin types as classified in the Fitzpatrick Classification Scale were included in the study."[^k123999]
- **Evidence quality: solid for that claim.** Including all Fitzpatrick types is worth noting, because several device pages here cover products whose cleared indications or tested populations exclude deeper skin tones, such as [[home-fractional-nonablative-laser]] and [[newa-skin-therapy-system]].

## The Evidence

**What the non-clinical section covers, for completeness.** Conformity with "IEC 60601-1 3rd Edition electrical safety testing", EMC testing and "photobiological safety testing", plus usability and self-selection studies showing "that the intended users of the device could successfully follow the instructions and use the device as intended".[^k123999] A self-selection study is the same special-control idea seen on [[newa-skin-therapy-system]] and the one [[foreo-faq-led-masks]] skipped.

**Safety as reported.** 27 of 30 subjects completed each period, with dropouts attributed to "lack of patient follow-through for reasons outside the study" and the file stating that "no subjects were dropped because of adverse reactions or dislike of the product", and "no serious adverse events reported".[^k123999]

**One internal inconsistency, reported as printed.** The file describes "the last 9 weeks being the Treatment Period" in one sentence and "the 8 week Treatment Period" in another, which cannot both be right in a 12-week study with a 4-week washout.[^k123999] This page uses 8 weeks where results are reported, because that is the figure attached to the numbers.

**Why this changes the reading of the other two masks.** The lineage finding is not that nobody studied these devices. It is that one small company did, once, and the clearance then travelled to Pulsaderm and to Neutrogena without further clinical work. A reader choosing the Neutrogena mask is relying, whether they know it or not, on an uncontrolled 2013 study of a different company's device. That is how 510(k) substantial equivalence works, and [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] explains the mechanism.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The cleared indication stops at mild to moderate facial acne; nodular, cystic or scarring acne is a reason to see a dermatologist.

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand copy is quoted here, because no brand page was fetched for a 2013 device and no metered call was spent looking. The claims examined are the ones La Lumiere made to the FDA, and in one respect they are unusually well supported for this category: the company ran a study rather than relying on bench testing alone. The criticism is of the study's design, not of its existence.
