CeraVe Acne Control Gel is a leave-on facial acne treatment labeled with 2% salicylic acid, sold on CeraVe's own site for $20.99.[^1] Its FDA-regulated Drug Facts panel lists salicylic acid 2% as the sole active ingredient, with glycolic acid, lactic acid, niacinamide, and three ceramides (NP, AP, EOP) among the inactive ingredients.[^2]

> A leave-on gel built on the same acne-active concentration, 2% salicylic acid, that sits at the top of the OTC acne monograph range, paired with CeraVe's usual ceramide-and-niacinamide base and undisclosed amounts of two AHAs. Salicylic acid has real but second-tier evidence for acne, [set out on [[salicylic-acid]]](#the-evidence), and a leave-on gel gives it longer skin contact than a rinse-off cleanser.[^1][^2]

## Summary

CeraVe markets Acne Control Gel as a "hydrating, daily acne treatment with 2% salicylic acid that clears acne and helps prevent new breakouts from forming," also describing it as offering "gentle exfoliation with alpha- and beta-hydroxy acids (AHA and BHA)" alongside "calming niacinamide and barrier-strengthening ceramides."[^1] The product's Drug Facts label, filed with the FDA, confirms salicylic acid 2% as the single active ingredient, with the stated purpose "acne treatment" and uses "clears acne blemishes and allows skin to heal" and "helps prevent new acne blemishes from forming."[^2]

[[salicylic-acid]] is an oil-soluble beta-hydroxy acid that sits in the US OTC drug monograph as an acne active at 0.5% to 2%; at 2%, this gel is dosed at the top of that range. It has real but second-tier evidence for acne, guideline-recommended by the American Academy of Dermatology only conditionally, behind benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence).

The Drug Facts inactive-ingredient list also names glycolic acid and lactic acid, two AHAs with their own exfoliation evidence, [linked out on their own pages](#the-evidence), but CeraVe does not disclose their concentrations, and no trial isolates their contribution within this specific three-acid formula. The rest of the base is CeraVe's familiar barrier combination: three ceramides (NP, AP, EOP) and niacinamide, also at undisclosed concentrations.[^2]

The manufacturer's own directions, printed on the Drug Facts label, call for cleaning the skin thoroughly before applying, covering the entire affected area with a thin layer one to three times daily, and starting with one application daily before gradually increasing "because excessive drying of the skin may occur," reducing to once a day or every other day "if bothersome dryness or peeling occurs."[^2] The label also warns that skin irritation and dryness are more likely if this product is used alongside another topical acne medication, and advises using only one topical acne medication at a time if irritation occurs.[^2]

## The Evidence

The active-ingredient evidence for salicylic acid as an acne treatment, including its guideline standing behind benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids, is set out on [[salicylic-acid]] and not repeated here. No published clinical trial isolates this specific gel, its three-acid combination, or its ceramide-and-niacinamide base; the grades above reflect category-level evidence for each ingredient class rather than a trial of this product.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Hydrating, daily acne treatment with 2% salicylic acid that clears acne and helps prevent new breakouts from forming."** CeraVe's product-page claim; the salicylic acid 2% acne indication is FDA-monograph-supported (see [[salicylic-acid]]), but no trial isolates this specific gel.[^1]
- **"Gentle exfoliation with alpha- and beta-hydroxy acids (AHA and BHA)."** Refers to the added glycolic and lactic acids, whose concentrations are undisclosed and whose contribution within this three-acid formula is not separately studied.[^1]
- **"Calming niacinamide and barrier-strengthening ceramides."** A formulation-positioning claim consistent with CeraVe's ceramide-and-niacinamide base, not an acne-efficacy result.[^1]
