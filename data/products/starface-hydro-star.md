The Starface Hydro-Star is a star-shaped [[hydrocolloid]] pimple patch that the brand describes as "made with 100% hydrocolloid": yellow star-shaped adhesive dots with no added active ingredient. Starface's own store lists the 32-patch refill at $10.99.[^starface]

> This is hydrocolloid and nothing else, cut into a star shape: no salicylic acid, tea tree, or benzoyl peroxide. Its evidence is exactly the material's evidence on [[hydrocolloid]], modest in effect and weak in quality: a single small pilot trial found it absorbs fluid from and shields an already-open or surfaced pustule faster than a plain adhesive over three to seven days. It does nothing for a closed comedone, a cystic lesion under the skin, or preventing the next breakout, and being plain hydrocolloid, it cannot outperform that material's own ceiling. The star shape and yellow color are cosmetic, not clinical.

## Summary

Starface markets the patch as "clinically proven to shrink pimples in just six hours" (with an asterisk on that claim) and describes it as "made with 100% hydrocolloid" that helps "absorb gunk and reduce redness."[^starface] The brand's own description states the sole material is hydrocolloid; there is no added acne active. The refill ships as 32 star-shaped patches for $10.99 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-30); the same patches also refill a companion mirrored compact the brand calls "Big Yellow."[^starface]

## The Evidence

There is no trial of this specific product. Because it adds no active ingredient beyond the hydrocolloid base, its evidence is simply the material's own, modest in effect and weak in quality, set out in full on [[hydrocolloid]]: it absorbs fluid from and shields an already-open or surfaced pustule, on the strength of a single small pilot trial, and does nothing for closed comedones, cystic lesions, or preventing new breakouts. It cannot exceed what that material has shown, so the grade here matches [[hydrocolloid]]'s rubric exactly, the same as the sibling [[cosrx-acne-pimple-master-patch]] and [[hero-cosmetics-mighty-patch-original]].

Two effects are plausible but were not what the trial measured: the patch is a physical barrier that can keep fingers off the spot, and its opaque star flags the area, both practical conveniences rather than treatment effects.

> **Bottom line.** A plain hydrocolloid patch in a star shape (32 for $10.99).[^starface] It absorbs and shields an already-open pustule, on evidence that is modest and narrow (a single small trial, short-term, surfaced lesions only). It is not a treatment for acne itself and does nothing for the closed or deep lesions covered on [[acne]] and [[blackheads-comedones-congestion]].

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by independent evidence, attributed to the brand:

- **"Clinically proven to shrink pimples in just six hours."**[^starface] Starface footnotes this claim with an asterisk that resolves to "based on a 23-subject clinical study over 5 days," an unpublished, uncited study rather than the peer-reviewed pilot trial discussed on [[hydrocolloid]], so it carries none of that trial's (already modest) design disclosure: no control group named, no defined endpoint, no peer review. The disclosed five-day duration does not even match the "six hours" headline it supports. The one published controlled trial of the material (see [[hydrocolloid]]) measured differences over three to seven days against a plain tape, not six hours, and none on this specific product.
- **"Made with 100% hydrocolloid."**[^starface] This is consistent with the confirmed absence of an added active; it also means the patch's ceiling is exactly the hydrocolloid material's, no higher.
- **"Help absorb gunk and reduce redness for happy skin."**[^starface] The material's trial evidence covers reduced severity of an already-present, surfaced pustule over several days; "absorb gunk" describes the hydrocolloid mechanism set out on [[hydrocolloid]].
