Beauty of Joseon Revive Eye Serum: Ginseng + Retinal is a 30ml Korean under-eye serum from [[beauty-of-joseon|Beauty of Joseon]] pairing panax ginseng root extract with retinal (retinaldehyde) at an undisclosed concentration, priced at $17.00 ($15.30 at a listed discount) on the brand's own site.[^boj][^yesstyle]

> Retinaldehyde is one oxidation step from retinoic acid and the best-evidenced over-the-counter retinoid, with modest, category-level evidence for wrinkle and texture improvement, including a vehicle-controlled trial that measured the crow's-feet area specifically; see [the evidence](#the-evidence) and [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]. This serum does not disclose its retinal percentage, and no trial tests this exact formula: the brand's own wrinkle claim comes from an unpublished, brand-commissioned study on 21 subjects with no disclosed vehicle arm.[^boj]

## Summary

Revive Eye Serum: Ginseng + Retinal is a 30ml fragrance-free eye-area serum sold on Beauty of Joseon's own site for $17.00, currently discounted to $15.30, with a 30ml x 2-pack at $34.00 ($25.50 discounted) and a 50ml x 2-pack at $56.00 ($42.00 discounted); an independent YesStyle listing for the same 30ml SKU confirms the $17.00 regular price.[^boj][^yesstyle] The brand's own directions describe pumping the applicator once or twice and patting the product under and around the eyes "morning and night," always layering sunscreen during the day.[^boj]

The product name and the brand's own key-ingredient labeling both lead with two actives: ginseng and retinal.[^boj] Retinal is [[retinaldehyde|retinaldehyde]] itself, the closest-to-active retinoid available without a prescription and the best-evidenced over-the-counter retinoid form, [set out in full on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]](#the-evidence). Unlike [[avene-retrinal-0-1-intensive-cream]] (labeled 0.1%) or [[medik8-crystal-retinal-3]] (a labeled 0.01-0.24% ladder), this serum does not state a retinal percentage anywhere on the product page, and Retinal sits 28th of 36 ingredients, below both Panax Ginseng Root Extract (14th) and Niacinamide (7th), a composition detail consistent with a low concentration.[^boj]

Ginseng's cosmetic evidence, covered on [[panax-ginseng]], rests on a single small trial of an enzyme-modified ginseng cream in 23 people; this product's ingredient list names plain Panax Ginseng Root Extract, a different form than the one tested, and no percentage is declared.[^boj] Niacinamide also appears, seventh on the list and ahead of both named actives, though the brand's own marketing for this product does not single it out; niacinamide's general evidence is covered on [[niacinamide]].

No published trial tests this exact eye serum. The brand's own product page cites a wrinkle-improvement figure and a brightening-satisfaction figure, both from small, unpublished studies discussed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^boj]

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinaldehyde improves photoaged skin is covered on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]: in short, it carries the strongest over-the-counter retinoid evidence, ahead of retinol's thinner data, though not shown to match prescription [[tretinoin]]. One detail is specifically relevant to an eye product: the vehicle-controlled trial anchoring that evidence measured wrinkle and roughness reduction using silicone replicas taken from the crow's-feet area, the periorbital region this serum targets, though that trial used a 0.05% retinaldehyde cream on the general face rather than a dedicated eye formula.[^retinaldehyde] No published trial tests this serum, its undisclosed retinal concentration, or its combination with ginseng and niacinamide.

Whether topical ginseng affects skin appearance is covered on [[panax-ginseng]]: the one cited trial of an actual topical ginseng cream, in 23 people, found reduced photo-damage and roughness scores against placebo, but tested an enzyme-modified extract, not the plain root extract this serum lists, and most of the larger ginseng-and-skin numbers in circulation trace back to trials of oral ginseng supplements rather than a topical product.[^panax-ginseng]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims from the brand's own product page, not established by independent, product-specific evidence above:

- **"Clinically tested to improve the appearance of wrinkles by 8.65% in two weeks."** The brand attributes this to "a study conducted by Korea Dermatology Research Institute on 21 subjects over two weeks"; no vehicle or placebo arm, no publication, and no peer review are disclosed.[^boj]
- **"100% were satisfied with the brightening effect."** The brand attributes this to "a consumer perception survey of 21 subjects over two weeks," a self-reported satisfaction measure rather than an instrument-based brightening result.[^boj]
- **"Retinal liposomes and ginseng extract work together to help improve skin firmness and visible signs of aging."** No cited trial of this formula's combination, and no percentage is disclosed for either the retinal or the ginseng extract.[^boj]
