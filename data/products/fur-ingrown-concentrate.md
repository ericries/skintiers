The Fur Ingrown Concentrate is a botanical face-and-body oil that Fur markets for ingrown hairs in sensitive areas such as the bikini line. Fur lists it at $34.00.[^fur]

> This is a blend of carrier oils and essential oils, not a keratolytic. The topicals with real evidence for ingrown hairs and razor bumps are the exfoliating acids and retinoids set out on [[ingrown-hairs]] and [[pseudofolliculitis-barbae]]; this product contains none of them, so it cannot do that job. Its tea tree oil is mildly antimicrobial but unproven for ingrowns, and the tea tree, lavender, and clary sage essential oils in it are fragrance that can irritate the already-inflamed skin it is applied to. There is no independent trial of this product.

## Summary

Fur markets this as an "ingrown hair solution" "designed for sensitive areas" that "smooths skin, and helps prevent future irritation," and suggests pairing it with physical exfoliation.[^fur] It is an oil concentrate, not an acid or retinoid treatment. The evidence-based approach to ingrown hairs (changing the hair removal, plus keratolytic exfoliating acids or retinoids) is set out on [[ingrown-hairs]]; this page covers what is specific to this product. It costs $34.00 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-10-02).[^fur]

## The Evidence

There is no independent published trial of this specific product, and its formula does not match the mechanism the ingrown-hair evidence supports. The topical mainstays for ingrown hairs and razor bumps are keratolytics, the exfoliating acids ([[glycolic-acid|glycolic]], [[salicylic-acid|salicylic]]) and [[retinoids]] that help the skin shed around the follicle so trapped hairs release, graded on [[ingrown-hairs]] and [[pseudofolliculitis-barbae]]. The Fur Ingrown Concentrate contains none of these; its ingredient list is a blend of carrier oils (fractionated coconut, grape seed, jojoba, castor, safflower, almond, borage, evening primrose, tamanu) with vitamin E, bisabolol, and the essential oils tea tree, lavender, and clary sage.

Two honest points follow. First, the one ingredient with a plausible mechanism is tea tree (Melaleuca) oil, which is mildly antimicrobial and could in theory calm the folliculitis-type inflammation around an ingrown hair, but there is no controlled trial of it for ingrown hairs, and it is low on the list. Second, applying an essential-oil-containing oil (tea tree, lavender, clary sage are all fragrance components and recognized contact sensitizers for some people) to freshly shaved or waxed, already-irritated skin in a sensitive area is a real irritation risk, not a given benefit. A plain fragrance-free oil would soften skin without that risk.

> **Bottom line.** A $34.00 botanical oil blend for ingrown hairs that omits the keratolytic actives the condition's evidence actually supports.[^fur] It can soften and condition skin like any oil, but it is not a treatment for ingrown hairs in the way an exfoliating acid or retinoid is (see [[ingrown-hairs]]), and its tea tree, lavender, and clary sage essential oils are a sensitization risk on freshly shaved skin. The keratolytic alternative on this site is [[tend-skin-the-skin-care-solution]].

Notable: the base is emollient carrier oils that soften skin; vitamin E (tocopherol) and bisabolol are antioxidant and soothing. The functional pitch rests on tea tree oil (mildly antimicrobial). There is no salicylic acid, glycolic acid, or retinoid, the actives graded for ingrown hairs on [[ingrown-hairs]]. Tea tree, lavender, and clary sage are essential oils that function as fragrance and are potential sensitizers.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms attributed to the brand:

- **"Ingrown hair solution" / "smooths skin, and helps prevent future irritation."**[^fur] The "smooths skin" part is consistent with any emollient oil; "ingrown hair solution" overstates what an oil with no keratolytic can do for the follicular problem described on [[ingrown-hairs]].
- **"Designed for sensitive areas."**[^fur] It is positioned for the bikini line and other sensitive sites, but it contains tea tree, lavender, and clary sage essential oils, which are common sensitizers, so "for sensitive areas" is a positioning claim rather than a low-irritation guarantee.
