Prequel Redness Reform Serum is a fragrance-free facial serum Prequel markets to "optically reduce" the look of redness and irritation, built on a green-tinted, oat- and arnica-based soothing formula, made by Prequel, a skincare brand whose product page identifies its founder, Dr. Samantha Ellis, as a dermatologist.[^pdp]

> A hydrating base serum with a distinctive twist: its formula includes a green cosmetic pigment, Chromium Oxide Greens, that visually cancels out red tones on contact, which is what "optically reduces redness" describes.[^pdp][^target] Around that sits a blend of common calming ingredients (oat kernel extract, panthenol, bisabolol, licorice-root extract, arnica) with no independent evidence located for this specific formula; the ingredient list contains no niacinamide, azelaic acid, or other clinically studied anti-redness active.[^pdp][^target] Prequel positions it among its products carrying the National Rosacea Society Seal of Acceptance, a claim on Prequel's own page and [reported here as marketing, not independently confirmed for this SKU](#common-marketing-claims).[^pdp] It is inexpensive for the category: Prequel lists the 1 fl oz bottle at $20.00 and Target lists the same size at $19.99 (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^pdp][^target]

## Summary

Redness Reform Serum is a lightweight, fragrance-free serum Prequel positions for redness, irritation, and sensitivity, directing it to be applied "to dry, cleansed skin," followed by moisturizer and sun protection.[^pdp] The base is built on water, propanediol, glycerin, and dimethicone, the same kind of humectant-and-light-occlusive combination behind ordinary hydrating serums; that general moisturizing case is set out on [[moisturizing]].

What makes this serum distinct from a plain hydrating serum is not a clinically studied active ingredient. Its ingredient list carries no niacinamide, no azelaic acid, and no other topical active with an established clinical evidence base for rosacea or redness.[^pdp][^target] Instead, two things do the work its marketing describes. First, the formula lists Chromium Oxide Greens (CI 77288), a green cosmetic pigment, well down the ingredient list; a green tint is a standard color-correcting technique that visually offsets red tones on the skin, and its presence is the direct explanation for Prequel's "optically reduces redness" language.[^pdp] Second, the formula carries a blend of ingredients commonly marketed for calming and comfort: oat kernel extract, beta-glucan, panthenol, bisabolol, dipotassium glycyrrhizate (a licorice-root derivative), and arnica montana flower extract, plus linoleic and linolenic acid, conventionally grouped together in cosmetic ingredient naming as "vitamin F" (a term Prequel's own description uses without naming the fatty acids).[^pdp] No independent trial of this formula, or of these ingredients in this combination, was located.

Prequel's product page separately reports its own consumer-perception testing: after one use, "96% agreed that their skin felt soothed" and "90% agreed it felt cooling on their skin"; after 28 days, "100% agreed that their skin felt soothed," "100% agreed that their skin felt hydrated," "100% agreed it visually evened their skin tone," and "90% neutralized skin redness."[^pdp] These figures come only from Prequel's own site, with no named testing facility, and Target's listing does not repeat or corroborate them, so [they are reported as marketing, not folded into the grades above](#common-marketing-claims).[^pdp][^target]

The serum is inexpensive for the category. Prequel lists the 1 fl oz (30 mL) bottle at $20.00, and Target lists the same size at $19.99 (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^pdp][^target]

## The Evidence

No published, independent trial of this exact serum was located. Its hydration base rests on the general, well-evidenced case for humectant-and-light-occlusive moisturizing (glycerin, propanediol, and dimethicone), set out on [[moisturizing]].

The product-specific fact behind the "optically reduces redness" claim is compositional: the ingredient list includes Chromium Oxide Greens (CI 77288), a green cosmetic pigment.[^pdp][^target] A green tint is a standard color-correcting technique, since green and red sit opposite each other and visually cancel out on skin; that mechanism, not a treatment effect on the skin itself, is what the marketing phrase describes.

Beyond the pigment and the humectant base, the formula's remaining distinguishing ingredients (oat kernel extract, beta-glucan, panthenol, bisabolol, dipotassium glycyrrhizate, and arnica montana flower extract) are common cosmetic ingredients marketed for calming and comfort.[^pdp] The only reported outcome data for this exact serum is Prequel's own consumer-perception testing, quoted above, run and published solely on the manufacturer's own site with no named facility or independent replication.[^pdp]

> **Bottom line.** The serum's redness claim is explained by a color-correcting green pigment in the formula, a composition fact, not a treatment effect confirmed by independent testing. Its measurable, well-evidenced job is ordinary humectant hydration.[^pdp]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer, corroborated on Target's listing for the same SKU.[^pdp][^target]

Notable and functional ingredients:

- Water (Aqua), propanediol, [[glycerin]], butylene glycol, and pentylene glycol, humectants that bind water in the outer skin
- [[dimethicone]], a silicone that adds a light occlusive, slip-improving finish
- Linoleic acid and linolenic acid, essential fatty acids conventionally grouped together in cosmetic ingredient naming as "vitamin F"; Prequel's marketing mentions only "vitamin F" and does not name these fatty acids[^pdp]
- Avena sativa (oat) kernel extract, beta-glucan, and magnesium carboxymethyl beta-glucan, oat-derived ingredients
- Hydroxyphenyl propamidobenzoic acid, a cosmetic ingredient marketed for redness reduction
- Dipotassium glycyrrhizate, a licorice-root extract
- Arnica montana flower extract
- [[panthenol]] and bisabolol, common skin-conditioning and soothing ingredients
- Aloe barbadensis leaf juice
- [[tocopherol]], an antioxidant
- Chromium Oxide Greens (CI 77288), a green cosmetic pigment; see [Summary](#summary) for its color-correcting role

Base, texture, and preservative system: hydroxyacetophenone, piperonyl glucoside, carbomer, polysilicone-11, ammonium acryloyldimethyltaurate/VP copolymer, algin, xanthan gum, oleic acid, palmitic acid, stearic acid, 1,2-hexanediol, caprylyl glycol, benzoic acid, sorbic acid, citric acid, ascorbyl palmitate, phenoxyethanol, glycolic acid, galactoarabinan, potassium sorbate, sodium benzoate, and sodium hydroxide.[^pdp]

The ingredient list declares no niacinamide, no azelaic acid, and no added fragrance.[^pdp]
