Palmer's Cocoa Butter Formula Massage Lotion for Stretch Marks is an inexpensive (about $8.99 for 8.5 fl oz), widely sold cocoa-butter body lotion marketed to help prevent and reduce the appearance of pregnancy stretch marks.[^brand] The brand calls itself the "#1 Stretch Mark Brand," and this is one of the most recognizable stretch-mark products on the drugstore shelf.[^brand]

> The marketing claim fails the best test there is. A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial found a cocoa butter lotion did no better than placebo at preventing stretch marks in pregnancy, and a dermatology review concluded cocoa butter shows no effect on them.[^osman][^review] The brand's own supporting evidence is an 8-week in-home study of 102 women with no control group, not a comparison against placebo. It is a competent, cheap emollient body moisturizer; it is not an evidence-based stretch-mark treatment.

## Summary

This is Palmer's flagship stretch-mark lotion: a cocoa-butter emollient sold specifically for striae during and after pregnancy or weight change, with the brand stating it "helps visibly improve skin elasticity and helps improve the appearance of stretch marks" and keeps skin "moisturized and supple for 48 hours."[^brand] The current formula is marketed as fragrance-free and free of mineral oil, parabens, phthalates, and dyes.[^brand]

The health claim is the reason to be skeptical. The single best study of cocoa butter for this exact use is a multicentre, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial: 175 first-time mothers applied either a cocoa butter lotion or a placebo lotion daily from the first trimester until delivery, and there was no difference in whether they developed stretch marks (45.1% versus 48.8%, P = 0.730) or in their severity.[^osman] A 2016 review of topical stretch-mark management reached the same verdict, that cocoa butter "did not demonstrate any effect."[^review]

Set that against the brand's evidence: it states "over 98% of women saw improved skin elasticity, texture and tone," based on "an 8 week in-home trial by 102 female panelists."[^brand] That is an uncontrolled consumer self-assessment with no placebo group, which is exactly the kind of study the placebo-controlled trial was designed to see past.

What the product does do is moisturize. Cocoa butter, shea butter, argan, almond, and coconut oils and vitamin E make a genuinely effective emollient, so skin feels softer and more comfortable; that is a cosmetic benefit, and the general evidence for moisturization lives on [[moisturizing]]. It is just not the same as preventing or erasing stretch marks.

## The Evidence

The health question, does it prevent or reduce stretch marks, has a direct answer. In Osman and colleagues' trial (multicentre, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled; 210 enrolled, 175 completed), women applied cocoa butter lotion or placebo daily throughout pregnancy, and "there was no difference in the development of SG (45.1% versus 48.8%; P = 0.730) or the severity of SG"; the result held after accounting for pre-existing stretch marks and compliance, and the authors concluded cocoa butter "does not appear to reduce the likelihood of developing striae gravidarum."[^osman] A 2016 dermatology review of striae treatments similarly found cocoa butter "did not demonstrate any effect," while noting the field lacks strong trials overall.[^review]

No independent trial of this exact Palmer's lotion was located; the marketed "over 98%" figure comes from the brand's own uncontrolled 8-week consumer panel, not a controlled study.[^brand] The marketed "collagen" and "elastin" are worth flagging separately: topically applied collagen and elastin are large molecules that do not penetrate to rebuild the skin's own dermal matrix, so their presence on the label is not a mechanism for treating stretch marks.

> **Bottom line.** A cheap, pleasant, fragrance-free cocoa-butter body moisturizer sold on a stretch-mark claim that a placebo-controlled trial does not support.[^osman][^brand] Buy it if you want an inexpensive emollient; do not expect it to prevent or remove stretch marks.

## What's In It

Palmer's lists the key ingredients as cocoa butter and shea butter (emollient butters), vitamin E (antioxidant), lutein, and argan, almond, and coconut oils.[^brand] Functionally this is a rich emollient body lotion built on plant butters and oils. The brand states the current formula is fragrance-free and free of mineral oil, parabens, phthalates, and dyes, which is relevant for applying it over large areas of pregnant or sensitive skin.[^brand]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Helps prevent and reduce the appearance of stretch marks" / "#1 Stretch Mark Brand."** Not supported by the controlled evidence: a double-blind placebo-controlled trial found no benefit over placebo for preventing pregnancy stretch marks.[^osman][^brand]
- **"Over 98% of women saw improved skin elasticity, texture and tone."** A brand consumer study of 102 panelists over 8 weeks with no control group; self-reported and not comparable to a placebo-controlled trial.[^brand]
- **"Collagen, Elastin and Lutein."** Ingredient-deck marketing; topical collagen and elastin do not penetrate to rebuild dermal structure, so they are not a plausible stretch-mark mechanism.[^brand]
- **"Keeps skin moisturised and supple for 48 hours."** A moisturization claim; the formula is a capable emollient, but "48 hours" is a brand-stated figure not independently verified here.[^brand]
