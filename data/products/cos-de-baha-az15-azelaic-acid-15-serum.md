Cos De BAHA's AZ15 is a 30 ml over-the-counter serum built on azelaic acid at a stated 15%, with 1% MSM and a supporting cast of hyaluronates, ceramide NP, centella and licorice-derived soothers.[^brand] It sits between the brand's own [[cos-de-baha-az20-azelaic-acid-20-serum|AZ20]] and the prescription gel on [[finacea-azelaic-acid-15-gel]].

> **Bottom line.** A sensibly built azelaic serum at a genuinely useful strength: azelaic acid is declared third on the ingredient list, consistent with the stated 15%, and azelaic acid has real evidence for acne and the marks it leaves (see [[azelaic-acid]]).[^brand] The trap to avoid is the one the number invites: **matching Finacea's 15% does not make this the prescription gel.** No trial tests this serum, the vehicle is different, and a concentration is not a formulation. Treat the brand's "whitening and antioxidant" claim for its 1% MSM as unproven.

## Summary

The declared ingredient list runs water, propylene glycol, **azelaic acid**, sodium hydroxide, 1,2-hexanediol, polysorbate 20, **dimethyl sulfone** (MSM), propanediol, betaine, panthenol, dipotassium glycyrrhizate, allantoin, ceramide NP, sodium hyaluronate, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, centella asiatica extract, madecassoside, then thickeners and plant extracts.[^brand] Azelaic acid in third position is consistent with the stated 15%, and the brand states MSM at 1%.[^brand]

Two things in that list are worth naming plainly. **Sodium hydroxide sits fourth**, which is a pH adjuster and a frequent target of social-media chemistry claims that it must have neutralised the acid. That inference is not supported: the studied prescription azelaic formulation also uses sodium hydroxide for pH adjustment, and neither this page nor the brand publishes a final pH, so the formula's pH and delivery are simply **unknown** rather than bad.[^brand] And the supporting ingredients, ceramide NP, panthenol, the hyaluronates, centella and madecassoside, are a reasonable comfort package for an acid serum, but their amounts are undisclosed, so ingredient order cannot tell you how much barrier support any of them delivers.

The general case for azelaic acid, with its numbers and limits for acne, rosacea and pigment, is on [[azelaic-acid]] and is not repeated here.

## The Evidence

No published trial tests this serum, so its grade rests on the evidence for azelaic acid plus what the label supports. That evidence is genuinely good at the ingredient level, which is why the effect grade is modest rather than minimal.

The gap is transfer, and it is the whole argument on this page. The strongest azelaic evidence comes from prescription formulations in specific vehicles, tested in controlled trials, and those trials and their numbers are set out on [[azelaic-acid]] and [[finacea-azelaic-acid-15-gel]]. This is a cosmetic serum in a different vehicle with no trial of its own. Three things follow. Concentration is not formulation, so "15%, same as Finacea" is a statement about a number rather than about performance.[^brand] Penetration and tolerability depend on the vehicle and the final pH, neither of which is published here. And nothing in the cited evidence compares this serum with its sibling [[cos-de-baha-az20-azelaic-acid-20-serum|AZ20]] or with the 12% serums in the category, so a reader should not read the percentage ladder as an efficacy ladder.

A note on internal consistency, because it matters more than smoothing it over: the sibling [[cos-de-baha-az20-azelaic-acid-20-serum|AZ20]] page currently grades its acne evidence `solid`, reached by matching a prescription formulation's concentration. That is the same inference this page declines to make, so AZ20's grade is flagged for review rather than copied here. Where the two pages disagree, this is the reasoning to prefer.

## Common Marketing Claims

Claims quoted from the brand's own product page; treat each as unproven unless it also appears under The Evidence.

- **"Azelaic Acid 15% ... Reduces hyperpigmentation and improves skin tone."**[^brand] The direction is supported at the ingredient level for post-acne pigment (see [[azelaic-acid]] and [[hyperpigmentation]]), but no trial of this serum measures it, and pigment responses take months.
- **"MSM 1% (Methylsulfonylmethane) ... Whitening and antioxidant effects, improves skin tone."**[^brand] Not supported here. No trial of topical MSM for skin tone is cited on this site, so this is the claim to discount hardest. "Whitening" is also a cosmetic appearance claim, not a health one.
- **"Sodium Hyaluronate and Hydrolyzed Hyaluronic Acid provide deep, multi-layered hydration."**[^brand] Humectants do raise a measured hydration reading (see [[hyaluronic-acid]]); "deep" and "multi-layered" are marketing rather than measurements.
- **"Designed for sensitive and acne-prone skin."**[^brand] A positioning claim. The soothers are present and there is no fragrance in the declared list, but an acid serum at this strength can still sting, and no tolerability trial of this product is cited.
