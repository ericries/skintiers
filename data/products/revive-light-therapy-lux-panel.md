The Lux Panel is the $399 folding version of a device this site already covers at $199, and its claim list is word-for-word the same. **Bottom line:** everything graded on [[revive-light-therapy-lux-clinical]] applies here unchanged, so this page covers only what differs: a bigger but unquantified treatment area, a body indication the handheld does not claim, and eye protection in the box.

> **The brand never says how large the large area is.** It is sold on "Large treatment area for the face, neck, chest, back, and more" and as a "hands-free panel", and the product page gives no measurement anywhere.[^brand] Checked by string search of the cached page, it contains no treated-area figure in square centimetres or any other unit. Area is the one thing the extra $200 buys over the handheld, and it is the one specification not disclosed.

## Summary

Priced at $399.00 on 2026-10-21.[^brand] The spec list matches the handheld exactly: "Red, infrared, amber, and blue light", "Primary wavelength range: 415-880 nm", "Three treatment settings: Anti-Aging, Acne, and Acne + Anti-Aging", "3-minute treatments per area", "FDA-cleared acne treatment", "OTC Class II medical device".[^brand] Panel-specific additions are a "Folding panel design for storage", "Designed for direct skin contact", "FSA/HSA eligible", "No prescription required" and a "1-year manufacturer warranty".[^brand]

## The Rubric

**Clearing mild to moderate acne, and improving fine lines**
- **Effect size: modest for acne, minimal for wrinkles.** Identical to the handheld, for identical reasons: the blue plus red acne pairing carries the best modality grade in the 2026 review of home devices, while LED for rejuvenation sits a tier below home fractional nonablative lasers.[^grade] The full reading is on [[revive-light-therapy-lux-clinical]] and the modality case is on [[home-led-light-therapy]].
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** No FDA clearance bears the Lux name, so as with every unit in this collection there is no device-level file to read. That gap is documented on the sibling page and is not re-argued here.

**What the panel format changes**
- **Effect size: unknown, and that is the finding.** A larger emitting surface treating the same three minutes per area is a plausible convenience improvement over a handheld, and possibly a dose change too, but neither can be checked. The brand publishes no area and no irradiance, so a buyer cannot tell whether the panel delivers the same light over more skin or less light over more skin.[^brand]
- **Evidence quality: not applicable.** Nothing measured either way.

## The Evidence

**It is the only unit in the line sold for the body.** The handheld is described for the face, including the under-eye and nose areas; this one names "face, neck, chest, back, and more".[^brand] That makes it the first device on this site positioned against [[body-acne]] and the décolletage, so it is worth saying plainly that the 2026 review's Level B grade comes from trials of facial acne and facial rejuvenation, not back or chest treatment.[^grade] Nothing consulted here tested any LED device on the back.

**Eye protection is in this box and was not in the other one.** The Lux Panel's contents list includes "Comfort Goggles".[^brand] The handheld, which the brand advertises for the under-eye area, ships without them, and a customer review on that product page reports buying goggles separately.[^brand] For a device held near the eyes that is the wrong way round, and it is worth knowing before choosing between the two.

**The same two wording problems carry over.** The "clinically tested, multi-wavelength LED light therapy" phrasing attaches the testing to the modality rather than to this device, and the brand files both units under a collection whose page title claims FDA approval rather than clearance.[^brand] Both are set out with their sources on [[revive-light-therapy-lux-clinical]]; see [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] for why the second matters.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The brand positions this for mild to moderate acne; moderate to severe or scarring acne warrants a dermatologist rather than a light, and the ladder is on [[acne]]. Follow the manufacturer's eye-protection instructions, which is easier here than with the handheld because the goggles are included.

## Common Marketing Claims

Quarantined from the graded assessment above, and identical to the handheld's set: "Helps clear mild-to-moderate acne", "Improves the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles", "Supports collagen and elastin production" and "clinically tested, multi-wavelength LED light therapy".[^brand] The panel adds practical rather than efficacy claims, "FSA/HSA eligible" and "No prescription required", which describe how it is sold rather than what it does.
