Advanced Night Repair is Estee Lauder's long-running, best-selling night serum, built around bifida ferment lysate, a fermentation byproduct the brand markets as its "Night Peptide" technology, alongside the proprietary peptide tripeptide-32 and sodium hyaluronate for hydration.[^esteelauder] It costs $135.00 for the standard 1.7 oz (about 50 mL) bottle on the brand's own site.[^esteelauder]

> The formula is built on ferments and peptides, ingredient classes covered generally on [[probiotics]] and [[peptides]], both hydrating and antioxidant-supporting but with modest independent evidence. Bifida ferment lysate itself carries only preliminary support: an in vitro cell-culture study and one combination-product trial where its own contribution cannot be isolated (see [[bifida-ferment-lysate]]). The serum contains no retinoid and no vitamin C, the two actives with the strongest independently replicated evidence for wrinkles and photoaging (see [[retinoids]] and [[vitamin-c]]); Estee Lauder's own FAQ confirms "Advanced Night Repair does not contain Retinol."[^esteelauder] No published, independent trial tests this exact serum.

## Summary

Estee Lauder positions Advanced Night Repair as a "face and neck peptide serum visibly reduces signs of aging," describing it as "fast-penetrating" with "antioxidant defense" that "delivers overnight radiance ... even when you don't sleep well."[^esteelauder] The brand attributes this to its "exclusive Night Peptide," which it says was "pinpointed by Estée Lauder out of 60 million peptide variations" and is "formulated with technology inspired by Nobel Prize-winning research in the field of Circadian Rhythm."[^esteelauder] Read against the ingredient list, that Night Peptide branding covers bifida ferment lysate (second on the list, right after water) plus tripeptide-32, further down the list; the general evidence for both categories is covered on [[bifida-ferment-lysate]] and [[peptides]].

The formula also carries sodium hyaluronate for hydration, additional yeast and Lactobacillus ferments, caffeine, squalane, and tocopheryl acetate (vitamin E) as a supporting antioxidant ([full list below](#whats-in-it)).[^esteelauder] None of the actives has a disclosed concentration. Notably absent are the two ingredient classes with the strongest independent anti-aging evidence: a retinoid and vitamin C. Estee Lauder's own FAQ states plainly that "Advanced Night Repair does not contain Retinol," and suggests layering it with a separate retinol product rather than substituting for one.[^esteelauder] Neither ascorbic acid nor any vitamin C derivative appears on the ingredient list.

The formula also carries added colorants, Red 4 (CI 14700) and Yellow 5 (CI 19140), near the end of the list.[^esteelauder]

It costs $135.00 for the standard 1.7 oz (about 50 mL) size ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-21).[^esteelauder]

## The Evidence

No independently published, controlled trial of this exact serum was located. Estee Lauder's own product page cites several brand-run clinical and consumer studies without naming a publication or control arm: "+128% moisture surge" from "clinical testing on 148 women after 1 product application"; "-14% reduction in fine lines" and "+10% improvement in elasticity" from "clinical testing on 26 women, 12 hours after a single nighttime product application"; and "91% said skin looks more rested" from "consumer testing on 543 women after using product for 3 weeks."[^esteelauder] These read as unpublished brand-run studies, several with short follow-up (a single application, or 12 hours) and small sample sizes, rather than independently reviewed evidence.

The ingredient-level evidence is set out on the linked ingredient pages. Bifida ferment lysate's independent evidence is thin: a 2023 in vitro study found it changed barrier-gene and antioxidant markers in cultured skin cells (not human skin), and a 2022 trial found benefit for acne only as one of four actives in a combination product, so the result cannot be credited to bifida ferment lysate alone (see [[bifida-ferment-lysate]]). Peptides as a category carry weak independent evidence for topical anti-aging use, and tripeptide-32 specifically has no dedicated published human trial (see [[peptides]]). Sodium hyaluronate's hydration mechanism is well established generally (see [[hyaluronic-acid]], tier: strong), but this product does not disclose its concentration, so the strength of that general evidence cannot be assumed to transfer at whatever dose is used here.

The product contains neither a retinoid nor vitamin C, the two active categories with the deepest base of independently replicated human trials for wrinkles, texture, and photoaging (see [[retinoids]] and [[vitamin-c]]). Estee Lauder's own FAQ confirms the absence of retinol and recommends pairing the serum with a separate retinol product rather than treating it as a substitute.[^esteelauder]

> **Bottom line.** A well-tolerated, cosmetically elegant hydrating and antioxidant serum built on ferments and peptides, ingredient classes with modest, largely preliminary independent evidence rather than the deep trial base behind retinoids and vitamin C. No independently published trial tests this exact formula; the brand's own clinical figures are unpublished, short-duration, or use small samples. Readers looking for the best-evidenced topical anti-aging actives should start with [[retinoids]] or [[vitamin-c]], neither of which this serum contains.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Visibly reduces signs of aging."** The headline claim rests on bifida ferment lysate and tripeptide-32, both weak-evidence supporting actives with no independent trial isolating their effect in this formula (see [[bifida-ferment-lysate]] and [[peptides]]).[^esteelauder]
- **"Exclusive Night Peptide... pinpointed by Estée Lauder out of 60 million peptide variations" and "technology inspired by Nobel Prize-winning research in the field of Circadian Rhythm."** A proprietary-technology narrative around bifida ferment lysate and tripeptide-32; no independent, product-specific trial supports a circadian-timed mechanism for this serum.[^esteelauder]
- **"+128% moisture surge," "-14% reduction in fine lines," "+10% improvement in elasticity," "91% said skin looks more rested."** Brand-run clinical and consumer testing without a named publication, control arm, or peer review; several figures come from a single application or 12-hour follow-up.[^esteelauder]
- **"72-hour hydration."** Attributed to sodium hyaluronate and unnamed "advanced moisture boosters," at an undisclosed concentration; general hydration evidence for hyaluronic acid is covered on [[hyaluronic-acid]], but it does not establish this product's duration claim.[^esteelauder]
