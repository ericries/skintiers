Eryfotona Ageless is ISDIN's tinted, 100% mineral face sunscreen, built around zinc oxide 10.7% as its sole active and marketed with added peptides, antioxidants, and the brand's "DNA Repairsomes" technology for photoaging repair.[^1][^2] Its FDA Drug Facts label, filed by ISDIN Corp and available on DailyMed, names it "ERYFOTONA AGELESS" (an "Ultralight Emulsion" zinc oxide lotion) and rates it Broad Spectrum SPF 50.[^1]

> A single-filter zinc oxide mineral sunscreen tinted with iron oxides, sold at a premium price against a well-evidenced category. Broad-spectrum UV protection's health case, slowing photoaging (RCT-solid) and, with a more mixed evidence base, lowering skin-cancer risk, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; zinc oxide's own UVA reach is the broader of the two mineral filters, a comparative point drawn from [[zinc-oxide]].[^1][^3] The brand's added-value claims, DNA Repairsomes, peptides, and antioxidant "anti-aging" repair, are marketing language, weighed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^2]

## Summary

Eryfotona Ageless is part of ISDIN's Eryfotona photoaging-focused sunscreen line (alongside Eryfotona Actinica). This tinted version lists zinc oxide 10.7% as its only active ingredient, confirmed on its FDA Drug Facts label filed by ISDIN Corp and independently matched, ingredient for ingredient, on a Dermstore retailer listing.[^1][^2] The label rates it "Broad Spectrum SPF 50."[^1] Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]: RCT-solid for slowing photoaging, and more mixed (a melanoma signal that falls just short of statistical significance, and no significant person-level reduction in keratinocyte cancers per an independent Cochrane review) for lowering skin-cancer risk.

Unlike tinted mineral sunscreens that pair titanium dioxide with zinc oxide, or that use titanium dioxide alone (as in [[la-roche-posay-anthelios-mineral-tinted-spf-50|La Roche-Posay's Anthelios Mineral Tinted]]), this formula relies on zinc oxide by itself. [[zinc-oxide]]'s evidence page cites a comparative review stating zinc oxide "has a broad UVA-UVB absorption curve" versus titanium dioxide's better UVB-only performance, which favors this product's UVA (photoaging) coverage relative to a titanium-only formula ([detail below](#the-evidence)).[^3]

The retailer listing describes the product as combining the mineral filter with "peptides, antioxidants, and DNA Repairsomes® to repair existing sun damage and reduce the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles."[^2] Those cosmetic claims are marketing language, weighed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims). It is listed at $78 for the 100 ml size on a Dermstore retailer listing, currently this page's only price source ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)).[^2]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has RCT-level support for slowing photoaging, and a more mixed but still notable signal for reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden; that evidence is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. The product-specific facts available for this formula are its FDA-labeled 10.7% zinc oxide active and Broad Spectrum SPF 50 rating.[^1]

This product's FDA Drug Facts label, filed by ISDIN Corp and hosted on DailyMed, lists "Zinc Oxide 10.7%" as the sole active ingredient and rates the product "Broad Spectrum SPF 50."[^1] A Dermstore retailer listing independently corroborates the same 10.7% zinc oxide active and matches the full inactive-ingredient list ingredient for ingredient.[^2]

On the UVA question: [[zinc-oxide]]'s evidence page cites Schneider and Lim's comparative review stating "ZnO has a broad UVA-UVB absorption curve, while TiO2 provides better UVB protection," and notes zinc oxide's absorption reaches further into long-wave UVA (UVA1) than titanium dioxide, and that it is photostable.[^3] Because this product's only active is zinc oxide, that comparative advantage applies directly: relative to a titanium-dioxide-only mineral sunscreen, its UVA coverage is plausibly the stronger side, though the exact protection any single product delivers still depends on applied amount and how evenly the particles are dispersed, a limitation [[zinc-oxide]] notes generally rather than a measurement of this exact formula.[^3]

The brand's added actives, peptides (Palmitoyl Tripeptide-38, Pentapeptide-34 Trifluoroacetate) and antioxidants (tocopherol, ascorbic acid, ascorbyl palmitate), sit well down the inactive-ingredient list. General peptide evidence for skin appearance is covered on [[peptides]]; the brand's own repair and anti-aging framing for this specific formula is marketing language, [weighed under Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1][^2]

> **Bottom line.** A single-filter, FDA-labeled Broad Spectrum SPF 50 zinc oxide sunscreen. Its zinc-oxide-only composition favors UVA coverage relative to titanium-only competitors, a comparative point from [[zinc-oxide]]; its marketed "anti-aging" peptide and DNA Repairsomes claims are marketing language, [weighed under Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1][^2][^3]
