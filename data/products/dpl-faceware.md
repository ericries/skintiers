The dpl Faceware is a five-wavelength LED face device from LED Technologies cleared for two different jobs at once. **Bottom line:** it is the clearest example this site has found of a device whose entire regulatory case is built out of its own maker's back catalogue. Three predicates are named, and all three belong to the same company. The modality evidence is on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

> **Three predicates, one firm, no clinical data at any step.** The Summary names "K180320 - dpl® II a Panel (LED Technologies, Inc.)", "K180447- reVive Light Therapy® LED Cleansing System (LED Technologies, Inc.)" and "K171386- dpl® Spectralite (LED Technologies, Inc.)".[^k183247] One of those, the Cleansing System, has a page here, and its own clearance was nonclinical too. The only human study in this file is a usability test: "A Usability Study was conducted with 15 participants", of whom "100% of the participants were able to use the device successfully".[^k183247]

## Summary

Cleared 2019-03-14 as K183247 under the dual product code OHS/OLP, regulation 878.4810, class II.[^openfda][^k183247] The indication covers both jobs in one sentence: "The dpl® Faceware is an Over-the-Counter (OTC) LED device intended for use in treating wrinkles and mild to moderate inflammatory acne."[^k183247] It carries five LED types, reported in the Summary as 605nm amber, 630nm red, 660nm red, 880nm infrared and 415nm blue, runs three minutes per treatment, and is applied directly to the skin with "no user settings or adjustments required".[^k183247] No price is quoted here; the brand's own site returned a Cloudflare challenge on two consecutive days and no metered lookup was spent on it.

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate inflammatory acne**
- **Effect size: modest.** The 415nm blue and red combination is the one that earned the best grade any home acne device received in the 2026 review: "blue light-emitting diodes (LED) and combination blue-red LED devices demonstrated moderate quality evidence (Level B) with favorable safety profiles and Grade I recommendations".[^grade] This device emits those wavelengths.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** The grade belongs to the modality, from a review that names no manufacturer or model, and this file contributes nothing to it. Its own closing sentence on testing reads "The conclusions drawn from nonclinical tests demonstrate that the device is safe, as effective, and performs as well as the legally marketed devices".[^k183247]

**Wrinkles**
- **Effect size: minimal.** The wrinkle function runs amber, two reds and infrared with no blue. LED reached Level B for facial rejuvenation in the same review, behind home fractional nonablative lasers at Level A.[^grade] Grading it minimal rather than modest follows the review's own ordering and the complete absence of device-level data, matching [[revive-light-therapy-wrinkle-and-acne-led-device]].
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** Nothing in the file reports a wrinkle outcome. The testing listed is electrical safety (IEC 60601-1 and 60601-1-2), biocompatibility (ISO 10993-5 cytotoxicity, ISO 10993-10 intracutaneous reactivity and skin sensitisation) and software validation.[^k183247]

**Dose, which the file again does not give**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a gap in the record.** The comparison chart's irradiance row reads "LED" for the Faceware and for all three predicates, and the equivalence claim names the unit of measurement without ever supplying a figure, asserting only that the device has the similar output in mW/cm² as the predicate devices.[^k183247]
- **Evidence quality: not applicable.** Treatment area is the one quantity the chart does give, and it varies enormously across the four devices: 81 cm² for acne and 135.8 cm² for wrinkles on the Faceware, against 415 cm² for the dpl IIa Panel, 18.86 cm² for the reVive Cleansing System and 28 cm² for the dpl Spectralite.[^k183247] Area is not irradiance, so these figures do not let a reader reconstruct the dose; they do show that devices spanning a more than twentyfold range in treated area were argued to be equivalent.

## The Evidence

**What each predicate was actually cleared for.** The chart sets the four indications side by side, and they are not the same. The dpl IIa Panel already carried the identical dual indication for wrinkles and mild to moderate inflammatory acne, so the breadth of this device's claim comes from there. The reVive Cleansing System is cleared for acne only. The dpl Spectralite is cleared for periorbital wrinkles, meaning around the eyes.[^k183247] A reader should not conclude that three devices with three different labels were pooled into a broader claim, because one predicate already held the full claim. What the arrangement does show is a firm building each clearance on the last.

**Five weeks after the SpotLite, with one fewer participant.** [[dpl-spotlite]] was cleared on 2019-02-07 and this device on 2019-03-14, both by the same firm, and the two Summaries share wording down to the phrasing of the testing sentence and the nonclinical conclusion.[^k183247][^openfda] The usability study here enrolled 15 people where the SpotLite's enrolled 16, and in both cases every participant passed. Whether a device can be switched on correctly is a reasonable thing to test. It is not a measure of whether light cleared anyone's skin.

**The conclusion is the submitter's, as written.** The document states that "the manufacturer asserts that no significant differences exist between the subject device and predicate, and no different questions of safety and effectiveness arise".[^k183247] That attribution is in the file itself. What a 510(k) clearance does and does not establish is covered at [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]].

**Where this sits among the full-face devices here.** [[neutrogena-light-therapy-acne-mask-plus]] and [[pulsaderm-acne-mask-28]] are acne-only masks, so they are comparable on the acne function alone. The closest match is [[revive-light-therapy-wrinkle-and-acne-led-device]], the same firm's later dual-indication unit, which is graded the same way here and for the same reason.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The cleared indications are wrinkles and mild to moderate inflammatory acne; moderate to severe or scarring acne warrants a dermatologist rather than a light, and the treatment ladder is on [[acne]].
