Supergoop! Unseen Sunscreen SPF 50 is a clear, gel-like face sunscreen built entirely on organic (chemical) UV filters, formulated to leave no visible cast and to double as a makeup primer.[^1][^2]

> A broad-spectrum SPF 50 sunscreen using an all-chemical filter set (avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, octocrylene). Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Its avobenzone-based UVA coverage is genuine once photostabilized by octocrylene, though older and less photostable than the modern filters (Tinosorb, Mexoryl) sold abroad. It is the current, reformulated SKU that replaced the discontinued [[supergoop-unseen-sunscreen-spf-40]] on Supergoop's own site.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Unseen Sunscreen SPF 50 is a facial sunscreen from Supergoop, a US suncare brand built around clear, cosmetically elegant formulas. It is an oil-free, gel-cream sunscreen positioned to sink in without a white cast or greasy finish and to sit under makeup as a primer.[^1][^2] Its core purpose is the same as any sunscreen's: broad-spectrum UV protection is the best-evidenced way to slow photoaging and lower the risk of sun-related skin cancer, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

This SPF 50 formula is the one Supergoop currently sells under the Unseen Sunscreen name; it replaced an earlier SPF 40 version ([[supergoop-unseen-sunscreen-spf-40]]) that no longer appears on the brand's site.[^1] Per the Drug Facts panel, confirmed independently on both Supergoop's own product page and Target's listing, the actives are avobenzone 3%, homosalate 7%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 9%, an all-organic (chemical) filter set rated Broad Spectrum SPF 50.[^1][^2] Avobenzone, at the US maximum concentration, is the formula's only dedicated UVA filter; octocrylene helps keep it from degrading in sunlight and adds its own UVB contribution ([more on the filter roles is in The Evidence](#the-evidence)), and it is present here at a higher share than in the discontinued SPF 40 formula ([[supergoop-unseen-sunscreen-spf-40]]).[^1][^2] Both sources describe the formula as fragrance-free ("scentless"); Target's listing additionally describes it as non-acnegenic.[^2]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific product was located; the product-specific facts are its filter composition and label claims.

Supergoop's own product page and Target's Drug Facts listing agree on the active ingredients: avobenzone 3%, homosalate 7%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 9%, at Broad Spectrum SPF 50.[^1][^2] SPF is a UVB (sunburn) number; the SPF 50 rating certifies UVB strength and does not by itself certify UVA (photoaging) protection, a separate axis described on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

Three of the four filters, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene, are UVB absorbers; UVA coverage rests on avobenzone alone.[^1][^2] [[avobenzone]] at its 3% US maximum provides genuine UVA coverage once photostabilized, but it degrades under sunlight unless a co-filter holds it stable. [[octocrylene]] serves that stabilizing role here, at 9% of the formula, in addition to contributing its own UVB and short-wave UVA-II absorption;[^1][^2] that is a larger share of octocrylene than the discontinued SPF 40 formula's 4% ([[supergoop-unseen-sunscreen-spf-40]]). Measured against the global field, this remains the older US filter set: avobenzone is a weaker, less photostable UVA filter than the modern organic filters (bemotrizinol/Tinosorb S, bisoctrizole/Tinosorb M, the Mexoryl filters) standard in the EU, Korea, and Australia, a comparison detailed on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

> **Bottom line.** The Broad Spectrum SPF 50 rating reflects a genuine, if not top-tier, US chemical filter set: homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene carry the UVB side, and avobenzone, at its permitted maximum and stabilized by a comparatively high share of octocrylene, carries the UVA side.[^1][^2] That UVA coverage is real but older and less photostable than the modern-filter products sold outside the US.

Target's listing describes the formula as "sweat & water resistant (40 min)";[^2] Supergoop's own product page states the same water-resistance window in different words, directing reapplication "after 40 minutes of swimming or sweating."[^1] How much protection any SPF delivers in practice depends on applying enough product, a point covered generally on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## What's In It

The active filters as declared on the Drug Facts panel, confirmed on both Supergoop's own product page and Target's listing, plus the inactive ingredient list from Supergoop's page.[^1][^2] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

UV filters (Drug Facts actives):

- [[avobenzone]] 3%, the formula's only dedicated UVA filter, at the US permitted maximum
- [[homosalate]] 7%, a UVB filter
- [[octisalate]] 5%, a weak, secondary UVB filter
- [[octocrylene]] 9%, a UVB and short-wave UVA-II filter that also stabilizes avobenzone

Other functional/notable ingredients: shea butter (Butyrospermum Parkii), Physalis Angulata extract, Himanthalia Elongata (seaweed) extract, meadowfoam estolide, tocopherol (vitamin E, an antioxidant), jojoba esters, and bisabolol.[^1]

Base, texture, and preservative system: isododecane, dimethicone/bis-isobutyl PPG-20 crosspolymer, dimethicone/vinyl dimethicone crosspolymer, dimethicone, dimethicone crosspolymer, caprylic/capric triglyceride, polymethylsilsesquioxane, caprylyl glycol, diethylhexyl syringylidenemalonate, silica, and dipotassium glycyrrhizate.[^1]

Not in the formula: no fragrance noted on Supergoop's ingredient listing; Target's listing does not publish an inactive-ingredient list to corroborate this.[^1]
