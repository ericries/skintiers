Allies of Skin Peptides & Antioxidants Advanced Firming Daily Treatment is a leave-on facial moisturizer built around six named signal peptides and a brand-stated antioxidant complex of ferulic acid, a vitamin C ester, and vitamin E, marketed for firmness, brightness, and fine lines.[^1]

> A firming daily cream that layers six peptides onto an antioxidant complex, none disclosed by percentage except a combined "5%" figure naming two of the ingredients. The general evidence for topical peptides, set out on [[peptides]], is a small and mostly hydration/brightness effect, with firmness and elasticity results "inconsistent" in the largest pooled trial review. The antioxidant trio echoes the well-studied vitamin C/E/ferulic acid combination, but at an unstated, lower concentration and with a weaker vitamin C ester than the studied formula. The brand's own 34-subject age-estimation study and headline percentage claims are undisclosed in method, uncontrolled, and unpublished.[^1]

## Summary

This is Allies of Skin's flagship firming moisturizer: a long-ingredient-list cream combining six named peptides (Palmitoyl Tripeptide-5, Palmitoyl Dipeptide-5 Diaminobutyroyl Hydroxythreonine, Palmitoyl Dipeptide-5 Diaminohydroxybutyrate, Palmitoyl Tripeptide-38, Palmitoyl Tripeptide-8, and Acetyl Tetrapeptide-40) with antioxidants including ferulic acid, ascorbyl palmitate (a vitamin C ester), tocopherol, glutathione, astaxanthin, and epigallocatechin gallate.[^1] The general case for topical peptides, modest and mostly cosmetic, drawn largely from small or industry-run trials, is set out on [[peptides]]; none of it isolates the exact peptides used here.

The product's own marketing leans on a brand-run study: an expert estimated the perceived age of 34 recruited subjects from Visia-CR facial images, single-blinded, before and after 28 and 56 days.[^1] Separately, the brand states percentage improvements in firmness, elasticity, moisturization, radiance, tone evenness, smoothness, and wrinkle appearance at 4 weeks, without publishing sample size, control group, or measurement method for those figures.[^1] Neither result is independently published or peer-reviewed; the product is graded here on the general category evidence for its named actives, and the brand's own numbers are discounted below (see [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).

It is listed at $125.00 for the standard 1.6 fl oz size and $165.00 for a 2.5 fl oz value size on the brand's own US site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^2] The same product is also independently listed by UK retailer Cult Beauty, in its own 20ml/48ml/75ml sizing and EUR pricing rather than the US fl oz sizes above, which corroborates the product's existence and formula name outside the brand's own site.[^3]

## The Evidence

**Peptides.** Six named peptides are declared, all signal-type palmitoyl or acetyl peptides similar in class to the best-studied topical peptide, pal-KTTKS, but not the same molecules and not disclosed by percentage.[^1] The pooled clinical picture for topical peptides as a class, including a 2026 systematic review of 19 RCTs (1,341 participants), found a significant improvement in hydration and brightness but "inconsistent" effects on elasticity and density, and a modest pooled wrinkle benefit "largely driven by oral polypeptides" rather than topical formulas like this cream; full detail is on [[peptides]]. No independent trial of this product's specific peptide blend was found.

**Antioxidant complex.** Ferulic acid, ascorbyl palmitate, and tocopherol appear together, echoing the well-known fixed combination of 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% alpha-tocopherol, and 0.5% ferulic acid shown in the studies that defined that formula to roughly double measured photoprotection versus either vitamin alone (full evidence on [[ferulic-acid]]).[^1] This product names ferulic acid within a combined "5% Teprenone & Ferulic Complex" on its label, but does not disclose the individual percentage of ferulic acid, ascorbyl palmitate, or tocopherol.[^1] It also substitutes ascorbyl palmitate, a fat-soluble vitamin C ester with no isolated human efficacy trial of its own, for the L-ascorbic acid the studied formula used (full evidence on [[ascorbyl-palmitate]]).[^1] The parallel to the studied combination is structural, not concentration-matched.

**The brand's own study.** The product page describes a single-blinded, expert facial-age-estimation study of 34 subjects using Visia-CR imaging at 28 and 56 days, plus separately stated percentage changes (12% firmness, 14% elasticity, 43% moisturization, 164% radiance, 25% tone evenness, 61% smoothness, and 29% for crow's feet and fine lines and wrinkles appearance) at an unspecified duration described elsewhere on the page as 4 weeks.[^1] No control or vehicle arm, measurement instrument for the percentage figures, or independent publication is disclosed.

> **Bottom line.** The peptide and antioxidant actives here draw on real but modest, mostly small-trial category evidence that does not test this product's exact blend or concentrations; the brand's own age-estimation study and percentage claims are uncontrolled, method-limited, and unpublished.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Reverse 1 year of aging in 1 month."** Drawn from the brand's own 34-subject, single-blinded expert age-estimation study using Visia-CR imaging; no control group, independent publication, or peer review is disclosed.[^1]
- **Headline clinical percentages (12% firmness, 14% elasticity, 43% moisturization, 164% radiance, 25% tone evenness, 61% smoothness, 29% wrinkle appearance).** Company-reported figures without a disclosed sample size, control or vehicle arm, or measurement method.[^1] The independent, published evidence for the product's peptide and antioxidant actives, summarized above, is more modest and does not test this exact formula.
- **"7 Firming Peptides, 9 Antioxidants, 13 Brighteners."** A counting claim; the pooled independent trial evidence for topical peptides as a class shows a small effect that is "inconsistent" for elasticity and firmness specifically, per [[peptides]].
- **"5% Teprenone & Ferulic Complex" and "3% Biotech Algae Complex" as headline actives.** The individual antioxidant percentages within these combined figures are not disclosed, so the studied 0.5% to 1% concentrations behind ferulic acid's best evidence (on [[ferulic-acid]]) cannot be confirmed against this product.
