Differin Gel is the original branded adapalene 0.1% gel from Galderma, a topical retinoid for acne and the first retinoid to be sold over the counter for acne in the United States.[^dailymed]

> This is a genuinely strong pick, and the reason is the active, not the brand. Differin is adapalene 0.1%, a guideline-recommended, well-evidenced acne retinoid whose case is set out on [[adapalene]] and [[acne]].[^dailymed] What the branded Differin adds over cheaper adapalene generics is essentially the name and packaging: the active, strength, and formulation are the same. Judge it as an excellent, accessible OTC retinoid for acne, and buy on price against identical generics.

## Summary

Differin Gel is regulated as a drug: the label lists the active ingredient as adapalene 0.1% and indicates it for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in patients 12 years of age and older.[^dailymed] Adapalene was first US-approved (by prescription) in 1996 and became the first retinoid available over the counter for acne when the 0.1% gel was approved for OTC sale in 2016, which is the version most people buy as "Differin."[^dailymed]

The evidence for adapalene as a first-line acne treatment, and how it stacks up against prescription tretinoin (similar efficacy, generally better tolerated), lives on [[adapalene]] and the condition-level ranking on [[acne]]; it is not repeated here. This page's job is the product: what Differin is, and how the branded version compares with the now-numerous adapalene generics.

## The Evidence

Two uses, graded above.

**Acne (the labeled use).** This is what Differin is approved and labeled for, and where the evidence is strong. Adapalene 0.1% is a first-line topical retinoid; the full evidence and comparisons are on [[adapalene]] and [[acne]].[^dailymed] Nothing about the branded Differin changes that evidence, because branded Differin and OTC adapalene generics are the same active at the same strength.

**Photoaging (off-label).** Retinoids as a class improve photoaged skin (see [[retinoids]]), and adapalene shares that receptor mechanism, so long-term use may give incidental anti-aging benefit. But Differin is labeled for acne, and the dedicated anti-aging evidence for adapalene is thinner than for tretinoin, so this is a plausible bonus rather than the reason to buy it.

> **Bottom line.** One of the best-value entries in skincare: a first-line, FDA-labeled acne retinoid available without a prescription.[^dailymed] The only real question is brand versus generic. Since branded Differin and store-brand adapalene 0.1% gel are the same drug, choose on price.

## What's In It

As a drug, the label separates the active from the rest. Active ingredient: adapalene 0.1%.[^dailymed] Inactive ingredients, verbatim from the label: "carbomer 940, edetate disodium, methylparaben, poloxamer 124, propylene glycol, purified water and sodium hydroxide," and it may contain hydrochloric acid for pH adjustment.[^dailymed] It is a lightweight, alcohol-free aqueous gel.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Clears breakouts / prevents new acne."** Consistent with adapalene's labeled indication and real evidence for acne (see [[adapalene]]); this is one of the few OTC "acne" products whose active is genuinely first-line.[^dailymed]
- **"Dermatologist-developed retinoid."** Accurate as far as it goes; the active is a proven retinoid, but the branded product is not superior to identical-strength adapalene generics.[^dailymed]
