Round Lab Birch Juice Moisturizing Sun is a South Korean facial sunscreen from [[round-lab]] marketed as a hybrid sunscreen-moisturizer; the current US listing, sold under the name "Birch Juice Moisturizing UV Lock," rates it SPF45+ and PA++++ and lists avobenzone, homosalate, and octisalate as its active UV filters.[^target]

> The US-sold version of this Round Lab sunscreen pairs a solid UVB rating, SPF45+, with UVA protection that rests on avobenzone, the same older-generation organic filter used in many US-market sunscreens, rather than the modern Tinosorb- or Mexoryl-type filters more typical of sunscreens formulated for the Korean domestic market.[^target] Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk; how SPF, PA/UVA, and the filter set fit together is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## Summary

Birch Juice Moisturizing Sun is marketed as a dual sunscreen-and-moisturizer built around birch sap (betula platyphylla japonica juice), paired with [[niacinamide]], [[hyaluronic-acid]] (as sodium hyaluronate and hyaluronic acid), and [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]] in the ingredient list Target carries for the SKU.[^target] Its core job is protective rather than cosmetic: regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing some skin cancers, the case set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

The Target listing for this product names it "Birch Juice Moisturizing UV Lock" rather than "SPF50+," and rates it SPF45+ and PA++++.[^target] Its three declared UV filters, avobenzone 2.7%, homosalate 13.5%, and octisalate 4.5%, [detailed in the evidence](#the-evidence), are the same class of older-generation organic filters (avobenzone for UVA; homosalate and octisalate for UVB) common in US-market sunscreens such as [[la-roche-posay-anthelios-melt-in-milk-spf-60]], rather than the modern Tinosorb- or Mexoryl-family filters more typical of sunscreens sold within Korea.

Target lists a 50 ml bottle at $24.99 ($14.78 per fluid ounce), and markets the birch sap, niacinamide, and hyaluronic acid/sodium hyaluronate as making it "a hydrating sunscreen that does double duty as a moisturizer," [addressed separately under common marketing claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^target]

## The Evidence

Whether regular sunscreen use works, and how, is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. The randomized evidence there is for the practice of applying a broad-spectrum sunscreen. What is specific to this product is its label rating and filter choice as sold in the US.

Target's listing for the 50 ml bottle states "SPF 45+, PA++++" and names the active UV filters as avobenzone (2.7%), homosalate (13.5%), and octisalate (4.5%).[^target] Avobenzone is the workhorse UVA filter in older US sunscreen formulas, giving real long-wave UVA coverage that becomes less reliable without a photostabilizing co-filter, and it trails the modern Tinosorb- and Mexoryl-family filters standard in Korea, the EU, and Australia; see [[avobenzone]]. Homosalate and octisalate are both UVB-only filters, contributing to the SPF45+ rating but nothing to UVA coverage; see [[homosalate]] and [[octisalate]]. [[la-roche-posay-anthelios-melt-in-milk-spf-60]] is built on the same older US filter set (avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate) at a lower homosalate concentration (10% vs. 13.5% here), but adds octocrylene as a fourth filter, which also helps photostabilize its avobenzone.

> **Bottom line.** As sold in the US, this Round Lab sunscreen carries a solid UVB (sunburn) rating, SPF45+, built on homosalate and octisalate, and UVA (photoaging) protection that rests on avobenzone, the older US UVA filter, short of the modern-filter sunscreens sold within Korea, the EU, and Australia.[^target] How SPF, PA/UVA, and the global filter landscape fit together is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].
