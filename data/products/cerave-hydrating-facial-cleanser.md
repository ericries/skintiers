CeraVe Hydrating Facial Cleanser is a fragrance-free, non-foaming cream cleanser for normal to dry skin whose ingredient list includes glycerin, three ceramides (ceramide NP, AP, EOP), and hyaluronic acid as sodium hyaluronate.[^1]

> A gentle cleanser's health-relevant job is to lift dirt, oil, and makeup without stripping the skin barrier. No published trial isolates this cleanser. The closest evidence, Spada 2021, tested a ceramide wash used together with a ceramide cream (a different brand, funded by its maker): the regimen improved a barrier measure (TEWL, P=.0342) but reduced eczema no more than a plain vehicle (EASI, P=.7804).[^2] The broader case for keeping barrier-impaired skin moisturized is strong and is set out on [[moisturizing]].

## Summary

CeraVe Hydrating Facial Cleanser is a fragrance-free, non-foaming cream cleanser aimed at normal to dry skin. It is designed to wash the face without the tight, stripped feeling that higher-foaming or higher-pH cleansers can leave, and it carries barrier ingredients: three ceramides, hyaluronic acid, and glycerin, confirmed [in the ingredient list](#whats-in-it).[^1]

What separates it from a plain gentle cleanser is that added ceramide, hyaluronic acid, and glycerin package rather than the surfactants that do the cleansing. Whether a rinse-off product deposits enough of those ingredients to matter is not something a published trial has [tested for this cleanser](#the-evidence).

Cleansers are rarely put through their own efficacy trials, and none isolates this one. The nearest evidence is an [industry-funded regimen study of a different ceramide wash used with a ceramide cream](#the-evidence), which improved a skin-barrier measure but did not reduce eczema more than a plain vehicle. The well-evidenced case is for leave-on moisturizing of barrier-impaired skin, one of the best-supported interventions in dermatology for eczema; see [[moisturizing]].

On the health-relevant question, it is a low-surfactant, fragrance-free wash that [contains no sulfate detergents and no added fragrance](#whats-in-it), the ingredients most often tied to cleanser irritation. It is not a treatment: it delivers no active drug, and its "hydrates and helps restore the barrier" positioning is a [manufacturer claim](#common-marketing-claims) that the product-specific evidence does not establish.

It is inexpensive. The manufacturer lists it at $15.99 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-07-28), and it is sold in 8, 12, 16, and 19 fl oz sizes.[^1][^3]

## The Evidence

No published trial isolates the CeraVe Hydrating Facial Cleanser. The closest adjacent evidence tests a ceramide-containing wash as part of a regimen rather than this product on its own.

Spada 2021 was a randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trial (n=100, 28 days) in adults with moderate eczema. It tested a regimen of a "QV intensive with ceramides light moisturizing cream and QV intensive with ceramides hydrating body wash" against vehicle, funded by Ego Pharmaceuticals, the products' maker. The active group "had significantly greater improvements in TEWL at all-time points compared to placebo" (P=.0342). For eczema severity, the between-group "change was not significantly different between groups" (EASI, P=.7804).[^2]

> **Bottom line.** A ceramide wash used together with a ceramide cream improved a skin-barrier measurement, but it reduced eczema no more than a plain vehicle. It tested a two-product regimen from one manufacturer, not this cleanser, and was funded by the products' maker.[^2]

Because it tested a body wash inside a two-product regimen, the study cannot separate the wash from the cream or stand in for a facial cleanser used on its own.

The general question, whether keeping barrier-impaired skin moisturized helps, is answered elsewhere and affirmatively: moisturizing reduces eczema flares and spares topical corticosteroids, and is one of the best-evidenced interventions in dermatology for barrier-impaired skin; see [[moisturizing]] and the underlying Cochrane review [[van-zuuren-2017-emollients-moisturisers-eczema]]. That evidence is for leave-on moisturizers, not rinse-off cleansers.

## What's In It

The ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the cleansing surfactants, texture, and preservative system.

Barrier and hydration ingredients:

- [[glycerin]], a humectant and the second ingredient
- three [[ceramides]] (ceramide NP, ceramide AP, ceramide EOP), lipids that match the skin's own barrier lipids
- sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]), a humectant
- [[cholesterol]] and [[phytosphingosine]], additional barrier lipids
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant

Cleansing, texture, and preservative system: water, cetearyl alcohol, stearyl alcohol and cetyl alcohol (emollient fatty alcohols and emulsifiers), PEG-40 stearate, glyceryl stearate and polysorbate 20 (nonionic surfactants), sodium lauroyl lactylate and behentrimonium methosulfate (a mild surfactant and a conditioning emulsifier), carbomer and xanthan gum (thickeners), potassium phosphate and dipotassium phosphate (pH buffers), disodium EDTA (chelator), and phenoxyethanol with ethylhexylglycerin (preservatives).[^1]

Three composition facts matter for a cleanser. It is fragrance-free, with no fragrance or parfum in the list. It is non-foaming and built on mild nonionic surfactants, with no sulfate detergents such as sodium lauryl or laureth sulfate. And it contains no [[niacinamide]], which the separate CeraVe Foaming Facial Cleanser does.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the evidence above:

- **"Cleanses, hydrates and helps restore the protective skin barrier."** A label claim.[^1] No trial isolates this cleanser; the closest study, Spada 2021, tested a ceramide wash plus cream and found a barrier (TEWL) benefit but no eczema-severity advantage over vehicle.[^2]
- **"With 3 essential ceramides and hyaluronic acid," and ceramides that "restore your skin's natural barrier."** The three ceramides and sodium hyaluronate appear in the ingredient list.[^1] Their listed presence in a rinse-off product is a composition fact, not a measured clinical outcome.
- **Non-comedogenic ("Won't clog pores") and "Developed with dermatologists."** A label claim and a brand positioning statement, not independently tested outcomes.[^1]
