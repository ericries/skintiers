Watermelon Glow Niacinamide Dew Drops is Glow Recipe's leave-on dewy serum built around 2% [[niacinamide]], sold in 15 ml, 40 ml, and jumbo sizes through the brand's own site.[^glowrecipe]

> A widely sold niacinamide serum at a concentration that falls within the low end of what topical niacinamide has actually been studied at, a case set out on [[niacinamide]]. No trial tests this exact formula; the manufacturer's headline pore, glow, and brightness figures come from an unpublished 30-subject study with no control arm or peer review disclosed.[^glowrecipe] It is listed at $36 for 40 ml, $20 for 15 ml, and $49 for a jumbo size directly from the manufacturer.[^glowrecipe]

## Summary

Watermelon Glow Niacinamide Dew Drops pairs 2% [[niacinamide]], a form of vitamin B3, with watermelon fruit extract (Citrullus Lanatus Fruit Extract), sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]), neem leaf extract (Melia Azadirachta Leaf Extract), and moringa seed oil in a lightweight gel serum, all declared on the manufacturer's own product page.[^glowrecipe] The general case for topical niacinamide, modest but real evidence for supporting the skin barrier, lightening facial hyperpigmentation, and mild inflammatory acne, drawn mostly from small or manufacturer-run trials, is set out on [[niacinamide]]. Unlike some competing serums, this product's 2% dose sits within the concentration range niacinamide has actually been tested at rather than above it.

The manufacturer describes the serum as "clinically proven to hydrate, brighten, smooth skin texture, and improve the look of pores over time," and markets it as usable as both a daily AM/PM serum and, layered under or mixed into makeup, as a dewy primer.[^glowrecipe] No published trial isolates this product or its exact formula; the manufacturer's own supporting figures, discussed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims), come from an unpublished 30-subject study.[^glowrecipe] It is listed at $36 for 40 ml, $20 for 15 ml, and $49 for a jumbo size on the manufacturer's own site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-23).[^glowrecipe]

## The Evidence

Topical niacinamide is a well-tolerated active with modest evidence for supporting the skin barrier, lightening facial hyperpigmentation, and mild inflammatory acne; how each use grades out, and how much of that evidence rests on small or manufacturer-run trials, is set out in full on [[niacinamide]]. Nothing specific to this product was found: no published trial tests Glow Recipe's watermelon-and-niacinamide formula. Its 2% niacinamide concentration falls within the roughly 2-5% range that [[niacinamide]] describes as where topical niacinamide is most studied, though it sits below the 4-5% used in the specific hyperpigmentation and acne trials summarized there.

The manufacturer's declared ingredient list runs water, propanediol, glycereth-26, glycerin, then niacinamide fifth, with niacinamide the only named percentage-labeled active; watermelon extract, sodium hyaluronate, and neem leaf extract appear further down the list, and no other named treatment active is paired with it.[^glowrecipe]

> Bottom line. Niacinamide at 2% carries the same modest, mostly small-trial case for barrier support, hyperpigmentation, and mild acne set out on [[niacinamide]], within the low end of the concentration range that evidence covers, though none of it tested this exact formula.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Clinically proven to hydrate, brighten, smooth skin texture, and improve the look of pores over time."** The manufacturer attributes its "After 4 Weeks" figure of 100% of subjects showing reduced pore size, and "After 2 Weeks" figures of 97% agreeing skin looked more glowy, 94% agreeing skin looked brighter, and 94% agreeing skin looked and felt more hydrated, to "a clinical study of 30 subjects," with no published methodology, control or vehicle arm, statistical result, or peer review disclosed.[^glowrecipe]
- **"Sephora's #1 Niacinamide Serum."** The manufacturer discloses this is "based on third party data for top ranking face serums at Sephora in 2025," a sales-ranking claim, not a clinical-efficacy claim.[^glowrecipe]
- **"Ophthalmologist-Tested."** A safety-testing claim from the manufacturer; no underlying report or methodology is disclosed on the product page.[^glowrecipe]
- **Award mentions (GQ, Allure, Marie Claire).** The manufacturer's product description lists these as awards received; each award was not independently confirmed against the naming publication.[^glowrecipe]
