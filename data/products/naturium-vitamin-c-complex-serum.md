Naturium Vitamin C Complex Serum is a leave-on serum built around a two-form vitamin C blend, L-ascorbic acid paired with the more stable derivative sodium ascorbyl phosphate, alongside glutathione and a blend of fruit extracts the brand calls a "bioactive fruit blend."[^naturium]

> A budget dual-form vitamin C serum. The declared ingredient list places sodium ascorbyl phosphate fourth, ahead of ascorbic acid at fifth, so the more stable derivative outweighs the reference form of vitamin C by concentration, and neither is dosed on the label.[^naturium][^target] That matters because the general antioxidant and collagen evidence for vitamin C is built almost entirely on L-ascorbic acid, and the phosphate derivatives convert to it slowly and absorb poorly through skin (see [[vitamin-c]]). The manufacturer's own "clinically proven" wrinkle claim is attributed to an 8-week clinical study, not cited to any published journal, with no sample size, control arm, statistical result, or peer review disclosed.[^naturium] At around $21 for 30 ml from the manufacturer, and discounted to $14.69 at Target, it is inexpensive relative to single-form, higher-dose L-ascorbic acid serums.[^naturium][^target]

## Summary

Vitamin C Complex Serum is a mass-market vitamin C serum from Naturium, sold through Target and the brand's own site. Its headline active is vitamin C, but the formula uses two forms of it together: [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]], the reference form with the strongest controlled human evidence, and sodium ascorbyl phosphate (SAP), a more stable but more slowly converting derivative. On the manufacturer's declared ingredient list, sodium ascorbyl phosphate is fourth, ahead of ascorbic acid at fifth, so by INCI convention the derivative is present at a higher concentration than the pure acid.[^naturium] A retailer listing for the same product confirms the same ingredient order.[^target] The general case for topical vitamin C, an antioxidant that supports collagen synthesis and helps blunt UV-driven oxidative damage, is mapped on [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]]; that evidence base is built almost entirely on L-ascorbic acid, and the wider derivative family, including sodium ascorbyl phosphate's slow, poorly absorbed conversion back to ascorbic acid, is covered on [[vitamin-c]].

Neither vitamin C form carries a disclosed percentage on this product, unlike single-form, higher-dose serums such as [[la-roche-posay-pure-vitamin-c-12-serum|La Roche-Posay's 12% L-ascorbic acid serum]]. The formula also includes glutathione, tocopheryl acetate ([[tocopherol|vitamin E]] ester), sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]), and a "bioactive fruit blend" of pineapple, papaya, mango, Kakadu plum, and two other fruit extracts, none dosed or independently tested as isolated actives in this formula.[^naturium] The manufacturer's benefit claims include being "clinically proven" to improve the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles within 4 weeks; that claim is attributed to an 8-week clinical study not cited to any published journal, with no sample size, control arm, statistical result, or peer review disclosed, detailed in [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^naturium] It is listed at $21 for 30 ml on the manufacturer's site and $14.69 (discounted from $16.89) for the same size at Target (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^naturium][^target]

## The Evidence

Topical L-ascorbic acid is a well-studied antioxidant with characterized roles in collagen synthesis and defense against UV-driven oxidative damage; that evidence is graded in full on [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]]. What is specific to this product is that L-ascorbic acid is not the dominant vitamin C form in the formula.

The manufacturer's declared ingredient list runs water, glycerin, propanediol, sodium ascorbyl phosphate, ascorbic acid, glutathione, then the named fruit extracts, with neither vitamin C form's concentration disclosed.[^naturium] A Target listing for the same SKU records an identical ingredient order, independently confirming that sodium ascorbyl phosphate precedes ascorbic acid on the list.[^target] The vitamin C derivative family page reports that phosphate-salt derivatives like sodium ascorbyl phosphate are converted to ascorbic acid slowly and absorb poorly through skin (see [[vitamin-c]]). No product-specific trial of this formula's antioxidant or collagen effects was found.

> Bottom line. The formula pairs L-ascorbic acid with a phosphate derivative that, per the general vitamin C evidence, converts back to the active form slowly and absorbs poorly, and the derivative is the more concentrated of the two by ingredient-list order. That weighs against the antioxidant and collagen case built on L-ascorbic acid alone (see [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]]).

The manufacturer's "clinically proven" fine-line and wrinkle claim is attributed to "an 8-week clinical study on subjects representing all skin types and self-perceived sensitive skin."[^naturium] No sample size, control or vehicle arm, statistical results, or peer review are described on the product page.

> Bottom line. The wrinkle claim rests on a manufacturer-cited study, not cited to any published journal, with no sample size, control arm, statistical result, or peer review disclosed, so it is not an independently verifiable trial.[^naturium]

## What's In It

Actives the manufacturer names as key ingredients:[^naturium]

- [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|Ascorbic acid]] (L-ascorbic acid), concentration not disclosed, fifth on the ingredient declaration
- Sodium ascorbyl phosphate, a stabilized vitamin C derivative (see [[vitamin-c]]), concentration not disclosed, fourth on the ingredient declaration, ahead of ascorbic acid
- Glutathione, an antioxidant, concentration not disclosed
- Terminalia Ferdinandiana (Kakadu plum) fruit extract and a blend of pineapple, papaya, and mango fruit extracts, described by the manufacturer as a "bioactive fruit blend"
- Tocopheryl acetate ([[tocopherol|vitamin E]] ester)
- Sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]])

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, glycerin, propanediol, carbomer, phenoxyethanol, caprylyl glycol, citric acid, hydroxyethylcellulose, sodium hydroxide, beta-glucan, potassium sorbate, hexylene glycol, sorbitol, xanthan gum, algin, benzoic acid, sorbic acid, 1,2-hexanediol, sodium benzoate, disodium phosphate, gold, polysorbate 60, and sodium phosphate.[^naturium]

The formula lists no fragrance/parfum ingredient, consistent with the manufacturer's "fragrance free" label claim.[^naturium] A retailer listing of the same product independently confirms the ingredient declaration, including the sodium ascorbyl phosphate/ascorbic acid order.[^target]
