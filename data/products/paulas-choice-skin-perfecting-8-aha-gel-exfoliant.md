Paula's Choice Skin Perfecting 8% AHA Gel Exfoliant is the alpha-hydroxy-acid (AHA) leave-on companion to the brand's better-known [[paulas-choice-skin-perfecting-2-bha-liquid-exfoliant|2% BHA Liquid Exfoliant]]: a glycolic acid gel you leave on rather than rinse. The AHA (glycolic acid) works mainly on surface texture and sun-related dullness, where the BHA (salicylic acid) is oil-soluble and gets into clogged, oily pores. Dermstore, an authorized Paula's Choice retailer, lists the 3.3 fl oz size at a recommended retail price of $34.00.[^dermstore]

> The active here is [[glycolic-acid|glycolic acid]] at 8%, a well-studied AHA, so the case for smoother texture and gradual photodamage improvement is modest and set out on its ingredient page, resting on mixed-quality evidence; gains in uneven tone are weaker. Two things to hold onto: this is an over-the-counter leave-on, so the effect is real but gradual rather than peel-level, and glycolic acid makes skin more sun-sensitive, so daily [[sunscreen-uv-filters|sunscreen]] is not optional with it. The AHA suits rough or dull skin and sun-related texture, where the [[paulas-choice-skin-perfecting-2-bha-liquid-exfoliant|2% BHA]] suits clogged, oily pores.

## Summary

Paula's Choice describes this as a "non-abrasive, leave-on exfoliant powered by glycolic acid" that "gently and naturally helps shed built-up layers of dead skin revealing smoother, firmer and younger-looking skin," with green tea and chamomile to soothe and hyaluronic acid to help retain moisture.[^dermstore] It is a fragrance-free gel applied after cleansing and left on, not rinsed.

The formula leads with water, then glycolic acid (the 8% AHA active) and sodium hydroxide (which buffers the acid to a usable pH), followed by chamomile and aloe, green tea leaf extract, sodium hyaluronate, panthenol, and sodium PCA as soothing and humectant support. It is listed at a $34.00 recommended retail price for the 3.3 fl oz size at Dermstore ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-25).[^dermstore]

## The Evidence

The product-specific question is whether an 8% glycolic acid leave-on (the concentration and formula per the retailer listing)[^dermstore] does what an AHA is supposed to do. Glycolic acid is a well-studied AHA, but its evidence at leave-on concentrations is modest and mixed in quality: the best-supported effects are smoother texture and improvement in the look of photodamage, while gains in uneven tone are weaker, and the closest controlled trial tested an 8% glycolic cream, all set out on [[glycolic-acid]]. No independently published trial of this exact product is cited here; its case rests on the ingredient. The effect is graded modest rather than large because it is an over-the-counter leave-on strength, well short of a professional glycolic peel, and results build over weeks.

The one non-negotiable caveat is on the label itself: as Paula's Choice states, this "contains 8% glycolic acid, an ingredient that may increase your skin's sensitivity to the sun and the possibility of sunburn," so daily broad-spectrum sunscreen is required while using it.[^dermstore] The green tea, chamomile, aloe, and hyaluronic acid are supporting soothing and humectant ingredients, not the reason the product works.

> **Bottom line.** A well-formulated, fragrance-free glycolic acid leave-on whose value is real but gradual, for texture and sun-related dullness rather than clogged pores. It leans on modest, mixed-quality ingredient-level AHA evidence, not a product-specific trial; it suits rough or dull skin where the [[paulas-choice-skin-perfecting-2-bha-liquid-exfoliant|BHA version]] suits clogged, oily pores, and daily sunscreen is part of using it.
