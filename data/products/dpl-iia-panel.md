The successor to [[dpl-ii-panel]], cleared a year later with a blue LED added and an acne claim attached. **Bottom line:** the indication grew and the disclosure shrank. Its predecessor's file printed an irradiance figure; this one prints the unit and no number. Whether LED does anything is on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

> **Same firm, same panel, one year apart, and the figure is gone.** The dpl II Panel's predicate chart carried an Energy Level row reading 70.16 mW/cm2.[^k171390] This filing's comparison chart has no Energy Level row at all, and its technological-characteristics list states only that the device "Has the same/similar output (mW/cm²) as predicate devices".[^k180320] Checked by string search of the full Summary, it contains no mW/cm2 value, no J/cm figure and no irradiance number anywhere; the word irradiance appears once, as the chart label "Irradiance source", answered "LED".[^k180320] That is the move [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]] calls rung 2: name the unit inside an equivalence claim and attach no figure.

## Summary

Cleared 2018-04-03 as K180320, applicant LED Technologies, Inc., product code OHS under 21 CFR 878.4810, a Traditional 510(k) published as a Summary.[^openfda] The cleared indication, quoted in full from the Indications for Use form: "The dpl® IIa Panel is an Over-the Counter (OTC) device intended for use in treating wrinkles, and treatment of mild to moderate inflammatory acne."[^k180320]

**What changed from the II Panel.** A fifth LED. The file describes "five types of LEDs: 605nm amber, 630nm red, 660nm red, and 880nm infrared, and 415nm blue", where its predecessor had four and no blue.[^k180320] Treatment area and time are unchanged at "415 cm²" and "3 minutes per treatment daily".[^k180320] Everything else in the description is the predecessor's language: no user settings, applied directly to the skin, panel plus power supply, goggles and a storage case.[^k180320]

**Four predicates, three of them other companies' handhelds.** Equivalence is claimed against its own dpl II Panel plus the Tanda Mini (K124042), the Silk'n Blue (K121435) and the Zuco Acne Light Therapy Wand (K160691).[^k180320] Silk'n is covered at [[silkn]].

**No price is quoted here.** No brand storefront page was fetched and no retailer was consulted.

## The Rubric

**Wrinkles (cosmetic)**
Graded modest on mixed evidence, inherited from red and infrared light as a modality rather than earned here, for the same reason as every LED page on this site: the file contains no clinical study. Checked by string search, it has no occurrence of randomi, placebo or sham, and no efficacy endpoint.[^k180320] The modality case is on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

**Mild to moderate inflammatory acne (health)**
Graded modest on mixed evidence, inherited from blue light, which has the better of the colour evidence as ranked on [[led-colours-for-acne-by-evidence]]. The 415 nm output sits in the range those trials used. What the chart shows is worth carrying into any purchase: the three acne predicates have treatment areas of "3.37 cm²", "7 cm²" and "1.594 cm²" against this panel's "415 cm²".[^k180320] A whole-face panel is arguing equivalence to spot treatments more than a hundred times smaller, and the file offers no measurement reconciling the two.

**Anything this device itself demonstrated**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. "A Usability Study was conducted with 16 participants", with "100% of the participants were able to demonstrate the light sensitivity test." and "100% of the participants were able to use the device successfully."[^k180320] Those are operability results. The file's own framing is that "The conclusions drawn from nonclinical tests demonstrate that the device is substantially equivalent to the legally marketed predicate devices."[^k180320]

## The Evidence

**The claim got broader as the file got thinner.** The II Panel was cleared for wrinkles alone and published its output; the IIa adds an acne indication and publishes none. Nothing improper follows from that, since a Summary is not required to state a dose, as [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]] sets out from the regulation. It is simply worth seeing: a reader comparing the two files learns less about the newer product.

**What was tested.** Functional performance testing, software validation and user safety testing, against AAMI/ANSI/ES 60601-1 (2012), IEC 60601-1-2 Edition 4 and ISO 10993-10:2010 for irritation and delayed-type hypersensitivity.[^k180320] A competent safety package that measures nothing about skin.

**The conclusion sentence is narrower than its predecessor's.** This file concludes that nonclinical tests demonstrate substantial equivalence; the II Panel's concluded that they demonstrate the device "is safe, as effective, and performs as well as the legally marketed devices".[^k180320][^k171390] Equivalence is the claim the pathway actually supports, so the newer wording is the more accurate of the two.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. The system ships with goggles and the one human study checked whether people could complete a light sensitivity test, so eye protection is part of using it as cleared. Acne that scars or does not respond is worth a dermatologist rather than another device; see [[acne]].

## Common Marketing Claims

**No brand marketing copy is quoted here.** No storefront page was fetched, so the only claims assessed are the regulated ones in the FDA file.

**Both halves of the indication carry limits that retail copy tends to drop.** The acne wording is "mild to moderate inflammatory acne", not acne generally, and the wrinkle wording carries no site restriction.[^k180320] The gap between a cleared indication and the marketing around it is the subject of [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]].
