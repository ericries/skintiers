Cabtreo is the brand name for a prescription fixed triple-combination topical gel of clindamycin phosphate 1.2%, adapalene 0.15%, and benzoyl peroxide 3.1%, made by Bausch Health and marketed under its Ortho Dermatologics brand. It was FDA-approved in 2023 as the first fixed triple-combination acne gel, "indicated for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in adult and pediatric patients 12 years of age and older."[^dailymed]

> The genuine innovation here is packaging, not chemistry: three already-proven acne actives, an antibiotic, a retinoid, and benzoyl peroxide, in one tube instead of two or three separate products. That is a real adherence advantage for patients who currently juggle multiple applications. Cabtreo did clearly beat an inert vehicle in both of its registration trials. But the label contains no trial comparing it against a cheaper two-ingredient combination, such as adapalene plus benzoyl peroxide, paired with a separate clindamycin product, so there is no evidence here that the triple combination outperforms dual therapy; it is a convenience product built from familiar parts. The general actives are owned by [[clindamycin-topical]], [[adapalene]], and [[benzoyl-peroxide]]; the broader condition is [[acne]].

## Summary

Cabtreo combines three well-established acne actives in a single gel: [[clindamycin-topical|clindamycin]] as clindamycin phosphate, the topical retinoid [[adapalene]], and [[benzoyl-peroxide]]. Each gram contains 12 mg (1.2%) clindamycin phosphate, 1.5 mg (0.15%) adapalene, and 31 mg (3.1%) benzoyl peroxide.[^dailymed] The label's exact indication, quoted above, sets an age floor of 12; the trials that supported approval enrolled some subjects as young as 10, but the label states plainly that "CABTREO is not approved for use in patients less than 12 years of age."[^dailymed] It is applied once daily, a thin layer to the affected area after gentle cleansing, avoiding the eyes, lips, and mucous membranes, and it is for topical use only.[^dailymed] It is supplied in 20 g and 50 g tubes and pumps, must be refrigerated before it is dispensed to the patient, and carries a 10-week expiration date once dispensed.[^dailymed] It is prescription-only.

The rationale for combining these three actives, an antibiotic, a retinoid, and an oxidizing agent that helps limit antibiotic resistance, is owned by [[clindamycin-topical]], [[adapalene]], and [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and is not re-derived here. The product-specific case rests on two randomized 12-week vehicle-controlled trials, described in full under [The Evidence](#the-evidence). In both trials Cabtreo beat vehicle on every co-primary endpoint, but the label does not include any trial comparing it against a cheaper dual combination, so no claim that it beats a two-ingredient product plus a separate clindamycin is supported.[^dailymed]

The most common adverse reactions were application site pain (13.6% vs 0.8% on vehicle), erythema (4.5% vs 0%), and dryness (4.1% vs 0.8%); about 2.5% of Cabtreo subjects discontinued because of local skin reactions.[^dailymed] This is a prescription drug; see a prescriber for use and dosing, and see [[acne]] for the broader condition context. Cabtreo is prescription-only and no official list price was found on a durable primary source; per house policy aggregator price-comparison sites are not cited, so no price is stated here.

## The Evidence

The general case for topical clindamycin as an acne antibiotic, for adapalene as a topical retinoid, and for benzoyl peroxide's role in limiting antibiotic resistance is covered on [[clindamycin-topical]], [[adapalene]], and [[benzoyl-peroxide]]; this section covers only what the Cabtreo label itself reports for this product.

Two multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials (Trial 1, NCT04214639, and Trial 2, NCT04214652) enrolled a combined 363 adult and pediatric subjects age 10 and older with moderate to severe facial acne vulgaris, defined at baseline as an Evaluator's Global Severity Score (EGSS) of moderate (3) or severe (4), 30 to 100 inflammatory lesions, 35 to 150 non-inflammatory lesions, and two or fewer facial nodules.[^dailymed] Subjects were randomized 2:1 to Cabtreo or vehicle applied once daily for 12 weeks. The co-primary endpoints, assessed at Week 12, were EGSS success (at least a 2-grade improvement from baseline and a score of clear or almost clear), absolute change in inflammatory lesion count, and absolute change in non-inflammatory lesion count.[^dailymed]

Efficacy results, as reported in the label's Table 3:

| | Trial 1 (Cabtreo N=122 vs vehicle N=61) | Trial 2 (Cabtreo N=120 vs vehicle N=60) |
|---|---|---|
| EGSS success (clear/almost clear, 2-grade improvement)[^dailymed] | 49.6% vs 24.9% | 50.5% vs 20.5% |
| Difference from vehicle (95% CI) | 24.7 (10.7, 38.7) | 30.0 (16.4, 43.6) |
| Inflammatory lesions, mean absolute reduction | 27.7 vs 21.7 | 30.1 vs 20.8 |
| Difference from vehicle (95% CI) | 5.9 (3.1, 8.7) | 9.3 (6.2, 12.4) |
| Non-inflammatory lesions, mean absolute reduction | 35.4 vs 23.5 | 35.2 vs 22.0 |
| Difference from vehicle (95% CI) | 11.9 (7.1, 16.6) | 13.3 (8.8, 17.7) |

Every confidence interval excluded zero in both trials, so Cabtreo clearly beat an inert vehicle gel on all three co-primary endpoints. That is a real, well-supported effect. What the label does not contain is any trial arm comparing Cabtreo against a cheaper two-active combination, for instance adapalene plus benzoyl peroxide with a separate clindamycin product applied alongside it, so the trials cannot support a claim that the triple combination works better than dual therapy; they only establish that it works better than nothing.[^dailymed]

> **Bottom line.** Cabtreo clearly beat vehicle on EGSS success and on both lesion counts in two well-designed trials, a real effect. The honest open question, which the label does not answer, is whether packaging three proven actives into one tube meaningfully outperforms a cheaper dual combination plus a separate antibiotic, since no head-to-head trial against dual therapy exists. The clearest advantage demonstrated here is convenience and likely better adherence from one application instead of two or three.[^dailymed]

## What's In It

The active ingredients as declared on the FDA-approved prescribing information.[^dailymed]

Active, per gram:

- [[clindamycin-topical|Clindamycin phosphate]] 12 mg (1.2%), equivalent to 10 mg (1%) clindamycin.[^dailymed]
- [[adapalene|Adapalene]] 1.5 mg (0.15%).[^dailymed]
- [[benzoyl-peroxide|Benzoyl peroxide]] 31 mg (3.1%).[^dailymed]

The label warns that Cabtreo may increase photosensitivity, so patients should minimize sun exposure and use sunscreen; that it may bleach hair or colored fabric because of its benzoyl peroxide component; and that it is contraindicated in anyone with a history of regional enteritis, ulcerative colitis, or antibiotic-associated colitis because of its clindamycin component.[^dailymed]

## Where to Buy

Prescription only; obtain through a dermatologist or prescriber. Cabtreo is supplied in 20 g and 50 g tubes and 20 g and 50 g pumps.[^dailymed] No official WAC or list price was found on a durable primary source, and per house policy aggregator price-comparison sites are not cited, so no price is stated here.

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand marketing page was reviewed for this product; the claims below come from the FDA label's own characterization rather than promotional copy.

- **"The first FDA-approved fixed triple-combination acne gel."** Accurate as an approval-history fact; Cabtreo was approved in 2023 combining an antibiotic, a retinoid, and benzoyl peroxide in one formulation.[^dailymed]
- **Framing the triple combination as a superior upgrade to standard two-ingredient acne combinations.** Not supported by the label. The registration trials only compared Cabtreo to vehicle, not to a dual combination like adapalene plus benzoyl peroxide with separate clindamycin, so no head-to-head superiority claim is justified.[^dailymed]
