La Roche-Posay Lipikar Balm AP+M, sold in the US as "Lipikar AP+M Triple Repair Body Moisturizing Cream (formerly Lipikar Balm AP+)," is a fragrance-free face-and-body moisturizer labeled with niacinamide, shea butter, and Vitreoscilla filiformis extract for very dry, eczema-prone, itch-prone skin.[^target][^lrpme]

> Niacinamide's barrier-lipid and water-loss benefits are established at the category level, see [[niacinamide]], and this balm's US-listed formula adds a single, low-positioned ceramide, ceramide NP, alongside it, see [[ceramides]]. The brand's own eczema-relapse and itch-reduction percentages are its own marketing figures, not from an independently reviewed trial, and are [quarantined below](#the-evidence). La Roche-Posay's own Middle East product page for the same-named line lists no ceramide at all, a composition difference between the two ingredient panels fetched for this page.[^target][^lrpme]

## Summary

Target lists this balm as "clinically shown to help reduce dry, rough skin," providing "long-lasting, 48-hour hydration," and formulated to "restore the skin's protective barrier," and states it is suitable for use as a facial moisturizer, body cream, or hand lotion, for babies two weeks and up, and for "patients undergoing chemotherapy and radiation."[^target] It carries the National Eczema Association's Seal of Acceptance and is labeled dermatologist- and pediatrician-tested, paraben-free, fragrance-free, and non-comedogenic.[^target] It is sold at 13.5 fl oz, among other sizes shown on the same listing (75 mL, 200 mL, 400 mL).[^target]

The [declared ingredient panel](#whats-in-it) leads water, shea butter, glycerin, and dimethicone, with niacinamide fifth and a single named ceramide, ceramide NP, thirteenth or fourteenth of roughly thirty ingredients.[^target] La Roche-Posay's own Middle East product page for "Lipikar Baume AP+M" calls out niacinamide, shea butter, and "Aqua Posae Filiformis" (declared as vitreoscilla ferment) as its three key ingredients, and its own full ingredient list contains no ceramide at all.[^lrpme] Both panels list vitreoscilla ferment near the end of the ingredient order, consistent with a low concentration.[^target][^lrpme] By comparison, [[cerave-moisturizing-cream]] lists three ceramides (NP, AP, EOP), where this balm's US panel has one and its Middle East panel has none.

Niacinamide's barrier-lipid and hydration effects are supported at the category level, covered on [[niacinamide]]. The evidence for Vitreoscilla filiformis extract, the ingredient La Roche-Posay brands "Aqua Posae Filiformis" and promotes as microbiome science, rests on one small, manufacturer-run trial and cell-culture mechanism work, covered on [[vitreoscilla-filiformis-lysate]]. The brand's own clinical figures on relapse prevention and itch reduction are reported and quarantined below rather than treated as independently verified results.

## The Evidence

No independent study of this specific balm was located. Topical niacinamide raising ceramide and free-fatty-acid synthesis and lowering water loss in dry skin is established at the category level, see [[niacinamide]]; this balm's US-listed panel places niacinamide fifth, ahead of a single named ceramide, ceramide NP, which sits much lower in the list with no stated concentration.[^target] Vitreoscilla filiformis extract's clinical case rests on a single double-blind trial of 13 atopic-dermatitis patients run at L'Oreal's own research facility, backed by cell-culture and tissue-explant mechanism work from the same lab, see [[vitreoscilla-filiformis-lysate]].

> **Bottom line.** The balm's case rests on the general evidence for niacinamide as a barrier-lipid ingredient and for Vitreoscilla filiformis extract, all of which comes from manufacturer-run or manufacturer-authored work.

## What's In It

Two ingredient panels for this product line were fetched, and they differ on one point: whether a ceramide is present.

Target's listing shows the declared ingredient list (INCI) from the label images for this product line, both including a ceramide: water, shea butter (Butyrospermum parkii butter), glycerin, dimethicone, niacinamide, cetearyl alcohol, rapeseed seed oil, glyceryl stearate, ammonium polyacryloyldimethyl taurate, PEG-100 stearate, propanediol, ophiopogon japonicus root extract, ceramide NP, sorbitan tristearate, dimethiconol, PEG-20 methyl glucose sesquistearate, sodium chloride, mannose, coco-betaine, vitreoscilla ferment, citric acid, maltodextrin, xanthan gum, tocopherol, and pentaerythrityl tetra-di-t-butyl hydroxyhydrocinnamate, with the two label photos differing slightly in the preservative and chelator system (one lists sodium benzoate and pea extract; the other lists disodium EDTA, capryloyl glycine, and caprylyl glycol).[^target]

La Roche-Posay's own Middle East product page for "Lipikar Baume AP+M" declares: water, shea butter, glycerin, dimethicone, niacinamide, cetearyl alcohol, rapeseed seed oil, glyceryl stearate, ammonium polyacryloyldimethyl taurate, PEG-100 stearate, propanediol, ophiopogon japonicus root extract, PEG-20 methyl glucose sesquistearate, sorbitan tristearate, dimethiconol, sodium chloride, mannose, coco-betaine, disodium EDTA, caprylyl glycine, caprylyl glycol, vitreoscilla ferment, citric acid, maltodextrin, xanthan gum, tocopherol, and pentaerythrityl tetra-di-t-butyl hydroxyhydrocinnamate. No ceramide appears anywhere in this list.[^lrpme]

Barrier and hydration ingredients common to both panels:

- [[glycerin]], a humectant, third in both declared lists
- [[niacinamide]], fifth in both declared lists
- Vitreoscilla ferment ([[vitreoscilla-filiformis-lysate]]), branded "Aqua Posae Filiformis," listed near the end of both panels

Present only on the Target-sourced panel: ceramide NP, one of the [[ceramides]], listed thirteenth or fourteenth of roughly thirty ingredients.[^target]

Base, texture, and emollient system common to both: dimethicone, cetearyl alcohol, rapeseed seed oil, glyceryl stearate, ammonium polyacryloyldimethyl taurate, PEG-100 stearate, propanediol, ophiopogon japonicus root extract, PEG-20 methyl glucose sesquistearate, sorbitan tristearate, dimethiconol, sodium chloride, mannose, coco-betaine, citric acid, maltodextrin, xanthan gum, tocopherol, and pentaerythrityl tetra-di-t-butyl hydroxyhydrocinnamate.[^target][^lrpme]

The formula is fragrance-free on both panels fetched.[^target][^lrpme]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"82% of patients using LIPIKAR Baume AP+M for one month don't relapse vs only 26% of people using a standard care."** La Roche-Posay's own Middle East product page cites this to "a clinical study on 100 subjects" without a published paper, journal, or independent review available to check.[^lrpme]
- **"Itching is decreased by -41%, sleep loss is decreased by -42%, quality of life is improved by +69%."** The same page attributes this to "a clinical study on 51 subjects (adults and children), during 2 weeks," using this balm together with a separate Lipikar cleanser, again without a published, independently checkable report.[^lrpme]
- **"Clinically shown to help reduce dry, rough skin" and "long-lasting, 48-hour hydration."** Target's brand-supplied benefit statements; no independently published trial of this product was located.[^target]
- **"Restores the skin's protective barrier."** [[Niacinamide|Niacinamide's]] barrier-lipid effect is evidenced at the category level; no product-specific barrier measurement of this balm was located.[^target]
- **"Suitable for patients undergoing chemotherapy and radiation."** A manufacturer/retailer suitability statement, not a reported clinical-trial outcome on the pages cited here.[^target]
- **"Aqua Posae Filiformis rebalances the skin's microbiome."** [[Vitreoscilla-filiformis-lysate|Vitreoscilla filiformis extract's]] cited trials measure eczema severity scores and lab-culture markers, not a direct measurement of the resident skin microbial community.
