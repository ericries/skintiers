Rhofade is prescription oxymetazoline hydrochloride 1% cream, currently distributed by Mayne Pharma and FDA-approved "for the topical treatment of persistent facial erythema associated with rosacea in adults."[^dailymed] It is an alpha 1A adrenoceptor agonist, a vasoconstrictor applied once daily to blanch the fixed background redness of [[rosacea]]; the current label text refers to the product by its generic name.[^dailymed]

> One of only two FDA-approved topicals for the persistent redness of rosacea, a symptom most other rosacea drugs do not touch. But the mechanism is a daily squeeze on facial blood vessels, not a treatment of the disease: the effect wears off in hours, and in its own two phase 3 trials only 12% to 18% of patients reached the strict composite success bar at any timepoint, versus 5% to 9% on an inert vehicle cream. It does nothing for the inflammatory bumps of rosacea (the label lists worsening of those lesions as an adverse reaction) and nothing for visible vessels, which need laser or light devices. Same playbook as [[mirvaso-brimonidine-gel|Mirvaso]], the brimonidine comparator; the full drug story, including the rebound question, is owned by [[oxymetazoline-topical]]. The condition context is [[rosacea-erythematotelangiectatic]].

## Summary

Each gram of the white to off-white cream contains 10 mg (1%) oxymetazoline hydrochloride, equivalent to 8.8 mg (0.88%) of oxymetazoline free base.[^dailymed] The label's mechanism section is two sentences: "Oxymetazoline is an alpha 1A adrenoceptor agonist. Oxymetazoline acts as a vasoconstrictor."[^dailymed] The indication is quoted above and is limited to adults; the label states "Safety and effectiveness of Oxymetazoline Hydrochloride Cream have not been established in pediatric patients below the age of 18 years."[^dailymed] Dosing is "a pea-sized amount ... once daily in a thin layer to cover the entire face (forehead, nose, each cheek, and chin) avoiding the eyes and lips," with hands washed immediately after applying.[^dailymed]

The indication matters as much as the numbers. Persistent facial erythema, the fixed background redness of [[rosacea-erythematotelangiectatic|erythematotelangiectatic rosacea]], is largely untouched by the antibiotics and anti-inflammatories that treat the papulopustular phenotype, so a drug approved specifically for it fills a real gap. What Rhofade delivers against that gap is temporary: a same-day blanching that a minority of trial patients rated as a 2-grade change, covered under [The Evidence](#the-evidence). It has no demonstrated effect on inflammatory papules and pustules, and the pooled trials logged "worsening inflammatory lesions of rosacea" in 1% of treated subjects versus under 1% on vehicle.[^dailymed] Fixed visible vessels (telangiectasias) are not an erythema and are not in the indication; vascular lasers and intense pulsed light handle those, per [[rosacea-erythematotelangiectatic]].

The mechanism, the REVEAL trial literature, the rebound-erythema question that shadows this drug class, and the comparison with [[brimonidine]] are owned by [[oxymetazoline-topical]] and are not re-derived here. Rhofade is prescription-only; see a prescriber for use and dosing.

## The Evidence

The general oxymetazoline record is on [[oxymetazoline-topical]]; this section covers only what the FDA label reports for this product.

The label describes two identical, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, parallel-group trials enrolling 885 subjects aged 18 years and older (90% Caucasian, 79% female), who applied drug or vehicle once daily for 29 days.[^dailymed] Severity was graded on a 5-point Clinician Erythema Assessment (CEA) scale and a similar 5-point Subject Self-Assessment (SSA) scale; subjects entered scoring "moderate" or "severe" on both.[^dailymed] The primary endpoint was strict: "the proportion of subjects with at least a 2-grade reduction in erythema (improvement) from baseline (pre-dose on Day 1) on both the CEA and SSA measured at hours 3, 6, 9, and 12 on Day 29."[^dailymed]

Composite success on Day 29, from the label's Table 2:[^dailymed]

| Hour post-dose | Trial 1: drug (N=222) vs vehicle (N=218)[^dailymed] | Trial 2: drug (N=224) vs vehicle (N=221)[^dailymed] |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 12% vs 6% | 14% vs 7% |
| 6 | 16% vs 8% | 13% vs 5% |
| 9 | 18% vs 6% | 16% vs 9% |
| 12 | 15% vs 6% | 12% vs 6% |

The drug arm beat vehicle at every timepoint in both trials, roughly doubling or tripling the vehicle response.[^dailymed] Read the absolute numbers, though: at best 18 in 100 treated patients achieved a 2-grade improvement on both scales, and the endpoint was measured within a 12-hour window after that day's dose because the vasoconstriction does not outlast the day.[^dailymed] Stop the cream and the redness returns; this is symptom control on a 24-hour clock, the same proposition as [[mirvaso-brimonidine-gel|Mirvaso]], whose FDA label reports hour-3 composite success around double Rhofade's (25% to 31% vs vehicle, versus Rhofade's 12% to 14%); the head-to-head picture is on [[oxymetazoline-topical]] and [[brimonidine]].

Adverse reactions in at least 1% of the 489 drug-treated subjects across the pooled 4-week controlled trials, versus 483 on vehicle: application site dermatitis 9 (2%) vs 0, worsening inflammatory lesions of rosacea 7 (1%) vs 1 (<1%), application site pruritus 5 (1%) vs 4 (1%), application site erythema 5 (1%) vs 2 (<1%), and application site pain 4 (1%) vs 1 (<1%).[^dailymed] In the one-year open-label trial (440 subjects), the rates were worsening inflammatory lesions of rosacea 3%, application site dermatitis 3%, pruritis 2%, pain 2%, and erythema 2%.[^dailymed] The label nowhere uses the word "rebound";[^dailymed] the trial-reported post-treatment rebound data and the case-report record for the sibling drug brimonidine are covered on [[oxymetazoline-topical]].

There are no contraindications, but the label advises caution in severe, unstable, or uncontrolled cardiovascular disease, orthostatic hypotension, and uncontrolled hypertension or hypotension; in conditions of vascular insufficiency such as Raynaud's phenomenon, thromboangitis obliterans, scleroderma, or Sjogren's syndrome; and warns it "may increase the risk of angle closure glaucoma in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma."[^dailymed] Systemic absorption through facial skin is low: mean peak plasma concentration was 66.4 pg/mL after 28 days of once-daily use.[^dailymed]

> **Bottom line.** A genuine, well-run pair of vehicle-controlled trials behind one of the few approved options for a hard-to-treat symptom. The honest reading is a modest, hours-long cosmetic blanching that most treated patients did not rate as a 2-grade change, with no effect on bumps or visible vessels and a daily reapplication requirement for as long as the redness matters to you.[^dailymed]

## What's In It

Active, per gram, as declared on the FDA-approved prescribing information: [[oxymetazoline-topical|oxymetazoline hydrochloride]] 10 mg (1%), equivalent to 8.8 mg (0.88%) oxymetazoline free base.[^dailymed]

Inactive ingredients: sodium citrate dihydrate, citric acid anhydrous, disodium edetate dihydrate, butylated hydroxytoluene, anhydrous lanolin, medium chain triglycerides, diisopropyl adipate, oleyl alcohol, polyethylene glycol 300, PEG-6 stearate, glycol stearate, PEG-32 stearate, cetostearyl alcohol, ceteareth-6, stearyl alcohol, ceteareth-25, methylparaben, propylparaben, phenoxyethanol, and purified water.[^dailymed] The label instructs not to apply it to irritated skin or open wounds.[^dailymed]

## Where to Buy

Prescription only; obtain through a dermatologist or prescriber. The current label lists a 30 gram laminated tube with a child-resistant closure (NDC 68308-766-30), distributed by Mayne Pharma, stored at 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F).[^dailymed] No official list price appears on a durable primary source, and per house policy aggregator price-comparison sites are not cited, so no price is stated here.

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand marketing page was reviewed for this product; the claims below reflect how this drug class is commonly framed, checked against the FDA label.

- **"Reduces facial redness for up to 12 hours."** Consistent with the trial design: composite success was measured at hours 3 through 12 post-dose and held across that window.[^dailymed] The same design detail is the limitation: the benefit is measured within hours of that day's dose, so this is a daily-use symptom manager, not a lasting fix.
- **"Treats rosacea."** Too broad. The indication is the persistent erythema only; the trials measured redness scales, not lesions, and the label lists worsening inflammatory lesions of rosacea among adverse reactions.[^dailymed] The papulopustular phenotype has its own treatments, covered on [[rosacea]].
