Numbuzin No.3 Skin Softening Serum is a Korean fermented-ingredient serum from the brand numbuzin, led by Bifida Ferment Lysate and Galactomyces Ferment Filtrate and marketed for enlarged pores and rough skin texture, sold in a 1.69 fl oz (50 mL) bottle for $28.00.[^numbuzin]

> The brand's headline pore- and roughness-reduction figures come from its own unpublished test and cannot be independently checked; see [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims). Niacinamide, the formula's other named active, sits sixth on the ingredient list with no declared concentration, below the 2-5% range the general niacinamide evidence is built on (see [[niacinamide]]). Independent evidence for the two lead ferments in isolation is thin (Bifida Ferment Lysate) to weak (Galactomyces Ferment Filtrate).[^numbuzin]

## Summary

No.3 Skin Softening Serum is part of numbuzin's "No.3 Pore Care" line, positioned by the brand as a "poreless skin serum" for textured, rough skin and enlarged pores.[^numbuzin] The brand's own directions position it as a serum-step product; it is formulated without added fragrance (none appears in its ingredient list) and without gluten-containing ingredients, per the product page.[^numbuzin]

The two ingredients the brand names as "key ingredients," Bifida Ferment Lysate and Galactomyces Ferment Filtrate, are also the first and second ingredients on the full list, ahead of the base solvents, niacinamide, and the humectants.[^numbuzin] Independent, ingredient-specific evidence for both ferments is limited: Bifida Ferment Lysate's supportive data is one in-vitro cell-culture study and one combination-product acne trial that cannot isolate the ferment's own contribution (see [[bifida-ferment-lysate]]); Galactomyces Ferment Filtrate's evidence is graded weak, built almost entirely on studies run or funded by its main commercial user, Procter & Gamble's SK-II (see [[galactomyces-ferment-filtrate]]). Niacinamide, the formula's other named active, appears sixth on the list with no declared concentration, so the general niacinamide case for barrier support and hyperpigmentation, which is dose-dependent, cannot be pinned to this formula (see [[niacinamide]]).

The brand states the serum was "clinically proven" to reduce pore area by 30.90% and skin roughness by 11.39% in two weeks, with a 214% larger effect when paired with the brand's own No.3 toner.[^numbuzin] No independent publication, sample size, or control arm for that test was found; it is attributed as an unverified brand claim under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims), not treated as established evidence.

It is priced at $28.00 for 1.69 fl oz (50 mL) on the brand's US site, where it was listed on a promotional sale price of $14.00 as of 2026-08-24.[^numbuzin]

## The Evidence

No published, independent trial of this exact serum or its specific ferment combination was found. The product's named actives each have their own general evidence base, summarized here and covered in full on their own pages:

- [[bifida-ferment-lysate|Bifida Ferment Lysate]]: a postbiotic ferment byproduct with preliminary evidence limited to one in-vitro keratinocyte/macrophage study and one combination-device acne trial that cannot isolate the ferment's individual contribution.
- [[galactomyces-ferment-filtrate|Galactomyces Ferment Filtrate]]: graded weak; nearly all cited evidence, in-vitro and clinical, comes from studies authored or funded by Procter & Gamble, the maker of SK-II, and no independent placebo-controlled trial isolating the ferment alone was found.
- [[niacinamide]]: modest, mostly small-trial evidence for barrier support and facial hyperpigmentation exists at typical 2-5% concentrations; this serum does not declare a concentration, and niacinamide's sixth-place position on the ingredient list suggests it is present at a lower share of the formula than the two ferments ahead of it.

The remaining formula (squalane, sodium hyaluronate, panthenol, adenosine, and the emulsifier/humectant base) is a standard soothing and moisturizing system; the general evidence for those ingredient classes is covered on [[squalane]], [[hyaluronic-acid]], [[panthenol]], and [[adenosine]].
