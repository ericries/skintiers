CeraVe Itch Relief Moisturizing Lotion is an over-the-counter anti-itch moisturizer built around pramoxine hydrochloride 1%, a topical anesthetic, in CeraVe's ceramide-and-glycerin base.[^1]

> An OTC drug, not a plain moisturizer: the Drug Facts label lists the active as "Pramoxine hydrochloride 1%" with the purpose "External analgesic," for the "temporary relief of itching associated with minor skin irritations."[^1] Pramoxine numbs the itch signal; it soothes symptoms rather than treating the cause. The base adds CeraVe's three ceramides and glycerin, so it doubles as a barrier-supporting moisturizer (see [[ceramides]]).

## Summary

This is the anti-itch member of the CeraVe line. It is regulated as an OTC drug: the label states the active as "pramoxine hydrochloride 1%," the purpose as "external analgesic," and the use as temporary relief of itching from minor skin irritations.[^1] Pramoxine is a topical local anesthetic, so it dampens the sensation of itch directly; that is symptomatic relief, useful for the itch of dry or irritated skin, not a treatment for the underlying condition (the eczema evidence lives on [[atopic-dermatitis]] and [[colloidal-oatmeal]]).

The rest of the formula is a standard CeraVe moisturizer: glycerin plus the brand's ceramides NP, AP, and EOP, which support the skin barrier, covered on [[ceramides]].[^1]

## The Evidence

No independent trial of this specific lotion was located. The defensible facts are its active (pramoxine 1%, an FDA-monograph external analgesic) and the ceramide-plus-glycerin base.[^1] Pramoxine's job is symptomatic: it relieves itch temporarily, which is genuinely useful for the itch of minor irritation and dry skin, but it does not reduce inflammation or resolve a rash on its own.

> **Bottom line.** A pramoxine 1% anti-itch lotion in a ceramide moisturizer base, well suited to calming the itch of dry or minorly irritated skin while supporting the barrier.[^1] Treat it as symptomatic itch relief plus a good moisturizer, not a treatment for eczema or any underlying cause; for that, see [[atopic-dermatitis]].

## What's In It

Active ingredient: "pramoxine hydrochloride 1%."[^1] Inactive ingredients, from the label: water, glycerin, petrolatum, cetyl alcohol, shea butter, cetearyl alcohol, glyceryl stearate, PEG-100 stearate, isopropyl myristate, stearic acid, dimethicone, palmitic acid, potassium phosphate, and ceramides NP, AP, and EOP, among others.[^1] The base pairs humectants (glycerin) and occlusives (petrolatum, dimethicone) with CeraVe's signature ceramides.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Itch relief."** Accurate and label-supported: pramoxine 1% is an external analgesic for temporary itch relief.[^1] It is symptomatic, not a cure.
- **Eczema and rash relief.** The label covers "minor skin irritations"; for eczema specifically, the evidence sits with barrier care and anti-inflammatory treatment on [[atopic-dermatitis]], not with this anesthetic.
