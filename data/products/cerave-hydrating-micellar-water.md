A 400 mL micellar water at $14.99, which works out at **3.7 cents a millilitre**. **Bottom line:** an ordinary job done cheaply, with a more interesting ingredient list than a makeup remover needs, and the claims on the bottle check out.

> **The same brand, the same price, and 3.4 times the product.** CeraVe's other new makeup remover, the [[cerave-cream-to-water-makeup-melter]], is also $14.99 and holds 118 mL, so it costs 12.7 cents a millilitre against this one's 3.7.[^brand][^photo] They are different formats for overlapping jobs, and the price gap between them is larger than anything in their ingredient lists.

## Summary

CeraVe sells it at **$14.99**, and the bottle gives the size as 13 FL OZ (400 ml).[^brand][^photo] The brand describes it as delivering "24-hour hydration while purifying skin by lifting SPF, makeup, and pollution particles", and calls it "Alcohol-free and non- comedogenic", reproducing the brand's own spacing.[^brand]

The declared list runs 20 entries: "AQUA / WATER / EAU • HEXYLENE GLYCOL • GLYCERIN • POLOXAMER 184 • CERAMIDE NP • CERAMIDE AP • CERAMIDE EOP • CARBOMER • NIACINAMIDE • TRIETHYL CITRATE • SODIUM HYALURONATE", then sodium lauroyl lactylate, cholesterol, disodium cocoamphodiacetate, disodium EDTA, caprylyl glycol, myrtrimonium bromide, phytosphingosine, xanthan gum and benzoic acid.[^brand]

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: modest, and that is the job.** Poloxamer 184 at position four is the micelle-forming surfactant. Wiping makeup off with a mild surfactant works; nobody needs a trial for it and none is offered.
- **The alcohol-free claim checks out.** Searching the declared list finds no alcohol denat or any other alcohol, so the claim on the page is supported by the list beneath it.[^brand]
- **Record finding: the hyaluronic acid claim is accurate here.** Both the bottle and the brand page's Key Ingredients block name hyaluronic acid, and sodium hyaluronate is eleventh of 20.[^brand][^photo] This matters because the sibling [[cerave-cream-to-water-makeup-melter]] carries the same claim on a formula whose 25 ingredients contain no hyaluronate at all. One page is wrong; the brand is not systematically wrong.
- **An unusually loaded formula for a wipe-off product.** Three ceramides, niacinamide, sodium hyaluronate, cholesterol and phytosphingosine is the lipid set from CeraVe's moisturisers, in something designed to be removed with a cotton pad. The ingredients are real; the contact time is the ceiling, and no amount of formulation changes that.
- **The bottle and the page disagree about who it is for.** The bottle reads "For Normal to Dry Skin"; the page says "Made For: All skin types".[^brand][^photo] That is the second CeraVe product in two days where the narrower claim is the one printed on the pack.

## The Evidence

None specific to this product. [[ceramides]], [[niacinamide]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]] carry their own evidence and none is restated here. The "24-hour hydration" and "stronger skin barrier from the 1st use" claims have no study cited anywhere on the page read.[^brand]

> **Bottom line.** Cheap, gentle, and honestly labelled. If you want the cheapest competent way to take makeup off, the volume is the argument.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own page and the bottle, and **not** endorsed here.

- "24-hour hydration while purifying skin by lifting SPF, makeup, and pollution particles"[^brand] No study is cited for the 24 hours, on a product that is wiped off.
- "stronger skin barrier from the 1st use"[^brand] A single-use barrier claim with no measurement offered.
- "With 3 essential ceramides & hyaluronic acid" on the bottle.[^photo] Both check out against the declared list.
- "Suitable For Face, Lips & Eyes" on the bottle.[^photo] A tolerability claim, with no test cited.
