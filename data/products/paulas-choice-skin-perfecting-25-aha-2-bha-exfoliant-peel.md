Paula's Choice Skin Perfecting 25% AHA + 2% BHA Exfoliant Peel is a rinse-off chemical peel that pairs a 25% blend of alpha-hydroxy acids (glycolic, lactic, mandelic, tartaric, and malic) with 2% salicylic acid, a beta-hydroxy acid. It is a large step up in strength from the brand's leave-on [[paulas-choice-skin-perfecting-8-aha-gel-exfoliant|8% AHA Gel Exfoliant]], which stays on skin at a much lower acid concentration; this product is applied, left on briefly, then rinsed off. Paula's Choice lists it at $39.20 on its own site.[^pc]

> The exfoliation case for this product belongs to its acids, not to a trial of this exact formula: glycolic acid and salicylic acid both have modest, mixed-quality evidence for smoother texture and clearer pores at the concentrations studied, set out on [[glycolic-acid]] and [[salicylic-acid]]. What is different here is concentration. A 25% AHA blend plus 2% BHA is far stronger than the brand's 8% leave-on gel, and higher acid strength brings a correspondingly higher chance of stinging, redness, and irritation, which is why it is formulated as a rinse-off product used occasionally rather than left on daily.

## Summary

Paula's Choice describes this as "a multi-acid, rinse-off exfoliant peel that dramatically resurfaces skin to visibly smooth wrinkles, refine rough, bumpy texture, brighten tone & unclog pores," listing "25% AHA blend addresses advanced signs of aging" and "2% BHA unclogs & decongests enlarged pores" among its bullet claims.[^pc] The brand's own product FAQ recommends using it "once weekly after cleansing and before applying any leave-on products" and advises finishing daytime routines with SPF 30+ afterward.[^pc]

The formula leads with water and aminomethyl propanol (a pH-adjusting amine used to buffer the acid blend to a usable pH), followed by the acid blend itself: glycolic, lactic, and mandelic acids, then isoamyl laurate, tartaric acid, propanediol, salicylic acid, and malic acid. Soothing and hydrating ingredients (glycyrrhetinic acid, sodium hyaluronate, glycerin, bisabolol, allantoin) follow further down the list, at concentrations too low to be primary actives.

## The Evidence

No independently published trial evaluates this exact 25% AHA / 2% BHA combination product;[^pc] the case for what it does rests on its individual acids. Glycolic acid, the largest single AHA in the blend, has modest, mixed-quality evidence for smoother texture and improved photodamage at studied concentrations, with weaker support for evening skin tone, as set out on [[glycolic-acid]]. Salicylic acid, the BHA at 2%, sits within the FDA's OTC acne-active range and has modest, low-quality trial evidence for acne and pore congestion, ranked by dermatology guidelines below benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids, as set out on [[salicylic-acid]]. Lactic and mandelic acids each have their own modest-to-mixed evidence bases on their respective ingredient pages; none of them was tested in this exact combination or at this exact 25%/2% ratio.

What is genuinely different about this product, relative to the acid pages generally, is concentration and contact time: a 25% total AHA load applied as a short-contact rinse-off treatment is a materially stronger exposure than an 8% leave-on gel. Higher acid concentration is a known driver of irritation, stinging, and skin sensitization risk, independent of whether it improves the exfoliation outcome. Paula's Choice's own guidance to patch-test other high-strength actives and use this peel only once weekly reflects that higher-strength acids are handled with more caution than a daily leave-on.[^pc]

> **Bottom line.** A high-strength, multi-acid rinse-off peel whose exfoliation benefit rests on the same modest, mixed-quality acid evidence covered on [[glycolic-acid]] and [[salicylic-acid]], not a trial of this specific 25%/2% formula.[^pc] Its main practical difference from a leave-on AHA/BHA product is strength: a bigger acid dose applied briefly and rinsed off, with a correspondingly higher risk of irritation that comes with any high-percentage acid product.
