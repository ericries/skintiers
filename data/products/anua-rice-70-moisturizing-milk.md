Anua Rice 70 Intensive Moisturizing Milk is a lightweight, fragrance-marketed Korean lotion built on a rice bran water base, carrying niacinamide fifth on a 51-ingredient list alongside five ceramide types, hyaluronic acid, and squalane, sold in a 150 ml bottle for $19 to $20.[^anua][^stylevana]

> The brand markets it for "instant glow" and a "glass skin" finish; niacinamide, the formula's one active with an established evidence base, is proven for facial hyperpigmentation rather than generic glow, covered on [[niacinamide]], and no published trial tests this lotion, its rice bran water base, or its separate rice extract.[^anua]

## Summary

Anua Rice 70 Intensive Moisturizing Milk is a fragrance-marketed, lightweight lotion the brand positions for "instant glow and moisture" and a "glass skin" finish, describing it as fast-absorbing and suitable for daily use.[^anua] Rice bran water leads the 51-ingredient list, ahead of plain water at position 11, though Anua does not state a percentage for it on this product's page, unlike the brand's own [[anua-rice-70-glow-milky-toner|Rice 70 Glow Milky Toner]], which discloses 71% rice bran water.

Niacinamide is the formula's one active with an established evidence base. It sits fifth on the ingredient list, ahead of several texture and preservative ingredients, but no concentration is declared. Topical niacinamide is well tolerated and has modest, mostly small-trial evidence for lightening facial hyperpigmentation and for supporting the skin barrier and lowering water loss; that case is set out in full on [[niacinamide]]. The brand's "glow" and "glass skin" marketing goes beyond that evidence, which is specific to visible pigmentation rather than generic radiance.

The rest of the formula carries barrier and hydration ingredients that back the moisturizing claim in principle: five ceramide types (NP, AS, AP, NS, EOP), three forms of hyaluronic acid, cholesterol, phytosphingosine, squalane, allantoin, tocopherol, ectoin, and adenosine. All of them cluster in the bottom third of the ingredient list, from position 30 (cholesterol) down to position 51 of 51 (Ceramide EOP, the last ingredient listed), which suggests small supporting amounts rather than headline concentrations. The general evidence that barrier lipids and humectants like these support moisturizing is set out on [[moisturizing]]; no trial isolates this product's combination or amounts.

It is priced at $20.00 on the brand's own US site and $19.00 at the Korean-beauty retailer Stylevana, both for the 150 ml size, as of 2026-09-17.[^anua][^stylevana] The manufacturer's own claim of a 101.688% glow boost is quarantined under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims) rather than treated as independent evidence.

## The Evidence

No published trial tests this lotion, its rice bran water base, or its separate rice extract.[^anua] The product's one active with a documented clinical case is niacinamide: topical niacinamide has modest, mostly small-trial evidence for lightening facial hyperpigmentation and for supporting the skin barrier and lowering water loss in dry skin, set out in full on [[niacinamide]]. That evidence was generated at declared concentrations; this lotion does not state how much niacinamide it contains, so the product's own effect size cannot be estimated from the general niacinamide case.
