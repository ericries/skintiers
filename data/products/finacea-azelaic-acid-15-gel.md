Finacea Gel, 15% is a prescription topical containing azelaic acid, a naturally occurring dicarboxylic acid, applied twice daily to the face to treat the inflammatory papules and pustules of mild to moderate rosacea.[^dailymed] It is the branded gel formulation that FDA approval was based on, not a compounded or generic azelaic acid product, and it was later joined by a foam formulation from the same maker.[^dailymed]

> A first-line prescription option for [[rosacea-papulopustular|papulopustular rosacea]], with a long, twice-daily vehicle-controlled trial base behind it and a head-to-head trial showing it beats the older standard topical, metronidazole.[^finacea-registration][^elewski] The tradeoff is tolerability: in the registration safety pool 22% of azelaic-acid patients had a mild cutaneous adverse event (such as burning, stinging, or tingling), 13% moderate and 6% severe, versus 14%, 9%, and 2% on vehicle, and irritation is dose-limiting for some users.[^dailymed] The label makes no claim about background erythema or visible vessels apart from the inflammatory lesions it treats.[^dailymed]

## Summary

Finacea is the brand name for azelaic acid 15% gel, a twice-daily prescription treatment for the inflammatory lesions of [[rosacea-papulopustular|papulopustular rosacea]], the papules and pustules that distinguish that subtype from the persistent-redness pattern of [[rosacea-erythematotelangiectatic|erythematotelangiectatic rosacea]].[^dailymed] Azelaic acid is a well-evidenced anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial acid with a broader cross-condition evidence base (acne, hyperpigmentation) set out on [[azelaic-acid]]; this page covers only what Finacea's own FDA trials showed for rosacea.

The FDA approved Finacea Gel, 15% on the strength of two identically designed, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, 12-week, vehicle-controlled trials in 664 patients (333 azelaic acid, 331 vehicle) with mild to moderate rosacea and a mean baseline lesion count of 18.[^dailymed] In both trials, azelaic acid significantly outperformed vehicle on lesion-count reduction and on the Investigator's Global Assessment (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^dailymed] A separate 251-patient head-to-head trial then compared it against twice-daily metronidazole 0.75% gel, the older standard topical, and found azelaic acid ahead on lesion reduction and erythema improvement.[^elewski]

Tolerability is the main tradeoff. In the registration safety pool of 457 azelaic-acid-treated patients, cutaneous adverse events including burning, stinging, or tingling occurred in 22% (mild), 13% (moderate), and 6% (severe) of patients, versus 14%, 9%, and 2% on vehicle; the label separately notes azelaic acid caused significantly more irritation than its vehicle in a cumulative irritation study.[^dailymed] Patients are also instructed to avoid spicy foods, hot foods and drinks, and alcohol during treatment, and to use only mild soaps or a soapless cleanser, general rosacea-trigger advice folded into the label itself rather than a claim specific to the drug's mechanism.[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

**Vehicle-controlled registration trials.** Two identically designed, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, 12-week trials compared azelaic acid gel 15% applied twice daily against its vehicle gel in 664 patients with mild to moderate papulopustular rosacea (mean baseline lesion count 18, range 8 to 60; 92.5% Caucasian, 73% women, mean age 49).[^dailymed] On the intent-to-treat population with last observation carried forward, mean inflammatory lesion counts fell 57.9% with azelaic acid versus 39.9% on vehicle in Study One (baseline 17.5 vs 17.6, end-of-treatment 6.8 vs 10.5), and 50.0% versus 38.2% in Study Two (baseline 17.9 vs 18.5, end-of-treatment 9.0 vs 12.1).[^dailymed] On the Investigator's Global Assessment, the rate of success (defined by the label as a score of clear or minimal with at least a 2-step reduction from baseline) was 61% with azelaic acid in both studies, versus 40% on vehicle in Study One and 48% on vehicle in Study Two.[^dailymed] The label notes some reduction in erythema occurred in these trials among patients who also had papules and pustules, but states efficacy for erythema in the absence of papules and pustules has not been evaluated.[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: against an inactive vehicle, twice-daily azelaic acid reached the label's success mark (clear or minimal on the investigator's global scale, with a 2-step improvement) in 61% of patients versus 40% and 48% on vehicle, and produced a larger percentage reduction in lesion counts in both registration trials.[^dailymed]

**Head-to-head against metronidazole (Elewski and colleagues, 2003).** A multicenter, double-blind, randomized trial at 13 US centers assigned 251 patients with papulopustular rosacea and persistent erythema and telangiectasia to azelaic acid gel 15% or metronidazole gel 0.75%, both applied twice daily for 15 weeks.[^elewski] Azelaic acid was superior on mean lesion-count reduction (-12.9 vs -10.7, P=.003) and mean percent decrease in inflammatory lesions (-72.7% vs -55.8%, P<.001); 56% of azelaic-acid patients were rated improved on erythema severity versus 42% on metronidazole (P=.02), and both the investigator's global assessment (P=.02) and overall improvement assessment (P=.005) favored azelaic acid.[^elewski] Neither drug had a clinically appreciable effect on telangiectasia, and no serious or systemic treatment-related adverse events were reported in either arm.[^elewski] Full study detail on [[elewski-2003-azelaic-acid-15-vs-metronidazole-0-75-rosacea-rct]].

> Bottom line: head-to-head against the older standard topical, azelaic acid produced a significantly larger lesion reduction and more erythema improvement over 15 weeks.

## What's In It

- **Active: azelaic acid 15%** (0.15 g azelaic acid per gram of gel). See [[azelaic-acid]] for the graded mechanism and cross-condition evidence base.[^dailymed]
- **Base:** benzoic acid (preservative), disodium EDTA, 1,2-diarachidoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine, medium-chain triglycerides, polyacrylic acid (250,000 MW), polysorbate 80, propylene glycol, sodium hydroxide, and purified water, as declared on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

The label contraindicates Finacea in individuals with a history of hypersensitivity to propylene glycol or any other component of the formulation, and states it is for dermatologic use only, not for ophthalmic, oral, or intravaginal use.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Superior to the vehicle" and outperforms metronidazole.** This is the label's and the published trial's own language, not an unverified marketing claim; both the registration vehicle-controlled trials and the independent Elewski head-to-head trial support it as described above.[^dailymed][^elewski]
- **Azelaic acid as a gentle, well-tolerated option.** The registration safety data complicate a blanket "gentle" framing: 22% of azelaic-acid patients had a mild cutaneous adverse event such as burning, stinging, or tingling (13% moderate, 6% severe), and the label states azelaic acid caused significantly more irritation than its vehicle in a cumulative irritation study; tolerability is real but not negligible.[^dailymed]
