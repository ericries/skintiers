Medicube's Triple Collagen Serum is a viral K-beauty serum built around seven low-list collagen ingredients, niacinamide, several hyaluronic acid forms, and film-forming silicones, in a base that also carries alcohol denat high on the list and added fragrance with seven declared allergens. YesStyle, an authorized Medicube retailer, lists the current 2026-version 55 mL bottle at $21.90, marked down 37% from a $35.00 list price.[^yesstyle]

> The "Triple Collagen" name describes how many collagen ingredients the formula contains, not how deep any of them reach. Seven collagen-family ingredients appear on the ingredient list, collagen extract, atelocollagen, desamido collagen, hydrolyzed collagen, soluble collagen, collagen, and collagen amino acids, plus procollagen, but all of them sit eighteenth on the list or lower, after the silicones and well below niacinamide and alcohol denat. Topical collagen, hydrolyzed or intact, is a molecule too large to penetrate to the dermis and rebuild the skin's own collagen the way a retinoid does; it works on the surface as a humectant and film former, giving temporary hydration and a plumped, light-reflecting look rather than a structural change. A board-certified dermatologist reviewing this exact serum reaches the same conclusion.[^sugai] The one well-evidenced active here is niacinamide, fifth on the list (see [[niacinamide]]); adenosine sits further down with only thin evidence of its own (see [[adenosine]]), and the "glass skin" glow the brand markets reads largely as the formula's silicones, cyclopentasiloxane, cyclohexasiloxane, and dimethicone.

## Summary

Medicube describes Triple Collagen Serum as "a serum enriched with triple collagen" that "offers essential nutrients to the skin to improve suppleness and glow," built on a "Triple Collagen Complex" the brand describes as "1000x fortified collagen, hydrolyzed collagen, atelocollagen, and soluble collagen," which it says "delivers nutrients to the deepest skin layer to plump your skin up" and "helps refine skin texture and improve skin elasticity to a radiant and glowy skin appearance."[^yesstyle] It also markets the serum as "clinically tested, suitable for sensitive skin."[^yesstyle] Read against the ingredient list, the formula does contain more collagen-derived ingredients than the three or four named in the "Triple Collagen Complex" marketing, but every collagen fraction on the list sits at position eighteen or lower ([full list below](#whats-in-it)), after two silicones, a thickener, and two emulsifiers.

The formula leads with water, glycerin, butylene glycol, and cetyl ethylhexanoate, then niacinamide fifth on the list, followed by glycereth-26 and alcohol denat seventh. Two silicones, cyclopentasiloxane and cyclohexasiloxane, and a third, dimethicone, sit in the first third of the list ahead of any collagen ingredient. Adenosine appears twentieth, after fragrance, and multiple hyaluronic acid forms (sodium hyaluronate, hyaluronic acid, sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer, hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, sodium acetylated hyaluronate, hydroxypropyltrimonium hyaluronate) are interspersed with the collagen fractions in the back half of the list. The formula also carries seven declared fragrance allergens: benzyl benzoate, linalool, limonene, geraniol, citronellol, hydroxycitronellal, and citral.

It is listed at $21.90 for the 55 mL bottle (37% off a $35.00 list price) at YesStyle, an authorized Medicube retailer ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-22).[^yesstyle]

## The Evidence

No independently published controlled trial tests this exact serum. Medicube's "clinically tested" claim refers to testing the brand has not published for outside review.[^yesstyle]

The ingredient-level evidence is set out on the linked ingredient pages. Niacinamide, fifth on the list, has general evidence at a mid tier for its studied uses; this product discloses no concentration, so the strength of that general evidence cannot be assumed to transfer at whatever dose is used here (see [[niacinamide]]). Hyaluronic acid and its several derivative forms have strong general evidence as surface humectants, again without a disclosed concentration (see [[hyaluronic-acid]]). Adenosine's topical anti-wrinkle evidence comes from two small trials run or funded by the companies selling the product tested, both measuring surface appearance rather than a biological change in skin (see [[adenosine]]).

Collagen itself, whatever the form, is a large protein or protein fragment that does not cross the stratum corneum to rebuild the skin's own dermal collagen network; its cosmetic role is as a humectant and film former on the surface, the same limitation covered generally on [[peptides]] for other collagen-signaling ingredients. A board-certified dermatologist's review of this exact serum reaches the same conclusion: its collagen ingredients are not signal peptides and cannot penetrate to the dermis to rebuild lost collagen the way a retinoid can, so the firming effect is short-term surface plumping rather than structural change.[^sugai]

> **Bottom line.** A well-marketed hydrating and film-forming serum whose headline "Triple Collagen" ingredients sit low on the list and cannot reach the dermis. The one active with meaningful independent evidence is niacinamide, at an undisclosed concentration, and the brand's own elasticity and firmness figures are unpublished testing rather than an independent trial. Readers looking for a topical active with demonstrated collagen-stimulating effect should start with [[retinoids]], which this serum does not contain.
