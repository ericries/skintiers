## Summary

Youth To The People Retinal + Niacinamide Youth Serum is a 30 ml leave-on night serum from [[youth-to-the-people]], the L'Oreal-owned brand, priced at £66.00 on Space NK's UK site and listed at EUR75.90 by Cult Beauty, both of which also independently confirm the same ingredient list.[^spacenk][^cultbeauty] Its headline actives are retinal (retinaldehyde) and niacinamide; Space NK describes it as harnessing "the skin-perfecting powers of stabilised retinaldehyde to smooth the skin and reduce fine and wrinkles," combined with "a blend of 5% niacinamide, ceramides and adaptogens," and Cult Beauty independently states the same "5% niacinamide" figure.[^spacenk][^cultbeauty]

Retinaldehyde sits one enzymatic step from the receptor-active retinoic acid, closer than retinol's two steps, and is the best-evidenced over-the-counter retinoid form; [that category-level case is set out on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]](#the-evidence).[^spacenk] What is unusual here is that neither retailer's product page states a percentage for the retinal itself, only for the niacinamide: the ingredient list places "retinal" thirteenth, well below niacinamide's fourth position, consistent with a modest but unquantified concentration.[^spacenk][^cultbeauty] That is a real gap next to labeled competitors: [[medik8-crystal-retinal-3]], for example, names its 0.03% strength on the bottle as one rung of a six-strength ladder running from 0.01% to 0.24%. No published trial tests this exact YTTP serum.

Niacinamide's own case, covered on [[niacinamide]], is modest topical evidence for barrier support and skin tone rather than a proven anti-aging active on its own; here it is marketed less as an independent benefit and more as a way to "boost the efficacy of the formula by refining the pores and preventing any signs of irritation" alongside ceramides and ashwagandha, per Space NK, or to "soothe skin with hydration while ceramides, ashwagandha... and cloudberry seed... support your skin barrier and bolster the effect of retinal," per Cult Beauty.[^spacenk][^cultbeauty]

Against comparators already covered: [[medik8-crystal-retinal-3]] is a similarly priced ($66) retinaldehyde serum that discloses its strength on the label; [[anua-nano-retinol-0-3-niacin-renewing-serum]] and [[the-inkey-list-retinol-serum]] are cheaper serums using retinol, a form that needs an extra conversion step to reach retinoic acid; [[differin-adapalene-0-1-gel]] is an FDA-cleared over-the-counter retinoid with real acne trials, about $15; and [[tretinoin-0-025-cream]] is prescription retinoic acid, the benchmark every over-the-counter retinoid, including this one, remains milder than.

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinaldehyde improves aging skin, and how it compares with retinol and prescription retinoids, is covered on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]: in short, it has the strongest over-the-counter retinoid evidence, ahead of retinol's thinner data, though not shown to match prescription [[tretinoin]]. No published trial tests this exact serum, and because neither Space NK nor Cult Beauty discloses a percentage for its retinal, this formula's concentration cannot be placed against those trial doses.[^spacenk][^cultbeauty]

Topical niacinamide's evidence, covered on [[niacinamide]], is modest and mostly preliminary: it raises keratinocyte ceramide synthesis and lowers water loss in dry skin, and has separate modest evidence for hyperpigmentation and tone. No cited trial isolates a 5% niacinamide-plus-retinaldehyde combination, and no cited trial tests whether adding niacinamide measurably reduces retinoid irritation in this formula specifically; that is the retailers' own rationale for including it, not a cited outcome.[^spacenk][^cultbeauty]

Among over-the-counter retinoids, retinaldehyde's single enzymatic step to retinoic acid puts it a step ahead of the retinol forms used in [[anua-nano-retinol-0-3-niacin-renewing-serum]] and [[the-inkey-list-retinol-serum]]; the FDA-cleared adapalene in [[differin-adapalene-0-1-gel]] works by a different route, and it remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin-0-025-cream]] (retinoic acid itself).

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:
