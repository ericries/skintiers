Youth To The People Retinal + Niacinamide Youth Serum is a 1 oz (30 ml) leave-on night serum from [[youth-to-the-people]] that pairs 0.15% retinaldehyde with 5% niacinamide, ceramides, and adaptogenic plant extracts, priced at $72.00 direct from the manufacturer.[^yttppage]

> NYT Wirecutter named this serum its "Best for sensitive skin" retinoid pick.[^wirecutter] Its case rests on the category-level evidence for retinaldehyde and niacinamide, covered on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[niacinamide]]. The 0.15% retinaldehyde dose is five times [[medik8-crystal-retinal-3]]'s 0.03%, and buyer reviews report the serum leaves a temporary yellow tint on skin.[^cultbeauty]

## Summary

Youth To The People Retinal + Niacinamide Youth Serum is a 1 oz (30 ml) leave-on night serum from [[youth-to-the-people]], a California-based brand, priced at $72.00 direct from the manufacturer.[^yttppage] Its primary active is retinaldehyde (retinal), declared at 0.15% by the brand's own product team.[^yttpblog] That is five times the 0.03% dose in [[medik8-crystal-retinal-3]], a comparatively high over-the-counter retinal concentration.

What is meant to offset that relatively high dose is the rest of the formula. The serum also carries 5% niacinamide plus ceramides (Ceramide NP, AP, and EOP) and a complex of adaptogenic plant and mushroom extracts (ashwagandha, reishi, maitake, and shiitake), all confirmed on the ingredient declarations at Space NK and Cult Beauty.[^spacenk][^cultbeauty] Youth To The People's own Director of Field Sales and Education, Jacob Tomas del Rosario, describes the 0.15% strength as "chosen by our chemists for its potent level of actives while still being gentle on your skin."[^yttpblog]

Retinaldehyde sits one enzymatic step from the receptor-active retinoic acid, closer than retinol's two steps; its category-level evidence for photoaging is [set out in full on retinaldehyde and retinoids](#the-evidence), the strongest over-the-counter retinoid evidence but still milder than prescription [[tretinoin]]. The niacinamide is at 5%, the same concentration used in the pigmentation trials covered on [[niacinamide]], unlike the untested 10% in [[the-ordinary-niacinamide-10-zinc-1]].[^spacenk][^cultbeauty]

The main catches, per buyer reviews, are cosmetic and financial rather than medical: reviewers report the serum leaves a temporary yellow tint and feels tacky until it fully dries down, and at $72 an ounce it is expensive.[^cultbeauty][^yttppage] Against comparators already covered on the site: [[medik8-crystal-retinal-3]] is a cheaper ($66) retinaldehyde serum at a fifth of this product's dose (0.03%); [[skinmedica-retinol-complex-1-0]] is a pricier ($96) retinol serum; [[anua-nano-retinol-0-3-niacin-renewing-serum]] and [[trader-joes-retinol-night-serum]] are cheaper 0.3% retinol serums, a form that needs an extra conversion step to reach retinoic acid; [[differin-adapalene-0-1-gel]] is an FDA-cleared over-the-counter retinoid with real acne trials, about $15; and [[tretinoin-0-025-cream]] is prescription retinoic acid, the benchmark every over-the-counter retinoid, including this one, remains milder than.

The manufacturer directs applying "a pea-sized amount to clean, dry face and neck, avoiding the eye area" at night, starting "once a week," and "gradually increasing frequency to every night, as tolerated," followed by a moisturizer, with sunscreen recommended during the day.[^yttppage][^spacenk] The label's other claims are weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinaldehyde improves aging skin, and how it compares with retinol and prescription retinoids, is covered on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]: in short, one vehicle-controlled trial plus two independent concentration-comparison trials converge on measurable improvement in fine wrinkles, roughness, and texture, the strongest over-the-counter retinoid evidence, ahead of retinol's thinner data, though not shown to match prescription [[tretinoin]]. Whether topical niacinamide lightens hyperpigmentation and improves photoaging appearance is covered on [[niacinamide]]: a split-face trial found 5% niacinamide reduced facial hyperpigmentation versus vehicle after four weeks, and a separate half-face trial found 5% niacinamide improved the appearance of fine lines, wrinkles, and hyperpigmented spots versus vehicle over 12 weeks; both are the same 5% concentration declared in this serum.[^spacenk][^cultbeauty] No published trial tests this exact serum, and no cited trial isolates its 0.15% retinaldehyde strength or its combination with niacinamide.[^yttpblog]

NYT Wirecutter named this serum its "Best for sensitive skin" retinoid pick, an editorial and tester-experience endorsement rather than a controlled trial.[^wirecutter] That designation speaks to tolerability and cosmetic feel as judged by a review panel, not to measured clinical outcomes.

> **Bottom line.** Wirecutter's "Best for sensitive skin" designation is a credible outside endorsement for tolerability, but it is editorial and testimonial rather than clinical evidence, and no study isolates this product's retinaldehyde-plus-niacinamide formula.[^wirecutter]

Among over-the-counter retinoids, retinaldehyde's single enzymatic step to retinoic acid puts it a step ahead of the retinol forms used in [[anua-nano-retinol-0-3-niacin-renewing-serum]] and [[trader-joes-retinol-night-serum]]; the FDA-cleared adapalene in [[differin-adapalene-0-1-gel]] works by a different route, and this serum, like [[medik8-crystal-retinal-3]], remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin-0-025-cream]] (retinoic acid itself).

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Reduces fine lines and wrinkles in 1 week" and "Improves skin brightness after 4 weeks."** Sourced by the manufacturer to "a 34-person clinical trial," a small, unpublished, brand-run study.[^yttppage]
- **"91% agree skin looked smoother after 1 week," "91% agree skin looked clear after 1 week," and "91% agree skin is visibly firmer after 2 weeks."** Self-reported consumer-perception results from the same 34-person, brand-run study, not a controlled clinical outcome.[^yttppage]
- **"Refines the look of large pores."** Neither the retinaldehyde nor the niacinamide evidence reviewed on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[niacinamide]] establishes a pore-size effect for either active at these concentrations.[^yttppage]
