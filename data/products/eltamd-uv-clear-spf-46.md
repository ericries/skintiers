EltaMD UV Clear is an oil-free, fragrance-free face sunscreen lotion rated Broad Spectrum SPF 46, with the UV filters zinc oxide 9.0% and octinoxate 7.5% and niacinamide in the base.[^1][^5]

> This product pairs a high UVB rating (FDA Broad Spectrum SPF 46) with UVA (photoaging) protection that comes from its zinc oxide 9%, a photostable mineral filter, because its other filter, octinoxate, protects only against UVB.[^1][^2][^3] Its sunburn (UVB) protection is strong; its UVA protection is genuine but moderate, and below the high-load modern-filter sunscreens (Tinosorb, Mexoryl) sold in the EU, Korea, and Australia.[^4] Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing some skin cancers; how SPF, UVA, and the filter set fit together is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## The Evidence

Whether regular sunscreen use works, and how, is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. The randomized evidence there is for the practice of applying a broad-spectrum sunscreen. What is specific to this product is its filter set and its FDA label rating.

The Drug Facts panel lists the actives as zinc oxide 9.0% and octinoxate 7.5%, at SPF 46.[^1] SPF is a UVB (sunburn) number: the SPF 46 rating certifies UVB strength and does not by itself certify UVA (photoaging) protection, which is a separate axis described on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

In this formula the two axes come from two different filters. Octinoxate (ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate) is a UVB filter; a 2013 review describes ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate as an agent "which filters UVB."[^3] The UVA coverage therefore rests on the zinc oxide. A review of the two mineral filters reports that titanium dioxide is "primarily a UVB absorbing compound, while ZnO is more efficient in UVA absorption," and that zinc oxide "absorbs more UVA-1 radiation," the longest-wavelength UVA.[^2] Zinc oxide is a photostable, broad-spectrum mineral filter, so unlike the avobenzone-based US sunscreens it does not depend on a stabilizer; at 9% it provides genuine but moderate UVA attenuation.

Two limits keep this off the top of the UVA scale. The US "Broad Spectrum" claim is a single pass/fail test, not a graded UVA rating, so this product carries no numeric UVA figure of the kind the EU (UVA-PF) and Asian (PA) systems require.[^1] And measured against the global field, a 9% zinc oxide load sits below the high-load modern-filter products (built on Tinosorb or Mexoryl) sold in the EU, Korea, and Australia: a 2020 Journal of Drugs in Dermatology review argues that US sunscreens as a group let through substantially more UVA than European sunscreens, and that the modern filters available abroad protect against UVA and free radicals better than avobenzone.[^4] See [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] for the pass/fail mechanism, the critical-wavelength definition, and the global filter landscape.

> **Bottom line.** The FDA-tested SPF 46 is a high UVB (sunburn) rating.[^1] The UVA (photoaging) protection comes from zinc oxide 9%, a photostable mineral filter more efficient at UVA than titanium dioxide, because octinoxate protects only against UVB.[^2][^3] That UVA coverage is genuine but moderate; the US "Broad Spectrum" mark is a pass/fail test rather than a graded UVA rating, and a 9% zinc oxide load is below the high-load modern-filter (Tinosorb, Mexoryl) sunscreens sold in the EU, Korea, and Australia.[^1][^4] How SPF, UVA, and the filter set fit together is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## What's In It

The active filters and inactive ingredients as declared on the FDA Drug Facts label.[^1] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

UV filters (Drug Facts actives):

- [[zinc-oxide]] 9.0%, a mineral (physical) broad-spectrum filter and the source of this formula's UVA coverage
- [[octinoxate]] 7.5% (ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate), a UVB filter

Other functional ingredients:

- [[niacinamide]] (vitamin B3), listed high on the inactive list; EltaMD markets the formula as containing 5% niacinamide[^5]
- [[hyaluronic-acid]] (as sodium hyaluronate), a humectant
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E, as tocopheryl acetate), an antioxidant
- lactic acid

Base, texture, and preservative system: purified water, cyclopentasiloxane and triethoxycaprylylsilane (silicone system), octyldodecyl neopentanoate (emollient), hydroxyethyl acrylate/sodium acryloyldimethyl taurate copolymer and polyisobutene (texture and film formers), butylene glycol, PEG-7 trimethylolpropane coconut ether and oleth-3 phosphate (emulsifier and surfactant), phenoxyethanol and iodopropynyl butylcarbamate (preservatives), and phosphoric acid (pH adjuster).[^1]

Not in the formula: no added fragrance, and no organic (chemical) UVA filter such as avobenzone; the UVA coverage is entirely from the mineral zinc oxide.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"5% niacinamide," and skin prone to breakouts and rosacea.** EltaMD markets the niacinamide content (5%), says the formula "helps calm and protect skin prone to discoloration and breakouts," and lists the product as good for "Acne Prone" and "Rosacea" skin.[^5] Niacinamide's own evidence is on [[niacinamide]]; the FDA-rated benefit on this product's label is the Broad Spectrum SPF 46 UV protection.[^1] The acne and rosacea positioning is a manufacturer claim, not an FDA-tested outcome for this product.
- **"Oil-free," "fragrance-free," "non-comedogenic," "hypoallergenic," "paraben-free," "dermatologist recommended."** Manufacturer label and marketing claims, not independently tested outcomes.[^5]
