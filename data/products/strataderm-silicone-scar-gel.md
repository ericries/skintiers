A self-drying silicone gel for scars, from the maker of [[stratacel-film-forming-dressing]] and [[stratamed-film-forming-wound-dressing]]. **Bottom line:** it is the one product in this family named in a properly conducted randomised trial, and that trial's finding is that it did not beat the alternative it was tested against.

> **A 153-child randomised trial, and a mostly null result.** Children referred for burn scar management were randomised to topical silicone gel, pressure garment therapy, or both. The authors' conclusion: "No difference was identified in the effectiveness of silicone and pressure interventions alone. No benefit to a combined silicone and pressure intervention was identified".[^rct] The trial had no untreated arm, so it compares silicone against another active treatment rather than against nothing.

## Summary

### What the trial found, precisely

Primary outcomes were scar thickness and itch at six months, with the outcome assessor and data analyst blinded for thickness.[^rct] Across 153 children, one between-group difference reached significance: thinner scars on silicone alone than on the combined treatment, "mean difference (95% confidence interval) = -0.04 cm (-0.07, -0.00), P = 0.05".[^rct]

That is four tenths of a millimetre, with a confidence interval whose upper bound touches zero and a p value exactly at the conventional threshold. Everything else was null: "No other between-group differences were identified for scar thickness or itch intensity at six months post-burn."[^rct]

### The disclosure is worth reading, and it is a good one

Stratpharma appears in this paper only in its conflicts statement, and the statement is unusually thorough. "Financial support was provided by Stratpharma for the open access publication of this manuscript", while "Stratpharma had no part in the final study design, data collection, or analysis of results", "The funders did not have access to the results prior to them being released publicly", "Only the investigators have access to the final trial data set", and "The investigators have no financial interest in Strataderm or the Stratpharma Company".[^rct]

A manufacturer paying a publication fee for a trial that did not favour its product, disclosed in that much detail, is close to the opposite of the pattern [[ingredient-evidence-by-who-ran-the-only-trial]] documents.

## The Rubric

**Against other scar treatments: no advantage shown.** The trial's own conclusion. If you are choosing between silicone gel and a pressure garment for a child's burn scar, this evidence does not favour either.

**Against nothing: not tested here.** Every arm received an active treatment, so the trial cannot answer whether the gel beats leaving a scar alone. The broader case for silicone as a class sits on [[silicone-gel-and-sheeting-for-scars]].

**Combining treatments: actively unsupported.** The one significant result pointed the other way, with silicone alone marginally better than silicone plus a pressure garment.

**Regulatory status: no US device listing found.** The openFDA registration and listing database returns four Stratpharma listings, Stratacel, Stratamark, Colsigen and a vaginal applicator, and Strataderm is not among them.[^fda] Same finding as its sibling [[stratamed-film-forming-wound-dressing]], and scoped the same way.

**No price recorded.** The maker's cached products page carries neither a price nor a photograph for this item.[^brand]

## The Evidence

Six PubMed records mention Strataderm. The one used here is the randomised trial above; a 2017 Trials paper is its published protocol, and the rest are a cost-effectiveness analysis, a moisturiser hydration study and two non-English papers whose abstracts were not read.[^pubmed] Only the trial is cited, because it is the only one that randomised anything.

## Common Marketing Claims

Quarantined, not endorsed. Stratpharma describes Strataderm as "a once-per-day professional scar therapy gel" and "a rapidly drying, non-sticky, transparent, silicone gel formulation for the prevention and treatment of both old and new scars as well as abnormal scars, in the form of hypertrophic scars and keloids", which "dries to form a silicone gel sheet, a protective layer that is gas-permeable and waterproof which hydrates, softens and protects your scar".[^brand] The physical description is checkable; the prevention and treatment claim across old, new, hypertrophic and keloid scars is considerably broader than the one trial read for this page, which studied burn scars in children and found no advantage over a pressure garment.

## Who it is for

Someone managing a scar who wants a silicone product with a real randomised trial attached and understands that the trial's answer was null. The gel is convenient and the evidence for the class, not this product, is what supports using silicone at all.
