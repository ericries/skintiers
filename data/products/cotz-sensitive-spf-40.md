A zinc-only mineral sunscreen at 20%, sold under a registered trademark that calls it the healthier sunscreen. **Bottom line:** the sun protection is real and FDA-regulated, the "healthier" part is a trademark rather than a finding, and the formula is more silicone than cream. Filter evidence is on [[zinc-oxide]].

> **Zinc-only, which is rarer than the mineral label suggests.** The sole active is zinc oxide 20%, with no titanium dioxide and no organic filter anywhere in the formula.[^label] Across the brand's whole range the FDA directory lists 13 products and every one is mineral, either zinc alone or zinc with titanium dioxide, with no organic filters at all.[^openfda] That consistency is unusual and it is verifiable, unlike most "mineral" positioning.

## Summary

Active ingredient zinc oxide 20%, purpose "Sunscreen", an OTC monograph drug listed as NDC 58892-418 by Fallien Cosmeceuticals, Ltd.[^label][^openfda] The printed panel reads "CoTZ ® the HEALTHIER SUNSCREEN ® Sensitive Non-Tinted SPF 40 Gentle Zinc Oxide MINERAL SUNSCREEN FACE & BODY Broad Spectrum SPF 40 Water Resistant (40 minutes) Net Wt. 3.5 oz/100 g".[^label]

**The inactive list is a silicone system, not a moisturiser.** In the order the label gives them: Alkyl (C12-15) Benzoate, Caprylhydroxamic Acid, Caprylyl Glycol, Cetyl PEG/PPG-10/1 Dimethicone, Cyclomethicone 6, Cyclomethicone 5, Dimethicone 100, Dimethicone/Diene Dimethicone Crosspolymer, Edetate Disodium, Methicone, Microcrystalline Wax, PEG-10 Dimethicone, PEG-30 Dipolyhydroxystearate, Polyglyceryl-4 Isostearate, Polyhydroxystearic Acid, Polysorbate 20, Sodium Chloride, Stearyl Dimethicone, Tetrahexyldecyl Ascorbate, Alpha-Tocopherol Acetate, Trolamine, Triethoxycaprylylsilane and Water.[^label] Eight of those are silicones or silicone derivatives, which is what carries a 20% mineral load without it feeling like paste, and it is the opposite construction from [[vanicream-facial-moisturizer-spf-30]]. No fragrance or parfum appears in the list, which is consistent with the Sensitive name.

**No price is quoted here.** The brand's own storefront returned HTTP 441 when its catalogue endpoint was requested, no retailer was consulted, and no paid scraping service was used.

## The Rubric

**Sunburn prevention (Broad Spectrum SPF 40)**
Graded notable on solid evidence. The label's Uses section reads "Helps prevent sunburn", and SPF and Broad Spectrum are FDA-tested categories rather than marketing adjectives, which is the whole reason sunscreen outranks every cosmetic claim on this site.[^label] What zinc oxide does and does not do across the UV band is on [[zinc-oxide]], and the filter comparison is on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

**Reducing skin cancer and photoaging risk**
Graded notable on solid evidence, with the label's own conditional kept attached. The full wording is that if used as directed with other sun protection measures, it "decreases the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging caused by the sun".[^label] That sentence is standard monograph language, it is earned by the category rather than by this product, and the conditional clause is not decoration.

**Staying on through swimming or sweating**
Graded modest on solid evidence, because the tier matters. This is "Water Resistant (40 minutes)", the lower of the two durations FDA allows a sunscreen to claim, not 80 minutes.[^label] The label's reapplication instruction is specific and worth following: after 40 minutes of swimming or sweating, immediately after towel drying, and at least every two hours.[^label]

**Antioxidant benefit from the vitamin C and E esters**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. Tetrahexyldecyl ascorbate and alpha-tocopherol acetate are both in the formula, so the ingredients are genuinely present.[^label] They sit in the inactive list at undisclosed concentrations, and the Drug Facts panel makes no antioxidant claim, so there is nothing to grade beyond their presence. The ester's own case is on [[tetrahexyldecyl-ascorbate]].

## The Evidence

**Twenty percent sits under the ceiling FDA proposed.** In its 2019 proposed rule the agency put forward zinc oxide and titanium dioxide as generally recognised as safe and effective at concentrations up to 25%, so a 20% load is within that, as set out on [[zinc-oxide]].[^openfda] Higher is not automatically better: SPF is measured on the finished formula, and this one is tested at 40.

**What the label does not claim is as useful as what it does.** Checked by reading the Drug Facts panel in full, it makes no claim about blue light, no claim about pollution, no antioxidant claim, no anti-ageing claim beyond the monograph's early-skin-aging conditional, and no reef or environmental claim.[^label] For a brand whose trademark is a comparative health claim, the regulated panel is notably disciplined.

**The warnings are slightly unusual, and worth reading.** Alongside the standard external-use wording the label says "Do not use on damaged or broken skin" and "Stop use and ask a doctor if rash occurs", and directs that children under 6 months ask a doctor.[^label] The damaged-skin instruction is not on every sunscreen label.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. Sunscreen is one step among several; the label itself says it works as directed alongside other sun protection measures. Anyone with a changing or new mole should have it looked at rather than relying on prevention.

## Common Marketing Claims

**"the HEALTHIER SUNSCREEN ®".** The registered trademark symbol is doing the work here. A trademark is a brand asset, not a substantiated comparison, and the panel never says healthier than what.[^label] The defensible version of the claim is narrower and still real: this is a mineral-only formula with a single well-characterised filter and no fragrance.

**"Gentle Zinc Oxide".** Gentle is not a regulated term. What supports the positioning is the absence of fragrance and of organic filters from the list, both checkable.[^label]

**"MINERAL SUNSCREEN", on this product.** Accurate, and unusually so. A 2026 analysis of 150 top-selling sunscreens found nearly half of products labelled mineral also contained organic UV filters, a finding covered on [[zinc-oxide]]; this label's single active and silicone-only inactive list contain none.[^label]
