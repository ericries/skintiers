FOREO's FAQ line is its LED range, and it spans five FDA clearances. **Bottom line:** not one of the facial masks publishes a word about how it was tested, the only FAQ file that does publish is for a **hair-growth** device, and that file says clinical testing was not needed.[^openfda][^k302] Category evidence is on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

> **The one readable file in the family is not a face mask.** FOREO holds 10 clearances and 8 are 510(k) **Statements**, which publish nothing.[^openfda] Within the FAQ line, exactly one is a **Summary**: K242747, the FAQ 302. Reading it shows why that matters less than it sounds. The FAQ 302 is a scalp device "indicated to promote hair growth in males with androgenetic alopecia", built around "20 laser diodes (wavelength 650±10nm) and 20 RED LED beads (640-660nm)", and its own testing section states: "Clinical testing was considered not to be needed for this 510(k)."[^k302] So the single FAQ device whose file you can read is for hair, and it was cleared on nonclinical testing.

## Summary

Five clearances: FAQ 101 (K222012), FAQ 201 and 202 (K240378), FAQ 102 (K240616), FAQ 302 (K242747) and the FAQ LED Panel and Dual LED Panel (K253683).[^openfda] The most recent sets out the facial indication plainly: "FAQ LED Panels are over-the-counter (OTC) devices intended for the treatment of wrinkles with red+ infrared light and yellow + infrared light, and the treatment of mild to moderate acne with blue+infrared light".[^k253683] Note the pairing in that acne claim. No price is quoted on this page; the brand URL tried returned a 404 and no metered lookup was spent on it.

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate acne, with the blue plus infrared mode**
- **Effect size: minimal.** This is the detail that decides the grade. The home-device evidence that earns a Level B and a Grade I recommendation is for blue plus **red** LED.[^home] The cleared mode here is blue plus **infrared**.[^k253683] Infrared is not red, the GRADE review does not grade that pairing, and no source consulted says what it achieves. The device cannot inherit a grade for a combination it does not emit.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** K253683 is a Statement, so there is no public file describing its testing at all.[^openfda]

**Wrinkles, with the red plus infrared and yellow plus infrared modes**
- **Effect size: minimal.** LED for facial rejuvenation is Level B, behind home fractional nonablative lasers at Level A.[^home] Red is in this mode, so the modality grade is at least arguable here, unlike the acne claim. Still nothing measures this device.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** Same Statement filing, same absence of a public file.[^openfda]

**Whether you can check the maker's work**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a disclosure finding.** Eight of ten FOREO clearances publish nothing, and both Summaries are for devices outside the facial LED range: the FAQ 302 hair device and the Luna 4 plus.[^openfda] Compare [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]], where all 14 of that firm's clearances are Summaries, and [[revive-light-therapy-lux-glo]], where all 15 are.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable; read directly from the clearance records.**

## The Evidence

**What a Statement withholds.** A 510(k) Statement certifies that the firm will supply safety and effectiveness information on request; a Summary publishes it. The practical difference, for every FAQ facial mask, is that there is no public document naming the predicate, the emitted intensity, or the testing performed. [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] covers why clearance is a weaker claim than approval; this is the weaker version of the weaker claim, because the file itself is closed.

**What the one open file does show, in its favour.** The FAQ 302 Summary is a real engineering document. It names its predicates, "Primary Predicate: K210169 Hair Growth Device" and "Secondary Predicate: K142573 HairMax LaserComb 41", lists biocompatibility testing to ISO 10993-5 and ISO 10993-10, and electrical safety to the IEC 60601-1 series.[^k302] Its argument for equivalence is that "The number of light sources, treatment area, wavelength, and intensity, are similar to the predicate devices, and do not raise new types of questions with regard to safety and effectiveness."[^k302] That is a legitimate 510(k) argument. It is simply not a clinical result, and the file says so in as many words.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The cleared facial indication stops at mild to moderate acne and at wrinkles; nodular or scarring acne is a reason to see a dermatologist. The FAQ 302 hair indication is narrower still, restricted by the label to specific Hamilton-Norwood and Ludwig-Savin severity grades and to Fitzpatrick skin phototypes I to IV, which is itself worth knowing: deeper phototypes are outside what that device was cleared for.[^k302]
