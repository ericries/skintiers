The FAQ 302 is FOREO's scalp device, cleared in December 2024 under K242747, and the one product in the FAQ line whose regulatory file you can actually read.[^openfda] **Bottom line:** reading it is the point, and what it says is that nobody tested whether this device grows hair. The clearance rests on the device's lights resembling two earlier devices' lights. Why the rest of the FAQ range publishes nothing is on [[foreo-faq-led-masks]] and not repeated here.

> **The file says so itself.** Under the heading "8. Non-Clinical Tests Performed:", the Summary states: "Clinical testing was considered not to be needed for this 510(k)."[^k302] What was submitted instead is biocompatibility and electrical safety testing, plus an equivalence argument: "The number of light sources, treatment area, wavelength, and intensity, are similar to the predicate devices, and do not raise new types of questions with regard to safety and effectiveness."[^k302] That is a valid 510(k) argument. It is not evidence about hair.

## Summary

The clearance letter gives the regulation as "21 CFR 890.5500" with the regulation name "Infrared Lamp", Class II, product code OAP, reviewed by the Physical Medicine panel.[^k302][^openfda] openFDA records the classification name for OAP as "Laser, Comb, Hair".[^classification] So a red-light scalp device is filed under an infrared lamp regulation, inside a category named for laser combs. That naming mismatch is the same pattern as an LED panel cleared under a laser regulation, documented at [[dpl-spotlite]], and it tells you how the agency filed the device rather than what it does.

Hardware, from the device description: a handheld rechargeable square device "with 20 laser diodes (wavelength 650±10nm) and 20 RED LED beads (640-660nm) on the inside surface", Bluetooth-connected to an app.[^k302] No price appears here: the device was cleared in December 2024, and three FOREO product-URL patterns plus the site search all returned 404 on 2026-10-23, so no US retail listing was found and no metered lookup was spent chasing one.

## The Rubric

**Promoting hair growth, the cleared indication**
Graded minimal on preliminary evidence. The indication covers men with androgenetic alopecia at Hamilton-Norwood classifications IIa to V and women with androgenetic alopecia at Ludwig-Savin classifications I to II, restricted to Fitzpatrick skin phototypes I to IV.[^k302] The severity grades and phototype range are reported in words rather than quoted, because the PDF text layer renders the Roman numerals ambiguously. Two things follow. The phototype ceiling means the device was not cleared for the two deepest skin phototypes. And no hair count, shedding measure or photographic assessment appears anywhere in the file.

**Use by women**
Graded minimal on preliminary evidence. Women are genuinely in the cleared wording, which is worth saying because the FDA classification record for product code OAP defines the category as "Promote hair growth in males with androgenic alopecia and norwood hamilton classification of iia to v", with no mention of women.[^classification] The device's own indication is broader than the category description it sits inside, and the women's arm is capped at Ludwig-Savin I to II, meaning early thinning rather than established loss.

## The Evidence

**Both predicates are other companies' devices.** The Summary names "Primary Predicate:" K210169, a "Hair Growth Device", and "Secondary Predicate:" K142573, the "HairMax LaserComb 41".[^k302] That is a stronger position than comparing a device against your own earlier model, which is the pattern on [[nuface-mini-microcurrent-device]]. It still transfers no clinical data, because a 510(k) does not require the predicate's evidence to be reproduced or even cited.

**The published specifications are modest and nearly identical to the predicates.** The comparison table gives the energy per light source as "<5mW" for the FAQ 302 and for both predicates alike, and the treatment area as "Approx. 32cm2" against "Approx. 40cm2" for the HairMax; for the primary predicate the area is listed as "Unknown".[^k302] A comparison table with "Unknown" in it is doing less work than a comparison table normally implies.

**What was actually tested.** Biocompatibility to ISO 10993-5, ISO 10993-10 and ISO 10993-23, and electrical safety and electromagnetic compatibility to the IEC 60601-1 series, including IEC 60601-2-57, the standard for "non-laser light source equipment".[^k302] These establish that the device is safe to hold against a scalp and will not interfere with other equipment. They say nothing about hair.

**The category evidence lives somewhere else.** Grades above cover this device, not low-level light therapy as a treatment. The general case for hair loss, including the drugs with trial evidence behind them, is on [[hair-loss-thinning]]; no page here yet grades red-light scalp devices as a category, and this file supplies nothing toward that.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. Hair loss has causes that need diagnosis rather than a gadget, including thyroid disease, iron deficiency and scarring alopecias such as [[frontal-fibrosing-alopecia]], where the wrong treatment wastes the window in which the condition is still treatable. Sudden or patchy loss is worth a doctor's appointment first.
