Make It Matte SPF 45 is a Broad Spectrum SPF 45 gel sunscreen for the face from Black Girl Sunscreen, marketed as an oil-control, matte-finish option for melanin-rich skin that can be worn under makeup.[^1]

> A Broad Spectrum SPF 45 gel sunscreen built on the same four organic UV filters as Black Girl Sunscreen's Original SPF 30, avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene, but at a higher SPF and with octocrylene raised above the 2.75% in the Original, to 10% of the formula on the manufacturer's own page (5% on a Target listing for the same SKU; see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1][^2] Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. The higher octocrylene share means avobenzone's UVA coverage is better stabilized here than in the brand's original formula. The "matte finish," "no white cast," and hyperpigmentation-prevention framing are the brand's own cosmetic and health-adjacent positioning, addressed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1]

## Summary

Make It Matte SPF 45 is a gel-texture facial sunscreen from Black Girl Sunscreen (BGS), positioned as an oil-control, matte-finish alternative to the brand's original lotion, aimed at oily and melanin-rich skin and marketed as suitable for wear under makeup.[^1]

Per the Drug Facts panel on Black Girl Sunscreen's own product page, the actives are avobenzone 3%, homosalate 10%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 10%, an all-organic (chemical) filter set rated Broad Spectrum SPF 45.[^1] A Target listing for the same 1.7 fl oz SKU independently confirms avobenzone 3%, homosalate 10%, octisalate 5%, and Broad Spectrum SPF 45, but states octocrylene at 5%, not 10%; this page could not resolve that discrepancy and flags it wherever the octocrylene share matters to a grade (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1][^2] That is the same four-filter family used in BGS's [[black-girl-sunscreen-spf-30|Original SPF 30]], but octocrylene is raised well above the Original's 2.75%, and the printed SPF is higher (45 vs. 30).[^1][^2] Broad-spectrum protection against UV, the part of any sunscreen's case with the strongest evidence, is set out at the category level on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

Both sources describe the formula as free of parabens, oxybenzone, and octinoxate, and vegan and cruelty-free; Black Girl Sunscreen's page additionally states 80-minute water resistance, and Target's listing describes it more generally as water-resistant with reapplication every two hours.[^1][^2] The brand markets it as leaving "no white residue" with a "matte finish," and claims the formula (built around aloe, squalane, and shea butter) helps "prevent hyperpigmentation and dark spots."[^1] That cosmetic-elegance and hyperpigmentation-prevention framing is the brand's own claim, addressed separately under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims) and not part of the graded UV-protection evidence below. It is listed at $21.99 on Black Girl Sunscreen's own site and $17.99 on Target (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), both accessed 2026-08-16).[^1][^2]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific product was located; the product-specific facts are its filter composition and label claims, drawn from the manufacturer's own product page and, for corroboration, a Target listing for the same SKU.[^1][^2]

Black Girl Sunscreen's product page states the actives as avobenzone 3%, homosalate 10%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 10%, at Broad Spectrum SPF 45.[^1] Target's Drug Facts listing for the identical 1.7 fl oz SKU independently confirms avobenzone 3%, homosalate 10%, and Broad Spectrum SPF 45, but states octocrylene at 5%, not 10%.[^2] Both figures come from the product's own labeling data rather than a scraped or estimated value, and this page could not determine which is current; the octocrylene share is treated as uncertain wherever it affects a grade below. SPF is a UVB (sunburn) number; the SPF 45 rating certifies UVB strength and does not by itself certify UVA (photoaging) protection, a separate axis described on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

Three of the four filters, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene, are UVB absorbers; UVA coverage rests on avobenzone alone.[^1][^2] [[avobenzone]] at 3% provides genuine UVA coverage once photostabilized, but it degrades under sunlight unless a co-filter holds it stable. [[octocrylene]] serves that stabilizing role here, at either 10% (manufacturer) or 5% (Target) of the formula, both above the 2.75% used in BGS's own [[black-girl-sunscreen-spf-30|Original SPF 30]].[^1][^2] Because the two cited sources disagree on the exact share, this page does not claim it matches or beats the 7% and 9% used to stabilize avobenzone in [[la-roche-posay-anthelios-melt-in-milk-spf-60]] and [[supergoop-unseen-sunscreen-spf-50]], and grades the UVA row at the same tier as the Original rather than the higher tier used for those two products. Measured against the global field, this is still the older US filter set: avobenzone is a weaker, less photostable UVA filter than the modern organic filters (bemotrizinol/Tinosorb S, bisoctrizole/Tinosorb M, the Mexoryl filters) standard in the EU, Korea, and Australia, a comparison detailed on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

> **Bottom line.** The Broad Spectrum SPF 45 rating reflects the same US chemical filter family as BGS's Original SPF 30, with octocrylene raised above the Original's 2.75% (to 10% per the manufacturer, 5% per Target) to stabilize avobenzone's UVA coverage.[^1][^2] That UVA coverage is real but rests on an older filter than the modern-filter products sold outside the US.

How much protection any SPF delivers in practice depends on applying enough product, a point covered generally on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## What's In It

The active filters and inactive-ingredient list as declared on Black Girl Sunscreen's own product page.[^1] A Target listing for the same SKU lists a shorter, partly different base-ingredient set (it includes dimethicone crosspolymer-3 and isododecane, absent from the manufacturer's list, and omits several items the manufacturer lists), alongside its lower octocrylene figure; that divergence is noted here rather than resolved, and the full ingredient list below follows the manufacturer's own page.[^1][^2] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

UV filters (Drug Facts actives):

- [[avobenzone]] 3%, the formula's only dedicated UVA filter
- [[homosalate]] 10%, a UVB filter
- [[octisalate]] 5%, a weak, secondary UVB filter
- [[octocrylene]] 10% per the manufacturer (5% per a Target listing for the same SKU), a UVB filter that here also stabilizes avobenzone

Other functional/notable ingredients: Aloe Barbadensis Leaf Extract, Butyrospermum Parkii (Shea) Butter, Cocos Nucifera (Coconut) Oil, Squalane, and [[tocopherol|Tocopheryl Acetate]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant.[^1]

Base, texture, and preservative system: Cetyl Ethylhexanoate, Diisopropyl Adipate, Diisopropyl Sebacate, Neopentyl Glycol Diethylhexanoate (emollient esters), Silica and Silica Silylate (mattifying/texture agents), and VP/Hexadecene Copolymer (a film former).[^1]

Not in the formula: no oxybenzone and no octinoxate, two organic filters the brand markets itself as free of; no added fragrance appears on the ingredient list.[^1][^2]
