Eucerin Roughness Relief Lotion is a fragrance-free body lotion sold by [[eucerin|Eucerin]] built around 10% urea, alongside an AHA (lactic acid) and a ceramide (Ceramide NP), at 16.9 fl oz for $14.97 as listed by Walmart.[^pdp][^walmart]

> A urea-led body lotion, 10% urea confirmed on both Eucerin's own product page and a Walmart listing, alongside an undisclosed-percentage AHA (lactic acid) and a ceramide (Ceramide NP); neither source shows a Drug Facts Active Ingredient panel, meaning the formula is built as a cosmetic rather than a regulated OTC exfoliant.[^pdp][^walmart] Urea's dual humectant-and-keratolytic evidence lives on [[urea]]; the same "rough, bumpy skin" complaint this lotion targets has its own dedicated urea evidence graded minimal on [[keratosis-pilaris]] (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)).

## Summary

Eucerin markets this lotion for "dry, rough, and bumpy skin," describing a formula "enriched with 10% Urea, AHA, and Ceramide-3" that "provides 72-hour hydration" and is "clinically shown to provide a smoother, healthy-looking appearance."[^pdp] Walmart's listing separately describes a "Triple Moisturizing Complex of 10% Urea, Ceramide and Natural Moisturizing Factors" that "exfoliates skin to smooth rough and bumpy skin."[^walmart] (Walmart's own on-page listing title renders the product as "Roughness Smoothing Body Lotion," though the page's title tag and product URL both name it "Roughness Relief."[^walmart]) The declared ingredient list, matching between Eucerin's own product page and Walmart's listing, names the AHA as Lactic Acid and the ceramide as Ceramide NP; unlike the 10% urea figure, neither carries a stated percentage on either source.[^pdp][^walmart]

Neither Eucerin's own page nor Walmart's listing shows a Drug Facts Active Ingredient panel for this lotion; urea, lactic acid, and Ceramide NP appear only among the ordinary declared ingredients, so all three are formulated as cosmetic ingredients rather than as regulated OTC monograph actives at a disclosed concentration.[^pdp][^walmart] Eucerin's own directions carry a sunburn caution tied to the AHA content: "This product contains Alpha Hydroxy Acid (AHA), which may increase skin's sensitivity to the sun and, particularly, the possibility of sunburn. Use a sunscreen, wear protective clothing, and limit sun exposure while using this product and for a week afterward."[^pdp]

Urea's own dual humectant-and-keratolytic evidence, including its dedicated (minimal-graded) evidence for the "rough, bumpy skin" complaint this lotion targets, is set out on [[urea]] and [[keratosis-pilaris]] rather than restated here; how it compares to the acid-led alternatives [[gold-bond-rough-and-bumpy-daily-skin-therapy]] and [[cerave-sa-body-lotion-for-rough-and-bumpy-skin]] is covered under [The Evidence](#the-evidence).

The base of the formula is built on urea's own Natural Moisturizing Factor components (sodium PCA, arginine HCl, sodium lactate, and several free amino acids) plus shea butter, cetearyl alcohol, and dicaprylyl ether.[^pdp][^walmart] It is fragrance-free and, per Walmart's listing, also paraben- and dye-free, priced at $14.97 for 16.9 fl oz (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-12).[^walmart]

## The Evidence

No independently published trial of this exact lotion was located. Eucerin's own product page claims the lotion is "clinically shown to provide a smoother, healthy-looking appearance," but discloses no study design, sample size, or methodology behind that claim.[^pdp] The product-specific facts available are its declared composition, confirmed 10% urea, an undisclosed-percentage AHA (lactic acid), and Ceramide NP, none formulated in a Drug Facts Active Ingredient panel on either source cited here.[^pdp][^walmart] Urea's own evidence, across concentrations spanning humectant and keratolytic use, is set out on [[urea]]: a Cochrane review found low-concentration urea cream improved dryness and reduced flares against placebo in one isolated eczema trial, and a separate small trial of a 10% urea lotion, matching this product's concentration, improved a composite scaling-roughness score in ichthyosis vulgaris against a glycerol-based comparator. For the specific "rough, bumpy skin" complaint this lotion targets, [[keratosis-pilaris]] grades urea's own dedicated evidence minimal: a single small, uncontrolled, open-label trial of a 20% urea cream, twice this lotion's concentration, found improved skin texture with no comparison group. The acid-led alternatives marketed for the same complaint, [[gold-bond-rough-and-bumpy-daily-skin-therapy]] and [[cerave-sa-body-lotion-for-rough-and-bumpy-skin]], instead lead with lactic acid and salicylic acid, tested head to head in a blinded, controlled trial covered on [[keratosis-pilaris]] and [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]]; this lotion's own lactic acid content is not disclosed at a stated percentage on either source cited here.

> **Bottom line.** No independently reported study isolates this exact lotion's own effect. Its confirmed 10% urea concentration[^pdp][^walmart] fits the general, well-evidenced pattern for moisturizing dry and barrier-impaired skin covered on [[urea]]; the lotion's own dedicated evidence for the "rough, bumpy skin" complaint it targets is graded minimal on [[keratosis-pilaris]], and its undisclosed-percentage lactic acid content has not itself been tested at this concentration.

## What's In It

The full declared ingredient list (INCI), matching between Eucerin's own product page and a corroborating Walmart listing for this SKU.[^pdp][^walmart]

Notable/functional ingredients:

- [[urea|Urea]], stated at 10% in marketing copy on both sources though not disclosed at that percentage in a Drug Facts panel; Eucerin's own ingredient page for urea describes it as "hygroscopic" and "keratolytic," calling it "the gold standard in treating of dry skin"[^pdp]
- Lactic Acid, the AHA named in Eucerin's own "10% Urea, AHA, and Ceramide-3" callout and covered on [[lactic-acid]]; not disclosed at a stated percentage on either source
- [[ceramides|Ceramide NP]], marketed as "Ceramide-3" on Eucerin's own page
- Sodium PCA, Arginine HCl, Sodium Lactate, Histidine HCl, Carnitine, Mannitol, Serine, Sucrose, Citrulline, Glycogen, Alanine, Threonine, Glutamic Acid, and Lysine HCl, the skin's own Natural Moisturizing Factor components, declared on both sources[^pdp][^walmart]

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, glycerin, isopropyl stearate, dicaprylyl ether, glyceryl glucoside, shea butter, nylon-12 (Walmart's declared list only), polyglyceryl-4 diisostearate/polyhydroxystearate/sebacate, cetearyl alcohol, sodium chloride, 1,2-hexanediol and phenoxyethanol (preservatives), and potassium sorbate (preservative).[^pdp][^walmart]

Not on the label: no fragrance is declared, and Walmart's listing states the lotion is "paraben and dye free."[^walmart]
