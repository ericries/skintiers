The reVive Lux Spot is a pocket-sized blue-and-red LED spot device from LED Technologies, Inc., sold at $39.00 and described by the brand as something to "Hold it on the spot for three minutes".[^brand] It is the product the brand's poof! Acne Treatment URL now serves, which is the third reVive naming change this site has had to resolve. The device category is on [[home-led-light-therapy]] and the maker on [[revive-light-therapy]].

> **No FDA clearance names this device, and the brand's claim is carefully worded in a way worth noticing.** A query of all LED Technologies 510(k) records returns **15 clearances, and none is called Lux Spot or poof**.[^openfda] Meanwhile the product copy says it is "powered by clinically tested blue and red LED light therapy".[^brand] Read that precisely: the claim attaches to blue and red LED light therapy as a modality, which has indeed been tested, rather than to this device. It is narrower than it sounds and, unlike some claims this site has examined, it is defensible as written.

## Summary

A twist-to-activate spot device applied directly to a blemish. The brand's directions are to "Wash and dry the treatment area so skin is clean and free of light-blocking ingredients", then "Place the device gently on the skin and hold in place for three minutes", and not to "move or wave the device around".[^brand] Blue plus red is the pairing the 2026 GRADE review graded Level B with a Grade I recommendation for acne, the best grade in its acne section.[^home] What this page cannot tell you is which clearance, if any, covers this unit.

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate acne, as a spot treatment**
- **Effect size: modest, and carried by the modality.** Blue and red LED for acne is Level B in the GRADE review.[^home] The brand's own claim is scoped to that modality rather than to this product, and nothing here measures this device.[^brand]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary for the device.** No clearance document for it could be identified, so unlike the other reVive pages here there is not even a nonclinical file to read.[^openfda]

**Treating one spot at a time**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a form-factor point.** Three minutes per placement on a single blemish is a different proposition from a mask that covers the face in one session.[^brand] The contrast with [[neutrogena-light-therapy-acne-mask-plus]] and [[pulsaderm-acne-mask-28]], both ten-minute full-face devices, is about how much skin gets treated per session rather than about potency.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable; brand-stated directions.**

## The Evidence

**What the clearance record does and does not contain.** LED Technologies has 15 FDA 510(k) clearances, spanning the dpl line (THERAPY SYSTEM, NUVE, II Panel, SpectraLite, IIa Panel, SpotLite, Faceware) and the reVive line (Perioral, LED Ultrasonic Wrinkle System, LED Cleansing System, Essentials twice, and the Wrinkle and Acne LED Device).[^openfda] None is named Lux Spot or poof. There is a dpl SpotLite cleared in 2019 under K183118, which is a spot-format device from the same company, but no source consulted here establishes that it is this product under a different name, and this page does not assume it.[^openfda]

**Credit where it is due on disclosure.** All 15 of those clearances are filed as 510(k) **Summaries** rather than Statements.[^openfda] That is the same pattern as [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]] and the opposite of [[foreo-espada-blue-light-pen]], where 8 of 10 publish nothing. When LED Technologies clears a device, you can read what it submitted. The issue here is matching a retail name to one of those files, not a company withholding them.

**The naming problem is now a pattern, not an incident.** The device cleared as K223482 does not appear in the current range at all. The Essentials clearances cover a wrinkle configuration that is not sold. And the poof! URL now serves Lux Spot. Three separate mismatches between cleared names and shelf names, which is why [[revive-light-therapy]] tells readers to match stated wavelengths and indication rather than brand.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The cleared indications across this category stop at mild to moderate acne; scarring, nodular or severe acne is a reason to see a dermatologist rather than buy a device.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"clinically tested blue and red LED light therapy"**. Accurate as scoped.[^brand] The modality has been tested; the sentence does not claim this device was, and the difference is the whole point.
- **"treats blemishes the moment they appear"** and **"help reduce acne-causing bacteria and clear mild-to-moderate acne"**. Brand wording.[^brand] The mechanism is the one the GRADE review grades at Level B for the modality.[^home] No device-level outcome is cited by the brand or found here.
- **"FDA Cleared"**, used as a browse category on the brand's site.[^brand] True of the company's range generally; this page could not identify a clearance for this particular device.[^openfda]
