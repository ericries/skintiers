An over-the-counter LED face mask, cleared by the FDA in June 2025 under product code OHS.[^k250966] **Bottom line:** the clearance is real and says less than the marketing around these devices implies, because the file itself records that no clinical testing was done and that the device delivers the same dose, at the same intensity, for the same time, as the mask it was compared against.

> **"Clinical testing is not needed for this 510(k)."**[^k250966] That sentence is from the manufacturer's own summary, which adds that the non-clinical testing, meaning electrical safety, electromagnetic compatibility, photobiological safety and battery safety, "is sufficient to demonstrate that the device can be used safely and effectively".[^k250966] A 510(k) establishes that a device resembles one already sold, which is a different question from whether it works.

## Summary

### Who actually makes it

This is worth stating because the brand name is on six different FDA clearances held by three different companies. The mask was submitted by **Shenzhen Kaiyan Medical Equipment Co., Ltd** of Shenzhen, China, with **CurrentBody.Com Ltd** of Stockport, United Kingdom named in the file as the distributor.[^k250966] Its predicate is a mask from a third firm, Light Tree Ventures Europe B.V.[^k250966] The sibling [[currentbody-skin-rf]] comes from a fourth, El Global Trade of Israel.[^clearances]

CurrentBody is a retailer and brand that badges hardware built by contract manufacturers, each holding its own clearance. That is ordinary in this category, and it means a claim proven for one CurrentBody device tells you nothing about another.

## The Rubric

**What it is cleared to do, in the file's own words.** The device "is an over-the-counter device that is intended for the use in the treatment of full-face wrinkles."[^k250966] Product code OHS is one of the FDA codes that actually defines its own scope, as [[home-devices-by-whether-the-code-defines-the-claim]] sets out, so a wrinkle claim here is checkable rather than vague.

**What the clearance did not test.** No clinical data. The summary's clinical section reads in full that clinical testing is not needed and that the bench testing suffices.[^k250966] Whether the mask reduces anyone's wrinkles was not a question the clearance asked.

**More LEDs, identical light.** This is the number most worth understanding. Against its predicate the mask is recorded as "Different" on exactly one performance row, LED count, with "633nm:110" and "830nm:110" against the predicate's 66 at each wavelength.[^k250966] Every other row is marked "Same": total intensity "30 mW/cm²" on both sides, dose per session 18 J/cm² on both sides, and "10 minutes per treatment" at "5 times weekly for 6 weeks" on both.[^k250966] Doubling the diode count while holding irradiance and dose constant spreads the same light over the same area. It is a coverage and uniformity change, not a stronger treatment.

**Wavelengths.** Red at 633 nm and infrared at 830 nm, against the predicate's 630 plus or minus 5 nm and 830 nm, which the file marks "Similar".[^k250966] What those wavelengths are and are not shown to do belongs to [[home-led-light-therapy]] and [[led-dose-j-per-cm2-and-wavelength]], not here.

**Price: not recorded.** The brand site is a JavaScript application that did not serve a usable page, and the single gated attempt at a paid fetch was refused by the provider before it ran. Rather than guess, this page carries no price. The sibling RF device's page records a bundle that includes an LED mask, but a bundle price is not this product's price and is not used as one.

## The Evidence

None specific to this device. The 510(k) contains no clinical study, and this page cites no trial of the MK-90H because none appears in the file.[^k250966] That is not the same as saying red light does nothing; it means the evidence lives at the ingredient level, on [[home-led-light-therapy]], and anyone buying this mask is buying a delivery system for a dose that its own paperwork says matches a competitor's.

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand marketing copy was retrieved for this page, so there is nothing to quarantine from the brand's own site. The claim that *is* documented is the regulatory one above, and it is narrower than the category's advertising usually sounds: treatment of full-face wrinkles, with no clinical testing behind the clearance.

## Who it is for

Someone who wants an at-home red and near-infrared mask and values the FDA clearance as evidence that the device is electrically safe and photobiologically within limits, which is what it genuinely shows. Anyone buying it because the clearance implies proven wrinkle reduction is buying something the file does not claim.
