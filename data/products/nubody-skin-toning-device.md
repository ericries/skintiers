The NuBODY is a $399 four-electrode microcurrent device for arms, abs, thighs and glutes, and its FDA file is the most numerically detailed in this category. **Bottom line:** it was cleared by comparing its electricity against the company's own *face* device, and those published numbers show it running more than three times that device's average power density. Why a cleared stimulation claim is not a toning claim is argued at [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]].

> **The name makes a claim the clearance does not.** It is sold as the NuBODY Skin Toning Device, and the cleared indication reads "NuBODY Skin Toning Device is intended for body skin stimulation and is indicated for over-the-counter cosmetic use."[^k171588] Stimulation is what was cleared. Toning is in the product's name.

## Summary

Cleared 2017-07-31 as K171588, a Traditional 510(k) filed as a Summary under product code NFO.[^openfda] Four chrome-plated spherical electrodes deliver microcurrent, against two on the facial predicate, and the comparison table lists the anatomic site as "Areas of the body other than" the face.[^k171588] The brand sells it at $399.00 in a starter kit with an activator gel, instructing users to "glide the device in slow, upward motions for 5 minutes until the built-in auto timer shuts off".[^brand]

## The Rubric

**Body skin stimulation, the thing actually cleared**
- **Effect size: minimal.** The cleared words name an action. What the file demonstrates is that this device's electrical output resembles an already-cleared device's: "Nonclinical testing was performed to demonstrate that the NuBODY product met design specifications and is substantially equivalent to the NuFACE Plus predicate device", and "The testing consisted of the evaluation of Output Waveform Characteristics and Output Energy Characteristics."[^k171588]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** No body outcome appears anywhere in the filing. The predicate is "510(k) Number: K103472", the firm's own "NuFACE® Plus", a facial device, so a body device reached market by resembling a face device.[^k171588]

**Firming and smoothing the appearance of body skin, the thing sold**
- **Effect size: minimal, graded on absence of evidence.** The brand's headline is that "FDA-cleared microcurrent firms, smooths, and tones arms, abs, thighs, and glutes", "reducing the look of dimpled, crepey skin with consistent use".[^brand] Nothing consulted here measured firmness, smoothness or dimpling for this device.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** There is no modality grade to borrow either: the 2026 GRADE review this site leans on for home devices covers LED and radiofrequency and does not assess microcurrent.[^grade]

**Output, which this filing actually publishes**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a record finding, and an unusually good one.** Unlike every LED filing covered here, this one prints its numbers. Against the facial predicate it lists maximum output current "900 µA @ 500Ω" versus "400 µA @ 500Ω", net charge "54 µC" versus "24 µC" per pulse, maximum average power density 4.18 versus 1.12 mW/cm², and maximum phase charge 1.08 versus 0.48 mC/Burst, while waveform, shape, maximum voltage "28 VDC", pulse width and frequency "Approximately 8.3 Hz" are identical.[^k171588]
- **Evidence quality: not applicable.** One caution against reading the gap too hard: the current *density* figures are close, "0.468 mA/cm2" against "0.419 mA/cm2", so the two density measures in this table point differently.[^k171588] This page reports both and does not offer a mechanism to reconcile them.

## The Evidence

**What substantial equivalence means here, in numbers.** A device delivering more than twice the current and charge, and more than three times the maximum average power density, was found substantially equivalent to its predecessor on the strength of bench testing alone.[^k171588] That is not a scandal; it is how the pathway works, and this filing is the clearest quantified illustration of it this site has. The general point is at [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]].

**Credit where it is due on the wording.** The brand's own benefit list is careful, reading "Firms the appearance of body skin", "Tightens and smooths the appearance of body skin" and "Reduces look of dimpled skin".[^brand] Appearance language is the honest register for a cosmetic device. Checked by string search of the cached page, the word cellulite does not appear anywhere on it, which is a restraint many competitors in this category do not show.

**The claim it does make is about the hardest thing to change.** The stated target is "dimpled, crepey skin", and crepey skin is where a dermatologist's framing is worth reading before buying anything: the card on [[skin-firmness-elasticity]] separates dry skin from crepey skin from loose skin and is blunt that past a point no topical or device overcomes excess skin.

**It is regulated as a pain-relief device, like its siblings.** Regulation 21 CFR 882.5890, regulation name "Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulator for Pain Relief", with the classification name given as "Stimulator, Transcutaneous" Electrical, "Aesthetic Purposes" and the medical specialty as Neurology.[^k171588] That mismatch is set out with its sources on [[nuface-mini-microcurrent-device]] and is not re-argued here.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. This is a cosmetic device cleared under a nerve-stimulation regulation; anyone with a pacemaker or another implanted electrical device, or who is pregnant, should read the manufacturer's contraindications rather than a general page before using any microcurrent device.

## Common Marketing Claims

Quarantined from the graded assessment above. All are the brand's own wording, cached 2026-10-22.[^brand]

- "FDA-cleared microcurrent firms, smooths, and tones arms, abs, thighs, and glutes". Accurate that clearances exist; none of those three verbs appears in the cleared indication.
- "reducing the look of dimpled, crepey skin with consistent use". The strongest claim, and the one with nothing measured behind it here.
- "Firms the appearance of body skin", "Tightens and smooths the appearance of body skin", "Reduces look of dimpled skin". Appearance-register versions of the same three claims.
