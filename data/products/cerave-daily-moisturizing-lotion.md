CeraVe Daily Moisturizing Lotion is a lightweight, oil-free, fragrance-free lotion for normal to dry skin whose ingredient list includes three ceramides (ceramide NP, AP, EOP) and hyaluronic acid, sold inexpensively for everyday face and body use.[^1]

> A cheap, fragrance-free everyday lotion for normal to dry skin. Keeping dry or barrier-impaired skin moisturized is one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines, and this lotion is a faithful example of that kind of formula, pairing humectants and an occlusive with three ceramides on top of the category case (see [[moisturizing]]). No trial has tested this exact lotion; the ceramide-specific angle rests on general ingredient evidence rather than a product-specific outcome.[^1][^2]

## Summary

CeraVe Daily Moisturizing Lotion is a thin, oil-free lotion marketed for everyday use on normal to dry skin, for both face and body. Its main job is a health one: slowing the water skin loses through its surface and supporting a dry or damaged barrier. It does that with a familiar combination of humectants (glycerin and sodium hyaluronate, [[hyaluronic-acid]]), an occlusive ([[dimethicone]]), and three [[ceramides]], the fatty molecules that make up part of the skin's own barrier. Keeping dry or barrier-impaired skin moisturized this way is one of the best-evidenced routines in dermatology; that general case is set out on [[moisturizing]].

What sets it apart from a plain lotion is the ceramide content and the dermatologist backing, not a unique active. CeraVe's own product page quotes Dr. Latanya Benjamin, a board-certified dermatologist, saying: "I recommend CeraVe Moisturizing Lotion for daily use on even the most sensitive skin types because it contains no sulfates or fragrance and helps restore and protect dry skin."[^1] CeraVe also markets the lotion as "Accepted by the National Eczema Association."[^1]

The thinner part of the case is the ceramide angle specifically. No published trial tests this exact lotion. The closest controlled test of a ceramide-dominant cream ([studied on the ceramides page](#the-evidence)) improved a barrier measurement but did not reduce eczema severity more than a plain vehicle. Those product-specific limits are in [The Evidence](#the-evidence), and the general case for each active is on [[ceramides]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]]. It is inexpensive: the manufacturer's page lists it at $14.99 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-13).

## The Evidence

Keeping dry or barrier-impaired skin moisturized reduces flares and supports the skin barrier, one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines; that general case, including the roles of occlusives, humectants, and barrier lipids like [[ceramides]], is set out on [[moisturizing]]. No published trial tests this exact lotion. Its case rests on the general evidence for its notable actives, applied to this formulation.

For the ceramides, the closest controlled test is a manufacturer-sponsored randomized trial of a *different* brand's ceramide-dominant cream, which improved a barrier measure (transepidermal water loss) but did not reduce eczema severity more than a plain vehicle; that trial, its industry funding, and its limits are set out on [[ceramides]].

For the hyaluronic acid, a placebo-controlled trial found topical hyaluronic acid raised skin-hydration readings across the molecular weights tested; that trial and the penetration data behind it are set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. This product's ingredient list does not specify which molecular weight it uses.

> **Bottom line.** No study has isolated this lotion's own effect. Its case rests on general evidence for ceramides and hyaluronic acid individually, plus a faithful, dermatologist-marketed formulation of both together.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on the manufacturer's product page.[^1] Target's listing for the 16 fl oz size declares a differing formulation: it adds methylparaben and propylparaben as preservatives and hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid in place of sodium hyaluronate, and omits phenoxyethanol, ethylhexylglycerin, and tocopherol.[^1][^2] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

Barrier and hydration ingredients:

- three [[ceramides]] (ceramide NP, AP, EOP), plus cholesterol and [[phytosphingosine]], the formula's barrier lipids
- [[glycerin]] and sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]), humectants
- [[dimethicone]], an occlusive that slows water loss; declared without a Drug Facts panel or stated percentage, a cosmetic ingredient here rather than an OTC skin-protectant active
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant, on the manufacturer's list only

Base, texture, and preservative system (manufacturer's page): water, caprylic/capric triglyceride (emollient), cetearyl alcohol and cetyl alcohol (emulsifiers), phenoxyethanol (preservative), polysorbate 20, ceteareth-20, behentrimonium methosulfate and sodium lauroyl lactylate (emulsifiers), polyglyceryl-3 diisostearate (emulsifier), ethylhexylglycerin (preservative), potassium phosphate and dipotassium phosphate (pH buffers), disodium EDTA (chelator), and xanthan gum and carbomer (thickeners).[^1]

The formula contains no [[niacinamide]] (unlike CeraVe's [[cerave-pm-facial-moisturizing-lotion|PM Facial Moisturizing Lotion]]), no [[petrolatum]] (unlike CeraVe's thicker [[cerave-moisturizing-cream|Moisturizing Cream]]), no added fragrance, and no drying alcohol (the cetearyl and cetyl alcohols are emollient fatty alcohols, not the drying kind).[^1]
