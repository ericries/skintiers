CeraVe Hydrating Mineral Sunscreen SPF 30 Face is a mineral (zinc oxide and titanium dioxide) face sunscreen lotion from [[cerave]], marketed as a lightweight, non-greasy formula with three ceramides and niacinamide.[^1][^2]

> A Broad Spectrum SPF 30 sunscreen built entirely on mineral filters, titanium dioxide 6% and zinc oxide 5%, per its FDA drug label.[^2] That is a lower filter load than the sibling [[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50]]'s own FDA label, titanium dioxide 9% and zinc oxide 7%. Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; a mineral-only filter set avoids the photostability issues of avobenzone, the older UVA filter in most US chemical sunscreens, though it commonly leaves a visible white cast that a chemical formula does not. The formula also carries [[ceramides]] and [[niacinamide]], ingredients aimed at the skin barrier rather than UV protection.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Hydrating Mineral Sunscreen SPF 30 Face is a facial sunscreen from CeraVe, a US skincare brand built around ceramide-based barrier repair and owned by L'Oreal.[^1] Its core purpose is the same as any sunscreen's: broad-spectrum UV protection is the best-evidenced way to slow photoaging and lower the risk of sun-related skin cancer, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

Per its FDA drug label on DailyMed, the actives are titanium dioxide 6% and zinc oxide 5%, a 100% mineral filter set.[^2] That is a lower filter load than CeraVe's own [[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50]], which carries titanium dioxide 9% and zinc oxide 7% per its own FDA drug label, consistent with the two products' different SPF ratings. CeraVe markets this SPF 30 version as a "lightweight, non-greasy feel" formula, distinguishing it from the plain Hydrating Mineral Sunscreen SPF 50 line.[^2] Both filters are physically opaque mineral particles rather than organic (chemical) filters like avobenzone; [[zinc-oxide]] and [[titanium-dioxide]] each have their own evidence and comparator pages, linked above and in [What's In It](#whats-in-it). The formula also lists niacinamide, three ceramides (Ceramide NP, Ceramide AP, Ceramide EOP), and sodium hyaluronate on its FDA-filed inactive-ingredient list, ingredients aimed at hydration and the skin barrier rather than UV protection.[^2]

CeraVe's own product page carries a quote from a named dermatologist recommending mineral sunscreens generally and describing the CeraVe Hydrating Sunscreen line's ceramide/niacinamide/hyaluronic-acid blend, brand-sourced marketing content rather than an independent product review ([quarantined under Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^1] No independent, product-specific study of this exact formula was located; the graded evidence above rests on the filter composition and category-level UV-filter evidence, not a trial of this SKU.

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific product was located; the product-specific facts are its filter composition and FDA label rating.

The FDA drug label on DailyMed (label version 3, published January 1, 2024, for "CeraVe Developed with Dermatologists Hydrating Sunscreen SPF 30 Face Lightweight, Non-Greasy Feel 3 Essential Ceramides Niacinamide Mineral Sunscreen," labeler L'Oreal USA Products Inc.) lists the active ingredients as titanium dioxide 6% and zinc oxide 5%.[^2] The Uses section states the product "helps prevent sunburn" and, "if used as directed with other sun protection measures," "decreases the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging caused by the sun," the FDA-mandated language reserved for Broad Spectrum SPF 15+ products.[^2] CeraVe's own product page describes the product as Broad Spectrum SPF 30.[^1]

Both actives are mineral (inorganic) filters rather than the organic filters, such as avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene, common in other US sunscreens. [[zinc-oxide]] is photostable with genuine long-wave UVA reach; [[titanium-dioxide]] contributes UVB and shorter-wave UVA coverage. Neither filter needs a photostabilizer the way avobenzone does, a comparison detailed on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Measured against the global field, mineral filters remain a genuine broad-spectrum option, though a formula's real-world protection still depends on how evenly and how much of it is applied, and mineral formulas are more prone to a visible white cast than a chemical formula at the same SPF.

> **Bottom line.** The FDA-registered Broad Spectrum SPF 30 rating reflects a 100% mineral filter set, titanium dioxide 6% and zinc oxide 5%, lower concentrations than CeraVe's own SPF 50 sibling and consistent with the lower SPF number.[^2] The formula's niacinamide, ceramides, and hyaluronic acid are barrier and hydration ingredients layered on top of, not evidence for, the UV protection itself.[^2]

CeraVe's directions call to apply the product "liberally 15 minutes before sun exposure" and to reapply "at least every 2 hours, immediately after towel drying," matching the FDA label's directions for sunscreen use.[^1][^2] How much protection any SPF delivers in practice depends on applying enough product, a point covered generally on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].
