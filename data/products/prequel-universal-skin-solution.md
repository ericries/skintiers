Prequel Universal Skin Solution is a fragrance-free, alcohol-free hypochlorous acid (HOCl) spray for face and body, made by Prequel, a skincare brand whose product page identifies its founder, Dr. Samantha Ellis, as a dermatologist.[^pdp]

> A three-ingredient hypochlorous acid spray marketed to calm and purify sensitized, reactive, or acne-prone skin. Prequel's founder, dermatologist Dr. Samantha Ellis, is quoted on the product page saying "Hypochlorous is a staple in my practice. I use it more than any other product. I love using it to prep the skin without irritation or dryness."[^pdp] The brand's own claims that the spray is "clinically proven to perform well on rosacea-prone skin" and "optically reduces redness" name no study, sample size, or independent source, so [they are reported here as marketing claims, not established findings](#common-marketing-claims).[^pdp] Topical hypochlorous acid's evidence for acne or redness specifically is preliminary; see [[hypochlorous-acid]]. It is inexpensive for the category, listed by the manufacturer at $17 for the 4 fl oz Standard size (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^pdp]

## Summary

Universal Skin Solution is a spray-format hypochlorous acid product for face and body. Prequel's own ingredient list names only three components: water, hypochlorous acid, and sodium chloride.[^pdp] HOCl sprays are generally produced by electrolyzing saline; see [[hypochlorous-acid]]. The manufacturer does not disclose the HOCl concentration; the product page states only a formulated pH of "5.5 +/-".[^pdp] It is fragrance-free and alcohol-free, and Prequel markets it as safe for eczema-prone skin and all skin types.[^pdp] Prequel's directions describe spraying it "directly onto dry, cleansed skin as needed morning and evening" and letting skin dry before applying moisturizer.[^pdp]

The product's most distinctive claim, that it is "clinically proven to perform well on rosacea-prone skin," and its companion claim that it "optically reduces redness," are stated on the brand's own product page without naming a study, a sample size, an investigator, or a publication.[^pdp] Prequel separately lists Universal Skin Solution among its products carrying the National Rosacea Society's Seal of Acceptance, a designation whose meaning and independent confirmation are set out on [[prequel]].

Hypochlorous acid is used in skincare as an antimicrobial and calming agent; the general evidence for it is covered on [[hypochlorous-acid]]. Evidence specific to topical acne or redness use is preliminary, and no independent, product-specific study of Universal Skin Solution was located.

The product is inexpensive for the category. Prequel lists the 4 fl oz Standard size at $17, with an 8 fl oz Jumbo size sold at a discount for buying two (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^pdp] Target also carries the product under a matching name and size, corroborating that it is a real, retail-stocked SKU.[^target]

## The Evidence

Hypochlorous acid sprays are marketed in skincare as antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory agents, generally produced by electrolyzing saline; the general evidence for the ingredient is covered on [[hypochlorous-acid]]. No independent, product-specific study of Universal Skin Solution was located. Prequel's own product page states the spray is "Clinically proven to perform well on rosacea-prone skin" and that it "Optically reduces redness," without naming a study, sample size, investigator, or publication.[^pdp] The brand's FAQ separately states the product "tested this product for eye irritation and found it safe for use around the eyes," again without naming an outside testing facility or methodology.[^pdp]

> Bottom line: the brand's clinical and eye-safety claims for this spray are stated without a named study, sample size, or independent source. Topical hypochlorous acid's evidence base for acne or redness specifically is preliminary; see [[hypochlorous-acid]].
