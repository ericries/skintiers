Prequel AM/PM Modern Moisturizer is a fragrance-free face moisturizer built around ceramides, sodium hyaluronate, and glycerin, made by Prequel, a skincare brand whose product page identifies its founder, Dr. Samantha Ellis, as a dermatologist.[^pdp]

> A ceramide-and-humectant moisturizer meant for both morning and night use, formulated with five ceramide types (NP, AS, NS, EOP, AP), sodium hyaluronate, and glycerin alongside jojoba seed oil, squalane, and dimethicone.[^pdp] Prequel markets the moisturizer as carrying the National Rosacea Society, National Eczema Association, and National Psoriasis Foundation seals; those are the brand's own claims on its product page and are [reported here as marketing, not independently confirmed for this SKU](#common-marketing-claims).[^pdp] It is inexpensive for the category: Prequel lists the 2 fl oz bottle at $18.00 and Target lists the same size at $17.99 (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^pdp][^target]

## Summary

Prequel AM/PM Modern Moisturizer is a mid-weight, fragrance-free face cream that Prequel positions for use both morning and night.[^pdp] Its formula leads with barrier and hydration ingredients: dimethicone as an occlusive, glycerin plus several polyglyceride humectants and sodium hyaluronate for water-binding, and five separate ceramide types (Ceramide NP, AS, NS, EOP, and AP) alongside jojoba seed oil and squalane as emollients.[^pdp] Those ingredient classes carry the general, well-evidenced case for moisturizing dry or barrier-impaired skin, set out on [[moisturizing]].

What distinguishes this formula from a single-ceramide moisturizer such as [[first-aid-beauty-ultra-repair-cream|First Aid Beauty Ultra Repair Cream]] is its five-ceramide blend, closer in count to the multi-ceramide systems used in the clinical literature on [[ceramides]], though no trial has tested this exact formula. Sodium hyaluronate, the salt form of hyaluronic acid, is a reliably evidenced humectant for raising skin hydration readings, detailed on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. Prequel's own site does not disclose the ingredient's molecular weight, a detail that governs how deep it penetrates.[^pdp]

Prequel markets the moisturizer as carrying the National Rosacea Society, National Eczema Association, and National Psoriasis Foundation seals, along with "dermatologist-developed & tested," hypoallergenic, and allergy-tested claims.[^pdp] Those rest on Prequel's own product page; Target's listing corroborates the general positioning (barrier strengthening, ceramides, "safe for sensitive and eczema-prone skin") but does not independently confirm the specific society seals, so [they are catalogued as marketing claims below](#common-marketing-claims) rather than folded into the grades above.[^pdp][^target]

It is inexpensive for the category. Prequel lists the 2 fl oz bottle at $18.00, and Target lists the same size at $17.99 (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^pdp][^target]

## The Evidence

No published trial tests this exact formula. Its case rests on the general evidence for its ingredient classes. Occlusive-plus-humectant moisturizing has strong category-level evidence for dry and barrier-impaired skin, set out on [[moisturizing]]. Within that, ceramides are a genuine structural lipid of the skin barrier, and a randomized trial of a ceramide-dominant cream improved a barrier measurement (transepidermal water loss) over an inactive base but did not reduce eczema severity any more than that base; that trial and its limits are set out on [[ceramides]]. This formula's five ceramide types (NP, AS, NS, EOP, AP) are a broader set than a single-ceramide formula, though the trial cited on the ceramides page did not test this specific combination or ratio.[^pdp] Hyaluronic acid, here as sodium hyaluronate, has placebo-controlled trial evidence for raising skin-hydration readings across the molecular weights tested; the general case, and the caveat that only lower-molecular-weight versions penetrate past the stratum corneum, is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. Prequel's product page does not state which molecular weight its sodium hyaluronate is.[^pdp]

> **Bottom line.** No study has isolated this exact moisturizer's own effect. Its case rests on the general evidence for occlusive-and-humectant moisturizing, for ceramides as a barrier lipid, and for hyaluronic acid as a hydration humectant, covered on [[moisturizing]], [[ceramides]], and [[hyaluronic-acid]].

## What's In It

The ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^pdp]

Barrier and hydration ingredients:

- [[ceramides|Ceramide NP, Ceramide AS, Ceramide NS, Ceramide EOP, and Ceramide AP]], five ceramide types
- [[glycerin]] and Oleic/Linoleic/Linolenic Polyglycerides, Polyglyceryl-6 Laurate, and Polyglyceryl-6 Oleate, humectants
- Sodium [[hyaluronic-acid|Hyaluronate]], a humectant
- Dimethicone, an occlusive that slows water loss
- Simmondsia Chinensis (Jojoba) Seed Oil and Squalane, emollients
- Acetyl Heptapeptide-4, a peptide the manufacturer names as part of its "ferment-peptide complex"[^pdp]
- Bacillus/Sea Salt Ferment Filtrate, a fermentation-derived ingredient the manufacturer names as part of the same complex[^pdp]

Base, texture, and preservative system: Water (Aqua), Glyceryl Stearate, Cetyl Alcohol, Stearyl Alcohol, Saccharide Isomerate, Lecithin, Sorbitan Oleate, Hydrogenated Lecithin, Linoleic Acid, Stearic Acid, Hydroxyacetophenone, Acrylamide/Sodium Acryloyldimethyltaurate Copolymer, Linolenic Acid, Palmitic Acid, Isohexadecane, Boron Nitride, 1,2-Hexanediol, Caprylyl Glycol, Chlorphenesin, Tocopheryl Acetate, Acrylates/C10-30 Alkyl Acrylate Crosspolymer, Dicaprylyl Ether, Polysorbate 80, Aluminum Starch Octenylsuccinate, Sodium Hydroxide, Citric Acid, Phenoxyethanol, Myristic Acid, Tocopherol, Sodium Citrate.[^pdp]

Not in the formula: no added fragrance; Prequel states the formula is fragrance-free.[^pdp]
