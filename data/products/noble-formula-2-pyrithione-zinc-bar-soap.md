Noble Formula's 2% ZnP Bar Soap is a handmade cleansing bar built around pyrithione zinc 2%, sold by the small skincare brand Noble Formula as a face, body, and scalp wash for flaking, itching, and irritated skin.[^brand]

> A cosmetic soap, not an FDA-registered drug: no Noble Formula pyrithione zinc product turns up in a DailyMed search, and the brand's own copy describes pyrithione zinc as "reputed to have antifungal and antibacterial properties" rather than citing an FDA Drug Facts label.[^dailymed][^brand] That is a real difference from monograph-registered 2% pyrithione zinc bars like [[vanicream-z-bar-pyrithione-zinc]]. The active is the same well-studied antifungal covered on [[zinc-pyrithione]], and a rinse-off bar is a reasonable, low-risk way to apply it to the face, chest, and back for seborrheic dermatitis or dandruff-prone skin, but brief wash-off contact time means results depend on regular, consistent use, and the "eczema" claim on the label is more about a gentle, oil-rich base than any eczema-specific evidence.

## Summary

This is Noble Formula's original "Emu Oil" 2% ZnP Bar Soap, a small-batch, handmade bar built on saponified olive oil (infused with calendula) and emu oil, oat flour, and pyrithione zinc 2%.[^brand] It sells for $12.99 per 3.25 oz bar ($4.00/oz), or $29.99 for a 3-pack, direct from the brand's site.[^brand] Noble Formula also sells the same 2% pyrithione zinc active in several other bases (vegan argan oil, vegan mango-and-cocoa-butter, activated-charcoal-plus-emu, activated-charcoal-plus-shea-and-tea-tree, sunflower-and-shea), all at the same $12.99 price, so shoppers searching for "Noble Formula 2% Pyrithione Zinc Bar Soap" may land on any of these; this page covers the original Emu Oil formula, named "NF Original Emu Bar Soap" in the brand's own product URL and rated 4.79 out of 5 from 14 reviews on the brand's site.[^brand]

Pyrithione zinc's general evidence sits at the ingredient-class level, set out on [[zinc-pyrithione]] and ranked moderate on [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]], a step below ketoconazole and selenium sulfide. No trial of this specific bar was located, so its grade here rests on that active plus the fact that it is a brief-contact wash rather than a leave-on product.

## The Evidence

Two health-adjacent uses, graded above:

**Seborrheic dermatitis and dandruff.** Pyrithione zinc is FDA-recognized as an anti-dandruff and anti-seborrheic-dermatitis active at 0.3 to 2 percent in wash-off products under the same OTC monograph, and the class evidence and its limits are set out on [[seborrheic-dermatitis]] and [[dandruff]].[^fda] But that monograph status attaches to the active, not automatically to every product containing it: this particular bar does not carry an FDA Drug Facts label on DailyMed, so it is marketed and worded as a cosmetic soap ("reputed to have antifungal...properties") rather than as a registered OTC drug the way [[vanicream-z-bar-pyrithione-zinc]] is.[^dailymed][^brand] As a rinse-off face-and-body bar rather than a leave-on scalp shampoo, it is a practical way to apply the active broadly, but the wash-off contact time is brief compared with a leave-on cream, so consistent, regular use matters more than it would for a leave-on formulation.

**Eczema-prone skin.** The brand markets this soap for skin affected by "conditions such as seborrheic dermatitis, eczema, psoriasis, atopy and dry skin."[^brand] For eczema specifically, most of the plausible benefit is from using a gentle, sulfate-light, fragrance-free cleanser instead of a stripping soap (this bar is saponified oils, not a syndet, so it is still a true soap and can run alkaline, which is worth flagging for very dry or barrier-compromised skin); pyrithione zinc's antimicrobial action against Malassezia and Staphylococcus aureus may help with the microbial component some eczema flares involve, but it is not a treatment for the underlying eczema barrier defect. See [[atopic-dermatitis|eczema]] for the actual evidence-ranked eczema treatments; this bar is a cleansing adjunct, not a substitute for them.

> **Bottom line.** A well-known, handmade 2% pyrithione zinc cleansing bar in a simple, olive-and-emu-oil soap base. Judge it as a reasonable, brief-contact delivery of a moderate-tier antifungal for seborrheic dermatitis and dandruff, sold and worded as a cosmetic soap rather than a registered OTC drug, not a stronger or better-verified treatment than the FDA-monograph-labeled 2% pyrithione zinc bars or the leave-on ketoconazole/selenium-sulfide options ranked above it.[^brand][^dailymed][^fda]

## What's In It

As a cosmetic soap rather than an FDA Drug Facts product, Noble Formula lists a single combined ingredient list rather than separating "active" from "inactive." Verbatim from the brand's product page: "Saponified oils of: olive (infused with calendula) & emu; avena sativa kernel flour (oat flour), pyrithione zinc (ZnP) 2%."[^brand]

This is a true saponified (cold-process-style) soap built on olive and emu oil, not a syndet bar like Vanicream's. The brand states its soaps are "free from coconut and mineral oils, artificial fragrance, artificial color, sulfates, parabens, phthalates, MPG, ETDA and PEG and are not tested on animals."[^brand] Each bar is handmade and "will always equal or exceed 3.25 oz."[^brand]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Specially formulated to help relieve symptoms of flaking, itching, irritated skin as may be caused by conditions such as seborrheic dermatitis, eczema, psoriasis, atopy and dry skin."** This is the brand's own framing, not an FDA-cleared indication (no DailyMed entry exists for the product); the underlying active has genuine anti-dandruff and anti-seborrheic-dermatitis monograph status, but psoriasis is not a monograph-recognized use for pyrithione zinc itself, and the broad "atopy" and general eczema language likewise goes beyond what pyrithione zinc is documented to do.[^brand][^dailymed][^fda]
- **"Pyrithione zinc (ZnP) is reputed to have antifungal and antibacterial properties."** The brand's own hedged wording ("reputed to"); pyrithione zinc's antifungal activity against Malassezia is in fact well established, covered on [[zinc-pyrithione]], so this claim understates rather than overstates the evidence.[^brand]
- **"Handmade and handcrafted in the USA."** A production-method claim, not an efficacy claim.[^brand]
