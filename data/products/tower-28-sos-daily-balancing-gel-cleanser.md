Tower 28's SOS Daily Balancing Gel Cleanser is a fragrance-free, sulfate-free gel cleanser from [[tower-28]], built on mild glucoside surfactants and marketed for sensitive, rosacea-, and acne-prone skin, sold at about $20 for 150 mL (5 fl oz).[^brand]

> A gentle, sugar-derived (glucoside) cleanser, which is the low-irritation end of the cleanser spectrum and a reasonable pick for reactive skin.[^brand] The value of a cleanser is cleansing without stripping, not treatment; the niacinamide, aloe, oat, and panthenol on the label are studied at leave-on doses, so in a rinse-off they are mostly composition appeal. The broader gentle-cleanser case is on [[best-gentle-cleansers-sensitive-skin]].

## Summary

This is Tower 28's face wash for its SOS sensitive-skin line: a clear gel whose surfactant system is decyl glucoside plus caprylyl/capryl glucoside, non-ionic sugar-derived cleansers that sit at the mild, low-irritation end of the range and contain no sulfates.[^brand] For sensitive, rosacea-, or acne-prone skin, that gentleness is the point, and it is the kind of thing the sensitive-skin acceptance seals the brand carries are testing for, rather than any treatment effect.[^brand]

The rest of the ingredient list, [[niacinamide]], aloe, [[glycerin]], [[panthenol]], oat kernel extract, and soothing botanicals like yarrow and balloon vine, reads well, but most of these are studied at leave-on concentrations and contact times.[^brand] In a cleanser you rinse off after less than a minute, they are a composition fact, not a delivered dose, so they should not be the reason to choose it. The reason to choose a cleanser is that it cleans without leaving skin tight or irritated.

## The Evidence

There is no product-specific trial of this cleanser; what can be judged is its surfactant chemistry and its category. Decyl glucoside and caprylyl/capryl glucoside are mild non-ionic surfactants, and the general evidence that gentle, low-pH, sulfate-free washes irritate less than soap or high-foaming cleansers is the case set out on [[best-gentle-cleansers-sensitive-skin]].[^brand] So this sits with the other gentle cleansers on the site as a tolerable option, not above them on any measured axis.

The leave-behind actives are where marketing outruns evidence. [[niacinamide]], [[glycerin]], and [[panthenol]] have real leave-on data, but their benefit depends on staying on the skin at an effective concentration, which a rinse-off does not provide.[^brand] Treat them as a bonus if the price and feel work for you, not as a functional serum step.

> **Bottom line.** A genuinely gentle, fragrance-free, sulfate-free glucoside cleanser, a fine choice for sensitive or reactive skin that wants a non-stripping wash.[^brand] Its "active" ingredients are wash-off label appeal; judge it as a cleanser, on gentleness and feel, and see [[best-gentle-cleansers-sensitive-skin]] for how it compares.

## What's In It

A cleanser has no drug active. The functional core is the surfactant system: decyl glucoside and caprylyl/capryl glucoside, both mild sugar-derived (glucoside) cleansers, with no sulfates.[^brand]

Supporting and marketed ingredients: aloe barbadensis leaf juice, [[niacinamide]], [[glycerin]], [[panthenol]], avena sativa (oat) kernel extract, plus yarrow, balloon vine, and soapberry extracts and zinc and copper gluconate.[^brand] The formula is fragrance-free.[^brand]
