Zoryve is the brand of topical roflumilast, a once-daily, steroid-free phosphodiesterase-4 (PDE4) inhibitor cream from Arcutis, FDA-approved for plaque psoriasis and, at lower strengths, for atopic dermatitis (eczema).[^dailymed]

> The appeal here is real and specific: a non-steroidal topical that can be used on delicate skin folds and faces where long-term steroids are a problem, applied once a day. Zoryve cream comes in three strengths for two different jobs, 0.3% for psoriasis and 0.15% or 0.05% for eczema, so match the strength to the condition. Its evidence is genuine FDA-registration data against a vehicle, not a head-to-head against steroids, and the condition-level rankings live on [[psoriasis]] and [[atopic-dermatitis]]. Judge it as a legitimate steroid-sparing option, not a cure.

## Summary

Zoryve cream is regulated as a drug. The label lists the active as roflumilast, a phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitor, supplied as a cream at 0.3% (3 mg per gram), 0.15% (1.5 mg per gram), and 0.05%, in 60-gram tubes, applied once daily.[^dailymed] The 0.3% cream is indicated for topical treatment of plaque psoriasis, including intertriginous areas, in adults and children 2 and older; the 0.15% cream for mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis in patients 6 and older; and the 0.05% cream for atopic dermatitis in children 2 to 5.[^dailymed] Being a non-steroidal topical is its main selling point, because it avoids the skin thinning and other limits that cap how long and where topical corticosteroids can be used.

## The Evidence

Two labeled uses, graded above; the condition-level evidence lives on the hub pages and is not repeated here.

**Plaque psoriasis (the lead use).** Approval rests on two identically designed, randomized, vehicle-controlled trials, DERMIS-1 and DERMIS-2, which together enrolled 881 subjects 6 and older with plaque psoriasis.[^dailymed] The primary endpoint was IGA Success at Week 8, defined on the label as an Investigator Global Assessment of "Clear" (0) or "Almost Clear" (1) plus a 2-grade improvement from baseline, and Zoryve 0.3% was superior to vehicle.[^dailymed] A notable, specific result is in the skin folds: among the roughly 22% (DERMIS-1) and 19% (DERMIS-2) of subjects with intertriginous involvement, intertriginous-IGA success at Week 8 reached "DERMIS-1: 71.5% vs. 13.8%; DERMIS-2: 67.5% vs. 17.4%" for Zoryve versus vehicle, which matters because intertriginous psoriasis is exactly where steroids are hardest to use.[^dailymed] The full psoriasis treatment ranking is on [[psoriasis]].

**Atopic dermatitis (eczema).** The lower-strength creams (0.15% and 0.05%) carry a separate FDA approval for mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis, also from randomized vehicle-controlled trials; the eczema evidence and comparison with other topicals are on [[atopic-dermatitis]].[^dailymed]

> **Bottom line.** A genuine, once-daily, steroid-free option with FDA-registration evidence, most distinctive for treating psoriasis in skin folds where corticosteroids are problematic.[^dailymed] It is a real steroid-sparing tool rather than a breakthrough cure, and it is compared against a vehicle, not against the steroids it aims to spare.

## What's In It

As a drug the label separates the active from the rest. Active ingredient: roflumilast (0.3%, 0.15%, or 0.05% depending on the cream).[^dailymed] Inactive ingredients, verbatim from the label: "ceteareth-10 phosphate, cetearyl phosphate, cetostearyl alcohol, diethylene glycol monoethyl ether, hexylene glycol, isopropyl palmitate, methylparaben, propylparaben, purified water, sodium hydroxide, and white petrolatum."[^dailymed] The most common adverse reactions reported in the psoriasis trials (in at least 1% of subjects) were diarrhea (3.1%), headache (2.4%), insomnia, nausea, application site pain, upper respiratory tract infection, and urinary tract infection.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Steroid-free / safe for sensitive areas and long-term use."** Accurate in kind: it is a non-steroidal PDE4 inhibitor labeled for use including intertriginous areas, which is a genuine advantage over topical steroids for skin folds and the face.[^dailymed]
- **"Once-daily."** Correct; the label directs once-daily application for both psoriasis and eczema.[^dailymed]
