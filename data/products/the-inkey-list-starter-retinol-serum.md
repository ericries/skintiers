The INKEY List Starter Retinol Serum is a 30 ml leave-on serum from [[the-inkey-list]], a British direct-to-consumer brand, priced at $15.00 on the brand's US site (EUR14,00 on its EU site).[^inkeyus][^inkeyeu]

> Despite the "Retinol" name, the declared ingredient list contains no [[retinol]]. Its actives are hydroxypinacolone retinoate, labeled "1% Granactive Retinoid Pro Plus," and 0.01% retinal (the aldehyde form of vitamin A, one enzymatic step from receptor-active retinoic acid).[^inkeyus][^inkeyeu] It replaced the brand's earlier [[the-inkey-list-retinol-serum|Retinol Serum]], which the brand's own FAQ says "has been discontinued."[^inkeyus] Retinal is the best-evidenced over-the-counter retinoid, but the controlled trials behind that evidence tested it at 0.05% to 0.1%, five to ten times the concentration labeled here, and this page found no fetchable source confirming what fraction of the "1% Granactive Retinoid Pro Plus" figure is actual hydroxypinacolone retinoate (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)).

## Summary

The INKEY List Starter Retinol Serum is a 30 ml serum sold for $15.00 direct from the manufacturer's US site and EUR14,00 on its EU site.[^inkeyus][^inkeyeu] The brand markets it as "a slow-release Dual-Retinoid complex" and describes it as "clinically proven to smooth fine lines in just 7 days without irritation," positioned as "a gentle yet effective beginner Retinol serum for first-time users and sensitive skin."[^inkeyus]

The label declares two treatment actives, neither of which is [[retinol]] itself. The first is "1% Granactive Retinoid Pro Plus," which the brand calls a "next-gen retinoid with encapsulation" and, elsewhere in its marketing, "encapsulated retinol... 2x more effective than standard retinol."[^inkeyus] The full INCI list independently confirms "Hydroxypinacolone Retinoate" among the ingredients, consistent with the Granactive Retinoid family of trade names, but this is branded "Pro Plus" rather than the standard Granactive Retinoid used in the brand's earlier formula, and neither the US nor the EU product page states what fraction of the labeled 1% is actual hydroxypinacolone retinoate versus carrier.[^inkeyus][^inkeyeu] The second active is "0.01% Retinal," which the brand's FAQ describes this way: "Retinal is stronger. It needs less conversion in the skin to get to active stage (Retinoic Acid)."[^inkeyus] The INCI list separately confirms "Retinal" among the declared ingredients.[^inkeyus][^inkeyeu] The formula also contains 0.5% "Amisol Trio," described as a lipid complex, and 0.2% "SymRelief," described as a calming ingredient, both cosmetic-support ingredients rather than retinoids.[^inkeyus][^inkeyeu]

The brand's own directions state: "Use PM only, 2-3 nights a week to start. Increase frequency as skin builds tolerance. After cleansing and applying hydrator, smooth pea-sized amount into face and neck. Follow with moisturiser. Always use SPF in AM routine."[^inkeyus] The brand also recommends the "moisture sandwich method": "thin layer of moisturizer -> pea-sized amount of serum -> thin layer of moisturiser."[^inkeyus] On combining with vitamin C, the brand's FAQ states: "While you can use Retinol and Vitamin C together, it's better to use vitamin C in daytime for antioxidant protection and Retinol in evening."[^inkeyus]

This serum directly replaced the brand's original [[the-inkey-list-retinol-serum|Retinol Serum]] (1% retinyl acetate plus 0.5% standard Granactive Retinoid). The brand's FAQ states: "Yes, the original INKEY Retinol has been discontinued. We believe this formula will give elevated performance whilst still being perfect for beginners to Retinoids."[^inkeyus] Against the category evidence set out on [[retinoids]], [[retinaldehyde]], and [[hydroxypinacolone-retinoate]], the swap trades a retinyl-ester/HPR pairing for an HPR/retinal pairing, moving toward the better-evidenced retinoid (retinal) while keeping the labeled concentration of that active far below what any cited trial has tested.

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinoids improve photoaged skin at all is the general question covered on [[retinoids]]. [[retinaldehyde|Retinal]] is the best-evidenced over-the-counter form: vehicle-controlled and concentration-comparison trials on that page show measurable improvement in wrinkles, roughness, and texture at 0.05% and 0.1%, the strongest OTC-retinoid record available, with meaningfully lower irritation than prescription retinoic acid. This product labels retinal at 0.01%, below every concentration tested in those trials, so how much of that evidence carries over at this dose is untested rather than established.

[[hydroxypinacolone-retinoate|Hydroxypinacolone retinoate]] is graded on its own page: an in-vitro human fibroblast study found HPR combined with retinyl propionate raised expression of collagen and retinoid-pathway genes, a laboratory result consistent with retinoid-receptor activity but not a clinical outcome, and every located human study tests HPR only inside multi-ingredient formulas, so no controlled trial isolates its own effect. This product's "Granactive Retinoid Pro Plus" is a differently-branded HPR blend from the standard Granactive Retinoid used in that evidence base, and no fetchable source reviewed here states its actual HPR fraction.

> **Bottom line.** Neither declared active has controlled human trial evidence isolating its own effect at the concentration used in this serum: retinal's strongest evidence was generated at five to ten times the labeled 0.01%, and HPR's human data all come from combination formulas using a differently-branded blend than this product's.

We found no published, independently reviewable trial of this exact serum, or of its specific 1% Granactive Retinoid Pro Plus / 0.01% retinal combination.[^inkeyus]
