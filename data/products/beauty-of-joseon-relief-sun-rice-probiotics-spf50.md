Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics is a lightweight Korean chemical sunscreen, 50 ml, rated SPF50+ PA++++, whose entire filter system is modern organic filters: diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate (Uvinul A Plus), ethylhexyl triazone (Uvinul T 150), methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol (bisoctrizole, Tinosorb M), and diethylhexyl butamido triazone (iscotrizinol), none of which is currently in the US OTC sunscreen monograph.[^1][^2]

> This product pairs a high UVB rating (SPF50+, the top of the Korean and EU label scale) with UVA (photoaging) protection built on modern filters and rated PA++++, the highest grade on the graded persistent-pigment-darkening UVA scale.[^1][^3] Its UVA protection grades above the older US-filter sunscreens it competes with: it uses two modern UVA filters, bisoctrizole (Tinosorb M) and Uvinul A Plus, which a Journal of Drugs in Dermatology review reports protect against UVA better than the avobenzone US products rely on, and it carries a graded PA rating where US labels give only a pass/fail "Broad Spectrum" check.[^3][^4][^5] SPF50+ and PA++++ are manufacturer and Korean-regulatory label claims, not FDA-tested, and the product reaches US buyers as an import.[^1][^2][^6] How SPF, UVA, and the filter set fit together is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## The Evidence

Whether regular sunscreen use works, and how, is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. The randomized evidence there is for the practice of applying a broad-spectrum sunscreen. What is specific to this product is its filter set and its Korean label ratings. This is a cosmetic sunscreen sold in Korea and imported to the US; it is not an FDA-reviewed OTC drug and carries no FDA Drug Facts panel.[^1][^2]

The label pairs two ratings on two different axes. SPF is a UVB (sunburn) number, and "SPF50+" is the top of the capped Korean and EU SPF scale.[^1][^2] PA is the UVA (photoaging) axis: protection against UVA "is measured by the Persistent Pigment Darkening (PPD) Protection Factor," a graded method "developed in Japan and ... routinely used by manufacturers," which sorts products into PA grades of increasing measured UVA protection.[^3] PA++++ is the highest of those grades.[^1] This is a graded UVA measure, unlike the US system, where "Broad Spectrum" is a single pass/fail test rather than a numeric UVA rating (see [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]).

The UVA coverage comes from modern organic filters. Bisoctrizole (methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol, Tinosorb M) is one of the filters a 2013 review lists among the "organic UVA sunscreening agents," and an NCBI photoprotection reference names it among the broad-spectrum filters.[^3][^4] The formula's second modern UVA filter is diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate (Uvinul A Plus), a dedicated UVA absorber; the two UVB filters are ethylhexyl triazone (Uvinul T 150) and diethylhexyl butamido triazone (iscotrizinol).[^1][^2] These are the filters standard in Korea, the EU, and Australia and outside the US monograph. A 2020 Journal of Drugs in Dermatology review argues that the modern filters protect against UVA and free radicals better than avobenzone, and that US sunscreens as a group let through substantially more UVA than European products; that general comparison is carried, cited, on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^5]

That places this product's UVA protection above the older US-filter sunscreens it competes with in the US import market. [[la-roche-posay-anthelios-melt-in-milk-spf-60]] draws its UVA coverage from avobenzone, an older US UVA filter that is photounstable (see [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]), and [[eltamd-uv-clear-spf-46]] from a 9% zinc oxide load, both graded modest for UVA on those pages; this formula instead combines a graded PA++++ rating with two modern UVA filters.[^3] The same brand's separate US product, "Daily Relief Sunscreen SPF 40," is an FDA OTC reformulation built on the older filters (avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, octocrylene) and is not this product.[^6]

> **Bottom line.** SPF50+ is a high UVB (sunburn) label rating and PA++++ is the highest grade on the graded PPD-based UVA scale.[^1][^3] The UVA protection rests on two modern filters, bisoctrizole (Tinosorb M) and Uvinul A Plus, which the cited review reports outprotect the avobenzone older US products use, so it grades above US-market sunscreens such as [[la-roche-posay-anthelios-melt-in-milk-spf-60]] and [[eltamd-uv-clear-spf-46]] on the UVA axis.[^3][^4][^5] These SPF and PA figures are Korean-regulatory label claims, not FDA-tested, and the product reaches US buyers as an import.[^1][^2][^6]

## What's In It

The ingredient list as declared on the importer listings and cross-checked against the INCIDecoder ingredient database.[^1][^2] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

UV filters (all modern organic filters, none in the current US OTC monograph):

- [[diethylamino-hydroxybenzoyl-hexyl-benzoate]] (Uvinul A Plus), a UVA filter
- [[bisoctrizole]] (methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol, Tinosorb M), a broad-spectrum filter
- [[ethylhexyl-triazone]] (Uvinul T 150), a UVB filter
- [[diethylhexyl-butamido-triazone]] (iscotrizinol), a UVB filter

Other functional ingredients:

- [[niacinamide]] (vitamin B3), listed high on the ingredient list
- oryza sativa (rice) extract, listed at 30%, plus rice germ extract and rice ferments, the "rice" positioning
- lactobacillus, bacillus, aspergillus, saccharomyces, and monascus ferments, the "probiotics" positioning
- camellia sinensis (green tea) leaf extract and panax ginseng root extract, plant extracts
- adenosine, a skin-conditioning agent
- [[glycerin]], a humectant, and [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, dibutyl adipate and coco-caprylate/caprate (emollients), propanediol, butylene glycol, pentylene glycol and 1,2-hexanediol (solvents and humectants), polymethylsilsesquioxane and caprylyl methicone (silicones for feel), behenyl alcohol, poly C10-30 alkyl acrylate, polyglyceryl-3 methylglucose distearate, decyl glucoside and sodium stearoyl glutamate (emulsifiers), carbomer, acrylates/C10-30 alkyl acrylate crosspolymer, polyacrylate crosspolymer-6 and xanthan gum (thickeners), tromethamine (pH adjuster), and ethylhexylglycerin.[^1]

Not in the formula: no ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate (octinoxate) and no avobenzone; the whole filter system is modern organic filters. This SPF50+ import is a different formula from the brand's US "Daily Relief Sunscreen SPF 40," which uses avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene.[^1][^6]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded UV evidence above:

- **"Rice + Probiotics," "rice extracts, fermented ingredients and niacinamide to nourish and brighten."** The formula lists rice extract at 30% and several ferments, and niacinamide high on the list.[^1] Niacinamide's own evidence is on [[niacinamide]]. The rice, ferment, and "relief" or soothing positioning is a manufacturer claim, not the graded sun-protection benefit, which is the SPF50+ PA++++ UV rating.
- **"Organic," "lightweight," "no white cast," "no greasy finish."** Texture and positioning claims from importer and marketing copy, not tested outcomes.[^1][^2]
