Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics is a lightweight Korean daily chemical sunscreen, 50 ml, rated SPF50+ PA++++, built entirely on modern organic UV filters that are standard in Korea and the EU but sit outside the current US OTC sunscreen monograph.[^1][^2]

> A daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, and broad-spectrum protection is the best-evidenced way to lower photoaging and sun-related skin-cancer risk. Its distinction is the UVA side of that protection: it pairs a high SPF (a UVB rating) with PA++++ and two modern UVA filters, and an independent Consumer Reports lab test scored both its UVA and UVB protection at the top of the scale, above the older US-filter sunscreens it competes with. Beauty editors praise it as weightless with no white cast, though a blind sensory panel was more reserved. The SPF and UVA filter science is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^7][^8]

## Summary

Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics is a daily broad-spectrum sunscreen from the Korean brand Beauty of Joseon, made for everyday facial use in a light, fluid texture. Its core purpose is health rather than cosmetics: broad-spectrum protection against UV is the single best-evidenced way to reduce photoaging and lower the risk of sun-related skin cancer, a case with randomized-trial support that is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

What sets this version apart is the UVA side of that protection. It is built entirely on modern organic UV filters, including two dedicated UVA absorbers, bisoctrizole ([[bisoctrizole|Tinosorb M]]) and [[diethylamino-hydroxybenzoyl-hexyl-benzoate|Uvinul A Plus]], and carries a PA++++ rating, the top of the graded UVA scale used in Korea and the EU. Those filters are standard abroad but outside the current US monograph, so many US-shelf sunscreens still rely on older filters with weaker UVA coverage ([the filter comparison is in The Evidence](#the-evidence)). An independent Consumer Reports lab test scored this formula's UVA and UVB protection at the top of its scale, above the brand's own US reformulation.[^7]

On reputation, beauty editors rate the feel highly while an independent panel was more measured. Allure's Sarah Han calls it "a prime example of what Korean sunscreens can do" and says it "leaves white cast out of the equation."[^8] A blind Consumer Reports sensory panel was less sure the K-beauty reputation held up: its sensory lead, Mely Llamas, said the tested Korean sunscreens lacked the "barely-there feel" people expect, and the panel found the products "initially felt greasy and left a white cast" and, after ten minutes, "absorbed fairly well, but a slight greasy feeling remained."[^7] On how it is worn, Allure's editor describes it as "your last skin-care step in the morning," applied by "the good 'ol two-finger rule."[^8]

Two honest limits. The SPF and PA figures are Korean-regulatory label claims, not FDA-tested, and the product reaches US buyers as an import; Consumer Reports' own measurement put its average tested SPF at 36, below the 50+ on the label though far above the brand's US version at 19.[^7][^1] It is inexpensive, about $18 for 50 ml as listed at [YesStyle and Stylevana](#where-to-buy) (accessed 2026-07-28).[^1][^2][^8]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and for reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. What is specific to this product is its modern-filter set, its Korean label ratings, and an independent lab test of this exact formula. It is a cosmetic sunscreen sold in Korea and imported to the US, not an FDA-reviewed OTC drug, and it carries no FDA Drug Facts panel.[^1][^2]

The label pairs two ratings on two different axes. SPF is a UVB (sunburn) number, and "SPF50+" is the top of the capped Korean and EU SPF scale.[^1][^2] PA is the UVA (photoaging) axis: UVA protection "is measured by the Persistent Pigment Darkening (PPD) Protection Factor," a graded method "developed in Japan and ... routinely used by manufacturers," which sorts products into PA grades of increasing measured UVA protection.[^3] PA++++ is the highest of those grades.[^1] That is a graded UVA measure, unlike the US system, where "Broad Spectrum" is a single pass/fail test rather than a numeric UVA rating; how the two systems compare is explained on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

The UVA coverage comes from modern organic filters. Bisoctrizole (methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol, Tinosorb M) is one of the filters a 2013 review lists among the "organic UVA sunscreening agents," and an NCBI photoprotection reference names it among the broad-spectrum filters.[^3][^4] The formula's second modern UVA filter is diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate (Uvinul A Plus); the two UVB filters are ethylhexyl triazone (Uvinul T 150) and diethylhexyl butamido triazone (iscotrizinol).[^1][^2] These are the filters standard in Korea, the EU, and Australia and outside the US monograph. A 2020 Journal of Drugs in Dermatology review reports that the modern filters protect against UVA and free radicals better than avobenzone, and that US sunscreens as a group let through substantially more UVA than European products; that comparison is carried, cited, on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^5]

Consumer Reports lab-tested this exact Korean formula head-to-head against the brand's US reformulation. It scored the Korean Relief Sun 5 out of 5 for both UVA and UVB protection, its top marks, with an average tested SPF of 36, and found that "all three Korean formulas provided better protection than their U.S. counterparts."[^7] The brand's US "Day Dew Sunscreen SPF 50" tested at an average SPF of 19.[^7] CR did not assign an overall score, because the import does not comply with FDA rules and cannot be sold legally in the US.[^7]

> **Bottom line.** An independent lab scored this product's UVA and UVB protection at the top of its scale and measured an average SPF of 36, below the 50+ label but well above the brand's US version at 19.[^7]

That places its UVA protection above the older US-filter sunscreens it competes with in the import market. [[la-roche-posay-anthelios-melt-in-milk-spf-60]] draws its UVA coverage from avobenzone, an older US UVA filter that is photounstable, and [[eltamd-uv-clear-spf-46]] from a 9% zinc oxide load, both graded modest for UVA on those pages; this formula instead combines a graded PA++++ rating and two modern UVA filters with a top independent UVA score.[^3][^7] The same brand's separate US product, "Daily Relief Sunscreen SPF 40," is an FDA OTC reformulation built on the older filters (avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, octocrylene) and is not this product.[^6]

> **Bottom line.** SPF50+ is a high UVB label rating and PA++++ is the top grade of the PPD-based UVA scale; an independent lab put both this product's UVA and UVB protection at the top of its scale, above the avobenzone- and zinc-based US-market sunscreens it competes with.[^3][^7] These SPF and PA figures are Korean-regulatory label claims, not FDA-tested, and the product reaches US buyers as an import.[^1][^6]

## What's In It

The ingredient list as declared on the importer listings and cross-checked against the INCIDecoder ingredient database.[^1][^2] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

UV filters (all modern organic filters, none in the current US OTC monograph):

- [[diethylamino-hydroxybenzoyl-hexyl-benzoate]] (Uvinul A Plus), a UVA filter
- [[bisoctrizole]] (methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol, Tinosorb M), a broad-spectrum filter
- [[ethylhexyl-triazone]] (Uvinul T 150), a UVB filter
- [[diethylhexyl-butamido-triazone]] (iscotrizinol), a UVB filter

Other functional ingredients:

- [[niacinamide]] (vitamin B3), listed high on the ingredient list
- oryza sativa (rice) extract, listed at 30%, plus rice germ extract and rice ferments, the "rice" positioning
- lactobacillus, bacillus, aspergillus, saccharomyces, and monascus ferments, the "probiotics" positioning
- camellia sinensis (green tea) leaf extract and panax ginseng root extract, plant extracts
- adenosine, a skin-conditioning agent
- [[glycerin]], a humectant, and [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, dibutyl adipate and coco-caprylate/caprate (emollients), propanediol, butylene glycol, pentylene glycol and 1,2-hexanediol (solvents and humectants), polymethylsilsesquioxane and caprylyl methicone (silicones for feel), behenyl alcohol, poly C10-30 alkyl acrylate, polyglyceryl-3 methylglucose distearate, decyl glucoside and sodium stearoyl glutamate (emulsifiers), carbomer, acrylates/C10-30 alkyl acrylate crosspolymer, polyacrylate crosspolymer-6 and xanthan gum (thickeners), tromethamine (pH adjuster), and ethylhexylglycerin.[^1]

Not in the formula: no ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate (octinoxate) and no avobenzone; the whole filter system is modern organic filters. This SPF50+ import is a different formula from the brand's US "Daily Relief Sunscreen SPF 40," which uses avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene.[^1][^6]

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded UV evidence above:

- **"Rice + Probiotics," "rice extracts, fermented ingredients and niacinamide to nourish and brighten."** The formula lists rice extract at 30% and several ferments, and niacinamide high on the list.[^1] Niacinamide's own evidence is on [[niacinamide]]. The rice, ferment, and soothing positioning is a manufacturer claim, and its brightening benefit is cosmetic, separate from the graded sun-protection benefit, which is the SPF50+ PA++++ UV rating.
