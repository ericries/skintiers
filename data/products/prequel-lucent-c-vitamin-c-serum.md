Prequel Lucent-C Brightening Vitamin C Serum is a leave-on serum the manufacturer formulates with 15% L-ascorbic acid (pure vitamin C), ferulic acid, ergothioneine, and niacinamide at a labeled pH of 3.2.[^1]

> Whether topical vitamin C brightens or protects skin is covered on [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]]. What is specific to this product is that it delivers the same 15% pure L-ascorbic acid at low pH as SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic, at a much lower price, but leaves out the vitamin E of that reference formula. The ferulic-acid photoprotection studies (Lin 2005, Murray 2008) tested vitamin C combined with vitamin E and ferulic acid, a combination this serum does not contain.[^1][^3][^4]

## The Evidence

Whether topical vitamin C brightens skin or supports collagen, and how, is covered on [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]]. What is specific to this product is how its composition maps onto the pure L-ascorbic acid formulation research and the ferulic-acid combination that C E Ferulic is built on.

Pinnell 2001 set out the rules a pure vitamin C serum has to follow. In percutaneous absorption studies, "L-ascorbic acid must be formulated at pH levels less than 3.5 to enter the skin," the maximal concentration for optimal absorption was 20%, and tissue levels were saturated after three daily applications.[^2] Prequel labels Lucent-C at 15% L-ascorbic acid and pH 3.2.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** For pure vitamin C to penetrate skin, the serum has to be acidic, below about pH 3.5. Lucent-C's labeled pH of 3.2 and 15% concentration fall inside the range Pinnell described.[^1][^2]

The published photoprotection evidence for a ferulic-acid vitamin C serum tested a three-antioxidant combination, not vitamin C and ferulic acid alone. Lin 2005 found that adding 0.5% ferulic acid to a solution of 15% L-ascorbic acid and 1% alpha-tocopherol (vitamin E) "doubled photoprotection to solar-simulated irradiation of skin from 4-fold to approximately 8-fold as measured by both erythema and sunburn cell formation" in pig skin.[^3] Murray 2008 applied the same 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% alpha-tocopherol, and 0.5% ferulic acid formula to human skin and reported it "provided significant and meaningful photoprotection for skin by all methods of evaluation" on surrogate markers such as erythema, sunburn cells, and thymine dimers.[^4] Both studies came from the formula's inventors at Duke University. Lucent-C contains ferulic acid but no vitamin E; its other declared antioxidants are ergothioneine and niacinamide.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** The measured photoprotection in those studies came from vitamin C paired with both vitamin E and ferulic acid. Lucent-C has the vitamin C and the ferulic acid but not the vitamin E, so that specific evidence does not describe this formula.[^1][^3][^4]

## What's In It

The ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the solvent, humectant, and preservative system. [INCIDecoder](https://incidecoder.com/products/prequel-lucent-c-brightening-vitamin-c-serum) lists the same ingredients.

Antioxidant and active ingredients:

- [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]] 15%, pure vitamin C, the antioxidant the product is built around[^1]
- [[ferulic-acid]], a plant antioxidant that stabilizes vitamin C[^3]
- ergothioneine, an amino-acid-derived antioxidant
- [[niacinamide]] (vitamin B3)
- inulin, a prebiotic plant sugar

Base, solvent, and preservative system: water, propanediol and isopentyldiol (humectant solvents), ethoxydiglycol (a penetration solvent), aminomethyl propanol (pH adjuster), hydroxyacetophenone, 1,2-hexanediol and caprylyl glycol (preservative boosters), and mannitol.

Two composition facts separate this serum from the SkinCeuticals reference formula. It contains no vitamin E (alpha-tocopherol), one of the three antioxidants in the C E Ferulic combination the photoprotection studies tested; in its place Lucent-C uses ergothioneine and niacinamide.[^1][^3] It is acidic by design, labeled at pH 3.2, consistent with the low pH pure L-ascorbic acid requires to penetrate skin.[^1][^2]
