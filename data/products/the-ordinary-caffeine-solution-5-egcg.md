The Ordinary Caffeine Solution 5% + EGCG is a 30ml water-based eye serum from [[the-ordinary|The Ordinary]], listing caffeine at a disclosed 5% alongside a stabilized EGCG derivative, priced at $9.20 on the brand's own site.[^to][^inci]

> A cheap, disclosed-concentration caffeine eye serum whose brand claims are about temporary puffiness and dark-circle appearance, not permanent change. Caffeine's plausible mechanism (vasoconstriction, mild fluid drainage) is real, but no trial of this exact product was found, and the broader literature on topical caffeine for the under-eye area is thin and mostly small, uncontrolled, or multi-ingredient studies, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence).

## Summary

The Ordinary Caffeine Solution 5% + EGCG is a 30ml (1 fl oz) serum sold for $9.20 on the brand's own site, positioned specifically for the eye area.[^to] The product name and ingredient list both disclose caffeine at 5%; caffeine is the second ingredient listed after water, consistent with a high, disclosed concentration.[^inci] The formula also lists Epigallocatechin Gallatyl Glucoside and Gallyl Glucoside, glucoside-stabilized derivatives of EGCG (a green tea catechin) and gallic acid rather than free EGCG itself, plus [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]], oxidized glutathione, and a small amount of melanin.[^inci]

The brand's own marketing describes the product as targeting "dark circles and puffiness in just 3 weeks" and calls it "a potent, water-based serum to brighten and refresh tired eyes."[^to] These are cosmetic, appearance-level claims about a chronically puffy or discolored under-eye area, not claims about treating a medical condition, and none of them are supported by a published trial of this specific formula.

## The Evidence

Caffeine is a xanthine that can cause local vasoconstriction and has been proposed to help temporarily reduce under-eye puffiness by narrowing blood vessels and drawing fluid out of the skin; that mechanism, and the thin evidence behind topical caffeine for the eye area, are covered on [[caffeine]]. No published clinical trial of The Ordinary's own finished serum was found.

A 30-year literature review of topical therapies for periorbital dyschromia (dark circles) that searched PubMed, Scopus, and OVID through 2023 identified 22 studies covering topical ingredients including "acids, bark extract, bicyclic monoterpene diols, caffeine, combo ingredient serums, ethyl ximenynate, gentiopicroside, glycosaminoglycans, growth factors, topical PRP, and vitamins," reporting that "the database searches yielded 339 unique studies. After screening and review, 22 studies were included totaling 13 nonrandomized control trials, 7 randomized clinical trials, 1 retrospective case series, and 1 prospective single-arm study. Studies included all reported statistically significant results with topical therapies inclusive of acids, bark extract, bicyclic monoterpene diols, caffeine, combo ingredient serums, ethyl ximenynate, gentiopicroside, glycosaminoglycans, growth factors, topical PRP, and vitamins."[^review] This review groups caffeine with many other ingredients across a mix of small trial designs and does not isolate caffeine's individual contribution or report effect sizes specific to caffeine.

A separate 2020 overview of periorbital hyperpigmentation management similarly lists caffeine among many "nature-based agents" (alongside alpha-arbutin, niacinamide, retinol, and others) reported to show "significant benefits" in small studies, again without isolating caffeine's standalone effect.[^overview]

> **Bottom line.** Topical caffeine has a plausible mechanism for temporary de-puffing and is repeatedly included in small studies of periorbital appearance, but the evidence for caffeine specifically, apart from combination formulas, is thin, and no trial tests this product.
