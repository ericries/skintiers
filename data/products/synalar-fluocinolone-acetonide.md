Synalar is the flagship brand of prescription [[fluocinolone-acetonide]], sold by Medimetriks Pharmaceuticals, Inc. as a 0.025% cream, a 0.025% ointment, and a 0.01% topical solution, each FDA-approved for "the relief of the inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of corticosteriod-responsive dermatoses" [sic].[^cream][^ointment][^solution]

> An old, well-established, low-to-medium potency [[topical-corticosteroids|topical corticosteroid]], a workhorse for calming eczema flares and other steroid-responsive rashes, not anything special. Its lower potency cuts both ways: less skin-atrophy risk than the potent Class I-III steroids at typical use, but also a milder effect, which is why prescribers reach for it on lower-intensity disease or more delicate sites. Like most decades-old steroid brands, its labels publish no clinical-trial results table, the efficacy case is the class evidence, and cheaper generic fluocinolone acetonide exists. The drug-level potency story is owned by [[fluocinolone-acetonide]] and the class evidence by [[topical-corticosteroids]].[^cream][^ointment][^solution]

## Summary

Synalar is generic-name [[fluocinolone-acetonide]] in three dosage forms: a 0.025% cream containing 0.25 mg/g "in a water-washable aqueous base," a 0.025% ointment containing 0.25 mg/g "in a white petrolatum USP vehicle," and a 0.01% topical solution containing 0.1 mg/mL "in a water-washable base of citric acid and propylene glycol."[^cream][^ointment][^solution] All three labels carry the identical, deliberately broad indication: "indicated for the relief of the inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of corticosteriod-responsive dermatoses" [sic, the labels' own spelling], which in practice means flares of conditions like [[atopic-dermatitis]] and other inflammatory, itchy rashes a corticosteroid can quiet.[^cream][^ointment][^solution] No age floor is stated; the pediatric-use sections instead warn that children absorb proportionally more drug and should get "the least amount compatible with an effective therapeutic regimen."[^cream][^ointment]

The cream and ointment labels direct application "as a thin film from two to four times daily depending on the severity of the condition."[^cream][^ointment] Where fluocinolone acetonide sits in the US seven-class steroid potency system, low-to-medium, several rungs below agents like betamethasone dipropionate, is covered on [[fluocinolone-acetonide]]; the class-wide efficacy and safety evidence, including the atrophy and HPA-axis-suppression risks the Synalar labels warn about, is covered on [[topical-corticosteroids]]. This page does not re-derive either. This is a prescription drug; see a prescriber for use and dosing.

## The Evidence

The Synalar labels are old-format prescribing information and publish no product-specific clinical-trial results: no responder rates, no vehicle comparison, no efficacy table.[^cream][^ointment][^solution] The clinical-pharmacology sections state only the class case, that "topical corticosteroids share anti-inflammatory, anti-pruritic and vasoconstrictive actions" and that "the mechanism of anti-inflammatory activity of the topical corticosteroids is unclear," with potency compared across products by vasoconstrictor assay rather than by head-to-head trials.[^cream][^ointment] The randomized-trial evidence that topical corticosteroids work in eczema and psoriasis is class evidence, made on [[topical-corticosteroids]]; fluocinolone acetonide's placement within that class is made on [[fluocinolone-acetonide]].

> **Bottom line.** The FDA-approved indication is real and durable, and the class evidence behind topical corticosteroids is strong, but nothing on the Synalar labels distinguishes these branded products from generic fluocinolone acetonide or from other steroids of similar potency.[^cream][^ointment][^solution]

The safety profile is likewise the class profile. All three labels list the same local adverse reactions, "reported infrequently with topical corticosteroids, but may occur more frequently with the use of occlusive dressings," in approximate decreasing order of occurrence: burning, itching, irritation, dryness, folliculitis, hypertrichosis, acneiform eruptions, hypopigmentation, perioral dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis, maceration of the skin, secondary infection, skin atrophy, striae, and miliaria.[^cream][^ointment][^solution] The precautions warn that "prolonged use may produce atrophy of the skin and subcutaneous tissues," that on intertriginous or flexor areas or the face "this may occur even with short-term use," and that systemic absorption has produced reversible HPA axis suppression, manifestations of Cushing's syndrome, hyperglycemia, and glucosuria in some patients.[^cream][^ointment]

## What's In It

The active and inactive ingredients as declared on the FDA-approved prescribing information.[^cream][^ointment][^solution]

Active:

- [[fluocinolone-acetonide]] 0.025% (0.25 mg/g, cream and ointment) or 0.01% (0.1 mg/mL, solution), a prescription [[topical-corticosteroids|topical corticosteroid]].[^cream][^ointment][^solution]

Cream base: "a water-washable aqueous base of butylated hydroxytoluene, cetyl alcohol, citric acid, edetate disodium, methylparaben and propylparaben (preservatives), mineral oil, polyoxyl 20 cetostearyl ether, propylene glycol, simethicone, stearyl alcohol, water (purified) and white beeswax."[^cream]

Ointment base: "a white petrolatum USP vehicle," petrolatum being the only inactive ingredient.[^ointment]

Solution base: "a water-washable base of citric acid and propylene glycol."[^solution]

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand marketing page was reviewed for this product; the labels themselves make no efficacy claims beyond the class indication.

- **A brand-name steroid for steroid-responsive rashes.** Accurate as far as it goes, but the indication wording is the same generic class language that appears on fluocinolone acetonide's generic labels, and the Synalar labels report no evidence that the branded vehicles outperform generic fluocinolone acetonide.[^cream][^ointment][^solution][^generic]

## Where to Buy

Prescription only; obtain through a dermatologist or prescriber. Synalar Cream is supplied in a 120 g tube (NDC 43538-900-12), the ointment in a 120 g tube (NDC 43538-910-12), and the solution in 60 mL and 90 mL bottles with applicator tips (NDC 43538-920-60 and 43538-920-90).[^cream][^ointment][^solution] Generic fluocinolone acetonide is also marketed, for example a 0.01% topical solution USP from multiple manufacturers, and no citable cash price for the brand was found on a durable primary source, so none is stated here.[^generic]
