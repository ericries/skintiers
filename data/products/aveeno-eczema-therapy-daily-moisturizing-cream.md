Aveeno Eczema Therapy Daily Moisturizing Cream is Kenvue's OTC eczema cream, carrying a Drug Facts panel that names colloidal oatmeal, at 1%, as its Active ingredient with the Purpose "Skin Protectant."[^1][^2]

> A fragrance-free, steroid-free eczema cream built around colloidal oatmeal declared, at a disclosed 1%, as a genuine Drug Facts skin-protectant active rather than a cosmetic ingredient. That concentration and use are backed by [[colloidal-oatmeal|the FDA skin-protectant monograph and randomized eczema trials]]. This is the disclosed-active counterpart to [[aveeno-daily-moisturizing-lotion|Aveeno's Daily Moisturizing Lotion]], which carries the same oat ingredient with no percentage and no Drug Facts panel. No independent study of this specific cream was located.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Aveeno Eczema Therapy Daily Moisturizing Cream is a fragrance-free body cream marketed for dry, itchy skin due to eczema.[^1] Its Drug Facts panel names one Active ingredient, "Colloidal Oatmeal (1%)," with the Purpose "Skin Protectant," and states the Uses as "Temporarily protects and helps relieve minor skin irritation and itching due to" rashes and eczema, with "Other information" adding "for use on mild to moderate eczema or as directed by a physician."[^1][^2] That is a materially different declaration from the same brand's cosmetic [[aveeno-daily-moisturizing-lotion|Daily Moisturizing Lotion]], whose oat ingredient carries no Drug Facts panel and no disclosed percentage; here the concentration and the regulatory category are both stated on the label. Colloidal oatmeal's underlying FDA skin-protectant monograph and its randomized-trial evidence for itch relief and barrier support in mild-to-moderate eczema are set out on [[colloidal-oatmeal]].

The inactive-ingredient list also includes glycerin, a well-evidenced humectant covered on [[glycerin]], and a single ceramide (Ceramide NP), one of the barrier lipids covered on [[ceramides]], alongside base occlusives and emollients (petrolatum, dimethicone, isopropyl palmitate). The cream holds the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance and is labeled steroid-free, fragrance-free, and allergy-tested.[^1] No independent trial of this exact cream was located; its case rests on the general evidence for colloidal oatmeal and glycerin, detailed in [The Evidence](#the-evidence). Sizes of 5oz, 7.3oz, and 12oz are listed on the manufacturer's page; neither the manufacturer's page nor the retailer listing fetched for this profile displayed a price ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)).

## The Evidence

Keeping dry, eczema-prone skin moisturized reduces flares and supports the skin barrier, one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines, set out on [[moisturizing]]. This cream's more specific case rests on its Drug Facts active. Colloidal oatmeal has a formal FDA skin-protectant monograph for relieving minor skin irritation and itching due to rashes and eczema, and randomized trials of a 1% colloidal oatmeal cream, matching the concentration declared on this cream's own label[^1][^2], found it non-inferior to a prescription barrier cream and ahead of a standard moisturizer on eczema-severity and barrier measures; that monograph status and trial evidence, along with their manufacturer funding and limits, are set out on [[colloidal-oatmeal]]. No independent study of this specific cream was located, so its own effect has not been isolated from those general findings.

For the other declared ingredients, glycerin is a well-evidenced humectant with vehicle-controlled hydration data set out on [[glycerin]], and the single ceramide (Ceramide NP) is one of the barrier lipids covered on [[ceramides]]; this formula does not disclose either ingredient's concentration.

> **Bottom line.** Colloidal oatmeal appears here as a disclosed, 1% Drug Facts skin-protectant active[^1][^2], rather than as an undisclosed cosmetic ingredient, and carries a real FDA monograph and randomized-trial support for itch and eczema relief at that use. No study has isolated this cream's own effect; its case otherwise rests on the general evidence for colloidal oatmeal and glycerin.

## What's In It

Drug Facts Active ingredient, at the stated concentration: [[colloidal-oatmeal|Colloidal Oatmeal (1%)]], Purpose "Skin Protectant."[^1][^2]

Other notable functional ingredients, declared as Inactive ingredients:

- [[glycerin]], a humectant
- Ceramide NP, one of the barrier lipids covered on [[ceramides]]
- Panthenol
- [[petrolatum]] and [[dimethicone]], occlusives
- isopropyl palmitate, an emollient
- Avena Sativa (Oat) Kernel Oil and Avena Sativa (Oat) Kernel Extract, additional oat-derived ingredients distinct from the Active ingredient

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, distearyldimonium chloride (conditioning/emulsifying agent), cetyl alcohol (emollient/emulsifier), steareth-20 (emulsifier), benzalkonium chloride (preservative), and sodium chloride (thickener).[^1][^2]

The formula contains no added fragrance and no [[hyaluronic-acid]].[^1][^2]
