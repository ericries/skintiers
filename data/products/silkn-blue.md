A battery-powered blue-light handheld from Home Skinovations, cleared in 2013 for mild to moderate acne. **Bottom line:** its filing is the rare over-the-counter device summary that actually publishes a clinical result, and the result is uncontrolled. Modality evidence is on [[home-led-light-therapy]]; the brand is [[silkn]].

> **It reports a number, and the number has no comparison group.** Fifty subjects, eight treatments over four weeks, and a mean improvement the filing gives as 56.7% plus or minus 8.9% at one month.[^k121435] There is no control arm, no placebo and no randomisation named anywhere in the document, and acne fluctuates on its own, so a single-arm percentage cannot separate the device from time.

## Summary

Cleared 2013-02-22 as K121435 under product code OLP, with the classification name given in the filing as "Over the counter powered light based laser for Acne, 21 CFR 878.4810".[^k121435][^openfda] The indication reads "The Silkn Blue is indicated as an over the counter phototherapy device for the treatment of mild to moderate acne".[^k121435] It is described as a hand-held, battery-operated device emitting LED light at 415 nm with a tolerance of 15 nm, combined with a tip temperature stabiliser, and the filing states that "The emitting optical power is in a uniform distribution with no hot spots".[^k121435] Its own technology table gives 50 mW/cm2, a treatment area of 7 cm2 and a targeted skin temperature of 41 plus or minus 2 degrees C.[^k121435] No price is given here.

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate acne**
- **Effect size: modest.** The filing's clinical summary states that the study "involves 50 subjects who used the Silk'n Blue twice a week over four weeks (a total of 8 treatments), and 2 follow-up visits to evaluate the results", that "All subjects demonstrated a reduction in lesion count", and that "The average improvement after one month was 56.7%".[^k121435] Blue LED for acne is the best-graded home modality in the 2026 scoping review, at Level B with a Grade I recommendation, and that grade belongs to the modality rather than to this device.[^grade]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** Single-arm. Checked by string search of the full extracted text, the document contains no occurrence of placebo, randomised or randomized, and no control group is described.[^k121435] A claim that every single subject improved is easier to achieve without a comparison group than with one.

**The three-month figure cannot be read**
- **Effect size: not reportable.** The filing gives the three-month result as a damaged string, "5 7.7%+69.4%", in a scanned document whose text layer is poor throughout. The central value is legible as 57.7%, but whether the dispersion is 9.4% or 69.4% cannot be determined from the scan, and those two readings mean very different things about consistency.[^k121435] It is left unresolved here rather than guessed.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable.**

**Its treatment window is the smallest of its own comparators**
- **Effect size: a practical fact, not an efficacy one.** The technology table puts the Silk'n Blue at a 7 cm2 treatment area against 10 cm2 for the Quasar Blue, 27 cm2 for the Tanda and 30 cm2 for the OmniLux, at a matching 415 nm and 50 mW/cm2 for the first two.[^k121435] Same light, similar intensity, roughly a quarter to a seventh of the window, which means proportionally more passes to cover the same face.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable; this is read off the filing's own comparison table.**

## The Evidence

**Every predicate belongs to another company.** The filing names four: ThermaClear by DermaCare at K060653, the Quasar Blue Light Therapy System by Silver Bay at K093963, the Tanda Skincare System by Pharos Life at K080591, and the OmniLux New-U by Photo Therapeutics.[^k121435] None is a Home Skinovations device. That is the outward-anchored pattern this site also documents at the start of the dpl line on [[dpl-nuve]], rather than the self-predication seen later at [[dpl-faceware]].

**The safety sentence is oddly worded.** It reads that safety "was evaluated by monitoring immediate reaction and adverse effects" and then "There were no other adverse effects associated with the treatment".[^k121435] The word other implies a preceding list that the summary never gives, so what, if anything, was observed is not recoverable from this document.

**Why this one is worth reading next to [[dpl-spotlite]].** Both are OLP-coded blue-light acne handhelds on this site. The dpl filings describe consensus-standard testing and no acne outcome at all; this one describes an actual study with subjects, a schedule and a percentage. Neither has a control group. The difference is in what the manufacturer chose to measure and publish, not in demonstrated superiority of either device.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The cleared indication is mild to moderate acne; moderate to severe or scarring acne warrants a dermatologist rather than a light, and the treatment ladder is on [[acne]]. A device that warms the skin to a targeted 41 degrees C is doing something thermal as well as optical, and the manufacturer's instructions on contact time exist for that reason.

## Common Marketing Claims

> No brand copy is quoted here. Everything above is what Home Skinovations told the FDA in 2012 and 2013, which is a different audience from a shopper.

The claim most likely to travel from this filing into advertising is the 56.7% figure. It is real, it is the manufacturer's own, and it comes from an uncontrolled study of fifty people, so it describes what happened to those fifty rather than what the device adds over leaving acne alone.
