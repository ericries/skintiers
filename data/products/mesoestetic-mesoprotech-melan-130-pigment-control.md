Mesoprotech Melan 130 Pigment Control is a tinted, very-high-SPF facial sunscreen from the Spanish brand Mesoestetic, sold in the US as an FDA OTC Drug Facts product with three declared active filters, avobenzone 3%, homosalate 8%, and octocrylene 8.5%, plus a titanium dioxide and iron oxide tint marketed for skin with pigmentation imperfections.[^dailymed][^brand]

> A genuine broad-spectrum chemical sunscreen (avobenzone, homosalate, octocrylene) with a real visible-light-absorbing iron oxide tint, both well-supported ingredient classes for pigment-prone skin, [set out on The Evidence](#the-evidence).[^dailymed] The "SPF 50+" on the box and the brand's "pigment control" framing are not independently verified by a product-specific trial here, and the label discloses no iron oxide percentage, so the depigmenting claim rests on the ingredient class, not a tested dose.[^dailymed][^brand]

## Summary

Melan 130 Pigment Control is a 50 mL (1.69 fl oz) "tinted suncream" that Mesoestetic's own US site listed at $60.49, filed under the site's "PHOTOPROTECTION SOLUTIONS" category and described for "skin with pigmentation imperfections."[^brand] The product carries an FDA OTC Drug Facts label registered under NDC 76450-001-01, labeler Mesoestetic Pharma Group SL, marketed under OTC Monograph M020 (the FDA's sunscreen monograph) since May 1, 2025; the DailyMed package labeling gives the product title as "Mesoprotech Melan 130 Pigment Control Sunscreen SPF 50+."[^dailymed]

The label's three declared active ingredients are avobenzone 3%, homosalate 8%, and octocrylene 8.5%, purpose "Sunscreen."[^dailymed] These are the same class of organic UV filters covered independently on [[avobenzone]], [[homosalate]], and [[octocrylene]], and their combined health benefit, broad-spectrum sunburn protection, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^dailymed] The tint that gives this product its "pigment control" name is not part of the three declared actives: titanium dioxide and, per the Drug Facts panel's inactive-ingredient list, "synthetic iron oxide" are both listed as inactive ingredients rather than active filters, alongside two additional organic UV filters common in European formulations but not on the US OTC monograph, bemotrizinol (listed by its INCI name, bis-ethylhexyloxyphenol methoxyphenyl triazine) and bisoctrizole (methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol).[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

The health-relevant claim, sunburn and broad-spectrum UV protection, rests on avobenzone, homosalate, and octocrylene at the label's declared 3%, 8%, and 8.5% concentrations, an established combination whose general evidence base is on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] and the individual ingredient pages [[avobenzone]], [[homosalate]], and [[octocrylene]].[^dailymed] No published trial tests this exact formulation.

> **Bottom line.** The sunscreen case is a well-evidenced combination of established organic filters at disclosed doses; the "pigment control" positioning is a real, plausible mechanism (visible-light-absorbing tint) but not a product-specific trial finding.[^dailymed]

The cosmetic, pigment-targeted claim rests on the product's tint. Titanium dioxide and synthetic iron oxide, both listed in the label's inactive-ingredient list rather than as declared actives, function as a visible tint; iron oxides in particular absorb visible light in a way that UV-only chemical filters do not, and that property is relevant to pigmentation-prone skin because visible light can worsen [[melasma]] and post-inflammatory [[hyperpigmentation]] even when a non-tinted broad-spectrum sunscreen is used correctly, [detailed on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] and [[iron-oxides]]](#the-evidence).[^dailymed] The label discloses no iron oxide percentage for this product, so that benefit is graded modest and preliminary here, resting on the ingredient class rather than a tested dose in this specific formula.

## What's In It

**Active ingredients (FDA Drug Facts label, verbatim):** "Avobenzone 3% ... Homosalate 8% ... Octocrylene 8.5%," purpose "Sunscreen."[^dailymed]

**Uses (verbatim):** "helps prevent sunburn. if used as directed with other sun protection measures (see Directions) decreases the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging caused by the sun."[^dailymed]

**Warnings (verbatim):** "For external use only. Do not use on damaged or broken skin. When using this product keep out of eyes. Rinse with water to remove. Stop use and ask a doctor if rash occurs. Keep out of reach of children. If swallowed, get medical help or contact a Poison Control Center right away."[^dailymed]

**Directions (verbatim):** "Apply liberally 15 minutes before sun exposure. Reapply: after 40 minutes of swimming or sweating, immediately after towel drying, at least every 2 hours."[^dailymed]

**Inactive ingredients (verbatim from the Drug Facts label):** "Water, dibutyl adipate, cyclopentasiloxane, bis-ethylhexyloxyphenol methoxyphenyl triazine, alcohol denat., titanium dioxide, methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol, cyclohexasiloxane, PEG-30 dipolyhydroxystearate, polysilicone-15, styrene/acrylates copolymer, PEG-10 dimethicone, silicon dioxide, bis-ethylhexyl hydroxydimethoxy benzylmalonate, polyamide-5, potassium azeloyl diglycinate, jojoba esters, hexyl laurate, sunflower (Helianthus annuus) seed oil, dimethicone, ethylhexylglycerin, green wattle (Acacia decurrens) flower wax, polyglycerin-3, polyamide-3, cetyl PEG/PPG-10/1 dimethicone, polyglyceryl-4 isostearate, sodium chloride, VP/hexadecane copolymer, decyl glucoside, disteardimonium hectorite, disodium EDTA, PEG-12 alkyl ether, hydrogenated lecithin, propylene glycol, xanthan gum, pentaerythrityl tetra-di-t-butyl hydroxyhydrocinnamate, phenoxyethanol, fragrance, synthetic iron oxide."[^dailymed] Among these, [[titanium-dioxide|titanium dioxide]] and synthetic [[iron-oxides|iron oxide]] supply the tint and visible-light protection, and bemotrizinol and bisoctrizole are additional organic UV filters not recognized as actives under the US monograph.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Sunscreen SPF 50+."**[^dailymed] This figure appears in the DailyMed package labeling as part of the product title, but the human-readable FDA Drug Facts panel shows only the standard monograph boilerplate ("regularly use a sunscreen with a Broad Spectrum SPF value of 15 or higher"), not a product-specific tested SPF number; the brand's own US product page instead expresses protection as a "high protection factor (UVB, 131, UVA 67)" rather than an "SPF 50+" claim.[^dailymed][^brand] The three declared active filters and their concentrations are independently verified above.[^dailymed]
- **"Pigment control" / "water resistant."**[^brand] The brand page lists the product as "water resistant," and the DailyMed directions specify reapplication "after 40 minutes of swimming or sweating," consistent with the standard 40-minute FDA water-resistance tier.[^dailymed][^brand] "Pigment control" is the brand's product name and positioning; the underlying mechanism, a titanium dioxide and iron oxide tint that can add visible-light protection, is real at the ingredient-class level but not confirmed by a product-specific pigmentation trial in the sources reviewed here.
