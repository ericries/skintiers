## Summary

Lac-Hydrin was the originator prescription brand of 12% ammonium lactate, and it is the reason the strength exists as a drug at all. **Bottom line:** it is discontinued in both forms, and FDA's own record says that was not for safety or effectiveness reasons.[^drugsfda] What remains is the same molecule at the same strength, as AB-rated prescription generics and as an over-the-counter cosmetic. The evidence lives on [[ammonium-lactate]], graded good; this page covers what is specific to the product, which is mostly its label.

> **Discontinued, not withdrawn.** Lac-Hydrin Lotion was approved as NDA 019155 on 24 April 1985 and Lac-Hydrin Cream as NDA 020508 on 29 August 1996, both now held by Sun Pharmaceutical Industries and both listed Discontinued. Each product entry carries the note that there was a "Federal Register determination that product was not discontinued or withdrawn for safety or effectiveness reasons".[^drugsfda] If you were prescribed Lac-Hydrin by name, what you will be dispensed is a generic.

## The Rubric

**For the approved indication, the grade is inherited and this page does not re-argue it.** Lac-Hydrin's label indication is "the treatment of dry, scaly skin (xerosis) and ichthyosis vulgaris and for temporary relief of itching associated with these conditions".[^spl] The double-blind trials behind that indication, including the ones that beat vehicle and beat 5% lactic acid, are set out on [[ammonium-lactate]], which grades the ingredient **good**. Effect size modest to notable for xerosis, evidence solid. Nothing about the brand changes that.

**What the product adds is a number almost nobody publishes: a pH.** The label states that Lac-Hydrin "specially formulates 12% lactic acid, neutralized with ammonium hydroxide, as ammonium lactate to provide a lotion pH of 4.4-5.5".[^spl] For an exfoliating acid product that is the second half of the information you need, and most of the category withholds it; compare [[dr-dennis-gross-alpha-beta-universal-daily-peel]], which names five acids and publishes neither a percentage nor a pH.

**It is fragranced and preserved with parabens.** Beyond the 12% lactic acid and the ammonium hydroxide that neutralises it, the label says the lotion "also contains cetyl alcohol, fragrance, glycerin, glyceryl stearate, laureth-4, light mineral oil, magnesium aluminum silicate, methylcellulose, methyl- and propyl parabens, PEG-100 stearate, polyoxyl 40 stearate, propylene glycol and water".[^spl] That is an alphabetical excipient list in the drug-label convention, not a concentration-ordered INCI, so it says what is present and not how much. Fragrance and parabens in a twice-daily leave-on product on already-compromised skin are worth knowing about; this page does not treat either as inherently harmful.

**Record finding: the Lotion label's indications paragraph names the Cream.** In the archived Lotion 12% label, the INDICATIONS AND USAGE section opens "Lac-Hydrin Cream is indicated for the treatment of dry, scaly skin (xerosis)".[^spl] That is what the structured section contains, verified in the source XML rather than inferred from stripped text. It is a transcription defect in a label revised January 2008, not a difference in approved use.

## The Evidence

The clinical evidence is the ingredient's, and it is set out with its citations on [[ammonium-lactate]]: independently reported double-blind trials in xerosis, a vehicle-controlled trial in ichthyosis, and foot-xerosis trials that found no difference against other keratolytics. No trial is specific to the brand rather than to the 12% formulation the label describes.[^spl]

Three label-level findings are specific enough to belong here, because none of them appear on the ingredient page.

**Absorption.** "An in vitro study of percutaneous absorption of Lac-Hydrin Lotion using human cadaver skin indicates that approximately 5.8% of the material was absorbed after 68 hours."[^spl] In vitro, on cadaver skin, over a period no one applies a lotion for; it bounds systemic exposure rather than measuring it.

**Adverse reaction rates, with denominators.** The label gives the most frequent adverse experiences in patients with xerosis as "transient stinging (1 in 30 patients), burning (1 in 30 patients), erythema (1 in 50 patients) and peeling (1 in 60 patients)", and reports that in ichthyosis, with more severe baseline skin, transient stinging, burning and erythema each occurred "in 1 in 10 patients".[^spl] Rates with denominators are rare on a moisturiser of any kind, and the ichthyosis figures are a threefold-or-worse jump.

**The sun warning has a study behind it.** The label reports that two years of topical 12%, 21% or 30% ammonium lactate on CD-1 mice "did not produce a significant increase in dermal or systemic tumors in the absence of increased exposure to ultraviolet radiation", but that "a long-term photocarcinogenicity study in hairless albino mice suggested that topically applied 12% ammonium lactate formulations enhanced the rate of ultraviolet light-induced skin tumor formation".[^spl] That is a mouse finding, and it is the stated reason the label tells patients to minimise or avoid sun on treated skin, including the face. Read alongside it: the Precautions state that "The potential for post-inflammatory hypo- or hyperpigmentation has not been studied", while hyperpigmentation is listed among the less frequent adverse reactions.[^spl] A gap the label itself names is worth more than a reassurance it does not give.

> **Bottom line.** A well-evidenced approved drug whose brand has left the market for commercial rather than safety reasons. Buy the generic or the cosmetic; the label is still the best-documented one in this category, and its sun warning rests on an animal photocarcinogenicity signal rather than on general acid caution.

## What you can buy instead

Drugs@FDA lists ammonium lactate 12% as currently marketed by prescription, AB-rated to the discontinued Lac-Hydrin, from three sponsors:[^drugsfda]

| Form | Sponsor | Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lotion | Padagis Israel | ANDA 075570 |
| Lotion | Sun Pharma Canada | ANDA 076216 |
| Lotion | Zydus Lifesciences | ANDA 217758 |
| Cream | Padagis Israel | ANDA 075774 |
| Cream | Sun Pharma Canada | ANDA 075883 |
| Cream | Zydus Lifesciences | ANDA 217815 |

Two further applications, Watson Labs' ANDA 075575 lotion and ANDA 076829 cream, are listed Discontinued.[^drugsfda] An AB rating means FDA considers the generic therapeutically equivalent to the brand, so the inherited grade above applies to all six.

Over the counter, [[amlactin-daily-moisturizing-lotion-12-lactic-acid]] is sold at the same 12% strength and is graded here as a cosmetic rather than a drug. That is the trade: same acid at the same strength, no approved claim for ichthyosis vulgaris, no prescription.
