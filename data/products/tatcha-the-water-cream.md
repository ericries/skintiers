Tatcha The Water Cream is a fragrance-containing, oil-free gel-cream facial moisturizer from Tatcha, sold with an included gold-tone spoon in a 1.7 fl oz / 50 mL size for $74.00.[^tatcha]

> An oil-free gel-cream whose real, defensible benefit is basic cosmetic hydration: glycerin, diglycerin, and sodium hyaluronate as humectants, backed by dimethicone and several related silicones for a smooth, non-greasy after-feel (see [[glycerin]], [[hyaluronic-acid]], and [[dimethicone]]). The brand's "pore-refining," "anti-aging," and 72-hour-hydration marketing rests on its own small, uncontrolled panel studies (27 to 36 people), not an independently published or controlled trial, and its on-page ingredient carousel highlights "Japanese coix seed" as a key active even though no coix-derived ingredient appears in the brand's own declared ingredient list for this product ([see What's In It](#whats-in-it) and [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^tatcha]

## Summary

Tatcha positions The Water Cream as a "water-burst" gel-cream that "bursts on contact to flood skin with hydration," claiming it is "proven to refine pores and smooth texture with a BHA alternative while restoring radiance."[^tatcha] It is marketed for oily and combination skin, and the brand's site-wide comparison widget summarizes it as delivering "3x hydration while visibly refining pores and smoothing texture with a BHA alternative" in a "Lightweight, water-burst gel cream" texture.[^tatcha] Directions on the brand's page call for scooping cream with the included gold spoon and massaging it onto "face, neck and decolletage in upward strokes," daily, morning and night.[^tatcha]

The part of that story the declared ingredient list actually supports is hydration and after-feel, not pore refinement or anti-aging. Glycerin and diglycerin, well-evidenced humectants, sit 5th and 6th in the formula; sodium hyaluronate, another humectant, is listed further down (16th of 35 ingredients), consistent with a modest, brand-undisclosed concentration. Dimethicone sits 3rd, and the formula layers in several more silicone derivatives lower down, giving the cream its light, non-greasy occlusive feel. The general evidence for these humectant and silicone-occlusive mechanisms is set out on [[glycerin]], [[hyaluronic-acid]], and [[dimethicone]] ([see What's In It](#whats-in-it)).

Tatcha's own "Clinical Results" section claims figures such as "97% showed improvement in clogged and enlarged pores," "+27% improvement in skin barrier function," and "100% showed a reduction in oil," each footnoted only as coming from a small brand-run panel (27 or 36 people), described as a "bio-instrumentation study" or "expert graded study."[^tatcha] No control group, blinding, or independent publication is mentioned, so these are the brand's own claim, not independently verified evidence ([see Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).

A specific discrepancy is worth flagging: the same product page highlights "Japanese coix seed" in an ingredient carousel, crediting it with smoothing texture and refining pores, but the brand's own "See Full Ingredients" list for this exact product contains no coix-derived ingredient at all.[^tatcha] This page cannot resolve that mismatch; it is reported as-is rather than assumed away.

The brand's own site lists the 1.7 fl oz / 50 mL jar at $74.00 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-15).[^tatcha]

## The Evidence

No independently published or product-specific clinical trial of this exact cream was located; the evidence below is category-level, applied to what the declared ingredient list actually contains.

Glycerin and diglycerin are humectants with a long, well-evidenced record of drawing and holding water at the skin's surface; that general case is set out on [[glycerin]]. Sodium hyaluronate, listed lower in the formula (16th of 35 ingredients, after several botanical extracts and Gold), is a further humectant whose general evidence, including the caveat that molecular weight affects how deeply it penetrates, is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]; Tatcha does not disclose a concentration or molecular weight for this product.

Dimethicone, listed 3rd, is a silicone occlusive and emollient; its documented cosmetic function, "a moisturizing effect as well as a softening effect" with fewer of the tackiness or comedogenicity concerns of a heavier occlusive, is set out on [[dimethicone]]. The formula pairs it with several more silicone derivatives (Diphenylsiloxy Phenyl Trimethicone, PEG-9 Polydimethylsiloxyethyl Dimethicone, and two dimethicone crosspolymers) rather than petrolatum or ceramides, the stronger-evidenced occlusives and barrier lipids ranked higher on [[moisturizing]]'s own tier list. That composition, not a study result, is why this page treats the cream's case as basic cosmetic hydration for non-dry skin rather than barrier repair for compromised or very dry skin.

The brand's "Clinical Results" panel data (bio-instrumentation and "expert graded" testing of 27 to 36 people) is brand-commissioned, uncontrolled testing rather than an independently published trial, and is quarantined under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims) rather than graded as evidence.

> **Bottom line.** No independent study has isolated this cream's effect. Its defensible case rests on general humectant evidence (glycerin, diglycerin, sodium hyaluronate) and general silicone-occlusive evidence (dimethicone), applied to a formula with no petrolatum or ceramides. The brand's pore-refining, anti-aging, and oil-reduction claims rest on its own small, uncontrolled panel studies, not independent evidence.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not independently established by the declared ingredient list above:

- **"Proven to refine pores and smooth texture with a BHA alternative while restoring radiance."**[^tatcha] No BHA (salicylic acid or a related beta-hydroxy acid) appears in the declared ingredient list; the brand does not name which ingredient(s) constitute its "BHA alternative."
- **"Clinically proven to smooth skin texture immediately," "3x hydration," "97% showed improvement in clogged and enlarged pores," "+27% improvement in skin barrier function," "100% showed a reduction in oil," and related figures.**[^tatcha] Each is footnoted only as a "bio-instrumentation study of 27 panelists" or an "expert graded study of 36 panelists," brand-commissioned, uncontrolled testing with no stated placebo or vehicle comparison, blinding, or independent publication.
- **"Japanese coix seed"** is highlighted in an on-page ingredient carousel as smoothing texture and refining pores, but no coix-derived ingredient (e.g., Coix Lacryma-Jobi seed extract) appears anywhere in the same page's own declared Full Ingredients list for this product.[^tatcha]
- **"Anti-Aging Moisturizers" category tag and general "restoring radiance" language.**[^tatcha] No named anti-aging active (such as a retinoid or a disclosed-percentage peptide) is present in the declared ingredients; the anti-aging positioning rests on the cosmetic hydration and smoothing effects above, not a demonstrated anti-aging mechanism.
