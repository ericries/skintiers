Naked Sundays SPF50 Clear Glow Sun Stick is an Australian stick-format sunscreen built on four organic UV filters, avobenzone 3%, octocrylene 8%, homosalate 10%, and octyl salicylate 5%, marketed as an invisible, no-white-cast top-up stick for face and body.[^1] No independent Australian retailer listing this SKU was found to corroborate the manufacturer's stated composition, size, or price; the figures below rest on the manufacturer's own page alone.

> A four-filter chemical-only stick sunscreen labeled SPF50 Broad Spectrum. Its filter set, avobenzone stabilized by octocrylene for UVA and octocrylene/homosalate/octisalate for UVB, is a well-established combination and the general evidence for it lives on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] and the individual filter pages.[^1] Its homosalate, at 10%, sits above the 7.34% cap the EU imposes on homosalate in face products; this is a face-and-body product not sold under EU rules, but the number is worth knowing on a stick marketed for face top-ups (on [[homosalate]]).[^2] It is listed at AUD $39.95 for 19 g, discounted to $27.95 at the time of access, per the manufacturer's own listing (see the note on sourcing above).[^1]

## Summary

SPF50 Clear Glow Sun Stick is Naked Sundays' clear (non-tinted) sunscreen stick, sold as a 19 g twist-up for reapplying SPF over makeup or on the body without sticky fingers.[^1] Its core purpose is the same as any sunscreen's: broad-spectrum UV protection is the best-evidenced way to slow photoaging and lower the risk of sun-related skin cancer, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

The brand's own listing states the active ingredients (w/w) as butyl methoxydibenzoylmethane (avobenzone) 3%, octocrylene 8%, homosalate 10%, and octyl salicylate (octisalate) 5%, an all-organic (chemical) filter set labeled SPF50 Broad Spectrum.[^1] Avobenzone is the formula's only dedicated UVA filter, and octocrylene, alongside its own UVB and short-wave UVA-II contribution, serves as its photostabilizer ([the filter roles are detailed in The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1] The full ingredient list is short and declares no added fragrance.[^1]

What distinguishes it from a lotion sunscreen in the same filter family, such as [[neutrogena-ultra-sheer-dry-touch-spf-55]], is mainly the stick format and the marketing pitch: "100% invisible" with "zero white cast," pitched at oily and acne-prone skin as a fragrance-free, makeup-safe top-up rather than a primary daily-wear lotion.[^1]

It is listed at AUD $39.95 for the single 19 g stick on Naked Sundays' own site, shown discounted to $27.95 during a site-wide sale at the time of access (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-15).[^1]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific product was located; what is specific to it is its filter composition and label rating.

The brand's listing states the actives as avobenzone 3%, octocrylene 8%, homosalate 10%, and octyl salicylate 5%, at SPF50, Broad Spectrum.[^1] SPF is a UVB (sunburn) number; the SPF50 rating certifies UVB strength and does not by itself certify UVA (photoaging) protection, a separate axis described on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

Three of the four filters, octocrylene, homosalate, and octisalate, are UVB absorbers; UVA coverage rests on avobenzone alone.[^1] [[avobenzone]] provides genuine UVA coverage once photostabilized, but it degrades under sunlight unless a co-filter holds it stable; [[octocrylene]], present here at 8%, serves that stabilizing role alongside its own UVB and short-wave UVA-II absorption. [[octisalate]] adds a weak, secondary UVB contribution at 5%, and [[homosalate]] carries the largest single UVB load at 10%. Measured against the global field, this is the same older organic-filter family used in most US and Australian sunscreens: avobenzone is a weaker, less photostable UVA filter than the modern organic filters (bemotrizinol/Tinosorb S, bisoctrizole/Tinosorb M, the Mexoryl filters) standard in the EU and Korea, a comparison detailed on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

On homosalate specifically, the EU's scientific committee restricted homosalate to 7.34% in face products after finding the previously permitted 10% "not safe," over concerns about possible endocrine activity; this product's 10% homosalate exceeds that face-product cap, though it is an Australian product not sold under EU rules (on [[homosalate]]).[^2]

> **Bottom line.** The labeled SPF50 Broad Spectrum rating reflects a genuine chemical filter set: octocrylene, homosalate, and octisalate carry the UVB side, and avobenzone, stabilized by octocrylene, carries the UVA side.[^1] That UVA coverage is real but older and less photostable than the modern-filter products sold outside the US and Australia, and the 10% homosalate load exceeds the EU's face-product cap for that filter.

The label's directions say to apply at least 20 minutes before sun exposure and to reapply every 2 hours outdoors, especially after swimming, sweating, or towel drying.[^1] How much protection any SPF delivers in practice depends on applying enough product, a point covered generally on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## What's In It

The active filters and full ingredient list as declared on the brand's own product page.[^1] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

UV filters (active ingredients, w/w):

- [[avobenzone]] (butyl methoxydibenzoylmethane) 3%, the formula's only dedicated UVA filter
- [[octocrylene]] 8%, a UVB and short-wave UVA-II filter that also stabilizes avobenzone
- [[homosalate]] 10%, a UVB-only filter, the largest single active load in the formula
- [[octisalate]] (octyl salicylate, listed as ethylhexyl salicylate in the full ingredient list) 5%, a weak, secondary UVB filter

Base, texture, and preservative system: caprylic/capric triglyceride, ozokerite (a wax base for the stick format), dicaprylyl carbonate, butyloctyl salicylate, polyethylene, and Citrullus Lanatus (watermelon) seed oil.[^1]

The full ingredient list is short, 10 ingredients total including the active filters, and declares no added fragrance.[^1]
