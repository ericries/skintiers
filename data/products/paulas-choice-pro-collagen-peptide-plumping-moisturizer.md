## The Evidence

Topical peptides are well-tolerated ingredients with a modest, mostly preliminary measured effect on the appearance of aging; the class-level trial evidence, and how it splits between better-studied peptide types, is set out in full on [[peptides]]. Nothing specific to this product or its three named peptides was found: acetyl sh-hexapeptide-5 amide acetate, oligopeptide-1, and tridecapeptide-1 do not appear among the individually controlled-trial peptides (pal-KTTKS, Argireline, GHK-Cu) reviewed there, so this product's grade rests on the pooled class evidence, not a peptide-specific or product-specific trial.[^1]

That pooled evidence, a 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of 19 randomized trials (1,341 participants) of oral and topical peptides, found a "modest pooled effect on wrinkle reduction" that was "largely driven by oral polypeptides" rather than topical formulations, with "inconsistent" effects on elasticity and density, though hydration and brightness both improved significantly (full citation and quotes on [[peptides]]).[^1] On the hydration side, this product's base leans on ingredients with a more solid track record: glycerin and a hyaluronic acid derivative as humectants, squalane and dimethicone as emollient and occlusive agents, all well-established for short-term skin hydration independent of the peptide story (general evidence on [[moisturizing]]).[^1]

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product or its three named peptides. Its plumping and firmness grade rests on the general, modest, mostly-preliminary case for topical peptides as a class; its hydration grade rests on the more solid, separate evidence for its humectant and emollient base.[^1]
