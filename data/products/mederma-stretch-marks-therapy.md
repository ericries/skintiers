Mederma Stretch Marks Therapy is a botanical stretch-mark cream from [[mederma]], built around Cepalin (an onion/Allium cepa extract), hyaluronic acid, and Centella asiatica, sold as a 150 g (5.29 oz) tube for twice-daily use.[^brand]

> A moisturizing botanical cream marketed to prevent and reduce stretch marks. Its three actives are the same ones the [[stretch-marks]] evidence review singles out as lacking the data to rank: onion extract, hyaluronic acid, and Centella asiatica. No independent trial of this specific cream was located, so the brand's "clinically shown" and "#1 doctor-recommended" are marketing, not a result you can lean on. Judge it as a hydrating cream that may make marks look temporarily better, not a treatment that erases them.

## Summary

This is Mederma's stretch-mark product, distinct from the onion-extract scar gels the brand is better known for. The brand describes it as a "cream formula bringing together Cepalin botanical extract, Hyaluronic Acid, and Centella Asiatica extract," and its own FAQ confirms Cepalin is "allium cepae (onion extract)."[^brand] It is applied twice a day, and the brand says results appear "after 4 weeks" with "greater improvements" by 12 weeks.[^brand]

The problem is the evidence, which lives on [[stretch-marks]]: a 2016 review concluded "there is no topical formulation which is shown to be most effective," and this cream's three actives are precisely the prevention creams that page flags as unranked for lack of data.[^brand] [[centella-asiatica]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]] are each graded weak on their own pages, and onion extract's own track record, built on scar studies, is thin.

## The Evidence

No independent, product-specific trial of this cream was located. The brand states it is "clinically shown to improve appearance of stretch marks," but points to no published, independent study, so that claim cannot be verified here.[^brand]

What is defensible is the general picture on [[stretch-marks]]: established stretch marks respond poorly to any topical, early red striae (striae rubrae) do better than old white ones, and the treatments most likely to help established marks are in-office procedures outside a cream's reach. Against that backdrop, a twice-daily emollient can hydrate skin and make marks look temporarily softer, but that is a moisturizer effect shared by any cream, not evidence that these botanicals prevent or erase striae.

> **Bottom line.** A pleasant, hydrating botanical cream whose active trio (onion extract, hyaluronic acid, Centella asiatica) has weak-to-absent evidence for stretch marks specifically, and no independent trial of this formula. Reasonable as a moisturizer; treat "clinically shown" and "#1 doctor-recommended" as marketing, and see [[stretch-marks]] for what topicals can and cannot do.[^brand]

## What's In It

The brand names three functional actives: Cepalin (onion/Allium cepa extract, Mederma's signature botanical), hyaluronic acid (a humectant), and Centella asiatica extract, in a moisturizing cream base.[^brand] A full INCI list is not printed on the brand product page; the site markets the trio above as the "unique triple action formula."[^brand]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Clinically shown to improve appearance of stretch marks."** A brand claim; no independent published trial of this specific cream was located, and the general evidence for these actives is weak (see [[stretch-marks]]).[^brand]
- **"#1 doctor-recommended brand of Stretch Mark Cream."** The brand footnotes this as "of U.S. physicians and pharmacists surveyed by an independent market research firm," a survey of recommendation habits, not evidence that the cream works.[^brand]
- **"Helps cell renewal and collagen formation."** A mechanism claim for the "triple action formula"; it is not supported by a product-specific outcome trial.[^brand]
