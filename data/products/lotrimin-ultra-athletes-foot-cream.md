Lotrimin Ultra is butenafine hydrochloride 1% cream from Bayer, and it is one of the few products on this site that is genuinely **FDA-approved** rather than sold under an OTC monograph.[^label] **Bottom line:** for athlete's foot between the toes it has the strongest regulatory footing of any antifungal covered here, and its own label tells you where that evidence stops. Active-ingredient evidence is on [[butenafine]].

> **The most useful sentence on the box is an admission.** Right after claiming it "cures most athlete's foot between the toes", the label adds: "Effectiveness on the bottom or sides of foot is unknown."[^label] That is a manufacturer volunteering a gap in its own evidence, in the Uses section, where buyers actually look. Athlete's foot on the sole and sides is a real and common pattern, and this product does not claim to treat it.

## Summary

Butenafine hydrochloride 1%, marketing category NDA, approval NDA021307, distributed by Bayer HealthCare LLC, on the market since 2002.[^label] For athlete's foot between the toes the label gives two options: "apply to affected skin between and around the toes twice a day for 1 week (morning and night), or once a day for 4 weeks".[^label] For jock itch and ringworm it is "once a day to affected skin for 2 weeks".[^label] Adults and children 12 and older; "children under 12 years: ask a doctor".[^label] The structured package listings cover 12 g, 15 g and 30 g tubes.[^label] No price is quoted here; no retailer was fetched and no metered lookup was spent.

## The Rubric

**Athlete's foot between the toes**
- **Effect size: notable.** The claim is a cure claim, not a relief claim: it "cures most athlete's foot between the toes".[^label] Most, not all.
- **Evidence quality: solid.** The marketing category in the FDA label is NDA, under approval NDA021307, which means FDA reviewed data for this product and this claim.[^label] That is a materially higher bar than the monograph route behind most OTC antifungals, which [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] explains. The clinical evidence for the molecule itself is graded on [[butenafine]] and is not repeated here.

**Athlete's foot on the bottom or sides of the foot**
- **Effect size: none that can be stated.** "Effectiveness on the bottom or sides of foot is unknown."[^label]
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal at best.** There is nothing to weigh. Treat any claim that it works for sole or side involvement as unsupported by the label, and see [[athletes-foot]] for what that pattern needs.

**Jock itch and ringworm**
- **Effect size: notable.** Again a cure claim, "cures most jock itch and ringworm", plus relief of "itching, burning, cracking, and scaling which accompany these conditions".[^label]
- **Evidence quality: solid.** Same NDA.[^label] Body ringworm context is on [[tinea-corporis-ringworm]]; scalp involvement is a different problem, on [[tinea-capitis]], and the label is explicit that you should not use this on nails or scalp.[^label]

## The Evidence

**What the one-week regimen is really saying.** A single week of twice-daily use is the shortest course among the antifungals on this site, and the label offers once daily for four weeks as the alternative for the same indication.[^label] Both are label-sanctioned, so the short course is not a shortcut. What it does mean is that stopping early because the itching resolved is a different thing from completing the stated course, and the directions also tell you to "wash the affected skin with soap and water and dry completely before applying".[^label]

**Where the label draws hard lines.** "For external use only". Do not use "on nails or scalp", "in or near the mouth or the eyes", or "for vaginal yeast infections".[^label] Those exclusions matter because fungal nail and scalp infections are common reasons people reach for an antifungal cream, and this one is not for either.

**Composition, briefly.** Inactive ingredients are "benzyl alcohol, cetyl alcohol, glycerin, glyceryl monostearate SE, polyoxyethylene (23) cetyl ether, propylene glycol dicaprylate, purified water, sodium benzoate, stearic acid, trolamine, white petrolatum".[^label] Benzyl alcohol and propylene glycol derivatives are the entries most likely to matter to anyone with a known contact sensitivity.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The label's own instruction applies: stop use and ask a doctor "if too much irritation occurs or irritation gets worse", and ask a doctor for children under 12.[^label]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"cures most athlete's foot between the toes"**. Supported, and precisely scoped.[^label] The qualifier "between the toes" is doing real work, and "most" is not "all".
- **"Effectiveness on the bottom or sides of foot is unknown"**. Not a marketing claim at all, but the most informative line on the package.[^label]
- **"cures most jock itch and ringworm"**. Supported under the same approval.[^label]
- **"relieves itching, burning, cracking, and scaling which accompany these conditions"**. A symptom-relief claim sitting alongside the cure claims; read it as describing the accompanying symptoms, not as a separate efficacy claim.[^label]

## Where to Buy

Not priced here. It is a widely stocked drugstore product sold under NDC 11523-4338 in 12 g, 15 g and 30 g tubes.[^label]
