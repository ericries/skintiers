StriVectin TL Advanced Tightening Neck Cream PLUS is a $99, 1.7 oz moisturizer from StriVectin marketed specifically for the neck and décolletage, built around a proprietary "Alpha-3 Peptide" complex and myristyl nicotinate, a niacin ester related to [[niacinamide]].[^strivectin] The brand's own copy describes it as going "beyond a typical moisturizer" to address "slack, sagging skin, horizontal neck lines, dehydrated skin and age spots," but no independent, peer-reviewed trial of this cream was found; its evidence rests on a brand-run consumer panel and on category-level ingredient science covered on [[peptides]] and [[neck-chest-decolletage-care]].[^strivectin]

> A neck-specific moisturizer priced at $99 for 1.7 oz, built on a blend of peptides, a niacin ester, and a mild botanical extract. The tightening and firming language is cosmetic, appearance-of-skin claims, not a health treatment, and the supporting numbers (a 35-subject, brand-run consumer-perception panel) are not an independent or vehicle-controlled trial. The formula's actives sit inside categories, peptides and niacinamide-family compounds, with genuine but modest category-level cosmetic evidence, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence).[^strivectin]

## Summary

StriVectin markets this as the flagship product in its neck-specific line, positioned above a typical face or body moisturizer for the thinner, more mobile skin of the neck and chest; general guidance on why that skin is treated differently is on [[neck-chest-decolletage-care]].[^strivectin] It ships in 0.25 oz, 1.7 oz, and 3.4 oz sizes, with the 1.7 oz size listed at $99.00 on the manufacturer's own site.[^strivectin]

The brand names three "key ingredients" in its own marketing: Alpha-3 Peptide (a proprietary peptide blend the brand says "targets skin's natural Collagen, Elastin, and Hyaluronic Acid to visibly plump and smooth"), Spilanthol (a "phytotensor" extract of *Acmella oleracea*, the same plant known as the "facelift plant" or paracress, that the brand says helps "improve the appearance of firmness by visibly tightening and strengthening skin"), and bearberry (*Arctostaphylos uva-ursi*) extract, which the brand says "helps visibly brighten and even skin tone."[^strivectin] These sit inside a base of shea butter, dimethicone-family emollients, and humectants. The formula's INCI also lists myristyl nicotinate, an ester of niacin chemically related to but distinct from [[niacinamide]], and several small peptides (Pentapeptide-84 Amide Acetate, Acetyl Dipeptide-1 Cetyl Ester); the general cosmetic evidence for that class of ingredients is set out on [[peptides]].[^strivectin] All of these are cosmetic (appearance-level) actives; none is an FDA-regulated drug ingredient, so this is a cosmetic, not a health, product.

## The Evidence

No published, peer-reviewed, controlled trial of this exact cream was found. The brand's own site cites three internal figures: "94% saw visible improvement in firmness" and "94% showed improvement in overall youthful appearance," both footnoted as "based on consumer perception of 35 subjects after 8 weeks," and "94% showed visible improvement in the look of age spots and radiance on the décolleté," footnoted as "based on expert grading of 35 subjects after 8 weeks."[^strivectin] The page also claims improvement "in just 4 weeks" in the look of slack/sagging skin, firmness and crepey texture, horizontal necklines, vertical "sleep wrinkles," and décolleté age spots, footnoted as "based on expert grading on 35 subjects after 4 weeks."[^strivectin]

These are self-reported, brand-run studies with no stated control group, no stated blinding, and no publication in a peer-reviewed journal, so they cannot be weighed the way an independent vehicle-controlled trial can. A 35-subject, uncontrolled, brand-sponsored panel supports, at most, a weak signal that some users perceived a cosmetic change; it is not evidence the product outperforms a plain moisturizer or a vehicle control.

> **Bottom line.** The case for "tightening" rests on a small, brand-run, unpublished panel, not an independent trial. The formula's individual active classes, peptides and niacin esters, have genuine but modest category-level cosmetic evidence, covered on [[peptides]] and [[niacinamide]]; no trial connects that category evidence to this specific $99 cream.

- **"Tightens. Firms. Lifts."** and "In just 4 weeks, see improvement in the look of: slack, sagging skin; firmness and crepey texture; horizontal necklines; vertical sleep wrinkles; age spots."[^strivectin] These are cosmetic, appearance-level claims footnoted to the brand's own 35-subject expert-grading panel, not an independent or vehicle-controlled trial; treat as a brand-reported signal, not established evidence.
- **"94% saw visible improvement in firmness."**[^strivectin] Footnoted as "consumer perception of 35 subjects after 8 weeks," meaning the 94% figure reflects what a small panel of paid or recruited users reported perceiving about their own skin, not a measured, independent outcome.
