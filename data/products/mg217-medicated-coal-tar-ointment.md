MG217 Psoriasis Medicated Multi-Symptom Coal Tar Ointment is an over-the-counter petrolatum-based ointment from Wisconsin Pharmacal Company, labeled under the FDA's OTC coal tar monograph (M032) at 2% coal tar for the relief of itching, scaling, flaking, redness, and irritation of psoriasis and/or seborrheic dermatitis.[^dailymed] It is not a disease-modifying drug: the label claims symptom relief and recurrence prevention, not plaque clearance, and it carries a California Proposition 65 cancer warning for coal tar.[^dailymed]

> An OTC monograph coal tar ointment, not a branded innovation with its own clinical trials. Coal tar has decades of use as a keratolytic and antipruritic for [[psoriasis]] and [[seborrheic-dermatitis|seborrheic dermatitis]], and this product's own label restricts its claims to symptom relief, itching, scaling, flaking, redness, and irritation, plus helping prevent recurrence, not to clearing plaques or slowing disease progression.[^dailymed] The full evidence base for coal tar as an ingredient is graded on [[coal-tar]], not repeated here; this page covers what is specific to this product, its formulation and its own label language.

## Summary

This is a petrolatum-based ointment containing coal tar 2%, derived from Coal Tar Topical Solution, USP 20%, as declared on the FDA-regulated Drug Facts label.[^dailymed] It is regulated as an OTC monograph drug (FDA OTC Monograph M032), meaning it is not FDA-approved through a New Drug Application with product-specific trials; instead it complies with the long-standing coal tar monograph that permits marketing without a fresh efficacy dossier for each product.[^dailymed]

The label's purpose line reads "Psoriasis, Seborrheic Dermatitis, Dandruff Treatment," and the uses section states the product is "For the relief of these symptoms associated with psoriasis and/or seborrheic dermatitis: itching, scaling, flaking, redness, irritation of the skin. Helps prevent recurrence."[^dailymed] That is a health claim, symptom relief for two diagnosed inflammatory skin conditions, not a cosmetic one, and it is honest in scope: the label does not claim to clear plaques, reduce lesion counts, or alter the underlying disease course of psoriasis. Coal tar's mechanism and the wider evidence for its keratolytic and antipruritic effects are graded on [[coal-tar]]; this page does not repeat that ingredient-level case.

The directions call for applying to affected areas 1 to 4 times daily, or as directed by a physician, a wider dosing range than the once-daily instructions typical of prescription topicals, reflecting a milder OTC-strength product meant to be titrated to symptoms.[^dailymed] The label also carries a Proposition 65 warning that coal tar is known to the State of California to cause cancer, and instructs users to avoid contact with the eyes, avoid prolonged use without consulting a physician, and use caution in sun exposure for up to 24 hours after application because of increased sunburn risk.[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

Coal tar's category-level evidence as a keratolytic and antipruritic for psoriasis and seborrheic dermatitis is set out on [[coal-tar]]; no product-specific clinical trial of this MG217 ointment formulation was found in the sources reviewed here. As an OTC monograph drug, it was not required to run one: FDA OTC monograph compliance rests on the ingredient (coal tar 2%, from a 20% USP topical solution) matching the concentration and formulation type the monograph allows, not on a per-brand efficacy trial.[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: the health claim on this label, symptom relief for psoriasis and seborrheic dermatitis, rests on coal tar's long-standing OTC monograph status and the ingredient-level evidence graded on [[coal-tar]], not on a trial of this specific ointment.

## What's In It

- **Active: Coal Tar 2%** (from Coal Tar Topical Solution, USP 20%), as declared on the FDA Drug Facts label.[^dailymed] See [[coal-tar]] for the graded mechanism and ingredient-level evidence base.
- **Inactive ingredients:** Petrolatum, C12-15 Alkyl Benzoate, Bis-Diglyceryl Polyacyladipate-2, PEG/PPG-18/18 Dimethicone, Glyceryl Stearate, PEG-100 Stearate, Vitamin E Acetate, Vitamin A (retinyl palmitate), Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol), as declared on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

The label warns against use around the rectum, genital area, or groin without asking a doctor first, against prolonged use without physician guidance, against use on large body areas without asking a doctor, and states the ointment is not recommended for scalp use unless directed by a physician because it may be difficult to remove from hair.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Helps prevent recurrence."**[^dailymed] This is drawn directly from the product's own FDA Drug Facts label, so it is a regulated OTC monograph claim rather than unregulated marketing copy; it is not accompanied on the label by trial data quantifying how much recurrence is reduced or over what time period.
- **"Multi-Symptom" naming implying broad relief.** The product name emphasizes breadth, but the label's actual uses section lists a specific, bounded symptom set, itching, scaling, flaking, redness, and irritation, tied to two named conditions; it is not a general-purpose skin treatment.
