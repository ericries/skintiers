## Summary

Vanicream Facial Moisturizer Broad Spectrum SPF 30 is a facial sunscreen from Vanicream, a brand positioned for sensitive and easily irritated skin (see [[vanicream-gentle-facial-cleanser]]), sold as a 2.5 oz tube.[^1][^2] Its single active ingredient is zinc oxide 19.5%, a mineral (inorganic) UV filter, rated Broad Spectrum SPF 30; both the manufacturer's product page and Target's listing agree on the active and the rating.[^1][^2] Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; zinc oxide's own filter profile, a real UVB absorber that is also photostable with strong long-wave UVA reach, is covered on [[zinc-oxide]].

What makes this a moisturizer as well as a sunscreen is the base: the inactive-ingredient list, matched on both sources, includes squalane and glycerin plus five named ceramide types (ceramide EOP, NG, NP, AS, and AP).[^1][^2] Ceramides are a genuine skin-barrier lipid with evidence for improving measured barrier function; whether this formula's ceramide content beats a plainer moisturizer has not been tested and is covered generally on [[ceramides]], not by a trial of this product.

Both sources describe the product as "dermatologist tested" and non-comedogenic, and both note it carries the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance.[^1][^2] Target's listing additionally states the product is free of dyes, fragrance, lanolin, and parabens.[^2] It sits at the moderate end of drugstore-adjacent mineral sunscreens on price: Target lists the 2.5 oz tube at $13.99 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^2]

## The Evidence

No trial isolates this product. Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing skin-cancer burden, set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Zinc oxide's own evidence as a UV filter, real UVB absorption plus photostable, distinctively long-reaching UVA coverage, is set out on [[zinc-oxide]], including the FDA's proposed concentration ceiling for the ingredient; this product uses zinc oxide 19.5% as its sole active, with no titanium dioxide or organic filter added.[^1][^2]

The moisturizer base is where this product goes beyond a plain sunscreen: it lists five named ceramide types (ceramide EOP, NG, NP, AS, AP) alongside glycerin and squalane, matched on both sources.[^1][^2] Ceramides have solid evidence for improving measured barrier function (transepidermal water loss) against an inactive base, without a demonstrated advantage over a plainer humectant-and-occlusive moisturizer, detailed on [[ceramides]]. No study of this specific formula's barrier performance is cited here.

> **Bottom line.** The sunscreen claim rests on a single, well-evidenced mineral filter at a substantial concentration (zinc oxide 19.5%, Broad Spectrum SPF 30), and the moisturizer claim rests on a ceramide-containing base whose general barrier-support case is established at the ingredient level, not by a trial of this specific product.[^1][^2]
