A $23 leave-on acid milk from Prequel, the brand founded by dermatologist Samantha Ellis.[^brand] **Bottom line:** the 15% figure belongs to a five-acid blend, and the ingredient list shows which one of the five is actually doing the work. Glycolic's own evidence is on [[glycolic-acid]].

> **Marketed as a multi-acid complex, built like a glycolic product.** The brand describes "our multi-acid complex including 15% glycolic, phytic, malic, mandelic and lactic acids along with enzymes".[^brand] On the ingredient list, Glycolic Acid sits second, directly after water, with Sodium Hydroxide third to neutralise it. Phytic, malic, mandelic and lactic acids all appear far lower, below the polymer thickener, the isoalkanes and the emollient; the two enzymes named, lipase and protease, sit lower still.[^inci] Four of the five acids are present, and present in small amounts.

## Summary

Priced at $23.00 for the peel itself, SKU PRQL-10035, from the brand's own catalogue; a wipes version is $20.00 and a duo $39.00.[^catalog] It is a leave-on: the directions say "Dispense a few shakes into hands or a cotton pad and apply to face and body. Do not rinse."[^brand] Frequency starts low by design, "Start with one application per week and build up to two applications per week as tolerated."[^brand]

**Full ingredient list, in label order:** Water (Aqua), Glycolic Acid, Sodium Hydroxide, Glycerin, Propanediol, Ethylhexyl Olivate, Hydroxyethyl Acrylate/Sodium Acryloyldimethyl Taurate Copolymer, C13-14 Isoalkane, C11-12 Isoalkane, Phytic Acid, Malic Acid, Mandelic Acid, Lactic Acid, Lipase, Protease, Squalane, Polyglyceryl-10 Stearate, C15-23 Alkane, Hydroxyacetophenone, Phenoxyethanol, Tricaprylin, Glyceryl Oleate, Ethylhexyl Polyhydroxystearate, Tetradecane, C13-14 Alkane, Decyl Glucoside, Maltodextrin, Xanthan Gum, Lecithin, Sclerotium Gum, Polyglyceryl-4 Oleate, Carrageenan, Silica.[^inci] The brand adds that lists shown online "may vary slightly from our packaging and distributor websites".[^inci]

**The brand says it is not for everyone, and says it twice.** "This product contains a high potency acid blend and is not designed for sensitive skin. Patch test is strongly advised." appears on the catalogue record, and the product page repeats that "Due to the high activity level of our AHA blend, this product is not recommended for all skin types."[^brand][^inci] That is unusually direct for a cosmetic listing and it should be read as written.

## The Rubric

**Surface exfoliation, texture and radiance (cosmetic)**
Graded modest on mixed evidence, inherited from [[glycolic-acid]] rather than earned here. Glycolic second on the list after water, with its neutraliser third, is a meaningful concentration; what the site grades glycolic as able to do for texture and tone is set out on that page and on [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]]. Nothing about adding four more acids at lower levels raises that grade, and no comparison in this product's own data tests the blend against glycolic alone.

**The brand's own clinical percentages**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence, because of what the numbers are. The claims are that after four weeks of twice-weekly use "+78% of subjects showed improvement in skin radiance/brightness" and "+100% of subjects showed improvement in the appearance of pores", and after eight weeks "+69% of subjects showed improvement in skin texture".[^brand] The brand's own disclaimer states the basis: "Based on a clinical study of 32 subjects after 8 weeks of use. Results were measured by image analysis."[^brand] Thirty-two people, no control arm mentioned, and every figure is a count of how many subjects changed at all rather than by how much. A hundred percent of thirty-two showing any detectable change on image analysis is a different claim from a measured effect size.

**Brightening the look of discoloration (cosmetic)**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. The brand's wording is careful, "brighten the look of discoloration", and the only supporting figure is the radiance responder count above.[^brand] What has measured evidence for pigment is on [[hyperpigmentation]].

## The Evidence

**Leave-on changes the calculation.** Directions say not to rinse, so the 15% acid blend stays on the skin,[^brand] which is the opposite of the contact-time argument that limits rinse-off acid cleansers. That is why the once-weekly start and the explicit sensitive-skin exclusion matter more here than on a wash-off product.

**The sunburn alert is the standard AHA one and it is longer than people expect.** "This product contains an alpha hydroxy acid (AHA) that may increase your skin's sensitivity to the sun and particularly the possibility of sunburn. Use a sunscreen, wear protective clothing, and limit sun exposure while using this product and for a week afterwards."[^inci] A week afterwards, which is longer than the application days themselves. Filter choice is on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

**Founder disclosure, because it affects how the brand's own content is treated here.** Prequel was founded by dermatologist Samantha Ellis, whose videos this site excludes when they mainly promote her own products, as recorded on [[prequel]] and in the site's creator roster. That has no bearing on whether the formula works; it is the reason no creator video is cited on this page.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. The brand states this is not designed for sensitive skin and advises a patch test, which is worth following. Acids on compromised or actively irritated skin make things worse rather than faster, which is the case [[skin-barrier-repair]] makes.

## Common Marketing Claims

**"our multi-acid complex including 15% glycolic, phytic, malic, mandelic and lactic acids".** The percentage attaches to the complex, and the ingredient order shows glycolic carrying it.[^brand][^inci] A reader could easily take the sentence to mean 15% glycolic specifically; the brand does not say that, and neither does this page.

**"CLINICALLY PROVEN RESULTS*".** Assessed above. The asterisk leads to a 32-subject image-analysis study, and responder counts are not effect sizes.[^brand]

**The badge row, quoted as the page gives it: "✓ Fragrance-Free ✓ Non-Comedogenic ✓ Allergy-Tested ✓ Alcohol-Free ✓ Dermatologist-Developed ✓ Dermatologist-Tested".** Non-comedogenic, allergy-tested, dermatologist-developed and dermatologist-tested are none of them regulated terms with a defined threshold. Dermatologist-developed is literally true here given the founder, and it is a statement about who formulated it rather than about what it does.[^inci]

**Two of the six badges are checkable, and both hold.** Fragrance-free and alcohol-free can be tested against the ingredient list above: no parfum, fragrance or alcohol denat. appears in it, confirmed by string search.[^inci]
