Elidel is the original branded formulation of pimecrolimus 1% cream, a prescription-only topical calcineurin inhibitor approved by the FDA as second-line, short-term and non-continuous therapy for mild to moderate atopic dermatitis in non-immunocompromised patients 2 years of age and older who have not responded adequately to other topical prescription treatments, or for whom those treatments are not advisable.[^dailymed] It carries the FDA's boxed warning over a rare, unconfirmed malignancy signal shared by the whole topical-calcineurin-inhibitor class.[^dailymed]

> A steroid-sparing option, not a stronger one: in the trials that supported approval it beat an inert vehicle cream clearly, but a large three-trial head-to-head found its sister drug, tacrolimus ointment, works faster and more effectively at similar safety.[^eichenfield2002][^paller2005] The label's boxed warning over rare malignancy reports remains unresolved by follow-up data, restricting continuous long-term use and blocking use under age 2 regardless.[^dailymed]

## Summary

Elidel is pimecrolimus 1% cream, applied twice daily to areas of active atopic dermatitis, formulated in a base of benzyl alcohol, cetyl alcohol, citric acid, mono- and di-glycerides, oleyl alcohol, propylene glycol, sodium cetostearyl sulphate, sodium hydroxide, stearyl alcohol, triglycerides, and water.[^dailymed] It is a health product, not a cosmetic one: its only FDA-cleared use is as a steroid-sparing second-line treatment for an inflammatory skin disease, prescribed when a topical corticosteroid has failed or is inadvisable.[^dailymed] The graded mechanism, the vehicle-controlled and steroid-comparator evidence base, and the FDA malignancy warning that shadows the whole calcineurin-inhibitor class are set out in full on [[pimecrolimus]]; this page covers what is specific to the branded Elidel product itself.

## The Evidence

Elidel is the actual product tested in the registration trials, not a copy of them. Two identically designed, 6-week, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, multicenter Phase 3 trials enrolled 403 pediatric patients ages 2 to 17 with atopic dermatitis (59% moderate disease at entry, mean 26% body surface area affected), applying Elidel or vehicle cream twice daily.[^dailymed] At the study endpoint, based on physician global evaluation, 35% of Elidel patients (93 of 267) were clear or almost clear of atopic dermatitis signs versus 18% of vehicle patients (25 of 136); 10% (28 of 267) were fully clear versus 4% (5 of 136) on vehicle.[^dailymed] More Elidel patients had mild or no pruritus at 6 weeks (57% vs 34% on vehicle), and a significant treatment effect was seen by day 15, with erythema and infiltration/papulation already reduced versus vehicle by day 8.[^dailymed]

Three further trials in 1,619 pediatric and adult patients provided additional safety data, including one active-comparator trial in adults.[^dailymed] That broader safety base is where the head-to-head loss to tacrolimus ointment comes from: a separate pooled analysis of three 6-week trials in 1,065 patients found tacrolimus ointment significantly more effective than pimecrolimus cream at study end in adults (54.1% vs 34.9% treatment success, P<.0001) and in children with moderate to severe disease (67.2% vs 56.4%, P=.04), with a faster onset of action and similar overall safety, detailed on [[pimecrolimus]] and [[tacrolimus-topical]].[^paller2005]

> Bottom line: against an inert vehicle, Elidel roughly doubled the rate of clear-or-almost-clear skin in children with atopic dermatitis; against the more potent sister drug tacrolimus ointment, it lost on speed and effect size.

## What's In It

- **Active: pimecrolimus 1%** (10 mg per gram of cream). See [[pimecrolimus]] for the graded mechanism and full cross-trial evidence base, including the boxed-warning malignancy question.[^dailymed]
- **Base:** benzyl alcohol, cetyl alcohol, citric acid, mono- and di-glycerides, oleyl alcohol, propylene glycol, sodium cetostearyl sulphate, sodium hydroxide, stearyl alcohol, triglycerides, and water, as declared on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

The boxed warning states: "Although a causal relationship has not been established, rare cases of malignancy (e.g., skin and lymphoma) have been reported in patients treated with topical calcineurin inhibitors, including ELIDEL Cream," and on that basis instructs that continuous long-term use in any age group be avoided, application be limited to areas of atopic-dermatitis involvement, and that Elidel not be used in children under 2.[^dailymed] The most common local adverse event in the short-term pediatric trials was application site burning (10% Elidel vs 13% vehicle); the label lists no contraindication beyond hypersensitivity to pimecrolimus or any cream component.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"A gentler, steroid-free alternative."** Elidel does avoid the corticosteroid skin-thinning risk and is genuinely effective against vehicle in the trials that supported approval, but the label's own head-to-head comparator data (via the related pimecrolimus-vs-tacrolimus trials) show it is the less potent, slower topical calcineurin inhibitor, and it still carries the boxed warning restricting continuous long-term use.[^dailymed][^paller2005]
- **"Safe for long-term daily use."** The label explicitly contradicts this: it instructs that continuous long-term use be avoided in any age group, states the drug should not be used under occlusion, and notes its safety has not been established beyond one year of non-continuous use.[^dailymed]

## Where to Buy

Elidel is available only by prescription; no over-the-counter purchase path exists, and generic pimecrolimus 1% cream versions are also FDA-approved.
