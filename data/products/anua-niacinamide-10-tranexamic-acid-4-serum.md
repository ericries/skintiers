Anua Niacinamide 10 TXA 4 Serum for Brightening and Dark Spots is a Korean serum that Anua states contains "10% Niacinamide, 4% Tranexamic Acid, and 2% Arbutin," in a base built around centella asiatica, ceramide NP, and several hyaluronic-acid derivatives.[^1]

> Niacinamide has modest evidence for evening hyperpigmentation, comparable in one independent trial to a standard skin-lightening drug (see [[niacinamide]]). Tranexamic acid has real but modest melasma evidence that is strongest for the oral route; its topical evidence is thinner, and the closest topical study combined it with niacinamide rather than testing it alone (see [[tranexamic-acid]]). This serum lists both actives, at a labeled 10% niacinamide and 4% tranexamic acid, but no trial has tested this specific three-active Anua formula.[^1]

## Summary

This Anua serum combines three brightening actives at labeled concentrations: 10% niacinamide, 4% tranexamic acid, and 2% arbutin, positioned by Anua as "Daily Dark Spot Care."[^1] Niacinamide is a well-tolerated, widely studied ingredient with modest evidence for supporting the skin barrier and for evening facial hyperpigmentation; tranexamic acid is a synthetic antifibrinolytic drug used off-label for melasma, with its strongest evidence at an oral dose and weaker, more mixed evidence for the topical route (see [[niacinamide]] and [[tranexamic-acid]]).

No trial has tested this Anua formula or its specific three-active combination. The case for it rests on each headline active's own evidence, read across to the labeled concentrations here, which sit above the concentrations behind that evidence ([the evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1]

Where niacinamide and tranexamic acid have evidence, it is a health effect on hyperpigmented or melasma-affected skin. Separately, Anua markets a cosmetic comfort and moisture claim from the added ceramide and hyaluronic-acid derivatives, and describes the texture as "non-greasy" and "lightweight" ([common marketing claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^1] The formula also carries Anua's labeled "2% Arbutin" claim; both arbutin and alpha-arbutin appear on the INCI, though the source does not state that the two sum to 2% (see [[arbutin]]), alongside a heavy hyaluronic-acid-derivative complex and a centella asiatica fraction ([what's in it](#whats-in-it)).

The serum is listed at $24.00 on Anua's own US site, inexpensive for a formula carrying three declared actives.[^1]

## The Evidence

Whether niacinamide and tranexamic acid work, and for what, is covered on [[niacinamide]] and [[tranexamic-acid]]. Topical niacinamide has modest evidence for evening hyperpigmentation, comparable in one small independent melasma trial to a standard skin-lightening drug. Tranexamic acid's best evidence for melasma is oral; its topical evidence is thinner and more mixed, and the topical study closest in composition to this product paired tranexamic acid with niacinamide rather than testing it alone, the same tranexamic-acid-plus-niacinamide pairing this Anua serum carries (that study also included kojic acid, so it is not the same formula). The product-specific question is how this Anua serum, at its labeled concentrations, sits against that evidence.[^1]

No trial has tested this Anua formula or any Anua serum. It is labeled 10% niacinamide, 4% tranexamic acid, and 2% arbutin.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** No trial has tested this specific three-active Anua formula; its expected benefit rests on niacinamide's and tranexamic acid's own evidence read across to the labeled concentrations here.[^1]
