Isntree Hyaluronic Acid Watery Sun Gel SPF 50+ is a Korean chemical sunscreen from [[isntree]], rated SPF 50+ PA++++ and built on modern organic UV filters (Tinosorb S, Tinosorb M, Uvinul A Plus, homosalate, and octisalate), with eight hyaluronic-acid ingredients in the formula.[^brand][^target]

> This sunscreen's UV filter set avoids avobenzone, the older UVA filter most US chemical sunscreens still rely on, using instead Tinosorb S, Tinosorb M, and Uvinul A Plus, the modern-filter tier [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] ranks ahead of avobenzone for UVA protection and photostability. Its SPF 50+ PA++++ rating, however, comes from Korean regulatory testing, not the FDA-standardized Drug Facts process US sunscreens use, and the brand's own store states the product [is not sold in the US because it "is not available in the U.S. due to OTC regulations,"](#the-evidence) pointing US shoppers to a reformulated version instead.[^brand] Hyaluronic acid is a well-evidenced humectant for skin hydration; [[hyaluronic-acid]] covers what the topical evidence does and does not show.

## Summary

Isntree Hyaluronic Acid Watery Sun Gel SPF 50+ is a lightweight, gel-textured chemical sunscreen from the Korean brand [[isntree]], positioned around hydration: the brand's own product description calls it a "water-based Sun Gel with 8 types of hyaluronic acid" that "supplies moisture with fresh finish."[^brand] What sets it apart from a typical US drugstore sunscreen is not the hyaluronic acid, a well-evidenced but modest humectant covered generally on [[hyaluronic-acid]], but the UV filter set: it uses Tinosorb S, Tinosorb M, and Uvinul A Plus, modern organic filters not built on avobenzone, [detailed under the evidence](#the-evidence) and in the broader filter landscape on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

The label rating is SPF 50+ PA++++, a high mark on both axes under the Korean testing system, but that system is not the FDA's Drug Facts process, and Isntree's own US store confirms this exact formula is not sold in the US, describing it as "not available in the U.S. due to OTC regulations" and pointing to an alternative "Hyaluronic Acid Daily Sun Gel" instead.[^brand] A separate retailer listing at Target sells a US-market version under the same name; that listing corroborates the modern-filter set (Tinosorb S, Tinosorb M, Uvinul A Plus, homosalate, octisalate) but does not carry an independent FDA Drug Facts rating either.[^target] The product is mid-priced for a Korean sunscreen, listed at $23.99 for 1.69 fl oz (50 mL) on sale from $33.00 on Target's site.[^target]

## The Evidence

Whether regular sunscreen use works, and how SPF and UVA protection differ, is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. What is specific to this product is its label filter set and its PA++++ rating.

The brand's own product page lists the full ingredient panel, including the UV filters ethylhexyl salicylate (octisalate), homosalate, bis-ethylhexyloxyphenol methoxyphenyl triazine (Tinosorb S / bemotrizinol), methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol (Tinosorb M / bisoctrizole), and diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate (Uvinul A Plus).[^brand] Target's US listing for the same product independently names the same modern-filter set, "Tinosorb S, Tinosorb M, Uvinul A Plus, Homosalate, Octisalate," corroborating the brand's ingredient panel.[^target] The general filter-landscape evidence on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] ranks the Tinosorb and Uvinul filters ahead of avobenzone, the workhorse UVA filter most US sunscreens still use, for UVA protection and photostability. Neither the brand page nor the Target listing states the SPF 50+ PA++++ rating was independently FDA-tested; the brand's own store instead flags that this exact SKU "is not available in the U.S. due to OTC regulations," directing US shoppers to a differently formulated "Hyaluronic Acid Daily Sun Gel."[^brand]

> **Bottom line.** The filter set (Tinosorb S, Tinosorb M, Uvinul A Plus, homosalate, octisalate) is a modern, non-avobenzone combination that the general filter-landscape evidence on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] ranks ahead of the older US-standard filters for UVA protection and photostability. The SPF 50+ PA++++ rating on the label comes from Korean testing, not an FDA Drug Facts process, and the brand's own store says this exact formula is not sold in the US market.[^brand]
