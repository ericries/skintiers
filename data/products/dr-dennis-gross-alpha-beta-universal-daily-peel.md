## Summary

A two-step pre-soaked pad system, $22 for five applications up to $155 for sixty, sold as a daily at-home peel.[^brand] **Bottom line:** five acids are genuinely in there, and not one of them has a published percentage or a published pH, which is the single thing that would let anyone judge a peel. The second pad is stranger than the first: its stated job is to neutralise the acid, and it also carries retinol. 

> **No number anywhere.** The brand's own copy says Step 1 "gently but effectively exfoliates skin with 5 AHA/BHA acids", and the ingredient list bears that out, naming glycolic, salicylic, citric, lactic and malic acid.[^brand] Nothing on the page gives a concentration for any of them, or a pH for the pad. Compare [[the-ordinary-glycolic-acid-7-toning-solution]] at a stated 7%, or [[pixi-glow-tonic]], which also states its strength. A peel with five acids and no numbers is asking to be taken on trust. 

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: unknown, for exfoliation.** Glycolic acid is third in Step 1, behind only water and alcohol, so it is present in quantity. Without a percentage or a pH, how much exfoliation that buys is unknowable, and acid strength and pH together are what determine it. Direction comes from [[glycolic-acid]] and [[lactic-acid]], both graded mid, and [[salicylic-acid]], graded good.
- **Composition note: there is a strong base in the acid pad.** Step 1 declares potassium hydroxide fourth, above citric, lactic and malic acid and above salicylic acid. Potassium hydroxide is used to raise pH, so the formula is partly neutralised before it reaches skin. That is normal formulation practice and it is also the reason the missing pH matters more here than it would in a single-acid product.
- **Composition note: alcohol is second.** Alcohol denat sits ahead of every active in Step 1. This page does not treat denatured alcohol as inherently harmful, but anyone choosing a daily exfoliant for dry or reactive skin should know it is the second-largest component rather than a trace solvent.
- **Effect size: unknown, for Step 2.** The brand describes Step 2 as "infused with antioxidants to neutralize acidity while delivering anti-aging ingredients".[^brand] Its list opens water, sodium bicarbonate, ascorbic acid, retinol. Sodium bicarbonate second is the neutraliser. Ascorbic acid third and [[retinol]] fourth mean the neutralising pad is simultaneously an actives pad, with no percentage given for either. A reader applying Step 2 to stop the acid is also applying a retinoid, and the packaging does not frame it that way.
- **Record finding: it is fragranced.** Step 1 declares Fragrance (Parfum). For a daily leave-on acid product aimed at first-time peel users, that is worth knowing before buying, and it is not mentioned in the marketing copy read for this page.

## The Evidence

No trial of this product was found. The brand's claim is "clinical results at home", which is a comparison to a professional peel rather than a result, and no study is cited on the page read for this profile.[^brand]

What can be said is inherited from the acids, each of which has its own page and its own grade here, and none of which can be applied to this product with any precision because the strengths are undisclosed. That is the honest position: the formula contains well-evidenced exfoliating acids in unknown amounts at an unknown pH, plus a retinoid in an unknown amount in the pad that is sold as the neutraliser. 

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own product page and **not** endorsed here. 
- "Ideal for first-time peel users, our best-selling, daily AHA/BHA peel transforms skin in 2 simple steps for clinical results at home."[^brand] Clinical results at home is the claim doing the work, and nothing on the page supports it.
- Step 1 "gently but effectively exfoliates skin with 5 AHA/BHA acids to immediately boost radiance, target pores, diminish fine lines, and even out tone and texture."[^brand] Five separate benefits, no percentages, no pH, no timeframe for any but radiance.
- Step 2 "is infused with antioxidants to neutralize acidity while delivering anti-aging ingredients for smooth, even, balanced skin."[^brand] Accurate as a description of the list; it omits that one of those anti-aging ingredients is retinol.
