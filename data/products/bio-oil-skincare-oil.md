Bio-Oil Skincare Oil is one of the most heavily marketed products in skincare, sold to improve the appearance of scars, stretch marks, uneven skin tone, aging, and dehydrated skin. Underneath the marketing it is a fragranced, mineral-oil-based emollient with cosmetic doses of vitamin A and vitamin E and a blend of botanical oils.

> Judge this as a moisturizer, not a scar or stretch-mark treatment. Its base is mineral oil plus light emollient esters (the "PurCellin Oil" the brand names), which soften skin and slow water loss, a real but ordinary effect. The vitamin A here is retinyl palmitate, a weak cosmetic ester, not the prescription retinoid [[tretinoin]] that has the (narrow) stretch-mark evidence. And the honest bottom line for the thing it is famous for is that no topical reliably fixes stretch marks, with the emollient-oil class it belongs to among the least supported; that evidence lives on [[stretch-marks]].

## Summary

The ingredient list, per [INCIDecoder's listing for this product](https://incidecoder.com/products/bio-oil-skincare-oil), begins with Paraffinum Liquidum (mineral oil) and three light emollient esters (triisononanoin, cetearyl ethylhexanoate, isopropyl myristate), followed by retinyl palmitate (a vitamin A ester), sunflower and soybean oils, tocopheryl acetate and tocopherol (vitamin E), the botanical oils of chamomile, lavender, and rosemary, calendula extract, bisabolol, a perfume, and CI 26100 (a red dye that gives the oil its orange color). Functionally that is an emollient oil: the actives that carry evidence elsewhere on this site are present here only as minor, cosmetic-level additions.

The marketed claims, that it improves the look of scars, stretch marks, uneven tone, aging, and dehydrated skin, are the brand's own positioning. Only two of those map onto anything this formula can actually do well, and neither is scar or stretch-mark removal.

## The Evidence

Two uses, graded above.

**Stretch marks and scars (the marketed use).** This is where the marketing and the evidence part ways. A 2016 review of topical stretch-mark management concluded flatly that "there is no topical formulation, which is shown to be most effective," and within that review the emollient-oil options fared worst: "tretinoin used therapeutically showed varying results whilst cocoa butter and olive oil did not demonstrate any effect."[^udin] A separate double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial of cocoa butter, a close cosmetic cousin of an oil like this one, found "no difference in the development of SG [striae gravidarum] (45.1% versus 48.8%; P = 0.730)."[^osman] Bio-Oil itself was not the product in those trials, so this is class-level rather than product-specific evidence, but it places an emollient oil at the weakest end of an already weak field. The one topical with any stretch-mark evidence is a true retinoid for early red marks only, covered on [[stretch-marks]] and [[tretinoin]]; the retinyl palmitate in Bio-Oil is not that.

**Moisturizing dry skin (what it actually is).** Here the product is on solid ground. A mineral-oil-based oil is an effective emollient and occlusive: it softens skin and reduces transepidermal water loss, which is a real, well-established cosmetic effect and most of what users are feeling when the skin looks temporarily smoother and more even. This is unremarkable, and any plain emollient does it, but it is honest.

> **Bottom line.** A pleasant, inexpensive emollient oil sold at a premium built on scar and stretch-mark claims its formulation does not support. If you want a moisturizing body oil and like the feel and scent, it works as one. If you specifically want to prevent or fade stretch marks or scars, the evidence, on [[stretch-marks]], says no topical reliably does that, and an emollient oil is among the least likely to.

## What's In It

An emollient-oil base (mineral oil plus triisononanoin, cetearyl ethylhexanoate, and isopropyl myristate) carrying retinyl palmitate, sunflower and soybean oils, vitamin E (tocopheryl acetate and tocopherol), the essential oils of chamomile, lavender, and rosemary, calendula extract, bisabolol, and a fragrance, colored with CI 26100, per [INCIDecoder's listing](https://incidecoder.com/products/bio-oil-skincare-oil). One practical caution: the perfume declares a long list of fragrance allergens, including limonene, linalool, geraniol, citronellol, eugenol, coumarin, benzyl salicylate, and amyl cinnamal. Fragranced oils are a common cause of contact allergy, which is worth weighing when the intended skin is scarred, broken, or reactive.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Helps improve the appearance of scars and stretch marks."** The brand's own positioning. No topical is proven to reliably prevent or erase stretch marks, and an emollient oil sits at the weakest end of that evidence; see [[stretch-marks]].[^udin]
- **"For uneven skin tone, aging and dehydrated skin."** The dehydrated-skin part is fair, that is what an emollient oil does; the tone and aging claims rest on cosmetic-dose retinyl palmitate and vitamin E, not on the evidence-backed forms of those actives.
