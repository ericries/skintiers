CLn Facial Cleanser is a physician-developed daily face wash built around dilute sodium hypochlorite, marketed for facial skin prone to eczema, rosacea, dryness, and irritation, sold for $35.00 for 3.4 fl oz on the brand's own site.[^cln]

> This is CLn's bleach-bath concept, [already graded honestly on [[cln-bodywash|CLn BodyWash]]](#the-evidence), scaled down for facial skin: sodium hypochlorite has real but contested evidence in eczema, [detailed on [[atopic-dermatitis]]](#the-evidence). A 2022 meta-analysis found bleach baths "probably improve" atopic-dermatitis severity, but an earlier 2017 meta-analysis found no significant benefit over plain water baths.[^bakaa][^chopra] This product is a two-minute rinse-off wash, not a soak, and no independent trial of this exact face wash was found, so treat it as a reasonable way to apply a mild antibacterial to problem facial skin, not a proven upgrade over gentle cleansing.[^cln]

## Summary

CLn (pronounced "clean") positions the Facial Cleanser as a gentler, glycerin-carrying counterpart to its original [[cln-bodywash|BodyWash]], built for "delicate facial skin" and marketed to "anyone with normal to dry or sensitive facial skin prone to dryness, eczema, rosacea, rashes, or irritation," including children 6 months and older.[^cln] The brand describes sodium hypochlorite as "the active cleansing ingredient used across the CLn product line," working "alongside glycerin to cleanse without stripping."[^cln]

The general evidence for dilute sodium hypochlorite in eczema lives on [[sodium-hypochlorite]] and [[atopic-dermatitis]]: genuinely mixed. A 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis with GRADE assessment found that in moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, "bleach baths probably improve clinician-reported severity by a relative 22%," with about one in ten improving severity by 50%.[^bakaa] An earlier 2017 meta-analysis that specifically compared bleach against water baths found "no significant differences" between them on the Eczema Area and Severity Index or body surface area, meaning much of the apparent benefit may come from bathing or washing itself rather than the bleach.[^chopra] This product is a facial wash used for a short contact time, further removed from the longer soak protocols those trials tested.

Unlike some other CLn products, and despite general guidance sometimes pairing sodium hypochlorite washes with salicylic acid, the Facial Cleanser's own declared ingredient list contains no salicylic acid; its only cleansing-relevant active is sodium hypochlorite.[^cln]

## The Evidence

No independent, controlled trial of CLn Facial Cleanser itself was found; its evidence is inferred from the sodium-hypochlorite class plus the brand's own claims. Two health uses are graded above.

**Eczema / atopic dermatitis-prone facial skin.** Sodium hypochlorite reduces skin bacterial load, and reducing *Staphylococcus aureus* colonization is a plausible mechanism in atopic dermatitis, [covered in full on [[sodium-hypochlorite]] and [[atopic-dermatitis]]](#the-evidence). The class evidence does not cleanly separate the bleach from the bathing or washing itself: the 2022 meta-analysis supports a modest severity benefit versus no bleach, while the 2017 meta-analysis found bleach baths no better than water baths.[^bakaa][^chopra] The brand markets this face wash as suitable for skin "prone to dryness, eczema, rosacea, rashes, or irritation" and for those "whose skin feels tight or stings after standard face washes," but discloses no independent methodology, control arm, or publication testing the face wash itself.[^cln]

**Folliculitis and Staph-colonized facial skin.** The antibacterial rationale is the strongest part of the case: lowering surface bacterial load is a sensible approach to facial folliculitis, razor-bump-prone skin, and rosacea flares with a bacterial component. The brand's own FAQ recommends the product "before shaving" to "minimize irritation prior to hair removal or shaving," a use consistent with this mechanism.[^cln] But this rests on mechanism and the brand's clinical positioning rather than a controlled trial of this wash.

> **Bottom line.** A physician-designed way to apply a mild antibacterial to eczema-, rosacea-, or folliculitis-prone facial skin, standing on the same genuine but contested bleach-bath evidence as CLn's body wash, [detailed on [[cln-bodywash]]](#the-evidence). Reasonable to try if bacterial overgrowth plausibly drives your flares; don't expect it to clearly outperform plain gentle cleansing.[^bakaa][^chopra]

## What's In It

Full ingredient list (INCI), as listed by [incidecoder](https://incidecoder.com/products/cln-facial-cleanser): Water (Aqua), Sodium Laureth Sulfate, Disodium Cocoamphodiacetate, Glycerin, Cocamide MEA, Guar Hydroxypropyltrimonium Chloride, Polyacrylate-1 Crosspolymer, Citric Acid, Sodium Hypochlorite, Disodium EDTA.

- [[sodium-hypochlorite|Sodium hypochlorite]], the antibacterial active, 9th of 10 ingredients (a lower-order position than in CLn's own BodyWash formula)
- Sodium laureth sulfate and disodium cocoamphodiacetate, the primary cleansing surfactants; despite the "gentle, non-drying" positioning, sodium laureth sulfate is a standard sulfate cleanser, not a sulfate-free base
- [[glycerin|Glycerin]], a humectant the brand credits with helping the wash "cleanse without stripping"
- Cocamide MEA, a foam booster/thickener; guar hydroxypropyltrimonium chloride, a conditioning polymer; polyacrylate-1 crosspolymer, a thickener; citric acid, a pH adjuster; disodium EDTA, a chelating preservative aid

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Physician-developed daily face wash... for skin prone to eczema, rosacea, dryness, and irritation."**[^cln] The brand's positioning claim. The underlying sodium-hypochlorite mechanism is real, [graded on [[sodium-hypochlorite]] and [[atopic-dermatitis]]](#the-evidence), but no independent trial of this specific face wash's effect on eczema or rosacea was found.
- **"Cleanses effectively without drying the skin or disrupting the skin's natural moisture balance."**[^cln] A tolerability claim; the formula's primary surfactant is still a sulfate (sodium laureth sulfate), so treat "non-drying" as the brand's own characterization rather than an independently tested outcome.
- **"Safe for ages 6 months and older."**[^cln] A safety/positioning statement, not evidence of efficacy for any condition.
