La Roche-Posay Effaclar Duo+ is a leave-on acne treatment cream for oily, acne-prone skin, built on salicylic acid 0.5% alongside niacinamide and a proprietary anti-marks complex the brand calls Procerad; it is sold internationally, and on current US retail listings, as "Effaclar Duo+M."[^1][^2]

> A leave-on acne cream whose FDA-recognized active is salicylic acid at 0.5%, the low end of the OTC acne-monograph range; salicylic acid is a real but second-tier acne active that guidelines rank below benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids ([[salicylic-acid]]). Neither the international formula nor the current US retail listing for this treatment cream contains benzoyl peroxide; a separately sold Effaclar cleanser, not this cream, carries that ingredient instead.[^1][^2][^3] It is mid-priced for a small tube, at $18.79 for a 1.35 fl oz (about 40 ml) travel size.[^2]

## Summary

Effaclar Duo+ is a leave-on acne cream from La Roche-Posay's Effaclar line. Its FDA-recognized active is [[salicylic-acid|salicylic acid]] at 0.5%, the low end of the 0.5% to 2% range the FDA's OTC acne monograph permits.[^2] Salicylic acid is a legitimate acne treatment, [but a second-tier one that the American Academy of Dermatology ranks below benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids](#the-evidence). The current formula, sold internationally and on the current US retail listing as "Effaclar Duo+M," does not contain [[benzoyl-peroxide|benzoyl peroxide]]; a separate product, La Roche-Posay's Effaclar Dual Action Acne Face Cleanser, carries 4% benzoyl peroxide instead and is a different, rinse-off item.[^1][^2][^3]

Alongside salicylic acid, the formula carries niacinamide fairly high on the ingredient list (fifth, after water, glycerin, dimethicone, and an emollient) at an undisclosed concentration, plus the brand's proprietary Procerad complex, marketed for fading post-acne marks.[^1] Niacinamide has modest, mixed-quality evidence for fading hyperpigmentation, [roughly comparable to hydroquinone 4% in one small independent trial](#the-evidence); see [[niacinamide]]. La Roche-Posay reports an uncontrolled four-week test in which blackheads fell 66% and blemishes fell 44% (45 subjects), figures repeated on the US retail listing;[^1][^2] a further reported 45% reduction in "marks" (42 subjects) and a claim of visible results within 8 hours (54 subjects, one application) appear only on the manufacturer's own page and are [held apart below](#common-marketing-claims). It is mid-priced for a small tube: $18.79 for the 1.35 fl oz (about 40 ml) travel size as listed on Target (accessed 2026-08-07).[^2]

## The Evidence

Two general bodies of evidence sit behind this product, and both live on other pages. Salicylic acid is an oil-soluble acid that penetrates the sebum-filled pore; it is a legitimate, guideline-recognized acne active, but the American Academy of Dermatology's current guideline recommends it only conditionally and ranks [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and topical retinoids above it, and the one head-to-head trial Cochrane could pool put it level with tretinoin rather than ahead of it. Niacinamide has modest, mixed-quality evidence for fading hyperpigmentation, including one small independent melasma trial where it performed comparably to a standard prescription lightening agent. The full graded verdicts, with the trial statistics, are on [[salicylic-acid]] and [[niacinamide]].

What is specific to this product is a manufacturer four-week test: "-66% Blackheads" and "-44% Blemishes" in 45 subjects, "-45% Marks" in 42 subjects, and "8 Hours visible results" in 54 subjects after one application.[^1] The blackhead and blemish figures are repeated on the US Target listing;[^2] the marks and 8-hour figures are not.

> **Bottom line.** The blackhead (66%) and blemish (44%) reduction figures appear on both the manufacturer's international product page and the US retail listing, but describe an uncontrolled four-week test rather than a randomized trial against a vehicle or a comparator product.[^1][^2] The "marks" and "8 hour" figures are reported only on the manufacturer's own page.[^1]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on the manufacturer's product page, corroborated against the US retail listing's Active/Inactive Ingredients disclosure.[^1][^2]

Functional ingredients:

- [[salicylic-acid|Salicylic acid]] (0.5%, the labeled active ingredient) and capryloyl salicylic acid (LHA), an oil-soluble salicylic-acid derivative
- [[niacinamide|Niacinamide]], concentration undisclosed
- Punica granatum (pomegranate) pericarp extract, a botanical extract
- Zinc PCA, an oil-control zinc salt
- [[silica|Silica]], an oil-absorbing mineral powder
- Vitreoscilla ferment, a bacterial ferment ingredient
- Piroctone olamine, an antimicrobial ingredient

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, glycerin, dimethicone, isocetyl stearate, isopropyl lauroyl sarcosinate, ammonium polyacryloyldimethyl taurate, oryza sativa (rice) starch, potassium cetyl phosphate, sorbitan oleate, glyceryl stearate SE, isohexadecane, sodium hydroxide, myristyl myristate, 2-oleamido-1,3-octadecanediol, mannose, poloxamer 338, propanediol, hydroxyethoxyphenyl butanone, caprylyl glycol, citric acid, trisodium ethylenediaminedisuccinate, maltodextrin, xanthan gum, pentylene glycol, polysorbate 80, acrylamide/sodium acryloyldimethyltaurate copolymer, and parfum/fragrance.[^1][^2]

The formula contains added fragrance (parfum) and no benzoyl peroxide. La Roche-Posay's separate Effaclar Dual Action Acne Face Cleanser, a rinse-off product, carries 4% benzoyl peroxide; this leave-on treatment cream does not.[^1][^2][^3]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"PROCERAD."** La Roche-Posay names Procerad as a patented ingredient among the product's key actives, alongside "Phylobioma Active," niacinamide, capryloyl salicylic acid, and salicylic acid.[^1] No independent trial of Procerad or Phylobioma Active was found; the graded evidence above rests on the disclosed salicylic acid and niacinamide.
- **"-45% Marks" and "8 Hours visible results."** These four-week and single-application figures appear only on the manufacturer's international product page, not on the US retail listing.[^1]
- **"Dermatologist Tested for Safety."** A safety/testing label term on the US retail listing.[^2]
