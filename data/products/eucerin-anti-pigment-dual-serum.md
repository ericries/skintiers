Eucerin Anti-Pigment Dual Serum is a 1 fl oz brightening serum sold by Eucerin under its "Radiant Tone" collection for dark spots, built around Thiamidol (isobutylamido thiazolyl resorcinol), the tyrosinase-inhibiting active Beiersdorf developed and uses across its Eucerin brightening line.[^eucerin] The product's own INCI declares Thiamidol without a stated percentage, so this serum cannot be matched with certainty to the 0.1% to 0.2% concentrations tested in Thiamidol's published trials, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence).

> Thiamidol has real, mostly Beiersdorf-funded RCT and split-face evidence behind it as an ingredient, covered in full on [[thiamidol]]. This specific serum discloses Thiamidol in its ingredient list but not its concentration, so the case for this exact bottle rests on brand identity with Eucerin's studied active, not a trial of this formula itself.

## Summary

The declared ingredient list, in INCI order, is Aqua, Alcohol Denat, Butylene Glycol, Glycerin, Octocrylene, Isopropyl Palmitate, Cetearyl Isononanoate, Distarch Phosphate, Methylpropanediol, Isobutylamido Thiazolyl Resorcinol (Thiamidol), Sodium Ascorbyl Phosphate, Sodium Hyaluronate, Glycyrrhiza Inflata Root Extract, Tocopherol, Glucosylrutin, Sodium Stearoyl Glutamate, Glyceryl Stearate, Sodium Chloride, Trisodium EDTA, Caprylyl Glycol, Phenoxyethanol, and Parfum. Eucerin's own product listing names the item "Dual Serum" under its "Radiant Tone" collection, tagged for "Dark Spots," in a 1 fl oz size, and shows a 4.3-out-of-5 average across 465 reviews on the brand's own site; no direct retail price is posted on that page, only links to third-party retailers.[^eucerin]

Thiamidol is the health-relevant active here: it is Eucerin's flagship tyrosinase inhibitor, discovered by Beiersdorf researchers screening 50,000 compounds against recombinant human tyrosinase, and it has RCT and split-face trial evidence for facial hyperpigmentation and melasma, [fully graded on [[thiamidol]] and [[hyperpigmentation]]](#the-evidence). This is a cosmetic appearance claim (fading visible dark spots), not a claim about skin health or disease. [[sodium-ascorbyl-phosphate|Sodium ascorbyl phosphate]] (a stable vitamin C ester), [[hyaluronic-acid|sodium hyaluronate]], [[licorice-root-extract|Glycyrrhiza inflata (licorice) root extract]], and [[tocopherol]] round out the active-adjacent ingredients, alongside [[octocrylene]], a chemical UV filter that appears unusually high in the list (5th of 22) for a product that carries no SPF claim on the brand's own listing.[^eucerin]

## The Evidence

Whether Thiamidol improves facial hyperpigmentation and melasma is covered in full on [[thiamidol]]: in the largest vehicle-controlled RCT it significantly reduced melasma severity versus vehicle, and in a split-face trial it outperformed hydroquinone on the same measure. Nearly all of that published evidence is Beiersdorf-funded or co-authored by a Beiersdorf employee, with one small independent case series as the exception, and the honest caveats (no significant edge on patient-rated outcomes, and no proven benefit on freckles or lentigines in the largest trial) are set out on [[thiamidol]].

This product's own ingredient declaration lists Thiamidol (as isobutylamido thiazolyl resorcinol) 10th of 22 ingredients, after several vehicle and texture components, but Eucerin does not disclose a percentage on this listing. Published Thiamidol trials tested 0.1% to 0.2% formulations; without a disclosed concentration for this specific serum, it cannot be confirmed which, if either, of those tested doses this product matches.[^eucerin] No published trial of this exact serum, or of its specific combination of Thiamidol with sodium ascorbyl phosphate, licorice root extract, and sodium hyaluronate, was found.

> **Bottom line.** The health-adjacent case for this serum's central claim rests on category-level trial evidence for Thiamidol at established but undisclosed concentrations, not on a trial of this exact product, and nearly all of that ingredient-level evidence is Beiersdorf-funded.
