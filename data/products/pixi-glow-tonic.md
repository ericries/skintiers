A $29 leave-on glycolic toner from Pixi Beauty, 250 ml, SKU 82100.[^brand] **Bottom line:** the formula is what it says it is, a 5% glycolic toner, but 5% sits below the strength the controlled trials used, and the three percentages on the product page measure opinion rather than skin. Glycolic acid's own evidence is on [[glycolic-acid]].

> **All three headline numbers are consumer perception.** The brand publishes "91% said the toner was hydrating", "82% said skin tone appeared more even" and "80% noticed fewer blemishes with continued use", each carrying an asterisk that resolves to "Based on consumer perception study results."[^brand] Nothing there is an instrument reading or a graded photograph, there is no comparison arm, and no sample size is given. A perception study records what users believed after using a product they knew they were using.

## Summary

The honest case for this toner is the ingredient list, which matches the marketing: as INCIDecoder transcribes it, Glycolic Acid is third of 29, directly after water and aloe juice. Two things qualify it. Sodium Hydroxide sits sixth, which means part of that 5% is neutralised into glycolate salt, and the brand states neither the finished pH nor the free-acid content, the two numbers that decide how much acid is available to work. And 5% is below the 8% used in the one vehicle-controlled glycolic trial summarised on [[glycolic-acid]], whose own authors called the result modestly useful.

It is a daily leave-on acid marketed as "suitable for all skin types" that contains both Fragrance (Parfum) and the colourant Red 4, the combination most likely to bother reactive or rosacea-prone skin.[^brand] Worth saying plainly in the other direction too: no alcohol appears anywhere in the declared list, so the common assumption that this toner is drying through alcohol is not supported by the declared list.

At $29 for 250 ml it works out near 12 cents a millilitre, against the 9 cents recorded for [[the-ordinary-glycolic-acid-7-toning-solution]] on its own page as of 5 August 2026, which states a higher glycolic strength.[^brand] The 100 ml size, at $18, is twice that per millilitre.[^brand]

## The Rubric

**Surface exfoliation, texture and radiance (cosmetic)**
Graded minimal on mixed evidence, one step below [[glycolic-acid]]'s own grade of mid. The grade is not a claim that 5% glycolic does nothing; it is a claim that nothing read for this page tests this strength in this kind of partially neutralised leave-on, and that the trial evidence sitting closest to it ran at 8% in a cream and returned a result its authors described as modestly useful. The brand's own mechanism line, that it works "to gently accelerate surface cell turnover", is a plausible description of what glycolic acid does rather than a measurement of this product.[^brand]

**The brand's three published percentages**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. See the quarantined section below. The figures describe how users felt about a product they knew they were using, which is a different quantity from whether it worked.

## The Evidence

There is no trial of this product read for this page. What exists is the brand's consumer-perception study, which is not published, not controlled, and not described beyond three percentages and a footnote.

The transferable evidence belongs to the active, not the bottle, and it is graded on [[glycolic-acid]] rather than re-argued here: a vehicle-controlled trial at a higher strength, a small forearm pilot at a much higher one, and a body of cosmetic-appearance endpoints. A toner at this strength inherits the direction of that evidence rather than its magnitude.

Sun sensitivity is the one safety point that the acid itself carries rather than this formula, and it is graded on its own axis on [[glycolic-acid]]. Daily use of any leave-on AHA belongs with daily sunscreen; the filters are on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## Common Marketing Claims

Quarantined: these are Pixi's own promotional lines, not findings, and nothing above rests on them.

Pixi describes it as "the cult-favorite exfoliating toner formulated with 5% Glycolic Acid to gently accelerate surface cell turnover for smoother, brighter, more radiant-looking skin".[^brand] The 5% figure is consistent with where glycolic acid sits on the ingredient list. The phrase "smoother, brighter, more radiant-looking" is appearance language, and the hedge in "radiant-looking" is doing the regulatory work.

The copy also calls it "Designed for daily use and suitable for all skin types".[^brand] The declared list includes fragrance and a colourant, so "all skin types" is a marketing generalisation rather than a tolerability finding.

The same copy calls it "one of the most recognized glycolic acid toners for visibly radiant, refined skin".[^brand] The first half is a statement about the product's fame rather than its performance, and "visibly radiant, refined" is appearance language again.
