EltaMD UV Elements Tinted Mineral Broad-Spectrum SPF 44 was a 100% mineral, tinted facial sunscreen lotion from EltaMD, combining zinc oxide 10.0% and titanium dioxide 5.7% with an iron-oxide tint and hyaluronic acid.[^archive] It is discontinued: as of this writing the brand's old product URL for it 301-redirects to EltaMD's newer tinted mineral sunscreen, UV AOX Elements Broad-Spectrum SPF 50, and the product no longer has an active FDA Drug Facts label on DailyMed.[^live][^dailymed-search]

> A genuinely mineral-only Broad Spectrum SPF 44, no octinoxate or other chemical filter, built on zinc oxide and titanium dioxide, both well-established UVA/UVB filters (see [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]).[^archive] The iron-oxide tint plausibly adds some visible-light protection relevant to [[melasma]], but the brand never disclosed an iron oxide percentage for it. The product itself is no longer sold; EltaMD has replaced it in its lineup with the reformulated, higher-SPF [[eltamd-uv-aox-elements-tinted-mineral-spf-50|UV AOX Elements SPF 50]], so treat this page as a historical record rather than a current buying option.

## Summary

UV Elements was a 2 oz (57 g) tinted lotion, SKU 02512, that EltaMD listed at $40.00 on its own site as of a December 2023 snapshot of that product page.[^archive] The brand described it as "a 100% mineral sunscreen in a tinted, moisturizing base," positioned as "gentle for even the most sensitive skin types, including post-procedure skin," with "chemical-free actives, zinc oxide and titanium dioxide."[^archive] That mineral-only formulation is what distinguished it from EltaMD's chemical-mineral hybrid tinted products, such as [[eltamd-uv-daily-tinted-broad-spectrum-spf-40|UV Daily Tinted SPF 40]], which pairs zinc oxide with the chemical filter octinoxate.[^dailymed-daily]

By the time of research for this page, the product's URL on eltamd.com no longer resolves to its own page: it 301-redirects to EltaMD's newer tinted mineral sunscreen, UV AOX Elements Broad-Spectrum SPF 50, which the brand markets as an antioxidant-enriched, higher-SPF successor.[^live] It also no longer appears in DailyMed's current EltaMD label listing, which covers the brand's presently marketed OTC sunscreen SKUs.[^dailymed-search] This page documents the discontinued product for reference; buyers looking for EltaMD's current tinted mineral sunscreen should see [[eltamd-uv-aox-elements-tinted-mineral-spf-50]].

## The Evidence

The core, well-established health benefit was broad-spectrum photoprotection: as a 100% mineral sunscreen with zinc oxide 10.0% and titanium dioxide 5.7% as its declared active ingredients, tested to Broad Spectrum SPF 44, it protected against sunburn and provided broad-spectrum UVA/UVB coverage from the mineral filters alone, with no chemical UV filter in the formula.[^archive] No published trial tests this exact discontinued product; the case for the filters rests on the general mineral-filter evidence on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] and [[zinc-oxide]].

The tinted, iron-oxide component is a separate and more modest claim. Iron oxides, listed last in the inactive ingredient list the brand published for this product, absorb visible light in a way UV-only filters do not, and that property is relevant to pigmentation-prone skin because visible light can worsen [[melasma]] and post-inflammatory [[hyperpigmentation]] even when a non-tinted broad-spectrum sunscreen is used correctly ([[sunscreen-uv-filters]]). EltaMD's own product page for this item did not disclose an iron oxide percentage, so that benefit is graded modest and preliminary here, resting on the ingredient class rather than a tested dose in this specific product.[^archive]

## What's In It

- **Active ingredients (per the brand's own listing):** "10.0% Zinc oxide, 5.7% Titanium dioxide."[^archive]
- **Brand's stated mechanism:** "Zinc Oxide: Natural mineral compound that works as a sunscreen agent by reflecting and scattering UVA and UVB rays. Titanium Dioxide: Natural mineral that works as a sunscreen agent by reflecting and scattering UV radiation."[^archive]
- **Inactive ingredients (verbatim from the brand's listing):** Water/Aqua, Cyclopentasiloxane, Dimethicone, Octyldodecyl Neopentanoate, Lauryl PEG-9 Polydimethylsiloxyethyl Dimethicone, Dimethicone/PEG-10/15 Crosspolymer, Sodium Hyaluronate, Ascorbyl Palmitate, Tocopherol, Sodium Chloride, Triethoxycaprylysilane, Alumina, Hydrogen Dimethicone, Potassium Sorbate, Phenoxyethanol, Citric Acid, Iron Oxides.[^archive] The [[iron-oxides|iron oxides]] supply the tint; the sodium hyaluronate is EltaMD's marketed [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]] ingredient, and ascorbyl palmitate and [[tocopherol]] are the antioxidant pairing.
- **How to use (per the brand):** "Apply liberally 15 minutes before sun exposure. Reapply: after 40 minutes of swimming or sweating, immediately after towel drying, at least every 2 hours."[^archive]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"A 100% mineral sunscreen in a tinted, moisturizing base... gentle for even the most sensitive skin types, including post-procedure skin."**[^archive] The mineral-only, chemical-free filter combination is confirmed by the declared actives; "gentle" and the post-procedure positioning are the brand's own framing, not tied to a specific irritation trial in the sources reviewed here.
- **"Hyaluronic acid boosts moisture-retention for a smoother, more youthful appearance."**[^archive] Hyaluronic acid's general humectant evidence is graded independently on [[hyaluronic-acid]]; this product did not disclose a hyaluronic acid concentration, so the claim is not tested at a product-specific dose.
- **"Water-resistant (40 minutes)."**[^archive] A standard FDA-monograph water-resistance claim tier tied to a defined test protocol, consistent with the "reapply after 40 minutes of swimming or sweating" directions the brand published for the product.
