## Summary

A home red and infrared LED device cleared by the FDA in March 2008 to reduce periorbital wrinkles.[^k072459] **Bottom line:** it is not in Omnilux's current catalogue, its clearance contains no clinical data at all, and the most interesting thing in the file is the manufacturer admitting it could not find a classification that fit its own product. Modality evidence lives on [[home-led-light-therapy]]; nothing here is evidence about this unit.

> **"Although this device is not a laser and is intended for OTC use..."** The firm's own 510(k) summary gives the classification name as "Laser Instrument, Surgical Powered - General and Plastic Surgery - Class II, 79-GEX", then adds that "Although this device is not a laser and is intended for OTC use, the manufacturer thinks this is the closest applicable classification name".[^k072459] The FDA's clearance letter in the same file assigns something else entirely: regulation 21 CFR 878.4810, regulatory name "Light based over the counter wrinkle reduction", product code OHS.[^k072459] A reader who only saw the summary page would come away thinking a laser had been cleared.

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: unknown.** The Indications for Use read in full: the device "is intended to emit energy in the red and IR region of the spectrum, specifically indicated to reduce periorbital wrinkles", with the target population "the same as that for the predicate devices, however the Omnilux New-U is designed for home use".[^k072459] Periorbital means around the eye. That is the whole of what was cleared.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal, because the file contains no study.** The entire Performance Data section is one paragraph: the firm "believes that no significant differences exist between the previously approved Omnilux revive (K030426), Omnilux plus (K043317), Omnilux revive/Omnilux plus combination (K050216) and the Omnilux New-U. Therefore, the Omnilux New-U raises no new issues of safety or effectiveness".[^k072459] No trial, no participants, no endpoint, no number.
- **Composition note: wavelengths yes, dose no.** The device description gives a red output of 633 plus or minus 6 nm and an infrared output of 830 plus or minus 5 nm, a red/off/IR selector switch, an LED array with air cooling and an external power supply.[^k072459] It gives no irradiance, no energy density and no session length, saying only that "Treatment time is controlled by the operator".[^k072459] A device whose dose is whatever the user decides cannot be compared with one that publishes mW/cm2.
- **Record finding: the firm and the FDA index disagree about a predicate's name.** The summary lists "Omnilux Revive (K030426)" among its predicates. openFDA's 510(k) record for K030426 gives the device name as "OMNILUX RED", cleared to Photo Therapeutics Limited on 2003-07-17.[^openfda] The other two predicates match: K043317 is OMNILUX PLUS and K050216 is OMNILUX REVIVE AND OMNILUX PLUS.[^openfda] This page does not resolve the first discrepancy; it reports it.

## The Evidence

There is none specific to this device, and the clearance file is explicit about that rather than evasive. Substantial equivalence asks whether a device resembles one already on the market, which is a different question from whether either one works. This file is a clean illustration: three predicates, a sentence asserting no significant difference, and a conclusion that no new safety or effectiveness issues arise.

What is known about home LED for wrinkles as a category is on [[home-led-light-therapy]]. Reading that across to this unit would require knowing its irradiance and session length, which the file does not give.

## Common Marketing Claims

There are none to quarantine. Omnilux publishes no product page for the New U, because it does not sell it; the feed pulled for this profile contains no such product.[^feed] The only promotional language this page could find for the device is in its own regulatory file, and that is quoted and examined above rather than repeated here. If you encounter the New U being advertised by a third-party seller, the cleared indication is periorbital wrinkles and nothing else.[^k072459]

## Where to Buy

Not from Omnilux. A pull of the brand's own product feed on 2026-11-01 returned 45 products and no New U; the current LED line is Contour Face, Men, Clear, Contour Neck and Décolleté and Contour Glove, priced between $345 and $395.[^feed] The clearance holder in 2008, Photo Therapeutics, Inc. of Carlsbad, California, is not the entity selling Omnilux devices today: the 2021 clearance for Omnilux CLEAR (K210948) is held by Globalmed Technologies.[^openfda] No price is recorded here because the product is not offered for sale by the brand.
