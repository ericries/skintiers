Tower 28's SOS Rescue + Relief Body Wash is a gentle, sulfate-free body wash from [[tower-28]] built around sodium hypochlorite (the dilute-bleach relative of [[hypochlorous-acid]]), marketed for body acne and sensitive, eczema-prone skin, sold at about $22 for 150 mL.[^brand]

> A mild body wash whose treatment claim rests on sodium hypochlorite, the same dilute-bleach chemistry as hypochlorous acid.[^brand] Two honest caveats: the topical evidence for that active on reactive skin is preliminary (covered on [[hypochlorous-acid]]), and the closest established uses of dilute sodium hypochlorite, bleach baths and wound rinses, are soaks or leave-on rinses, not a wash you rinse off in seconds. Judge it as a gentle cleanser with a plausible-but-unproven active.

## Summary

This is the body member of Tower 28's SOS sensitive-skin line. The active is sodium hypochlorite, which the ingredient list spells out as "hypochlorous acid, sodium salt," in a mild surfactant base of cocamidopropyl hydroxysultaine and sodium cocoyl glutamate with glycerin, so as a cleanser it is genuinely gentle and sulfate-free.[^brand]

The treatment case is the sodium hypochlorite / hypochlorous acid case, set out on [[hypochlorous-acid]]: it is antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory, but the controlled topical evidence for visibly clearing body acne or calming reactive skin is limited.[^brand] Dilute sodium hypochlorite is also the chemistry behind bleach baths and wound rinses, but those are soaks or leave-on rinses used to lower skin bacteria; this product is a wash rinsed off in seconds, so that use does not carry over, and the brand's own "smoother skin in as soon as two weeks" is a marketing claim, not a trial result.[^brand]

## The Evidence

No independent, product-specific trial of this body wash was located. The product-specific facts are its composition (a mild sulfate-free surfactant base plus sodium hypochlorite) and its price. The active's general evidence lives on [[hypochlorous-acid]]; that is a study of the ingredient, not of this wash, and the brief contact time of a rinse-off limits how much any active can do.[^brand]

Its most defensible value is the same as any gentle body wash: cleansing sensitive or body-acne-prone skin without stripping it, which the sulfate-free base supports.[^brand] The sodium hypochlorite is a reasonable, well-tolerated addition, but not a proven body-acne treatment on this evidence.

> **Bottom line.** A gentle, sulfate-free sodium hypochlorite body wash for sensitive or body-acne-prone skin. Fine as a mild cleanser; treat the eczema, rosacea, and "smoother in two weeks" claims as marketing, and see [[hypochlorous-acid]] for what the active can and cannot do.[^brand]

## What's In It

Full ingredient list from the brand: water, cocamidopropyl hydroxysultaine, glycerin, sodium cocoyl glutamate, citric acid, sodium chloride, butylene glycol, cactus flower extract, apple fruit extract, sodium hypochlorite (hypochlorous acid, sodium salt), and tetrasodium glutamate diacetate.[^brand] The functional core is two mild, sulfate-free surfactants (cocamidopropyl hydroxysultaine and sodium cocoyl glutamate) plus the sodium hypochlorite active; there is no sulfate detergent and no added fragrance on the list.[^brand]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Calm skin prone to eczema, rosacea, psoriasis" / "smoother skin in as soon as two weeks."** Brand claims for the sodium hypochlorite active; the topical evidence is preliminary and no trial of this wash was located.[^brand]
- **Sodium hypochlorite "used by medical professionals for centuries."** True of dilute sodium hypochlorite for wound care and bleach baths, but that use is a soak or a wound rinse, not a body wash rinsed off in the shower.[^brand]
