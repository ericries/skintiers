This is the LED head that clips onto the NuFACE Trinity+ handle, and it is the only part of the NuFACE line whose FDA clearance names a wrinkle. **Bottom line:** the clearance is real and its indication is genuinely therapeutic, but the file behind it contains no clinical testing, because the company said clinical testing was not needed. Modality evidence is on [[home-led-light-therapy]]; the microcurrent handle is at [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]].

> **The same brand, the same handle, two different regulatory regimes.** NuFACE's microcurrent devices are cleared under product code NFO, 21 CFR 882.5890, reviewed by the Neurology panel, and their indications say facial stimulation for over-the-counter cosmetic use and name no outcome.[^trinity] This attachment is cleared under product code OHS, 21 CFR 878.4810, reviewed by General and Plastic Surgery, and its indication says reduction of fine lines and wrinkles.[^k212947][^ohs] Of the 14 clearances openFDA lists for this applicant, 11 sit under the stimulation code and 2 under the wrinkle one, and both of those two are light devices.[^openfda] If you are buying a NuFACE for wrinkles, the wrinkle claim is in the $160 light head, not the $395 handle.

## Summary

Cleared 2022-04-02 as K212947, a Traditional 510(k) published as a Summary, applicant "Carol Cole Company Dba Nuface" of Vista, California, received 2021-09-15, decision "Substantially Equivalent".[^openfda] The indication, quoted in full: "The Trinity Plus Wrinkle Reducer is an over-the-counter hand-held device intended for the reduction of full-face fine lines and wrinkles, and increase in local circulation within the perioral region."[^k212947]

The device is "a hand-held phototherapy device that emits Red and Infrared (IR) light energy in the 605, 630, 645, 660 and 855 nanometer wavelengths via a light attachment head", whose head "comprises (34) Light Emitting Diodes (LED's) which are the source of the light energy".[^k212947] Output is "Fixed, not user adjustable", and "The device automatically Shuts OFF after 24 minutes of use to indicate the treatment is complete."[^k212947]

NuFACE sells it as the Trinity+ LED Red Light Wrinkle Reducer Attachment at **$160.00**, marked "FINAL SALE - No returns or exchanges" and "ONLY compatible with the TRINITY+ Device".[^feed]

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: minimal, and that is a statement about this file, not about red light.** LED is graded Level B for facial rejuvenation in the 2026 GRADE scoping review of home devices, behind home fractional nonablative lasers at Level A; that grade is set out on [[home-led-light-therapy]] and belongs to the modality.[^home] This filing adds nothing to it. Searched in full, the document contains no irradiance or power-density figure, no energy dose, no subject count, no endpoint and no statistical result.[^k212947]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary, and the file declines to be more.** Section 7.6 reads: "Clinical testing was not considered to be needed in this premarket notification in order to support substantial equivalence."[^k212947] Section 7.5 says no animal testing was conducted. What was done is listed in 7.1: a biocompatibility evaluation, "Safety, Performance and Bench Testing including EMC", and "Software Verification and Validation Testing".[^k212947] None of those measures a wrinkle.
- **The emitter is wider than either predecessor's, by exactly one wavelength.** The device description gives the subject's five wavelengths as 605, 630, 645, 660 and 855 nanometers; the comparison table puts the two predicates at "605, 630, 660 and 855 nm" (K120560) and "605, 630, 660 and 880 nm" (K172662).[^k212947] The 645 nm emitter is the one thing neither predicate has. The equivalence argument is that this does not matter: "The devices use the same type of red and infrared LED technology, and the proposed light-emitting device does not raise new types of questions regarding safety and efficacy for the proposed indications for use."[^k212947]
- **Record finding: the predicate chain runs through a competitor.** The two predicates are the company's own K120560, recorded by openFDA as "TRINITY WRINKLE REMOVER" and cleared 2012-11-06 under OHS, and K172662, the reVive Perioral LED Light Therapy System from LED Technologies.[^k212947][^openfda] That second one is the reVive family this site already documents; what those filings do and do not publish is on [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]].
- **Record finding: what is cleared and what is sold are not quite the same object.** The filing describes a complete device that "comprises the TWR Plus main body, a TWR Plus light attachment head, a charging cradle, and a wall-mount power adaptor", measuring 5.4" H x 2.6" W x 1.7" D and weighing 7.8 oz with the head attached.[^k212947] What $160 buys is the head alone, which the brand's own listing says works only with a TRINITY+ device.[^feed] This page does not resolve that difference; it reports both documents as they read.
- **Record finding: two attachments, two clearances, and the mapping is not stated anywhere this page could check.** The brand's feed currently lists a Trinity Classic attachment and a Trinity+ attachment, both at $160.00, with the note "Purchased your device before August 2022? You need a Classic attachment".[^feed] openFDA shows two Carol Cole OHS clearances, K120560 in 2012 and K212947 in 2022.[^openfda] It would be natural to pair them off that way, and this page does not, because neither source says so.

## The Evidence

None specific to this device, and the filing says as much in a single sentence quoted above. The only human-facing numbers in the document are a 24-minute automatic shut-off and a count of 34 diodes.[^k212947]

That makes this an unusually clean example of what a 510(k) establishes. The indication is therapeutic in a way the microcurrent clearances are not: compare "reduction of full-face fine lines and wrinkles" here against "intended for facial and neck stimulation and is indicated for over-the-counter cosmetic use" on [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]].[^k212947][^trinity] But the route to that indication was substantial equivalence to two older LED devices, not a trial. The wrinkle word in the paperwork is a scope, not a result. [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] covers that distinction in general; [[home-device-wrinkle-claims-by-cleared-scope]] ranks the home devices this site covers by exactly this.

> **Bottom line.** If you already own a Trinity+, this is the attachment with the better-evidenced modality behind it and the only NuFACE indication that mentions wrinkles. What it is not is a device with evidence of its own.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own product listing and **not** endorsed here.

- "Our professional-grade LED Red Light Wrinkle Reducer Attachment harnesses 3 targeted light therapies—red, amber, & infrared—to help smooth fine lines, boost radiance, calm skin, and even skin tone."[^feed] The FDA file lists **five** wavelengths, not three, and describes them only as "Red and Infrared (IR)" without naming a colour for any of them.[^k212947] Radiance, calming and even tone appear nowhere in the cleared indication, which covers fine lines, wrinkles and perioral circulation.
- "Its perfectly contoured design hugs every curve of the face to smooth skin texture and boost radiance for a brighter, more revitalized appearance in minutes."[^feed] Texture, radiance and brightness are outside the cleared scope, and the file contains no result for any of them.
- "professional-grade".[^feed] Not a regulatory category. The clearance is an over-the-counter one, in those words.[^k212947]
