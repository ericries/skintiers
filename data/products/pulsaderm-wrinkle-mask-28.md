A wearable red-and-infrared LED mask from Pulsaderm, LLC of Fort Myers, Florida, cleared on 14 April 2017 under 510(k) number K163329, which covers both the 28-diode and 72-diode versions.[^fda] **Bottom line:** the clearance is for facial wrinkles in Fitzpatrick I to III only, the file contains no clinical study of the device, and its own published dose numbers contradict each other. The modality is graded on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

> **The word "clinical" appears once in this file, in the heading "Non-clinical-testing".** What sits under that heading is a lay-user and self-selection study, run with Institutional Review Board oversight, to establish that buyers could read the box and the manual. Its reported conclusion is that "an average lay user can read and comprehend correctly the user manual and package labeling".[^fda] No wrinkle outcome is measured anywhere in the submission.

## Summary

The cleared indication is narrower than the category name suggests: the masks "are intended for the use in the treatment of facial wrinkles and for people with Fitzpatrick Skin Types I, II and III."[^fda] That skin-type ceiling is part of the indication, not a footnote, and it is the same ceiling found across the home device clearances compared on [[home-device-wrinkle-claims-by-cleared-scope]].

Equivalence was argued against two earlier devices, La Lumiere's Pro X OTC 5 Light Therapy (K140471) and Silver Bay's Quasar Calypso C100 with the Baby Quasar Plus and Quasar MD Plus (K130225), on the grounds that the devices "share the same indications for use, treatment regimen as the predicate devices".[^fda] The submission's own argument for the difference in diode count is that "the difference in number of diodes and slightly increase in power output does not affect the safety or efficacy of the device".[^fda]

**The dose table does not add up.** For the Mask 28 the file gives 620 to 630 nm red at 15.94 mW/cm2 and 850 nm infrared at 5.24 mW/cm2, a combined figure it labels "Total power (mW/cm2) for entire mask" of 21.18, with a treatment protocol of "15 minutes everyday".[^fda] An irradiance of 21.18 mW/cm2 sustained for fifteen minutes delivers roughly 19 J/cm2. The same row of the same table reports the total energy per 15-minute session as 0.10 joules.[^fda] Those two figures cannot both describe this device, and the file offers no reconciliation. Why dose matters at all is on [[led-dose-j-per-cm2-and-wavelength]].

What the performance section does contain is standards conformance: IEC 60601-1, IEC 60601-1-2 and IEC 62471, biocompatibility to ISO 10993, and development under ISO 14971.[^fda] Those are electrical, photobiological and biocompatibility safety standards. None of them measures whether a wrinkle changes.

## The Rubric

**Treatment of facial wrinkles (FDA-cleared indication)**
Graded minimal on preliminary evidence, inherited from red and infrared LED as a modality rather than earned by this device. A clearance granted by equivalence to two earlier masks, with no clinical study in the file, is a statement about regulatory sameness and not about effect.

**Use on skin darker than Fitzpatrick III**
Graded unknown. The indication names types I, II and III, and nothing in the submission addresses darker skin.

**The file's own dose figures**
Graded unknown, because they disagree with each other. See above.

## The Evidence

There is no clinical evidence specific to this device. The transferable evidence belongs to the wavelengths rather than the mask, and it is graded on [[home-led-light-therapy]] and ranked by dose on [[led-dose-j-per-cm2-and-wavelength]] rather than re-argued here.
