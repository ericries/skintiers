AmLactin's flagship 12% lactic acid body lotion, long sold as "AmLactin Daily Moisturizing Lotion," is now listed on the brand's own site as "Daily Nourish Lotion with 12% Lactic Acid," the same sizes (7.9, 14.1, and 20 oz) and the same core active, ammonium lactate, the salt form of lactic acid, at a declared 12%.[^1] AmLactin's own page notes it "recently upgraded" its formulations, so buyers may still encounter older packaging under the earlier name with the ingredient list on the physical box taking precedence.[^1]

> A body lotion built on 12% ammonium lactate (a buffered lactic-acid salt), the same active and concentration behind the "classic over-the-counter" keratosis-pilaris treatment described on [[keratosis-pilaris]], and the same concentration shown in one trial (see [[lactic-acid]]) to produce a dermal skin change beyond the surface that 5% lactic acid did not. Its grades also rest on the general moisturizing case for its humectant and occlusive ingredients.

## Summary

AmLactin markets this lotion as "the everyday exfoliant": a fragrance-free, dye-free, paraben-free, and phthalate-free body lotion for normal to dry skin, "powered by 12% lactic acid," sold in 7.9 oz ($15.99), 14.1 oz ($19.99), and 20 oz ($21.99) sizes.[^1] The declared ingredient list names the acid as Ammonium Lactate, the ammonium salt of lactic acid used to buffer the acid's pH in leave-on products, rather than free lactic acid.[^1] AmLactin calls itself "the #1 dermatologist-recommended moisturizer brand with lactic acid," a claim sourced to the company's own 2023 data on file rather than a published trial.[^1]

Lactic acid is a well-studied AHA with modest, concentration-dependent evidence for photoaging appearance, skin firmness and smoothness, and hydration relative to glycolic acid; see [[lactic-acid]]. This product's printed 12% concentration matches the higher dose in a 3-month trial that found increased epidermal and dermal thickness and firmness at 12% lactic acid, a dermal effect that 5% lactic acid did not produce in the same trial. Separately, [[keratosis-pilaris]] names ammonium lactate lotion (citing AmLactin by name) as "the classic over-the-counter form" of the best-tested topical treatment for that condition, a split-side trial in which 10% lactic acid outperformed 5% salicylic acid for papule reduction; this product's 12% is above that tested 10%. No trial isolates this exact ammonium-lactate formula.

The rest of the formula, mineral oil, glycerin, cetearyl alcohol, dimethicone, and related emollients and thickeners, targets general moisturization rather than exfoliation; see [What's In It](#whats-in-it).

## The Evidence

No independently published trial of this exact lotion, its ammonium-lactate salt form, or its specific ingredient combination was located. Its grades rest on category evidence for lactic acid, laid out in full on [[lactic-acid]], plus this product's own printed 12% concentration.[^1]

This product's declared 12% concentration[^1] matches the higher dose in a lactic-acid dose-comparison trial that found a measurable dermal effect (increased epidermal and dermal thickness and firmness) at 12% that 5% lactic acid did not produce; see [[lactic-acid]] for the full finding. That trial used free lactic acid, not the ammonium-lactate salt this product uses; ammonium lactate is the standard way over-the-counter lotions deliver lactic acid at a buffered, less acidic pH, and [[keratosis-pilaris]] names ammonium lactate lotion, citing AmLactin specifically, as the classic OTC form of lactic-acid treatment for that condition, on the strength of a split-side, assessor-blinded trial in which 10% lactic acid outperformed 5% salicylic acid for papule reduction. This product's 12% concentration sits above that 10% tested dose.

Glycerin is a well-evidenced humectant and dimethicone a common occlusive emollient, with their general moisturizing evidence covered on [[glycerin]] and [[dimethicone]]; neither is disclosed at a stated percentage here.

> **Bottom line.** Its declared 12% ammonium lactate[^1] sits at the same concentration shown to produce a measurable dermal effect in one lactic-acid dose-comparison trial (see [[lactic-acid]]), and above the 10% lactic acid shown effective for keratosis pilaris in a separate trial (see [[keratosis-pilaris]]); both bodies of evidence used free lactic acid rather than this product's buffered ammonium-lactate salt form. No trial isolates this exact formula.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on AmLactin's current product page.[^1] The active with a functional role is linked to its own page; the rest is the base, emollient, and preservative system.

Active:

- Ammonium Lactate (the ammonium salt of [[lactic-acid]]), 12%

Base, emollient, and preservative system: water, mineral oil (an occlusive emollient), glycerin (a humectant, see [[glycerin]]), cetearyl alcohol (an emulsifying wax), dimethicone (a silicone occlusive, see [[dimethicone]]), glyceryl stearate SE and steareth-2 (emulsifiers), stearyl alcohol (an emollient/thickener), xanthan gum (a thickener), phenoxyethanol and ethylhexylglycerin (a preservative system).[^1]

The list is 12 ingredients total and declares no added fragrance.[^1] AmLactin's own page flags a recent formulation update and directs buyers to the ingredient list printed on their specific product's package rather than assuming it matches the current site listing.[^1]

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Clinically proven 12% lactic acid (AHA)."** No linked trial, sample size, or methodology accompanies this phrase on the product page; the closest published match is a general lactic-acid dose-comparison trial at the same 12% figure, covered on [[lactic-acid]], which did not test this product or its ammonium-lactate form.[^1]
