Differin Gel is an over-the-counter acne treatment whose only active ingredient is adapalene 0.1%, a topical retinoid, sold by Galderma in a fragrance-free aqueous gel.[^label]

> Differin Gel is the same 0.1% adapalene gel the FDA first approved as a prescription drug in 1996, and in 2016 cleared for over-the-counter sale in the first prescription-to-OTC switch of a topical retinoid. The nonprescription product is the same strength and once-daily dose as the former prescription one, and its acne efficacy is carried over from that prescription record rather than retested.[^daf][^letter][^review]

## The Evidence

Whether topical adapalene clears acne, and where it sits among the retinoids, is covered on [[adapalene]] and [[retinoids]]. The facts specific to this product are regulatory. Differin Gel reached shelves through a prescription-to-OTC switch, not a fresh efficacy program. The FDA review states that "the efficacy of adapalene gel 0.1% for the treatment of acne has been established in the prescription setting," that "it is expected that the product would have similar efficacy in the OTC setting, as the acne indication is the same in the prescription and OTC settings," and that "the proposed OTC dosing is the same as the approved prescription dosing."[^review]

> **Bottom line.** The over-the-counter gel is the same 0.1% adapalene at the same once-daily dose as the former prescription Differin, so its acne benefit is the prescription product's benefit, inherited rather than measured again.[^review]

The switch made adapalene the first topical retinoid sold without a prescription. The FDA benefit-risk summary describes the approval as "a first in class switch for a topical retinoid product and the first new active ingredient for the treatment of acne available OTC for more than 40 years."[^review] Because efficacy carried over from the prescription record, the application turned on whether people can use the gel safely on their own. It was supported by a maximal-use pharmacokinetic trial and by "three consumer studies": a label comprehension study, a self-selection study focusing on pregnant and lactating women, and an actual use study.[^review] The approval letter names Galderma's "Juno trial, your actual use study that evaluated consumer behavior," and records that the July 8, 2016 supplement "provides for the over-the-counter use of Differin Gel (adapalene), 0.1%, for the treatment of acne in adults and children ages 12 years and older."[^letter][^daf]

> **Bottom line.** The OTC approval rested on studies of whether consumers can select and apply the gel correctly without a prescriber, not on new efficacy trials. The acne benefit is the one already established for the prescription drug.[^review][^letter]

The only use the FDA approved for this product is acne. Its approved indication reads "for the treatment of acne," and when Galderma proposed a reworded OTC acne claim, the agency found "there were no new efficacy data submitted as part of this application," so that wording "was not supported and was withdrawn."[^review][^label] Using adapalene for photoaging is off-label, and the retinoid photoaging evidence is class-level, on [[retinoids]] and [[adapalene]]; as an over-the-counter retinoid it is milder than prescription tretinoin ([[tretinoin-0-025-cream]]).

> **Bottom line.** Differin's approved use is acne. Any anti-aging use is off-label and rests on class-level retinoid evidence, not on a trial of this product.[^review]

## What's In It

The active and full inactive list as declared on the manufacturer's OTC Drug Facts label.[^label] The active with graded efficacy links to its own pages; the rest are the gel base and preservative system.

Active:

- [[adapalene]] 0.1%, a topical [[retinoids|retinoid]], the only ingredient with graded efficacy. The label lists it as "Adapalene 0.1% (retinoid)" with the purpose "acne treatment."[^label]

Base and preservative system: carbomer 940 (gelling agent), edetate disodium (chelator), methylparaben (preservative), poloxamer 182 (surfactant), propylene glycol (humectant and solvent), purified water, and sodium hydroxide (pH adjuster), with hydrochloric acid to adjust pH.[^label]

The gel's only active is adapalene. It contains no benzoyl peroxide and no added fragrance, unlike adapalene-plus-benzoyl-peroxide combination gels.[^label]

## Common Marketing Claims

Manufacturer claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Restore the skin's texture and tone."** A texture and tone claim. The product is FDA-approved only for acne, so this use is off-label, and the retinoid photoaging evidence is class-level (see [[retinoids]]), not from a trial of this product.[^differin][^review]
- **"Clear breakouts where they start (deep in the pores)" and "prevent acne before it begins."** Positioning for the approved acne use. Adapalene's acne efficacy is established in the prescription setting, per the FDA review.[^differin][^review]
- **A reworded acne claim.** When Galderma proposed "clears up acne pimples and acne blemishes," the FDA found no new efficacy data supported it and it was withdrawn; the approved indication is simply "for the treatment of acne."[^review][^label]
