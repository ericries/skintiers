NIOD Copper Amino Isolate Serum 3 1:1 (CAIS3) is a water-based leave-on serum built around a manufacturer-declared 1% GHK-Cu (copper tripeptide-1), paired with several other signal and carrier peptides in a glycerin and hyaluronic-acid base, sold by NIOD (a Deciem brand) in 15 mL and 30 mL sizes.[^1]

> Whether topical copper peptide does anything beyond the laboratory dish is covered in full on [[copper-peptides]]. In the lab and in animal models, GHK-Cu drives collagen, elastin, and glycosaminoglycan synthesis and speeds wound healing, but the one randomized human trial of a topical GHK-Cu product found no objective benefit over its control for redness, wrinkles, or skin quality, only higher patient-reported satisfaction,[^2] and a 2024 review found "a surprising absence of clinical studies" of topical GHK-Cu despite wide cosmetic use.[^3] No published trial tests this specific NIOD formula.

## Summary

Copper Amino Isolate Serum 3 1:1 (CAIS3) is NIOD's flagship copper-peptide serum: a water-based leave-on formula led by [[copper-peptides|copper tripeptide-1 (GHK-Cu)]] at a manufacturer-declared 1%, alongside several other peptides (tripeptide-29, tripeptide-1 acetate, myristoyl nonapeptide-3, tripeptide-2, acetyl tetrapeptide-2) in a glycerin, hyaluronic-acid, and glycogen base.[^1] NIOD markets it as restoring "the look of healthy skin," minimizing fine lines and wrinkles, and promoting natural collagen, with claims of visibly smoother, more even tone in 4 weeks and firmer, "visibly repaired" wrinkles in 8 weeks ([common marketing claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^1]

The general case for topical copper peptide, real and well-documented collagen and wound-healing biology in cell and animal studies, against a much thinner human cosmetic evidence base, is set out in full on [[copper-peptides]]. No published trial isolates this NIOD product or its exact peptide blend; the grades above rest on that general category evidence.

Composition-wise, the formula is a short, largely peptide-and-humectant list with no vitamin C, niacinamide, retinoid, or exfoliating acid, and it sits in the same category as comparators like [[the-ordinary-multi-peptide-copper-peptides-serum|The Ordinary's Multi-Peptide + Copper Peptides 1% Serum]], which supplies GHK-Cu at a similar declared level in a different peptide blend.[^1] NIOD's own site prices the 15 mL size at $62.00 USD; the 30 mL size is also listed but was out of stock with no separate price shown at last check (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)).[^1]

## The Evidence

Whether topical copper peptide (GHK-Cu) changes skin, and how solid that evidence is, is covered in full on [[copper-peptides]]: strong laboratory and animal evidence for collagen, elastin, and glycosaminoglycan synthesis and wound healing, against a thin, mostly discoverer-authored human cosmetic evidence base, and the one independent randomized human trial of a topical GHK-Cu product (Miller 2006) found no objective benefit over control for redness, wrinkles, or skin quality, only higher patient-reported satisfaction.[^2]

Nothing specific to this product was found: no published trial tests NIOD's CAIS3 formula, its exact 1% GHK-Cu level, or its combination with the other peptides on the label (tripeptide-29, myristoyl nonapeptide-3, tripeptide-2, acetyl tetrapeptide-2), so the grades above rest entirely on the general category evidence for copper peptide summarized on [[copper-peptides]].[^1] A 2024 review also notes a "surprising absence of clinical studies" of topical GHK-Cu despite wide cosmetic use;[^3] the separate delivery/permeation question for GHK-Cu is covered on [[copper-peptides]].

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its grades rest on copper peptide's general case: strong lab/animal biology, thin independent human cosmetic evidence, and a real delivery hurdle getting the peptide through the skin barrier ([[copper-peptides]]).

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] The peptides are grouped below; the rest is the humectant, texture, and preservative system.

Peptides:

- [[copper-peptides|Tripeptide-1 Copper Acetate]] (GHK-Cu, "copper peptide"), the product's headline active
- Tripeptide-29, a signal peptide
- Tripeptide-1 Acetate, the copper-free form of GHK
- Myristoyl Nonapeptide-3, a lipid-conjugated signal peptide
- Tripeptide-2, a signal peptide
- Acetyl Tetrapeptide-2, a signal peptide

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, glycerin (humectant), sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer ([[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]] derivative), glycogen (a humectant sugar), propanediol and pentylene glycol (solvents and humectants), dimethyl isosorbide and ethoxydiglycol (solvents), isoceteth-20 (emulsifier), Leuconostoc/radish root ferment filtrate (a bio-ferment), caprylyl glycol and ethylhexylglycerin (preservative boosters), and phenoxyethanol and chlorphenesin (preservatives).[^1]

The ingredient list is short, 20 ingredients total, and declares no added fragrance.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Minimizing the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles" and skin feels "firmer in 8 weeks."** Positioning claims from NIOD's own product page, without a linked published trial, sample size, or peer review; the one independent randomized human trial of a topical GHK-Cu product found no objective difference in wrinkles versus control.[^1][^2]
- **"Promote the appearance of natural collagen."** GHK-Cu does drive collagen synthesis in cell and animal studies, but the human cosmetic case for a topical serum rests mainly on reviews by the peptide's discoverer, not independent replication (on [[copper-peptides]]).
- **"Smoother and tone appears more even in 4 weeks."** An unpublished internal testing claim on the product page, without a linked methodology, control arm, or published result.[^1]
- **"A foundational copper peptide serum to restore the look of healthy skin."** General positioning language; copper peptide's better-documented benefits are laboratory-level matrix synthesis and wound healing, not a demonstrated cosmetic restoration effect in this specific serum (on [[copper-peptides]]).
