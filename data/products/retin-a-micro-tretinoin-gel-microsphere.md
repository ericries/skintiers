Retin-A Micro is a prescription-only gel that suspends [[tretinoin]] inside a methyl methacrylate/glycol dimethacrylate crosspolymer microsphere (the manufacturer's MICROSPONGE System), sold in four strengths, 0.04%, 0.06%, 0.08%, and 0.1%, and FDA-approved for acne vulgaris in patients 12 and older.[^1]

> Retin-A Micro is not a different drug from plain tretinoin; it is the same molecule, [tretinoin, with strong acne evidence and a well-replicated but gradual photoaging effect](#the-evidence), packaged in a polymer microsphere and sold across four strengths rather than the usual two. The FDA's own trial data show irritation and discontinuation rising with strength, from 1.3% of subjects discontinuing at 0.04% to 6% at 0.1%, so the strength chosen changes the tolerability trade-off more than it changes the underlying drug.[^1]

## Summary

Retin-A Micro is a prescription topical retinoid: [[tretinoin]], the direct, receptor-active form of vitamin A, formulated in a gel that suspends the drug inside a polymer microsphere rather than a plain cream or gel base.[^1] It is manufactured by [[bausch-health|Bausch Health]] and dispensed by prescription only, "Rx only" per its FDA label.[^1] It comes in four strengths, 0.04%, 0.06%, 0.08%, and 0.1%, all approved for the same use, "the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in adults and pediatric patients 12 years of age and older."[^1]

Tretinoin's own evidence, covered in full on [[tretinoin]], is solid for acne (a modest effect as monotherapy, matched by adapalene, strengthened when combined with benzoyl peroxide) and notable for photoaging (a well-replicated but gradual, partial improvement in fine wrinkles and mottled pigment). Acne is Retin-A Micro's actual FDA indication, and it carries that modest-effect, solid-evidence verdict on its own label rather than resting only on the molecule's general record ([the evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1]

What distinguishes this product from a plain tretinoin cream or gel is the delivery vehicle: the drug is entrapped in a methyl methacrylate/glycol dimethacrylate crosspolymer microsphere, which the label calls the MICROSPONGE System.[^1] The label's own trial data show irritation and discontinuation scale with strength within this product's own range, from 1.3% of subjects discontinuing due to cutaneous irritation at 0.04% to 6% at 0.1%, but no comparative trial against a non-microsphere tretinoin product is cited on the label, so a claim that the microsphere itself lowers irritation relative to plain tretinoin is not evaluated here ([what's in it](#whats-in-it)).[^1]

## The Evidence

Tretinoin has solid, FDA-approved evidence for acne as a first-line topical, with a modest monotherapy effect roughly matched by adapalene, and separately has well-replicated, solid evidence for photoaging, an off-label use for this specific acne-indicated product; both are graded in full on [[tretinoin]]. The question specific to this product is Retin-A Micro's own FDA record and dosing.

The FDA label states Retin-A Micro "is a retinoid indicated for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in adults and pediatric patients 12 years of age and older," across all four strengths, and directs patients to "apply a thin layer of RETIN-A MICRO (0.04%, 0.06%, 0.08%, or 0.1%) to skin where acne lesions appear (cover the entire affected area), once daily in the evening."[^1] The label's own trial data, broken out by strength, show tolerability declining as concentration rises: at 0.1%, "6% (14/224) of RETIN-A MICRO 0.1%-treated subjects discontinued treatment due to cutaneous irritation" and "no more than 3%... had cutaneous irritation scores indicative of severe cutaneous irritation," while at 0.04%, "1% (2/225) of subjects had cutaneous irritation scores indicative of a severe irritation" and "1.3% (3/225) of subjects discontinued treatment due to cutaneous irritation."[^1]

> **Bottom line.** Retin-A Micro's FDA indication is acne, at any of its four strengths, and its own trial data show discontinuation for irritation roughly quadrupling from the lowest strength (0.04%) to the highest (0.1%). No head-to-head trial against a conventional (non-microsphere) tretinoin product is cited on the label.[^1]

## What's In It

- **Active: tretinoin**, at a labeled 0.04%, 0.06%, 0.08%, or 0.1% depending on the SKU dispensed. This is the only ingredient with graded efficacy; its mechanism and full evidence are on [[tretinoin]].[^1]
- **Inactive ingredients, per the FDA label:** benzyl alcohol, butylated hydroxytoluene, carbomer 974P, cyclomethicone and dimethicone copolyol, disodium EDTA, glycerin, methyl methacrylate/glycol dimethacrylate crosspolymer (the microsphere/MICROSPONGE System), PPG-20 methyl glucose ether distearate, propylene glycol, propylene glycol dicaprylate/dicaprate, purified water, sorbic acid, and trolamine.[^1] These are formulation excipients; the label attributes no independent efficacy to them, and the microsphere polymer's role is to carry the drug rather than to act on the skin itself.

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined; treat it as unproven unless it also appears in The Evidence above.

- **Microsphere delivery as a tolerability advantage.** The MICROSPONGE System is a real, label-declared ingredient (methyl methacrylate/glycol dimethacrylate crosspolymer), and the label's within-product data do show lower discontinuation at the lowest strength than the highest.[^1] But the label does not include a controlled comparison against a plain (non-microsphere) tretinoin cream or gel, so a claim that the microsphere itself, independent of strength, reduces irritation relative to conventional tretinoin is not supported by anything cited here.
