Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun: Aqua-fresh Rice + B5 is a lightweight Korean daily chemical sunscreen, 50 ml, rated SPF50+ PA++++, positioned as a lighter, more matte-finish sibling to the brand's original Relief Sun formula and built on a five-filter set of modern organic UV filters.[^1]

> A daily broad-spectrum sunscreen carrying the same top Korean label as its sibling, [[beauty-of-joseon-relief-sun-rice-probiotics-spf50|Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics]], SPF50+ PA++++, but built on a different formula: its ingredient listing records five modern organic UV filters against the sibling's four, and it drops niacinamide in favor of panthenol, ferulic acid, and hyaluronic acid. Board-certified dermatologist Dr. Scott Walter, reviewing the two side by side, described Aqua-fresh as lighter and more matte-finishing than the original and measured lower on-skin hydration for it, 38 versus 46. The SPF and UVA filter science is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^1][^walter]

## Summary

Relief Sun: Aqua-fresh Rice + B5 is a daily broad-spectrum sunscreen from the Korean brand Beauty of Joseon, positioned within the brand's Relief Sun line as a lighter, faster-absorbing alternative to [[beauty-of-joseon-relief-sun-rice-probiotics-spf50|Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics]]. Its core purpose is health rather than cosmetics: broad-spectrum protection against UV is the single best-evidenced way to reduce photoaging and lower the risk of sun-related skin cancer, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

What distinguishes this version is its filter set and its ingredient list, not its UV rating. It carries the same top Korean label as Relief Sun, SPF50+ PA++++, but its ingredient listing records five modern organic filters where Relief Sun's carries four: [[ethylhexyl-triazone|ethylhexyl triazone]] (Uvinul T 150) for UVB, and four filters contributing to UVA coverage, [[ecamsule|ecamsule]] (Mexoryl SX), [[diethylamino-hydroxybenzoyl-hexyl-benzoate|DHHB]] (Uvinul A Plus), [[drometrizole-trisiloxane|drometrizole trisiloxane]] (Mexoryl XL), and [[bemotrizinol|bemotrizinol]] (Tinosorb S), [detailed in the evidence](#the-evidence). The formula also drops niacinamide, present high on Relief Sun's ingredient list, in favor of panthenol, ferulic acid, and sodium hyaluronate ([composition detailed below](#whats-in-it)).

On reception, board-certified dermatologist Dr. Scott Walter reviewed the two side by side and confirmed the formula change, stating in his video that the newer version omits niacinamide, and describing its texture as "lighter weight" with "a slightly more matte finish compared to the OG."[^walter] In his own on-skin hydration measurements, Aqua-fresh scored lower than Relief Sun, 38 versus 46, though he called both "moisturizing" and said people who found the original too rich, or who have oily skin, "might like this one more."[^walter]

Its SPF and PA figures are Korean-regulatory label claims, not FDA-tested, and the product reaches US buyers as an import.[^1] It is inexpensive, listed at $18.00 for 50 ml at [Stylevana](#where-to-buy) (accessed 2026-08-13), the same list price as Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics.

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. What is specific to this product is its filter set and its Korean label ratings. It is a cosmetic sunscreen sold as a Korean import, not an FDA-reviewed OTC drug, and carries no FDA Drug Facts panel.[^1]

The retailer listing names it "Relief Sun Aqua-fresh Rice + B5 SPF50+ PA++++" and rates it SPF50+ and PA++++, the same top labels carried by Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics.[^1] SPF50+ is the top of the capped Korean and EU SPF (UVB) scale; PA++++ is the highest grade of the PPD-based UVA scale. Both scales, and how they compare with the US system, are explained on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

Its ingredient listing records five modern organic UV filters, one more than Relief Sun's four, [enumerated below](#whats-in-it). [[ethylhexyl-triazone|Ethylhexyl triazone]] (Uvinul T 150) is a dedicated UVB filter. The other four contribute to UVA coverage: [[ecamsule|ecamsule]] (terephthalylidene dicamphor sulfonic acid, Mexoryl SX), [[diethylamino-hydroxybenzoyl-hexyl-benzoate|DHHB]] (Uvinul A Plus), [[drometrizole-trisiloxane|drometrizole trisiloxane]] (Mexoryl XL, which absorbs in both the UVB and UVA bands from a single molecule), and [[bemotrizinol|bemotrizinol]] (Tinosorb S, a broad-spectrum UVB and UVA filter). Four of the five sit outside the current US OTC sunscreen monograph; the fifth, bemotrizinol, was added to it only in June 2026, as set out on [[bemotrizinol]].

> **Bottom line.** SPF50+ and PA++++ are Korean-regulatory label claims resting on a five-filter modern organic UV filter set, one dedicated UVB filter and four contributing to UVA coverage, as recorded by the product's ingredient listing rather than an independent lab test of this exact SKU.[^1]

## What's In It

The ingredient list as recorded on [INCIDecoder's ingredient listing for this product](https://incidecoder.com/products/beauty-of-joseon-relief-sun-aqua-fresh-rice-b5-spf50-pa), corroborated by a second, independently submitted INCIDecoder listing for the same product. Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

UV filters (modern organic filters; of these, only bemotrizinol is in the current US OTC monograph, added June 2026):

- [[ethylhexyl-triazone]] (Uvinul T 150), a UVB filter
- [[drometrizole-trisiloxane]] (Mexoryl XL), a filter absorbing in both the UVB and UVA bands
- [[ecamsule]] (terephthalylidene dicamphor sulfonic acid, Mexoryl SX), a UVA filter
- [[diethylamino-hydroxybenzoyl-hexyl-benzoate]] (Uvinul A Plus, DHHB), a UVA filter
- [[bemotrizinol]] (Tinosorb S), a broad-spectrum filter

Other functional ingredients:

- [[panthenol]] (vitamin B5), the formula's "B5" positioning, and [[glycerin]], both humectants
- [[hyaluronic-acid|sodium hyaluronate]], a humectant
- [[ferulic-acid|ferulic acid]] and [[tocopherol|tocopherol]] (vitamin E), antioxidants
- [[ceramides|ceramide NP]] and phytosphingosine, barrier lipids
- oryza sativa (rice) seed water, rice amino acids, and rice SH-oligopeptide-1, the "rice" positioning
- avena sativa (oat) kernel extract, cynara scolymus (artichoke) leaf extract, camellia sinensis (green tea) leaf water, and beta-glucan, plant extracts and soothing agents

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, dibutyl adipate and butyloctyl salicylate (emollients), polyglyceryl-3 distearate, polymethylsilsesquioxane and caprylyl methicone (silicones for feel), tromethamine and citric acid (pH adjusters), 1,2-hexanediol and pentylene glycol (solvents and humectants), glyceryl stearate, silica, propanediol, potassium cetyl phosphate, poly C10-30 alkyl acrylate, methylpropanediol, carbomer, acrylates/C10-30 alkyl acrylate crosspolymer (thickeners and emulsifiers), ethylhexylglycerin, xanthan gum, polyether-1, polyquaternium-51, hydrogenated lecithin, biosaccharide gum-1, and inositol.

Not in the formula: no niacinamide, which sits high on the ingredient list of Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics; this formula relies on panthenol, ferulic acid, and sodium hyaluronate instead. Also not in the formula: ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate (octinoxate) and avobenzone, the whole filter system is modern organic filters.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded UV evidence above:

- **"Refreshing hydration."** The retailer listing describes the formula as "crafted with rice seed water and panthenol to deliver refreshing hydration while safeguarding your skin from harmful UV rays and environmental stressors."[^1] This is a cosmetic hydration claim, separate from the graded sun-protection benefit, which is the SPF50+ PA++++ UV rating.
- **"No white cast," "no stickiness," "a quick-absorbing, dewy finish."** The listing states Aqua-fresh "provides the same benefits as the original Relief Sun," naming "no white cast" and "no stickiness," plus "enhanced hydration and a quick-absorbing, dewy finish."[^1] Dr. Scott Walter's hands-on review instead describes the texture as "lighter weight" with "a slightly more matte finish compared to the OG," and his own hydration measurement scored it below the original.[^walter]
