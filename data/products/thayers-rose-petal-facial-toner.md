## Summary

The flagship of [[thayers]], an alcohol-free witch hazel and rose water toner, $10.95 for 12 oz on the brand's own site, with 8.5 oz at $7.97 and 3 oz at $4.49.[^brand] **Bottom line:** the ingredient list is published in full and it does not read the way the marketing does. Witch hazel, the ingredient the brand is built on, sits third, behind water and [[glycerin]].

> **The hero ingredient is outranked by the humectant.** The declared list opens "AQUA / WATER / EAU • GLYCERIN • HAMAMELIS VIRGINIANA BARK/LEAF/TWIG EXTRACT".[^brand] Glycerin has real evidence for drawing and holding water at the skin surface; [[witch-hazel]] is graded `weak` here. When a user reports this toner feels hydrating, glycerin in position two is the likeliest reason, and the brand's own "hydrates skin by 50%" claim is attached to the product rather than to witch hazel.[^brand]

## The Rubric

**Hydrating and toning the skin surface**
- **Effect size: minimal.** The brand's framing is that the toner is "Formulated with pure-grade alcohol-free witch hazel to soothe and balance skin, fused with aloe vera to immediately hydrate".[^brand] Soothe and balance are not measurable endpoints. The molecule evidence lives on [[witch-hazel]], [[glycerin]] and [[aloe-vera]] and is not restated here.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** No independent trial of this finished product was found.

**The brand's own numbers, and what is missing from them**
- **Effect size: unquantifiable from what is published.** The page claims the toner "visibly reduces pores, hydrates skin by 50%* and visibly smooths texture by 22%*".[^brand] The asterisk resolves to "In a clinical evaluation of the efficacy of facial toner in a panel of 73 subjects" in one place and the same sentence with "75 subjects" in another, on the same page.[^brand] Note the wording: a clinical evaluation of the efficacy of *facial toner*, not of this toner by name.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** No design, control arm, blinding, measurement method or timepoint is given, and the evaluation is not published or linked. A percentage without a comparator is a direction, not a result.

**Suitability for sensitive skin**
- **Effect size: a tension on the label.** The copy says the toner "Calms and nourishes with a gentle touch, making it ideal for soothing irritated or sensitive skin", and separately "Dermatologist-tested for safety, it's always alcohol-free, non-drying, and non-comedogenic".[^brand] The same page's ingredient list ends "PARFUM / FRAGRANCE • GERANIOL • CITRONELLOL".[^brand] Geraniol and citronellol are fragrance constituents labelled individually because they are recognised contact allergens, and their presence is the reason a fragranced toner is a poor first choice for reactive skin.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** Dermatologist-tested for safety is a tolerability procedure, not an efficacy finding, and the brand publishes no detail of it.

**Composition, in full**
- The declared list is AQUA / WATER / EAU, GLYCERIN, HAMAMELIS VIRGINIANA BARK/LEAF/TWIG EXTRACT, ROSA DAMASCENA FLOWER WATER, ALOE BARBADENSIS LEAF EXTRACT, POTASSIUM HYDROXIDE, CAPRYLYL GLYCOL, CITRIC ACID, ETHYLHEXYLGLYCERIN, POLYSORBATE 20, POTASSIUM SORBATE, SODIUM BENZOATE, PHENOXYETHANOL, PARFUM / FRAGRANCE, GERANIOL, CITRONELLOL.[^brand] Sixteen entries, no alcohol among them, which supports the alcohol-free claim.
- The brand highlights four of those as "Key Ingredients: Rose, Aloe, Witch Hazel, Glycerin".[^brand] Aloe sits fifth and rose water fourth, so three of the four marketed ingredients sit behind glycerin.

## The Evidence

None specific to this product from an independent source. What the page can state is what the label states, and the useful comparison is internal to the brand: this toner is built on witch hazel, while [[thayers-rapid-acne-treatment-sulfur-10]] carries none at all despite the same brand line.

The honest read is that this is a pleasant, cheap, fragranced humectant toner. The glycerin is doing the hydrating work the marketing attributes to the botanical, which is the same pattern [[best-hyaluronic-acid-products]] documents in hyaluronic acid serums whose glycerin outranks the named active.

> **Bottom line.** Well disclosed, cheaply priced, and weaker than its billing. Read it as a glycerin toner with witch hazel and fragrance rather than as a witch hazel treatment, and skip it if fragrance is a problem for your skin.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own site and **not** endorsed beyond what they say.

- "hydrates skin by 50%* and visibly smooths texture by 22%*"[^brand] Both asterisked to an unpublished evaluation of "facial toner" with two different panel sizes on one page.
- "98% Naturally Derived Ingredients"[^brand] A sourcing statement. Naturally derived is not a regulatory category and says nothing about efficacy or tolerability; the two fragrance allergens on this label are themselves naturally occurring.
- "Formula Free of Drying Alcohols"[^brand] Precisely worded. It excludes drying alcohols rather than all alcohols, and the declared list happens to contain neither.
- "backed by science to level up your skin with real, clinically tested results"[^brand] The clinical testing referenced is the asterisked evaluation above, which is not published.
