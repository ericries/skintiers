La Roche-Posay Toleriane Purifying Foaming Cleanser is a rinse-off foaming face wash for normal to oily, sensitive skin, formulated with the brand's prebiotic thermal spring water, niacinamide, and ceramide-3.[^lrp]

> A sulfate-free, soap-free, fragrance-free foaming cleanser positioned for normal to oily, sensitive skin. Its distinguishing ingredients, niacinamide and ceramide-3, are well-evidenced in leave-on formulas, covered on [[niacinamide]] and [[ceramides]], but this is a rinse-off wash and no product-specific test measures how much of either ingredient remains on skin after rinsing (see [What's In It](#whats-in-it)). The manufacturer's own comfort and cleansing figures come from an unpublished, brand-run four-week consumer survey, [reported here as a marketing claim rather than an established finding](#common-marketing-claims).[^lrp] It is a mid-priced drugstore cleanser: the manufacturer lists the 400 ml pump at $19.99 (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^lrp]

## Summary

Toleriane Purifying Foaming Cleanser is a daily face wash the manufacturer markets for normal to oily, sensitive skin.[^lrp] It foams on contact with water and is formulated without sulfate detergents (no sodium lauryl or laureth sulfate), without soap, and without added fragrance; La Roche-Posay states it is designed to remove dirt, makeup, and impurities while "maintaining the skin's natural moisture barrier and pH."[^lrp] Target's listing for the same 13.52 fl oz (400 ml) SKU independently confirms it is labeled Paraben Free, Sulfate Free, No Synthetic Fragrance, Non-Comedogenic, and Dermatologist Tested.[^target]

The formula's two named actives are niacinamide and ceramide-3 (ceramide NP), alongside glycerin and the brand's proprietary thermal spring water.[^lrp] Both niacinamide and ceramide-3 have real evidence bases in leave-on skincare, covered on [[niacinamide]] and [[ceramides]], but that evidence comes from products left on skin. This is a rinse-off wash, and [no product-specific study measures](#the-evidence) how much of either ingredient stays on skin after rinsing rather than going down the drain.

La Roche-Posay's own site cites a four-week consumer survey reporting that 94% of users said skin felt healthier, 94% said skin felt clean, and 92% said it effectively cleansed dirt, oil, and impurities.[^lrp] The same percentages appear reproduced on Target's product images for the identical SKU, so the claim is not confined to a single page, but neither source names an independent testing facility or a published methodology; it is a self-reported user-perception survey, not an objective barrier or hydration measurement, and is catalogued under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims) rather than folded into the grades above.[^lrp][^target]

It is a mid-priced drugstore cleanser. The manufacturer lists the 400 ml pump bottle at $19.99, with smaller 200 ml and 50 ml sizes at $15.99 and $8.99 (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^lrp]

## The Evidence

Topical niacinamide has modest, preliminary evidence for supporting the skin barrier in leave-on use, raising ceramide synthesis and lowering water loss in dry skin; the full evidence base is set out on [[niacinamide]]. Ceramide-3 (ceramide NP) is a skin-identical lipid with an established role in barrier repair when left on skin, covered on [[ceramides]]. Neither evidence base was generated on a rinse-off cleanser, and no study of this specific formula, or of a comparable niacinamide-and-ceramide foaming wash, tests how much of either ingredient is retained on skin after the product is rinsed away.

The only product-specific data located is La Roche-Posay's own four-week consumer-perception survey, described on the brand's product page as the basis for the "94% say skin feels healthier," "94% say skin feels clean," and "92% say it effectively cleanses skin of dirt/oil/impurities" figures.[^lrp] Target's listing for the same SKU displays product imagery carrying the identical "94%" and "92%" figures, so the claim is consistent across both a manufacturer and a retailer page rather than appearing only once, but neither page names an independent facility, a sample size, or a published methodology, and the survey measures subjective user perception rather than an instrumented barrier or hydration outcome.[^lrp][^target]

> Bottom line: the barrier- and ceramide-relevant ingredients in this wash are well-evidenced in leave-on skincare, but that evidence does not transfer automatically to a rinse-off product, and the only cleanser-specific data is an unpublished, brand-run consumer-perception survey rather than an instrumented measurement.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^lrp]

Notable functional ingredients:

- [[niacinamide]], a form of vitamin B3 the manufacturer describes as used "for its soothing properties, helping restore skin's moisture barrier, and visibly fading skin discolorations"[^lrp]
- Ceramide NP (branded [[ceramides|Ceramide-3]]), described by the manufacturer as "a skin-identical lipid used in our formulas to help support the skin moisture barrier"[^lrp]
- [[glycerin]], a humectant
- La Roche-Posay Prebiotic Thermal Water, the brand's proprietary sourced water, which the manufacturer describes as containing "minerals, trace elements, and a high concentration of selenium, a natural antioxidant"[^lrp]

Cleansing, texture, and preservative system: water, coco-betaine (mild surfactant), propanediol, PEG-120 methyl glucose dioleate, sodium hydroxide, sodium chloride, citric acid, disodium EDTA (chelator), hydroxyacetophenone, capryloyl glycine, caprylyl glycol, disodium cocoyl glycinate, trisodium ethylenediamine disuccinate, pentylene glycol, acrylates copolymer, and tocopherol (vitamin E).[^lrp]

Not in the formula: no sulfate detergents such as sodium lauryl or laureth sulfate, no soap, and no added fragrance, per the manufacturer's label claims, independently corroborated by Target's Paraben Free, Sulfate Free, and No Synthetic Fragrance labeling for the same SKU.[^lrp][^target]
