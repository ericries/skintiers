Paula's Choice 10% Niacinamide Booster is a fragrance-free, water-based serum from [[paulas-choice|Paula's Choice]] that states "concentrated 10% niacinamide" on its own product page, paired with ascorbyl glucoside (a vitamin C derivative) and licorice root extract, sold in a 20 ml bottle.[^1]

A US retailer lists the 20 ml bottle at $53.00, a premium price for the size relative to other 10% niacinamide serums.[^1][^2]

## Summary

10% Niacinamide Booster is a concentrated add-in serum built around [[niacinamide]], a form of vitamin B3, which Paula's Choice states at "concentrated 10%" on its own product page.[^1] Topical niacinamide is well tolerated and has modest evidence, much of it from small or manufacturer-run studies, for supporting the skin barrier and lowering water loss in dry skin, for lightening facial hyperpigmentation, and for mild inflammatory acne. That general case, with its primary trials and their limits, is set out on [[niacinamide]]. The formula also lists [[ascorbyl-glucoside]], a shelf-stable vitamin C derivative, without disclosing its concentration; the evidence for ascorbyl glucoside itself is thin, resting mostly on ex vivo skin and small trials of concentrated complexes rather than the plain ingredient at typical serum strength (on [[ascorbyl-glucoside]]).

The product is not distinguished by a novel active. Its 10% niacinamide concentration is, like most plain niacinamide serums on the market, above the 2% to 5% tested in the barrier, pigmentation, and acne trials behind [[niacinamide]]'s evidence, so its grades rest on that category evidence rather than on any trial of this exact formula (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)). A US retailer lists the 20 ml bottle at $53.00 (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07), a premium price for the size next to plainer 10% niacinamide serums.

Paula's Choice's own directions describe applying the serum "once or twice daily after cleansing, toning and exfoliating," dispensed as "2-3 drops and add[ed] to your favourite moisturiser or serum."[^1] The label's pore- and texture-refining marketing is weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## The Evidence

Topical niacinamide is a well-tolerated active with modest evidence for supporting the skin barrier, lightening facial hyperpigmentation, and mild inflammatory acne; how well each use holds up, and how much of it comes from small or manufacturer-run trials, is set out on [[niacinamide]]. No published clinical trial tests this exact 10% Niacinamide Booster formulation, so its grades rest on the category evidence for niacinamide at the concentrations that were actually studied, which were lower than the 10% Paula's Choice lists for this product.[^1]

In the barrier work summarized on [[niacinamide]], niacinamide raised stratum-corneum lipids and lowered water loss in dry skin at levels around 2%. In the pigmentation trials it was tested at 4% to 5%, where 4% niacinamide matched hydroquinone 4% in one small independent melasma study and was better tolerated. In acne, 4% nicotinamide matched topical clindamycin in a single randomized trial. That primary evidence, with its numbers, denominators, and limits, lives on [[niacinamide]], and none of it uses a 10% product.

> **Bottom line.** The 10% niacinamide concentration Paula's Choice states for this product sits above the 2% to 5% range actually tested in the barrier, pigmentation, and acne trials behind [[niacinamide]]'s evidence, and no independent trial has tested this formula itself.[^1]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), as declared on the manufacturer's own product page and independently corroborated, with minor formula differences, by a US retailer's listing.[^1][^2]

Actives:

- [[niacinamide]] 10%, a form of vitamin B3, as stated on the manufacturer's product page[^1]
- [[ascorbyl-glucoside]], a shelf-stable vitamin C derivative, listed without a disclosed concentration[^1]
- Glycyrrhiza glabra (licorice) root extract and dipotassium glycyrrhizate, which the manufacturer's retailer listing describes as skin-soothing[^2]
- Allantoin, boerhavia diffusa root extract, panthenol, and beta-glucan, additional skin-soothing ingredients per both listings[^1][^2]
- [[hyaluronic-acid|Sodium hyaluronate]], a humectant[^1]
- Epigallocatechin gallate, carnosine, and genistein, additional antioxidants per both listings[^1][^2]

Base, solvent, and preservative system, per the manufacturer's UK listing: water, acetyl glucosamine, butylene glycol, phospholipids, glycerin, citric acid, sodium citrate, sodium hydroxide, xanthan gum, ethylhexylglycerin, polysorbate 20, trisodium ethylenediamine disuccinate, pentylene glycol, caprylyl glycol, and the preservative phenoxyethanol.[^1] A US retailer's separately compiled listing names the same core actives and soothing agents but shows ubiquinone as an added antioxidant and disodium EDTA in place of trisodium ethylenediamine disuccinate, a minor difference between the two independently compiled lists.[^2]

The formula is fragrance-free and contains no exfoliating acids.[^1][^2]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **The 10% concentration.** Paula's Choice's stated 10% strength is above the 2% to 5% at which niacinamide was clinically tested in the barrier, pigmentation, and acne trials summarized on [[niacinamide]].[^1]
- **"Visibly minimises pore size and improve[s] skin's texture," and a US retailer's description of the product as targeting "enlarged pores, signs of aging and dullness."** Niacinamide's better-evidenced topical uses are barrier support, facial hyperpigmentation, and mild acne, set out on [[niacinamide]]; the pore-size and texture claims are the manufacturer's own positioning and are not established for this product by any cited trial.[^1][^2]
- **Ascorbyl glucoside's contribution.** The manufacturer's page lists it without a disclosed concentration, and the ingredient's own evidence rests mostly on ex vivo skin and small trials of concentrated complexes rather than the plain ingredient at typical serum strength (on [[ascorbyl-glucoside]]).
