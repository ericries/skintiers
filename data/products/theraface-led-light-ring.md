A $100 LED attachment for Therabody's TheraFace PRO handheld. **Bottom line:** its FDA file is unusually forthcoming, publishing the actual light output for all three modes, and it says in as many words that no clinical studies were performed. Category evidence is on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

> **It publishes the number almost nobody publishes, and ran no trial.** The Summary lists red at 73±5 mW/cm2, blue at 64±5 mW/cm2 and red plus infrared at 73±5/55±5 mW/cm2, and names a "TheraFace LED Light Power Density Test Report" among its bench testing.[^k212155] Under performance testing clinical it states: "There were no clinical studies performed."[^k212155] Good disclosure and no evidence are not the same axis.

## Summary

Cleared 2021-12-21 as K212155, applicant Theragun, Inc. of Los Angeles, product code OHS, a Traditional 510(k) published as a Summary.[^openfda] Three modes, each with its own stated indication and wavelength: red at 633±10 nm, blue at 415±10 nm, and a combined red plus infrared mode at 633 nm with 830±10 nm.[^k212155] The primary predicate is K171821 with K162489 as a supplement predicate.[^k212155]

**What the clearance actually permits**, quoted in full because the marketing diverges from it: "1）The red light is intended to treat periorbital wrinkles. 2）The blue light is intended to treat mild to moderate inflammatory acne. 3）The Red + IR is intended to treat periorbital wrinkles."[^k212155]

**Price and what you are buying.** The retail listing is the "TheraFace LED Light Ring" at $99.99, described by the brand as a "replacement" ring.[^brand] It is an attachment: the cleared device is the control unit together with its LED irradiation ring, so the ring alone is not a working device.[^k212155] The brand also sells a separate TheraFace Mask at $649.99 and a TheraFace Mask Glo at $379.99, which are different products under a different clearance.[^brand][^openfda]

**Disclosure record.** All five filings across the firm's two applicant names are Summaries: TheraFace Microcurrent, RecoveryAir PRO and this device under Theragun, Inc., then TheraFace Mask and JetBoots PRO Plus under Therabody.[^openfda] As with Silk'n, searching one name alone gives an incomplete record.

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate inflammatory acne (blue, 415 nm)**
Graded modest on mixed evidence, inherited from the modality rather than earned here. Blue is the cleared indication for this mode, and blue light has the better of the colour evidence, ranked on [[led-colours-for-acne-by-evidence]]. This device contributes no data to that case, because it ran no clinical study.[^k212155] Acne generally is on [[acne]].

**Periorbital wrinkles (red 633 nm, red plus infrared 830 nm)**
Graded minimal on anecdotal evidence, and read the word periorbital. Both red modes are cleared for the skin around the eyes, not the face at large.[^k212155] With no clinical study performed, the clearance rests on equivalence to two predicates, which is a statement about technology and intended use rather than a measured outcome, as [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] sets out.

**Dark spots and radiance**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence, and this is the sharpest gap on the page. The brand sells the red plus infrared mode "to help reduce dark spots and enhance radiance".[^brand] The clearance for that same mode reads "The Red + IR is intended to treat periorbital wrinkles."[^k212155] Those are different claims about different problems. What has evidence for pigment is on [[hyperpigmentation]].

**Firming skin**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. The brand's red mode claim is "to firm skin and reduce fine lines and wrinkles".[^brand] Firming appears nowhere in the cleared indication and nothing here measures it.

## The Evidence

**The dose figures are worth having, and they are not efficacy.** Published irradiance is rare in this category: the LED disclosure ranking at [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]] exists because most filings name the unit mW/cm2 without ever supplying a number. This one supplies three. What it buys you is the ability to do the arithmetic on [[led-dose-j-per-cm2-and-wavelength]] and compare against trial doses, not an answer about whether the device works.

**Three claims, three mismatches.** Lined up against the cleared indication, the brand's one-sentence product description diverges on each mode: red is sold for firming and facial wrinkles where the clearance says periorbital wrinkles; red plus infrared is sold for dark spots and radiance where the clearance says periorbital wrinkles; and blue is sold "to fight acne and acne-causing bacteria" where the clearance says mild to moderate inflammatory acne, dropping the severity limit and adding an antibacterial mechanism the indication does not state.[^brand][^k212155]

**What the file did test.** Bench and safety work, not people: biocompatibility, IEC 60601-1, IEC 60601-2-57 for photobiological safety of lamps, AAMI TIR69:2017, ANSI C63.27-2017 wireless coexistence, a usability study report and the light power density report.[^k212155] That is a competent safety package. It is silent on whether the light changes anyone's skin.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. The red and infrared modes are cleared for use around the eye, where a device is operating close to the eye itself; follow the manufacturer's eye-protection instructions. Acne that scars or does not respond is worth a dermatologist rather than another device.

## Common Marketing Claims

**"Red LED to firm skin and reduce fine lines and wrinkles".** Assessed above. The cleared red indication is periorbital wrinkles, and firming is not in it.[^brand][^k212155]

**"Red + Infrared LED to help reduce dark spots and enhance radiance".** The clearance for this mode says periorbital wrinkles.[^brand][^k212155]

**"Blue LED to fight acne and acne-causing bacteria".** The cleared wording is narrower, "mild to moderate inflammatory acne", and says nothing about bacteria.[^brand][^k212155]

**"3 powerful light therapies in one".** Accurate as a description of three modes at three wavelengths, all three of which are documented in the file.[^brand][^k212155]
