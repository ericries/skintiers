Weightless Body Treatment 2% BHA is a fragrance-free, leave-on body lotion from [[paulas-choice|Paula's Choice]] built around 2% salicylic acid, the same oil-soluble beta hydroxy acid used in the brand's facial BHA exfoliants, formulated in a body-lotion base the manufacturer lists at $35.[^pc]

> A lightweight, non-greasy lotion carrying 2% salicylic acid at a manufacturer-stated pH of 3.5-3.9, marketed for rough, bumpy skin on the arms and legs, including keratosis-pilaris-prone skin. The BHA-for-bumpy-skin evidence, tested at a higher 5% concentration in the one controlled keratosis pilaris trial, lives on [[keratosis-pilaris]]; no independent trial of this exact product was located.[^pc]

## Summary

Weightless Body Treatment 2% BHA pairs [[salicylic-acid|salicylic acid]] at 2% with a "silky lightweight lotion" base that Paula's Choice describes as feeling "beautiful on skin" and "ideal for hard, clogged pores on the arms and legs."[^pc] It is the body-format counterpart to the brand's facial 2% BHA products: Paula's Choice states the "primary differences between these body and facial exfoliants are their size, texture and base formula since they all contain the same key ingredient, 2% BHA," and positions the lotion base as more hydrating and suited to use "from the neck down."[^pc]

The manufacturer's own FAQ frames the product for the kind of small, hard bumps associated with keratosis pilaris, stating that "beta hydroxy acid (BHA, or salicylic acid, which is the key ingredient in this product)... [is] suitable for small hard bumps on skin, including skin prone to keratosis pilaris."[^pc] That is a brand positioning claim rather than a study of this product; the actual controlled evidence for salicylic acid against keratosis pilaris bumps, including how it compares to lactic acid, is graded on [[keratosis-pilaris]] rather than restated here. That trial tested a 5% salicylic acid concentration, higher than the 2% in this lotion, so its result cannot be assumed to carry over to this product.

Paula's Choice states the product's pH as 3.5-3.9, calling it "within the optimal pH range for effective exfoliation," and directs it to be applied to clean skin, either as a spot treatment or the way a regular body lotion is used, on wet or dry skin, daily or as often as needed.[^pc] The brand's products are marketed as fragrance-free, though Paula's Choice acknowledges this particular formula "may have a unique scent" from its ingredient combination that "dissipates quickly after application."[^pc]

## The Evidence

The active ingredient here, 2% salicylic acid, is the same monograph-strength BHA graded on [[salicylic-acid]], where its acne evidence (guideline-recognized but second-tier to benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids) is set out in full. For the bumpy-texture use this product is marketed for, the relevant controlled evidence is the split-side trial graded on [[keratosis-pilaris]], which tested a higher 5% salicylic acid concentration than this product's 2%, and the raw acid rather than this specific lotion formula, so its result is directional context rather than direct evidence for this product. The trial's figures and its comparison against lactic acid live on that page, not here.

Paula's Choice's own product page does not cite an independent clinical study of this lotion; its claims are description and FAQ content rather than trial data (see [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^pc]
