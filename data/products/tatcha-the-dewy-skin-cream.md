Tatcha The Dewy Skin Cream is a fragrance-containing, rich facial moisturizer from Tatcha, marketed as a "Replenishing and Plumping Moisturizer" for dry skin and sold with an included gold-tone spoon in a 1.7 fl oz / 50 mL size for $74.00, the same size and price as Tatcha's lighter sister product, [[tatcha-the-water-cream|The Water Cream]].[^tatcha]

> A rich, emollient-forward cream whose real, defensible benefit is basic cosmetic hydration: glycerin and sodium hyaluronate as humectants, backed by dimethicone, squalane, and a run of emollient esters and a fatty alcohol for a heavier, more occlusive feel than the gel-textured Water Cream (see [[glycerin]], [[hyaluronic-acid]], [[dimethicone]], and [[squalane]]). The brand's "plumping," "dewy glow," and "protect from oxidative stress" marketing rests on its own small, uncontrolled panel studies (30 to 36 people), not an independently published or controlled trial, and most of its named botanicals (Okinawa algae, rice bran, ginseng, marjoram, thyme, mallow) have no independent evidence page on this site to back a specific benefit ([see What's In It](#whats-in-it) and [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^tatcha]

## Summary

Tatcha positions The Dewy Skin Cream as a rich moisturizer that "drenches skin in long-lasting hydration with hyaluronic acid for a dewy glow and more," claiming it is "proven to visibly plump fine lines, seal in moisture, and protect skin from oxidative stress for softer, more supple skin."[^tatcha] It is marketed for dry skin, and the brand's site-wide comparison widget summarizes it as delivering "3x hydration, visibly reduces lines, seals in moisture, and imparts a dewy glow" in a "Rich decadent cream" texture.[^tatcha] Directions on the brand's page, under the heading "PLUMP - Cushion skin with rich, radiant hydration," call for scooping cream with the included gold spoon and massaging it onto "face, neck and decolletage in upward strokes," daily, morning and night.[^tatcha]

The part of that story the declared ingredient list actually supports is hydration and emollience, not plumping or oxidative-stress protection. Glycerin, a well-evidenced humectant, sits 3rd in the formula; sodium hyaluronate, another humectant, is listed much further down (25th of 47 ingredients, after most of the named botanical extracts), consistent with a modest, brand-undisclosed concentration. Dimethicone sits 5th and squalane 6th, followed by a run of emollient esters and a fatty alcohol (Diisostearyl Malate, Behenyl Alcohol, Myristyl Myristate, Dipentaerythrityl Hexahydroxystearate, Bis-Diglyceryl Polyacyladipate-2, Tridecyl Trimellitate) that gives the cream its heavier, richer feel compared with the more silicone-forward Water Cream gel. The general evidence for these humectant and emollient mechanisms is set out on [[glycerin]], [[hyaluronic-acid]], [[dimethicone]], and [[squalane]] ([see What's In It](#whats-in-it)).

Tatcha's own "Clinical Results" section claims figures such as "boosts skin moisturization by 225% after 1 use," "100% showed immediate improvement in skin plumpness, suppleness, and radiance after 1 use," and "88% showed an improvement in the look of dry fine lines after 2 weeks," each footnoted only as coming from a small brand-run panel (30 or 36 people), described as a "clinical study" or "bio-instrumentation study."[^tatcha] No control group, blinding, or independent publication is mentioned, so these are the brand's own claim, not independently verified evidence ([see Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).

The brand's own site lists the 1.7 fl oz / 50 mL jar at $74.00 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-15).[^tatcha]

## The Evidence

No independently published or product-specific clinical trial of this exact cream was located; the evidence below is category-level, applied to what the declared ingredient list actually contains.

Glycerin, listed 3rd, is a humectant with a long, well-evidenced record of drawing and holding water at the skin's surface; that general case is set out on [[glycerin]]. Sodium hyaluronate, listed much lower in the formula (25th of 47 ingredients, after nearly all the named botanical extracts), is a further humectant whose general evidence, including the caveat that molecular weight affects how deeply it penetrates, is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]; Tatcha does not disclose a concentration or molecular weight for this product.

Dimethicone, listed 5th, is a silicone occlusive and emollient documented to give a moisturizing and softening effect, set out on [[dimethicone]]. Squalane, listed 6th, is a skin-identical lipid used almost entirely as an emollient and occlusive rather than a treatment active, with a favorable safety review from the Cosmetic Ingredient Review; its general evidence, including the caveat that no controlled trial isolates squalane's own effect on human skin, is set out on [[squalane]]. Below squalane the formula adds a run of emollient esters and a fatty alcohol (Diisostearyl Malate, Behenyl Alcohol, Myristyl Myristate, Dipentaerythrityl Hexahydroxystearate, Bis-Diglyceryl Polyacyladipate-2, Tridecyl Trimellitate) rather than petrolatum or ceramides, the stronger-evidenced occlusives and barrier lipids ranked higher on [[moisturizing]]'s own tier list. That composition, not a study result, is why this page treats the cream's case as richer cosmetic hydration for dry, non-compromised skin rather than barrier repair for eczema-prone or very dry skin.

The brand's "Clinical Results" panel data (a "clinical study" of 36 panelists and a "bio-instrumentation study" of 30 panelists) is brand-commissioned, uncontrolled testing rather than an independently published trial, and is quarantined under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims) rather than graded as evidence.

> **Bottom line.** No independent study has isolated this cream's effect. Its defensible case rests on general humectant evidence (glycerin, sodium hyaluronate) and general emollient/occlusive evidence (dimethicone, squalane), applied to a formula with no petrolatum or ceramides. The brand's plumping, dewy-glow, and oxidative-stress-protection claims rest on its own small, uncontrolled panel studies, not independent evidence.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not independently established by the declared ingredient list above:

- **"Proven to visibly plump fine lines, seal in moisture, and protect skin from oxidative stress for softer, more supple skin."**[^tatcha] The only conventionally antioxidant-labeled ingredient in the declared list is Tocopherol (vitamin E), positioned relatively low (29th of 47) with no disclosed concentration; the brand does not name which ingredient(s) it credits with the "oxidative stress" protection claim.
- **"Boosts skin moisturization by 225% after 1 use," "100% showed immediate improvement in skin plumpness, suppleness, and radiance," "100% showed an all-day improvement in radiance, moisture, & skin barrier," "86% agreed the cream works well with makeup," "88% showed an improvement in the look of dry fine lines after 2 weeks," and related figures.**[^tatcha] Each is footnoted only as coming from a "clinical study of 36 panelists" or a "bio-instrumentation study of 30 panelists," brand-commissioned, uncontrolled testing with no stated placebo or vehicle comparison, blinding, or independent publication.
- **"Plumping Skincare" and "Dewy Skincare" category tags, and the "dewy glow" positioning.**[^tatcha] No named anti-aging active (such as a retinoid or a disclosed-percentage peptide) is present in the declared ingredients; the plumping and glow positioning rests on the cosmetic hydration and emollient effects above, not a demonstrated collagen- or volume-building mechanism.
- **Named botanicals presented as key actives (Okinawa algae, Japanese rice bran, ginseng, marjoram, thyme, mallow), part of the brand's "Hadasei-3" story.**[^tatcha] Only [[panax-ginseng|Panax Ginseng]] has an independent evidence page on this site, and its own tier there is weak; no human topical trial of this specific cream isolates a benefit from any of these extracts.
