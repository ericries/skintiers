IOPE sells this 50 mL serum in Korea as the XMD Stem 3 Clinical Recovery Serum (English on-pack name "XMD STEMIII CLINICAL RECOVERY SERUM"), built around a "PDRN H.A. Liposome" complex, [[centella-asiatica]] triterpenes, and [[niacinamide]], marketed for skin recovery and a plumping effect.[^1]

> A Korean "clinical recovery" serum whose headline ingredient, PDRN, has a well-documented mechanism for wound healing but almost no independent evidence as a leave-on cosmetic, a gap covered in full on [[polydeoxyribonucleotide-pdrn]]. Its centella triterpenes and niacinamide add modest, mostly preliminary support for soothing and barrier function, at concentrations the brand does not disclose. No published trial tests this specific product or formula.[^1]

## Summary

The Stem 3 Clinical Recovery Serum (sold under IOPE's "XMD" sub-line) is a 50 mL leave-on serum whose declared ingredient list opens with water, three humectants/solvents, then [[niacinamide]], followed by a mix of soothing and barrier ingredients: [[allantoin]], centella asiatica triterpenes (asiaticoside, madecassic acid, asiatic acid), [[cholesterol]], [[adenosine]], hydrolyzed DNA, and [[hyaluronic-acid]], plus a single peptide, acetyl tetrapeptide-11.[^1] No percentages are printed on the product page for any active.[^1]

The brand's marketing centers on a "PDRN H.A.™ LIPOSOME 92.3%" claim and a "물광 플럼핑" (glossy, plumping) effect.[^1] PDRN (polydeoxyribonucleotide, the "salmon DNA" ingredient behind injectable skin-booster treatments) has a real, well-mapped mechanism for tissue repair, but that evidence comes almost entirely from injected or wound-applied use; the leave-on cosmetic case is thin, resting on a single industry-run trial of a different, penetration-engineered PDRN variant, not this product, as set out on [[polydeoxyribonucleotide-pdrn]].[^2] The centella triterpenes and niacinamide add plausible but similarly unconfirmed-at-this-dose support for soothing and barrier function ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)).

No published trial isolates this product, and IOPE's own page does not state a price; it is currently listed there as sold out, with purchases directed to an external retailer link (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^1]

## The Evidence

No published trial tests this exact formula. Its grades rest on the general evidence for its three most heavily marketed actives, all reviewed in full on their own pages.

**PDRN / "PDRN H.A. Liposome."** IOPE's own page names this as the product's central active, citing "PDRN H.A.™ LIPOSOME 92.3%."[^1] PDRN activates the adenosine A2A receptor, a mechanism confirmed across in vitro, animal, and clinical wound-healing studies, almost all using injected or wound-applied PDRN, not a leave-on serum. The only topical, leave-on human trial identified anywhere is a 2026 split-face study of a proprietary "medium-length" PDRN eye cream run by the ingredient's own commercial developer, not independently replicated, and not a test of this or any standard PDRN serum.[^2] The INCI ingredient on this product's own list closest to "PDRN" is hydrolyzed DNA; IOPE's page does not state whether it uses purified PDRN specifically or a broader DNA hydrolysate.[^1]

**Centella asiatica triterpenes.** The serum lists asiaticoside, madecassic acid, and asiatic acid, the same triterpenes documented on [[centella-asiatica]] for wound healing and "cica" soothing. Their anti-inflammatory activity is reproducible in cell and animal models, but no controlled human trial isolates centella for redness or barrier soothing, and the concentration used here is not disclosed.[^3]

**Niacinamide.** [[niacinamide]]'s modest evidence for barrier support and reduced water loss comes from a single manufacturer research group, in cultured keratinocytes plus an in vivo transepidermal-water-loss measure, with no leave-on percentage stated in that trial. This product does not print a niacinamide percentage either, and niacinamide sits fifth on the ingredient list, after water and three glycol/glycerin solvents, so nothing here confirms it reaches a tested level.[^1][^4]

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its headline "PDRN H.A." claim borrows evidence generated almost entirely from injected or wound-applied PDRN, not a leave-on serum; its centella and niacinamide content add plausible but clinically unconfirmed, undisclosed-concentration support for soothing and barrier function.[^2][^3][^4]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (전성분/INCI) as declared on IOPE's official product page.[^1] Ingredients with a documented functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

Actives and functional ingredients, in declared order:

- [[niacinamide]]
- [[allantoin]], a soothing agent
- asiaticoside, madecassic acid, and asiatic acid, three [[centella-asiatica]] triterpenes
- [[cholesterol]], a barrier lipid
- [[adenosine]]
- hydrolyzed DNA, the ingredient closest to the brand's marketed "PDRN" claim (see [[polydeoxyribonucleotide-pdrn]])
- [[hyaluronic-acid]]
- acetyl tetrapeptide-11, a synthetic [[peptides|peptide]]
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant, last on the list
- [[dimethicone]], a silicone emollient

Base, solvent, and preservative system: water, butylene glycol and dipropylene glycol (solvents/humectants), glycerin, betaine, caprylic/capric triglyceride (an emollient), 1,2-hexanediol and ethylhexylglycerin (preservative boosters), ammonium acryloyldimethyltaurate/VP copolymer (thickener), polyglyceryl-6 caprylate and polyglyceryl-4 caprate (emulsifiers), propanediol, hydrogenated lecithin, ethanol, sodium methylstearoyl taurate (emulsifier), mannitol, chlorella protothecoides extract, dextrin, cocoa extract, and onion extract.[^1]

The page lists 32 ingredients total and does not print concentrations for any of them.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"PDRN H.A.™ LIPOSOME 92.3%."** A proprietary complex name and percentage without a linked published trial, sample size, or methodology; independent leave-on PDRN evidence is limited to one industry-run trial of a different engineered variant (on [[polydeoxyribonucleotide-pdrn]]).[^1][^2]
- **"물광 플럼핑 효과" (glossy, plumping effect "felt from deep within the skin").** A sensory/appearance claim not tied to any cited measurement or trial on this product.[^1]
- **"스킨부스팅 특수관리 액티브" (skin-boosting, specialized clinical-grade active).** Positioning language; the product's individually evidenced actives (PDRN, centella triterpenes, niacinamide) each carry modest, mostly preliminary, undisclosed-concentration evidence, not a demonstrated "clinical-grade" boosting effect as a formula.[^1]
