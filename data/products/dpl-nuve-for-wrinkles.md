The dpl Nuve for Wrinkles is the 2015 follow-on to the [[dpl-nuve]], and the two are different devices wearing almost the same name. **Bottom line:** it widens the cleared indication from the skin around the eyes to the whole face and replaces the older unit's two wavelengths with four, but its own file is the one place where a dose figure for the original Nuve appears in print. Modality evidence is on [[home-led-light-therapy]]; the family tree is at [[dpl]].

> **Confusing this device with the dpl Nuve is easy and wrong.** The 2010 clearance is indicated for "Periorbital" skin at "625, 830nm" with 120 LEDs over 30 cm² on a regimen of 20 minutes every other day, switching heads. This one is indicated for "Entire Face" at four wavelengths with 60 LEDs over 30cm2 on a regimen of 3 minutes daily, 5 days per week for 8 weeks. Both sets of figures are printed side by side in this filing's own predicate chart.[^k141181]

## Summary

Cleared 2015-03-31 as K141181 under product code OHS, regulation 878.4810, Class II, with the classification name given as "Light Based Over-The-Counter Wrinkle Reduction Device" and the common name as "Red Light Therapy Device".[^k141181][^openfda] The indication reads "The Nüve for Wrinkles is an Over-the-Counter (OTC) device intended for the use in treating full-face wrinkles".[^k141181] The device is described as "an over-the-counter hand-held light emitting diode (LED) device that emits energy for use in dermatology for the treatment of wrinkles and fine lines", using "four types of LEDs : 605nm amber, 630nm red, 660nm red, and 880nm infrared", with treatment time "controlled by the user" and "no user settings or adjustments required".[^k141181] It ships as "the handheld unit containing the LED module , power supply, goggles, and travel case".[^k141181] No price or photograph is available here.

## The Rubric

**Full-face wrinkles and fine lines**
- **Effect size: minimal.** The cleared indication covers the whole face, which is a wider claim than the periorbital one its predecessor carries.[^k141181] A wider cleared indication is a regulatory fact rather than a stronger result, and LED sits at Level B for facial rejuvenation, behind home fractional nonablative lasers at Level A.[^grade]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** The filing reports no wrinkle outcome. Checked by string search of the full extracted text on 2026-11-09, it contains no occurrence of wrinkle score, subjects, endpoint, statistical or photograph, and the word clinical appears exactly once in the document, inside "nonclinical".[^k141181]

**What was actually measured on people**
- **Effect size: not applicable.** The only human testing described is that "A Usability Study was conducted with 38 participants", reporting that "100% of the participants were able to demonstrate the light sensitivity test" and "100% of the participants were able to use the device successfully".[^k141181] Those are handling outcomes. Nobody's skin was assessed.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable.**

**Dose, printed in two incompatible units**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a flaw in the record.** The predicate chart gives this device's Energy Level as "65 mW total", which is a total power. Three pages later the equivalence discussion says it "Has the similar output (i.e., 65 mW/cm2 ) as the predicate devices", which is an irradiance.[^k141181] The same number carries two incompatible units inside one document, so neither figure can be used, and a reader cannot tell how much light reaches a given patch of skin.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable.**

## The Evidence

**This file publishes the dose figure that its predecessor's own file withholds.** The fourth column of the predicate chart is the original "Dpl NUVE (LED Technologies, LLC)", K101382, and it lists that device at "625, 830nm" and an Energy Level of "55 mW /cm2", with 120 LEDs over 30 cm² and a regimen of 20 minutes every other day, switching heads.[^k141181] None of those numbers appear in K101382's own summary, which is recorded as a documented absence on [[dpl-nuve]]. So the firm was printing dose figures for a Nuve-named device in 2015, and the place to find the 2010 unit's irradiance is the successor's paperwork rather than its own.

**Two of its three predicates belong to other companies.** The file names "LightStim for Wrinkles (LED Intellectual)" at K120775, "Trinity Wrinkle Remover (Carol Cole Company)" at K120560, and "Dpl NUVE (LED Technologies, LLC)" at K101382.[^k141181] Only the third is the firm's own device. The self-predication pattern documented at [[dpl-faceware]] was therefore still not in place in 2015, which puts outside predicates at both ends of this company's first five years of wrinkle filings rather than only at the start.

**It does not repeat its predecessor's claim of clinical testing.** Its conclusion reads "The conclusions drawn from nonclinical tests demonstrate that the device is as safe, as effective, and performs as well as the legally marketed devices".[^k141181] The 2010 filing instead said its conclusions came from nonclinical and clinical tests, reported rather than quoted here because that sentence spans a page break in the original, while describing no clinical study anywhere, as set out on [[dpl-nuve]]. Read together, the 2010 document is the outlier in this family rather than the beginning of a habit, because this 2015 summary and the 2019 ones all state plainly that the testing was nonclinical.

**The product name is printed five ways in one document.** The clearance line reads "NUVE FOR WRINKLES", the proprietary name "NÜVE for Wrinkles", the indication "The Nüve for Wrinkles", the predicate chart a hyphenated "dpl-" form of the same name, and the components paragraph "The Nuve for Wrinkles system components" with no diaeresis.[^k141181] That matters for anyone trying to match this clearance to a box on a shelf, and it is the same identification problem the degraded 2010 scan creates on [[dpl-nuve]].

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The cleared indication is cosmetic wrinkle reduction, so nothing here speaks to a medical skin condition. Goggles are listed as a shipped component and the emitter includes an 880nm infrared diode, which makes the manufacturer's eye-protection instructions worth following rather than skipping. The wider context is on [[anti-aging]].

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand copy is quoted on this page. Every claim examined above is one LED Technologies made to the FDA in its 2014 submission and 2015 clearance. The brand's site was readable on 2026-11-09, where earlier attempts recorded on [[dpl-nuve]] met a Cloudflare challenge, and it carries no product under this name. Its current range is the dpl Flex Pad, dpl IIa, dpl Joint Wrap and dpl Oral Care, plus the reVive Clinical Lux, Glo Lux, Lux Spot, Poof and Sonique collections.[^brand] So there is still no consumer copy about this device to compare against what the firm told the regulator, but the reason is that the product is gone rather than that the site could not be read. For the gap this site keeps finding between filings and advertising in this same line, see [[revive-light-therapy-lux-clinical]].
