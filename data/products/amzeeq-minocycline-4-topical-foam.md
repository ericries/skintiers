Amzeeq is the brand name for prescription minocycline 4% topical foam, made by Journey Medical Corporation and FDA-approved for "the topical treatment of inflammatory lesions of non-nodular moderate to severe acne vulgaris in adults and pediatric patients 9 years of age and older."[^dailymed]

> A real, first-of-its-kind topical minocycline that mostly delivers on its low-systemic-exposure promise, but the absolute benefit over an inert vehicle foam is modest and inconsistent across its own three registration trials. Inflammatory lesion counts fell significantly more than vehicle in all three studies, but the co-primary clear-or-almost-clear endpoint only clearly separated from vehicle in one of the three. It is a legitimate option for someone who specifically wants minocycline's effect without oral minocycline's systemic risk profile, not a dramatic upgrade over other topical acne antibiotics. The general minocycline story, oral versus topical, is owned by [[minocycline-topical-oral]]; the broader condition is [[acne]].

## Summary

Amzeeq is generic-name [[minocycline-topical-oral|minocycline]] formulated as a 4% topical foam, the first FDA-approved topical minocycline product, originally developed by Foamix Pharmaceuticals as FMX101 before Journey Medical Corporation took over marketing.[^fda-approval] Each gram contains 40 mg of micronized minocycline, equivalent to 43 mg of minocycline hydrochloride, dispensed as a yellow suspension foam from a pressurized aerosol can.[^dailymed] The label's exact indication, quoted above, has an age floor of 9 years and applies only to non-nodular disease; it explicitly states "this formulation of minocycline has not been evaluated in the treatment of infections."[^dailymed] Dosing is once daily, applied at least an hour before bedtime, with a cherry-sized amount rubbed into acne-affected areas of the face and, if present, the neck, shoulders, arms, back, or chest.[^dailymed] It is for topical use only, not oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use, and it is prescription-only.[^dailymed] The general mechanism and how the topical route compares to oral minocycline's systemic risk profile is owned by [[minocycline-topical-oral]]; the broader acne landscape is owned by [[acne]].

The product-specific case rests on three identically designed vehicle-controlled registration trials, described in full under [The Evidence](#the-evidence). Across the three studies, mean inflammatory lesion counts fell by 14.0, 13.7, and 16.4 with Amzeeq versus 11.2, 10.5, and 12.7 with vehicle, differences that were statistically significant in all three.[^dailymed] The other co-primary endpoint, IGA treatment success (an Investigator Global Assessment of clear or almost clear with at least a 2-grade improvement), was reached by 8.1%, 15.8%, and 30.8% of Amzeeq patients versus 4.8%, 8.4%, and 19.6% on vehicle; the first trial's gap was not statistically significant, with a 95% confidence interval spanning zero (-1.5%, 8.2%).[^dailymed] That is a real but modest and inconsistent effect over an inert vehicle, not a treatment most patients reach full clearance on.

Systemic exposure was low: a pharmacokinetic substudy found a mean peak plasma concentration of 1.3 ng/mL after 21 days of daily use, with no evidence of accumulation.[^dailymed] The most common adverse reaction was headache, reported in 3% of Amzeeq-treated subjects versus 2% on vehicle.[^dailymed] This is a prescription drug; see a prescriber for use and dosing, and see [[acne]] for the broader condition context.

## The Evidence

The general case for minocycline as a tetracycline-class antibiotic, and how this topical route compares to oral minocycline's documented systemic risks, is covered on [[minocycline-topical-oral]]; this section covers only what the Amzeeq label itself reports for this product.

Three multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled 12-week trials (Study 1, NCT02815267; Study 2, NCT02815280; and Study 3, NCT03271021) assessed a combined 2,418 subjects age 9 and older with moderate to severe acne vulgaris, defined at baseline as 20 to 50 inflammatory lesions, 25 to 100 non-inflammatory lesions, and an IGA score of 3 ("moderate") or 4 ("severe").[^dailymed] Subjects applied Amzeeq or vehicle once daily for 12 weeks with no other acne medication permitted. Forty-two subjects (2%) were age 9 to 11, 1,139 (47%) were 12 to 17, and 1,237 (51%) were 18 or older.[^dailymed]

The co-primary endpoints were the absolute change from baseline in inflammatory lesion count and IGA treatment success (clear or almost clear, at least a 2-grade improvement) at Week 12. Results, as reported in the label's Table 3:

| | Study 1 (N=307 vs 159) | Study 2 (N=312 vs 152) | Study 3 (N=738 vs 750) |
|---|---|---|---|
| IGA treatment success, Amzeeq vs vehicle[^dailymed] | 8.1% vs 4.8% | 15.8% vs 8.4% | 30.8% vs 19.6% |
| Difference from vehicle (95% CI) | 3.3% (-1.5%, 8.2%) | 7.4% (0%, 13.7%) | 11.2% (6.6%, 15.8%) |
| Inflammatory lesion count, mean absolute change | -14.0 vs -11.2 | -13.7 vs -10.5 | -16.4 vs -12.7 |
| Difference from vehicle (95% CI) | -2.8 (-4.9, -0.7) | -3.2 (-5.6, -0.9) | -3.7 (-4.8, -2.5) |

Every trial's inflammatory-lesion-count difference from vehicle excluded zero, a statistically significant reduction. IGA treatment success only did the same in Study 3; Study 1's confidence interval crossed zero and Study 2's touched it at the lower bound, meaning the clear-or-almost-clear endpoint did not reliably separate Amzeeq from an inert vehicle foam across its own trial base.[^dailymed]

> **Bottom line.** Amzeeq beat vehicle on lesion-count reduction in all three trials, a real effect, but its other co-primary endpoint, reaching a clear-or-almost-clear rating, only clearly beat vehicle in one of the three, and even there most treated patients did not reach that threshold.[^dailymed]

A pharmacokinetic study in 30 adults applying about 4 grams daily to the face, neck, upper chest, upper back, shoulders, and upper arms for 21 days found a mean peak plasma concentration (Cmax) of 1.3 +/- 0.6 ng/mL and AUC0-24h of 23.0 +/- 10.8 ng*h/mL at Day 21, with steady state reached by Day 6 and no evidence of systemic accumulation.[^dailymed] A separate pediatric pharmacokinetic study in subjects age 10 to under 17 found 2.4-fold and 2.7-fold higher Cmax and AUC0-24h than the adult population.[^dailymed] Full context on how this compares to oral minocycline's systemic exposure and adverse-effect profile is on [[minocycline-topical-oral]], which this page does not re-derive.

## What's In It

The active and inactive ingredients as declared on the FDA-approved prescribing information.[^dailymed]

Active:

- [[minocycline-topical-oral|Minocycline]] 4% (40 mg micronized minocycline per gram, equivalent to 43 mg minocycline hydrochloride), in a yellow suspension foam.[^dailymed]

Inactive ingredients: soybean oil, coconut oil, light mineral oil, cyclomethicone, cetostearyl alcohol, stearic acid, myristyl alcohol, hydrogenated castor oil, white wax (beeswax), stearyl alcohol, and docosanol. The foam is dispensed from an aluminum can pressurized with a butane, isobutane, and propane propellant.[^dailymed]

The label states the mechanism of action for treating acne "is unknown," and that "this formulation of minocycline has not been evaluated in the treatment of infections."[^dailymed] It is contraindicated in anyone with hypersensitivity to any tetracycline-class drug or to any ingredient in the formula, and the propellant is flammable, so the label instructs patients to avoid fire, flame, and smoking during and immediately after application.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand marketing page was reviewed for this product; the claims below come from the FDA label's own characterization rather than promotional copy.

- **"The first FDA-approved topical minocycline for acne."** Accurate as an approval-history fact.[^fda-approval] It does not follow that it clears most patients; treatment-success rates in its own trials topped out at 30.8%, and only one of three trials clearly beat vehicle on that endpoint.[^dailymed]
- **Framing topical minocycline as "safer" than oral minocycline because of low systemic exposure.** Supported specifically by Amzeeq's own pharmacokinetic data (mean Cmax 1.3 ng/mL, no accumulation over 21 days), but oral minocycline's documented systemic risks are a different drug and route; see [[minocycline-topical-oral]] for that separate profile.[^dailymed]
