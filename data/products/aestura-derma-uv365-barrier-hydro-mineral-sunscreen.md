AESTURA Derma UV365 Barrier Hydro Mineral Sunscreen is a Korean facial sunscreen from [[aestura]] built on a single mineral filter, nano zinc oxide, at a brand-declared SPF 50+, PA++++, sold in 40 mL and 25 mL sizes and marketed for dry and sensitive skin.[^1]

> A single-filter mineral sunscreen with no disclosed zinc oxide percentage, layered with Ceramide NP and hyaluronic acid for barrier and hydration claims. Broad-spectrum UV filtering has strong trial-backed evidence for slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], but that evidence rests on tested filter concentrations and independently verified SPF/PA ratings; here, both the SPF 50+ and PA++++ numbers and the zinc oxide load are brand-declared, on the international site and matched by an independent retailer, with no FDA label or lab test found.[^1][^2] The brand's own barrier and moisture figures, quoted in full under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims), are unpublished, brand-run testing.[^1]

## Summary

Derma UV365 Barrier Hydro Mineral Sunscreen is Aestura's mineral facial sunscreen, positioned for the same dry, sensitive, barrier-compromised skin the brand's ATOBARRIER365 ceramide moisturizers target.[^1] Its sole declared UV filter is zinc oxide (nano), with no titanium dioxide and no organic (chemical) filters on the ingredient list, at a brand-stated SPF 50+, PA++++ rating.[^1][^2] The general case for mineral, broad-spectrum UV filtering, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; [[zinc-oxide]] specifically is a photostable filter with genuine long-wave UVA reach.

Unlike a US drug-facts sunscreen such as [[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50]], which discloses titanium dioxide and zinc oxide percentages on an FDA label, Aestura's page states only "Zinc Oxide (Nano)" with no percentage, and no independently published SPF or PA test for this product was located.[^1] The formula also carries Ceramide NP and several hyaluronate forms, positioned by the brand as barrier and moisture ingredients rather than UV actives; the general evidence is modest for [[ceramides]] and notable for [[hyaluronic-acid]] on their own pages, and both sit in the back half of a 26-ingredient list, after the filter and a silicone-heavy base.[^1]

The brand's own clinical figures for barrier improvement and moisture after a single use, quoted verbatim under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims), are brand-run testing without a linked published trial, sample size, or control arm; they and the rest of the label's language are weighed there.[^1] An independent Korean-beauty retailer, Kim's Basket, lists the 40 mL size at $30.99, discounted from $40.99, and reproduces the same ingredient list verbatim, corroborating the formula (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^2]

## The Evidence

Broad-spectrum sunscreen use has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing skin-cancer risk, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that case, and how the SPF (UVB) and PA/UVA ratings differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent, published test of this specific product's SPF or PA rating was located; the grades above rest on the general case for mineral filtering and on what the brand itself discloses, not a verified lab measurement of this formula.

Zinc oxide is a photostable mineral filter with real broad-spectrum reach into long-wave UVA, detailed on [[zinc-oxide]], and it is this product's only named UV filter.[^1] What is missing, on both Aestura's own site and the independent retailer listing that otherwise matches the ingredient list, is a stated concentration. Concentration disclosure is a real, available norm this product lacks: a US drug-facts sunscreen such as [[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50]] discloses zinc oxide (and titanium dioxide) percentages on an FDA drug label, and a mineral-only EU-rated sunscreen such as [[avene-mineral-ultra-light-spf-50]] discloses titanium dioxide 4% and zinc oxide 6%; this Korean product, sold outside both systems, does not carry either kind of disclosure.[^1][^2] The SPF 50+ and PA++++ ratings are brand-declared on the product packaging and site; the box also carries a "DERMA UV EYE COMFORT" badge claiming it was "MADE EYE DIRECT TESTED," another brand claim without an independent source.[^2]

The barrier and hydration ingredients, Ceramide NP and several hyaluronate forms, follow the same pattern set out on [[ceramides]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]]: real, modest-to-notable general evidence that ceramides support measured barrier function and that hyaluronic acid holds meaningful surface water, short of a proven edge for this specific formula or its marketed percentages, since none are published.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** No independent test of this product's SPF, PA rating, or zinc oxide concentration was found; its UV-protection grades rest on zinc oxide's general category evidence and the brand's own undisclosed-concentration claim. Its barrier and moisture ingredients have real but modest general evidence, and the brand's specific 71% and 316% figures, quoted under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims), are unpublished, brand-run testing.[^1]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer and independently matched, ingredient-for-ingredient, by an independent retailer.[^1][^2] The filter and other functional actives are linked to their own pages; the rest is the silicone/emollient base and preservative system.

Actives:

- [[zinc-oxide]] (Nano), the sole UV filter, concentration not disclosed
- Ceramide NP, a barrier lipid ([[ceramides]]), placed after the base and emollients
- Sodium Hyaluronate, Sodium Acetylated Hyaluronate, and Hydrolyzed Hyaluronic Acid, three forms of [[hyaluronic-acid]]
- [[allantoin]], a soothing agent
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, cyclohexasiloxane, propanediol, butyloctyl salicylate, propylheptyl caprylate, isododecane, polyglyceryl-3 polydimethylsiloxyethyl dimethicone, caprylyl methicone, disiloxane, disteardimonium hectorite, magnesium sulfate, triethoxycaprylylsilane, 1,2-hexanediol (preservative booster), polymethylsilsesquioxane, polyglyceryl-2 dipolyhydroxystearate, lauryl polyglyceryl-3 polydimethylsiloxyethyl dimethicone, caprylyl glycol and glyceryl caprylate (preservative boosters), and ethylhexylglycerin (preservative booster).[^1][^2]

The ingredient list is 26 ingredients total. Aestura states the product is free of animal-derived ingredients, talc, polyacrylamides, added fragrance, imidazolidinyl urea, triethanolamine, PEG compounds, and tar colorants.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"71% skin barrier improvement after 1 use" and "316% Increases skin moisture after 1 use."** Brand-run testing described on Aestura's own product page without a linked published trial, sample size, control arm, or peer review.[^1] The independent evidence for ceramides and hyaluronic acid, set out on [[ceramides]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]], supports a real but more modest barrier and hydration effect, not these specific percentages.
- **"DERMA UV EYE COMFORT," badged "MADE EYE DIRECT TESTED."** A brand-stated tolerability claim on the product packaging, with no published methodology or independent source found.[^2]
- **"Non-sticky" and "without leaving a white cast."** Retailer-repeated brand positioning; mineral, zinc-oxide-only formulas are generally more prone to a visible cast than chemical filters, and finish depends on skin tone and how the product is applied.[^2]
- **SPF 50+, PA++++, and the underlying zinc oxide concentration.** All brand-declared, with no FDA drug label, independent lab test, or third-party certification located for this specific product.[^1][^2]
