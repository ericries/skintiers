The Lux Lip Care is a $99 red-light device for the mouth area, and it is the first product in this line whose function matches something the maker has actually had cleared. **Bottom line:** the lip-line claim sits in roughly defensible territory, the fuller-lips-and-better-colour claim has no cleared indication anywhere in the firm's record behind it, and the file that matches this device finally publishes the dose figure every other page in this family withholds. Modality evidence is on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

> **The dose numbers exist. They are just not on the brand's pages.** The firm's perioral clearance prints an "Energy Level" row: 67.7 mW/cm² over a 24 cm² treatment area, against 62.07, 70.16 and 61.59 mW/cm² for three dpl predecessors, all four at 605, 630, 660 and 880nm for three minutes.[^k172662] So this company does measure and report irradiance to the FDA. It is absent from this product page, absent from the Lux pages at [[revive-light-therapy-lux-clinical]] and [[revive-light-therapy-lux-panel]], and absent from the comparison charts on [[dpl-spotlite]] and [[dpl-faceware]], which list only "LED" where this one gives a number.

## Summary

Priced at $99.00 on 2026-10-21.[^brand] The brand describes "Red, deep red, amber, and infrared light" with a "Primary wavelength range: 605-880 nm" and an "Automatic 3-minute treatment cycle", calling it an "FDA-cleared OTC Class II medical device" with "No prescription required".[^brand] Those four wavelengths and that three-minute cycle match the firm's cleared perioral device exactly.[^k172662]

## The Rubric

**Softening lip lines and creases**
- **Effect size: minimal.** The brand claims it "Helps soften the appearance of smile lines, vertical lip lines, and creases".[^brand] Red and infrared LED for facial rejuvenation reached Level B in the 2026 review, behind home fractional nonablative lasers at Level A.[^grade] Lip lines are facial lines, so this claim is at least pointed at something the modality has been studied for.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** The grade belongs to a modality, from a review naming no model. The matching clearance rests on substantial equivalence to three of the firm's own earlier devices, not on a clinical study of this one.[^k172662]

**Fuller-looking lips and enhanced lip colour**
- **Effect size: minimal, and graded on absence of evidence.** The brand claims it "Supports fuller-looking lips and enhanced lip color".[^brand] Nothing in the firm's 15 FDA records covers lip fullness or lip colour, and nothing consulted here measured either.[^openfda][^k172662] No one has shown the device does it, which is a different statement from showing that it does not.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** There is no study to weigh, on this device or the modality, for either outcome.

**Dose, which is knowable here for once**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a record finding.** 67.7 mW/cm² over 24 cm² is a real specification, and it lets a reader compare this format against the firm's larger panels for the first time.[^k172662] What it cannot do is confirm the retail unit runs at that output, because the clearance and the product carry different names.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable.** A specification is not an outcome.

## The Evidence

**What the perioral clearance actually covers.** K172662 is cleared as an "Over-the-Counter (OTC) device intended for the treatment fine lines and wrinkles, and increase in circulation within the perioral region" (the missing word is the file's own).[^k172662] Perioral means around the mouth, which is skin. The lips themselves are a different tissue, and lip fullness and lip colour appear in neither the indication nor the classification name, which reads "Light Based Over-The-Counter Wrinkle Reduction device".[^k172662] An increase in circulation *is* cleared, and a reader could try to build a bridge from that to lip colour; the brand does not make that argument and this page will not make it for them.

**The correspondence is close, and it is still not identity.** The cleared perioral device uses "four types of LEDs: 605nm amber, 630nm red, 660nm red, and 880nm infrared", runs three minutes, and ships with a USB charging cord.[^k172662] The retail Lux Lip Care lists the same four colours, the same wavelength span, the same three-minute cycle, and a USB charging cord.[^brand] No clearance among the firm's 15 is named Lux.[^openfda] So this page reports the match and declines to assert that the two are the same unit, exactly as [[revive-light-therapy-lux-spot]] and [[revive-light-therapy-lux-clinical]] do.

**Three of its predicates are the dpl wrinkle devices.** The perioral file names "K141181 - dpl® Nuve for wrinkles", "K171390 - dpl® II Panel" and "K171386 - dpl® SpectraLite", all the firm's own.[^k172662] That is the same self-predication pattern documented at [[dpl-faceware]], and none of the three has a page here yet.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The cleared indication for the matching device is fine lines and circulation around the mouth; nothing here treats a lip condition. Persistent cracking, splitting or a non-healing sore at the lip needs a clinician rather than a light, and general lip care is at [[lip-care]].

## Common Marketing Claims

Quarantined from the graded assessment above. All are the brand's own wording, cached 2026-10-21.[^brand]

- "Helps soften the appearance of smile lines, vertical lip lines, and creases". Graded above; the closest thing to a claim the modality has been tested for.
- "Supports fuller-looking lips and enhanced lip color". The claim with nothing cleared behind it.
- "Supports collagen and elastin production" and "Supports healthier-looking lips". Mechanism and appearance language rather than measured outcomes, the same pair used across this line.
- "Clinically tested, multi-wavelength red light therapy". As on the sibling pages, the testing attaches to the therapy, not to this device.
