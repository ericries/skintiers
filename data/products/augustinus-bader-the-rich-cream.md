The Rich Cream is Augustinus Bader's dense, emollient-forward face moisturizer, built on a squalane, plant-oil, and shea-butter base with the brand's proprietary TFC8 complex and two named peptides, marketed for deep hydration and visible anti-aging.[^1]

> Augustinus Bader markets The Rich Cream around TFC8, a proprietary blend of vitamins, lipids, and peptides the brand does not fully disclose, backed by a brand-run, unpublished 30-participant 4-week study rather than an independent peer-reviewed trial.[^1] Strip TFC8 out and the rest of the ingredient list is a conventional rich-cream base: squalane, evening primrose, argan and avocado oils, shea butter, glycerin, panthenol, and sodium hyaluronate, actives whose general hydration and barrier evidence is set out on [[squalane]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]]. Two peptides are named on the label, oligopeptide-177 and palmitoyl tripeptide-8, whose class-level evidence, modest and mostly preliminary, is covered on [[peptides]].

## Summary

The Rich Cream is a thick, occlusive face moisturizer whose ingredient list leads with water, coco-caprylate/caprate, sunflower seed oil, and squalane, followed by a run of plant oils (argan, avocado, evening primrose) and shea butter, with glycerin, panthenol, and sodium hyaluronate for humectant support.[^1] It sells for approximately $99.00 for a 50 ml jar on the brand's own site (a 100 ml size is also referenced there without its own listed price).[^1] Augustinus Bader positions the cream around TFC8 ("Trigger Factor Complex"), described on the brand's own site as "a proprietary blend of high-grade vitamins, lipids, and high-performing, proprietary peptides," without disclosing the individual ingredients or their concentrations.[^1]

Two named peptides appear further down the ingredient list, oligopeptide-177 and palmitoyl tripeptide-8, alongside alanyl glutamine and arginine. Whether topical peptides in general change the appearance of skin, and how thin that evidence is by peptide class, is set out on [[peptides]]; nothing published isolates either of these two peptides specifically. The emollient and humectant ingredients that make up the bulk of the formula, squalane and sodium hyaluronate chief among them, have their own general evidence reviewed on [[squalane]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]].

No independent, peer-reviewed trial of this product exists. The brand cites its own 4-week, 30-participant study behind headline numbers like a 37% reduction in forehead wrinkles and a 92% improvement in firmness; that study is unpublished, has no disclosed control arm, and is weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1]

## The Evidence

The case for this cream's actives is a category-level one, not a product-specific one. Squalane is a well-tolerated, skin-identical emollient with a favorable safety review and no controlled trial isolating its own effect on human skin, as covered on [[squalane]].[^1] Sodium hyaluronate (a salt form of [[hyaluronic acid|hyaluronic-acid]]) and panthenol are common humectants included at undisclosed concentrations here; their general hydration evidence is reviewed on their own pages and is not specific to this formula. The brand's product page also states that transepidermal water loss was "reduced by 33%," a barrier-function figure that would bear directly on this grade if independently confirmed; it comes from the same unpublished, non-peer-reviewed 30-participant study as the other clinical-claim figures below and carries the same discount.[^1]

TFC8 is the ingredient the brand's marketing rests on most heavily, and it is the one this page can say the least about: the product page describes it only as "a proprietary blend of high-grade vitamins, lipids, and high-performing, proprietary peptides," without naming components or amounts, so no independent evaluation of TFC8 itself is possible from public information.[^1] The two peptides that are named on the INCI, oligopeptide-177 and palmitoyl tripeptide-8, fall under the general peptide framing on [[peptides]]: a modest, mostly preliminary anti-aging signal that varies by peptide class, well below prescription retinoids.

The brand's own clinical claims, a 37% reduction in forehead wrinkles, a 54% reduction in crow's feet, a 92% improvement in firmness, and a 2.45x improvement in hydration, come from a single 4-week study of 30 participants described on the product page, with no link to a published trial, no stated control arm, and no peer review.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** No independent trial exists for this specific cream. Its hydration and barrier grade rests on the general, modest evidence for its emollient and humectant ingredients; its anti-aging grade rests on an undisclosed proprietary complex and two peptides with thin, mostly preliminary category evidence, backed only by the brand's own unpublished study.[^1]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] Ingredients with a distinct functional role are linked to their own pages; TFC8 is a marketing name for a mixture spread across several of the individual listed ingredients below rather than a single INCI entry.

Leading ingredients (by list position, roughly concentration order):

- Water, coco-caprylate/caprate, sunflower seed oil, [[squalane]], glycerin, argan (Argania Spinosa) kernel oil, ethyl oleate, avocado oil, polyglyceryl-4 oleate, and magnesium sulfate open the list
- Evening primrose oil, panthenol, polyglyceryl-6 oleate, zinc PCA, polyhydroxystearic acid, and butylene glycol (a humectant comparable to glycerin and panthenol) follow, then shea (Butyrospermum Parkii) butter
- Sodium hyaluronate (a salt of [[hyaluronic acid|hyaluronic-acid]]), hydrolyzed rice protein, and tocopherol (vitamin E) appear further down
- The two named peptides, oligopeptide-177 and palmitoyl tripeptide-8, along with alanyl glutamine, arginine, and phenylalanine, are near the end of the list, positions that typically indicate low concentrations[^1]

Preservative and texture system: potassium sorbate and sodium benzoate (preservatives), xanthan gum (thickener), citric acid (pH), camellia sinensis (green tea) leaf extract, hydrogenated lecithin, tocopheryl acetate, sisymbrium irio seed oil, sodium chloride, dextran, and maltodextrin.[^1]

The label states the product is fragrance-free, vegan, and non-comedogenic.[^1] No published percentage is given for TFC8, sodium hyaluronate, or either peptide.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by independent, published evidence:

- **"Visibly smooths deep-set wrinkles" and the 37% forehead / 54% crow's-feet wrinkle-reduction figures.** From the brand's own 4-week, 30-participant, brand-run study, described on the product page without a link to a published trial, a stated control arm, or peer review.[^1]
- **"Enhances firmness and elasticity" and the 92% firmness improvement figure.** Same unpublished, brand-run study.[^1]
- **TFC8 as a named, evidence-backed technology "backed by over 35 years of research."** The brand does not disclose TFC8's individual components or concentrations, so its claimed research base cannot be independently checked from public information.[^1]
- **"Smooths texture and evens skin tone, visibly reducing age spots."** Positioning language on the product page; no pigmentation-specific active (such as [[niacinamide]] or [[azelaic-acid]]) is disclosed in the ingredient list, and no independent trial of this product measures pigmentation.[^1]
- **"94% noticed dark spots, age spots and imperfections are visibly reduced."** A self-reported consumer-perception survey figure on the product page, distinct from the 30-participant clinical study above; no methodology, sample size, or independence is disclosed, so it is treated as an unverified brand claim, not a measured endpoint.[^1]
- **"Award-winning."** The brand's marketing description opens by calling the cream "this award-winning, deeply hydrating cream" without naming the award or the awarding body anywhere on the cited page; no independent source for a specific award was located, so this is flagged here as an unverified brand claim rather than named or endorsed.[^1]
