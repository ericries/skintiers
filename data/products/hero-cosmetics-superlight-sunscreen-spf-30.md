The Hero Cosmetics Superlight Sunscreen SPF 30 (sold as "Force Shield Superlight") is a 100% mineral facial sunscreen whose only UV filter is zinc oxide at 17.53%, marketed as "The SPF for acne-prone skin."[^fda] Hero's own store lists the 50 ml tube at $19.99.[^hero]

> A 100% mineral (zinc oxide 17.53%), fragrance-free, broad-spectrum SPF 30 face sunscreen, and broad-spectrum protection is the best-evidenced way to lower photoaging and sun-related skin-cancer risk. The formula is fragrance-free and uses no chemical UV filters, the ingredients sometimes associated with stinging or breakouts. Its honest limits are two: SPF 30 is a moderate sunburn rating, below the SPF 50+ many dermatologists prefer, and mineral filters give more modest UVA coverage than modern EU and Korean filters, though the fairly high 17.53% zinc load helps. The SPF and UVA filter science is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^hero]

## Summary

Hero markets this as a sunscreen "for acne-prone skin" with "a smooth, skin-like finish that won't clog pores," describing it as "Broad spectrum UVA/UVB," "Reef safe," "100% mineral," "Fragrance-free," "Silicone-free," and "Clinically tested & non-irritating."[^hero] The FDA drug label lists zinc oxide 17.53% as the sole active, and incidecoder's decoded list confirms that single UV active in a lightweight emollient base with no added fragrance.[^fda] The general evidence for why broad-spectrum sunscreen matters, and the trade-offs of mineral versus organic filters, lives on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; this page covers what is specific to this product. It ships as a 50 ml (1.7 fl oz) tube for $19.99 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-30).[^hero]

## The Evidence

There is no published clinical trial of this specific product; its protection rests on its FDA sunscreen rating and its single mineral filter. Its FDA drug label lists zinc oxide 17.53% as the active, a "Broad Spectrum SPF 30" rating, and the standard sunscreen uses: it "helps prevent sunburn" and "decreases the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging caused by the sun."[^fda] The general proof that broad-spectrum sunscreen reduces photoaging and skin-cancer risk is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] and [[sun-damage-photoaging]]. Zinc oxide is a photostable mineral filter that covers both UVB and UVA.

Two honest limits follow from the label and formula. First, its SPF 30 is solid but moderate UVB protection, a step below the higher-SPF products many dermatologists recommend as a first choice (the SPF math is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]). Second, the US "Broad Spectrum" mark is a pass or fail test rather than a graded UVA rating, and zinc oxide, while genuinely broad-spectrum, is less UVA-efficient gram for gram than the modern organic filters (Tinosorb, Mexoryl) standard in the EU, Korea, and Australia; its fairly high zinc load narrows that gap but does not close it. The "won't clog pores" and "for acne-prone skin" positioning is a fragrance-free, non-comedogenic-style pitch, not a treatment claim: this product does not treat [[acne]], it is sun protection to use alongside acne care.

> **Bottom line.** A fragrance-free, 100% mineral (zinc oxide 17.53%) broad-spectrum SPF 30 (50 ml, $19.99).[^hero][^fda] SPF 30 is solid but moderate UVB protection, and mineral UVA coverage is genuine but below modern organic filters; the zinc-only, fragrance-free base avoids the chemical filters and fragrance that can bother reactive skin. Use enough and reapply, the points that decide real-world protection, covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), as decoded by incidecoder, begins with the single active, Zinc Oxide (17.53%), followed by water, caprylic/capric triglyceride, C13-15 alkane, propanediol, C15-19 alkane, and bis-diglyceryl polyacyladipate-2 (the lightweight emollient base), then butyloctyl salicylate, ectoin, and a large panel of plant extracts and antioxidants including tocopherol (vitamin E) and bisabolol.

Notable: zinc oxide is the sole UV filter, so the "100% mineral" claim is accurate; there is no avobenzone, oxybenzone, octinoxate, or other organic filter. Butyloctyl salicylate, despite the "salicylate" name, is a skin-conditioning and SPF-boosting solvent here, not a sunscreen active and not the exfoliant [[salicylic-acid]]. No fragrance is listed, consistent with the brand's fragrance-free claim.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms attributed to the brand:

- **"The SPF for acne-prone skin" / "won't clog pores."**[^hero] A positioning claim based on the fragrance-free, silicone-free, lightweight base; it is not a comedogenicity test result reported here, and the product treats sun exposure, not [[acne]].
- **"Reef safe."**[^hero] Consistent with a zinc-oxide-only formula that omits oxybenzone and octinoxate, the filters named in reef-toxicity concerns; "reef safe" is a term without a standardized definition, as covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].
- **"Clinically tested & non-irritating."**[^hero] A brand claim; no clinical trial of this product is published or cited here.
- **"Broad spectrum UVA/UVB."**[^hero] Backed by the FDA Broad Spectrum designation on an SPF 30 drug and by zinc oxide's inherent UVA/UVB coverage; the UVA side is genuine but moderate, as set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].
