Medik8 Crystal Retinal 10 is a 30 ml (1 fl. oz.) leave-on night serum whose active is encapsulated retinaldehyde at 0.10%, the fourth rung of a six-strength Crystal Retinal ladder that runs from 0.01% to 0.24%, priced at $111.00 per a Dermstore cross-sell listing, the only price found for this strength specifically.[^medik8us][^dermstore]

> Retinaldehyde sits one enzymatic step from the receptor-active acid, closer than retinol's two steps, covered in full on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]. No published, peer-reviewed trial tests this exact serum; two of the cited category trials, Kwon 2018 and Deda 2026, do test a 0.1% retinaldehyde concentration, just not this product's specific formula. Medik8's own claims on its product page cite an unpublished, company-run 33-participant study attributed to the Crystal Retinal 6 strength, not this one, discussed under Common Marketing Claims below.[^medik8us] Like every over-the-counter retinoid it remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin]].

## Summary

Medik8 Crystal Retinal 10 is a 30 ml (1 fl. oz.) leave-on night serum from [[medik8]], a British skincare brand. Medik8's own US site lists the line at $87 for its "Recommended" Crystal Retinal 6 strength but does not display this strength's own price on that fetch, and no dedicated Crystal Retinal 10 retailer listing was found; a Dermstore cross-sell blurb on the Crystal Retinal 6 page prices Crystal Retinal 10 Serum 30ml at $111.00, the only figure located for this strength.[^medik8us][^dermstore] Its active is retinaldehyde (retinal), declared at 0.10% in the product name and independently confirmed on both Medik8's US product page and the cross-referenced UK product page's strength labels, encapsulated in the same "double-layered stabilization system" Medik8 uses across the line.[^medik8us][^dermstore]

What differs from the lower rungs is strength and price, not format. Medik8's own strength-finder names all six rungs and their marketed user level: Crystal Retinal 1 (0.01%, "for sensitive skin"), Crystal Retinal 3 (0.03%, "for new vitamin A users"), Crystal Retinal 6 (0.06%, "for regular vitamin A users"), this Crystal Retinal 10 (0.10%, "for advanced vitamin A users"), Crystal Retinal 20 (0.20%, "for experienced vitamin A users"), and Crystal Retinal 24 (0.24%, "for expert vitamin A users").[^medik8us] Dermstore's cross-sell listing on the Crystal Retinal 6 page prices Crystal Retinal 3 at $66.00 and this Crystal Retinal 10 at $111.00 alongside an $87.00 listing for Crystal Retinal 6, so cost rises with each step up the ladder.[^dermstore]

Retinaldehyde's category-level evidence, [set out in full on retinaldehyde and retinoids](#the-evidence), is the strongest among over-the-counter retinoid forms; two of the cited trials, Kwon 2018 and Deda 2026, test a 0.1% retinaldehyde concentration, but no published trial tests this exact serum. Like every over-the-counter retinoid it remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin]], which is retinoic acid itself.

Against comparators already covered: [[medik8-crystal-retinal-6]] is the same line's next rung down, at 0.06% and $87.00, the strength Medik8 markets to "regular" vitamin A users before this "advanced" rung; [[skinmedica-retinol-complex-1-0]] is a pricier ($96) retinol serum whose own percentage is undisclosed by its manufacturer; [[trader-joes-retinol-night-serum]] (about $10) and [[anua-nano-retinol-0-3-niacin-renewing-serum]] are both retinol serums declared at 0.3%, a form that needs an extra conversion step to reach retinoic acid; [[differin-adapalene-0-1-gel]] is an FDA-cleared over-the-counter retinoid with real acne trials, about $15; and [[tretinoin-0-025-cream]] is prescription retinoic acid, the benchmark every over-the-counter retinoid, including this one, remains milder than.

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinaldehyde improves aging skin, and how it compares with retinol and prescription retinoids, is covered on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]: in short, one vehicle-controlled trial plus two independent concentration-comparison trials converge on measurable improvement in fine wrinkles, roughness, and texture, the strongest over-the-counter retinoid evidence, ahead of retinol's thinner data, though not shown to match prescription [[tretinoin]]. No published trial tests this exact serum; of the two concentration-comparison trials just referenced, both Kwon and colleagues and Deda and colleagues do test a 0.1% retinaldehyde concentration, so the gap is this specific formula, not the strength.[^medik8us][^dermstore]

Medik8's own marketing claims on its Crystal Retinal product page rest on an unpublished, company-run study: "Proven via independent clinical study on 1 tube of Crystal Retinal 6. Tested on 33 participants over 12 weeks."[^medik8us] That study is attributed to the 0.06% strength, not the 0.10% strength reviewed here, is manufacturer-run and unpublished, and is not independently peer-reviewed evidence.

> **Bottom line.** The case for this strength rests on the same category-level retinaldehyde evidence as every other rung of the ladder, not on a trial of this specific serum. That category evidence does include a 0.1% concentration arm (Kwon 2018, Deda 2026), the strength named on this label, but no cited trial tested Medik8's own formula. Medik8's own clinical claim on its product page is attributed to a different strength (Crystal Retinal 6) and is testimonial, company-run evidence, not independent clinical data for this product.[^medik8us]

Among over-the-counter retinoids, retinaldehyde sits a step ahead of the retinol forms such as [[trader-joes-retinol-night-serum]] and [[anua-nano-retinol-0-3-niacin-renewing-serum]]; the FDA-cleared adapalene [[differin-adapalene-0-1-gel]] works by a different route, and it remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin-0-025-cream]] (retinoic acid itself).

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Retinal... more effective and 11x faster-working than retinol."** Medik8's own product page attributes this to a single reference (Siegenthaler 1990), also cited alongside two others on the sibling Dermstore listing for Crystal Retinal 6. Cosmetic chemists Victoria Fu and Gloria Lu (Chemist Confessions), reviewing the Medik8 retinal line generally, characterized the study behind this claim as "not actually testing" Medik8's own retinal or formula and as "an in vitro paper in cell culture," an early metabolism study rather than a test of a finished product; they noted they did not personally test the Medik8 line.[^chemistconfessions] The retinaldehyde evidence review, drawing on five separate published trials (not including Siegenthaler), already discounts this class of claim: the peer-reviewed data support that retinaldehyde ranks closer to active retinoic acid than retinol does, not a specific "times faster" multiple.[^medik8us]
- **"97% felt their skin had a more youthful appearance," a 20% reduction in wrinkles, a 20% reduction in dark spots, an 11% increase in firmness, and "100% felt Crystal Retinal was gentle."** Medik8's page attributes these to "an independent clinical study on 1 tube of Crystal Retinal 6," tested on 33 participants over 12 weeks, a study run on a different strength (0.06%) than the 0.10% reviewed here. It is manufacturer-run and unpublished, not an independently peer-reviewed trial, and the page does not disclose a control arm or statistical methodology.[^medik8us]
- **"Clinically-Proven Formula" / "Dermatologist Recommended" badges.** Manufacturer-applied claim badges on the product page, not tied to a disclosed, citable trial for this strength.[^medik8us]
