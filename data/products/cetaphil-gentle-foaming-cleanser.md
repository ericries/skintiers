## Summary

Cetaphil Gentle Foaming Cleanser is a self-foaming facial wash positioned for normal to combination and sensitive skin, distinct from the brand's older, cream-based [[cetaphil-gentle-skin-cleanser|Gentle Skin Cleanser]]. Cetaphil describes it as "a gentle, self-foaming facial cleanser that gently removes dirt, oil and makeup without drying to maintain skin's natural moisture balance,"[^1] and Target lists it as suitable for "combination, mature, sensitive, normal, dry, and acne-prone skin."[^2]

It is a health question whether that gentleness holds up: does a mild, soap-free foaming cleanser protect the skin barrier more than a harsher wash? No published trial tests this exact product, so the case here is a composition one rather than a clinical one. The formula is [built on cocamidopropyl betaine and coco-glucoside](#whats-in-it), mild amphoteric and nonionic surfactants, with no sulfate detergent and no true soap.[^1][^2] The better-evidenced routine for keeping skin hydrated after cleansing is leave-on moisturizing, set out on [[moisturizing]].

The formula also differs from its cream-cleanser sibling in what it carries: it has [glycerin, panthenol, and tocopheryl acetate (vitamin E)](#whats-in-it) but, unlike [[cetaphil-gentle-skin-cleanser|Cetaphil Gentle Skin Cleanser]], no niacinamide.[^1][^2] Glycerin is a well-evidenced humectant and panthenol has controlled-trial support for soothing and short-term barrier recovery when left on the skin; see [[glycerin]] and [[panthenol]]. Whether a rinse-off wash leaves enough of either behind to matter is not something a trial has tested for this product.

It is inexpensive. Target lists the 8 fl oz pump at $8.99, about $1.12 per fluid ounce ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^2] The manufacturer's clinical-sounding label claims are separated out under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## The Evidence

A cleanser's health-relevant task is to remove dirt, oil, and makeup with as little disruption to the skin barrier as possible; keeping skin moisturized after cleansing is one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines, set out on [[moisturizing]]. No published trial tests Cetaphil Gentle Foaming Cleanser itself. What is verifiable is its composition: a soap-free, self-foaming formula built on cocamidopropyl betaine and coco-glucoside rather than a sulfate detergent or true alkaline soap, with no added fragrance.[^1][^2]

## What's In It

The ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer and matched on an independent retailer listing.[^1][^2] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the cleansing surfactant, texture, and preservative system.

Humectants and skin-conditioning ingredients:

- [[glycerin]], a humectant and the fifth ingredient
- panthenol (provitamin B5), a humectant and skin-conditioning agent; see [[panthenol]]
- tocopheryl acetate (vitamin E), an ester of the antioxidant [[tocopherol]]
- betaine, a humectant and osmolyte

Cleansing, texture, and preservative system: water, cocamidopropyl betaine and coco-glucoside (mild amphoteric and nonionic surfactants), polysorbate 20 (an emulsifier/solubilizer), polyquaternium-10 (a conditioning polymer), sodium benzoate and phenoxyethanol (preservatives), and citric acid (a pH adjuster).[^1][^2]

The formula contains no niacinamide and no ceramides, unlike some other Cetaphil and CeraVe cleansers, and no sulfate detergent such as sodium lauryl or laureth sulfate.[^1][^2]
