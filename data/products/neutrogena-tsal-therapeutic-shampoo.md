Neutrogena T/Sal Therapeutic Shampoo is an over-the-counter medicated shampoo from Kenvue Brands LLC, labeled under the FDA's OTC monograph with salicylic acid 3% as its active ingredient; the Drug Facts purpose line reads "Anti-Dandruff, Anti-Seborrheic Dermatitis, Anti-Psoriasis" and the use statement is "Controls the symptoms of dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis."[^dailymed] Sold as "Scalp Build-Up Control," it is a descaler: it softens and lifts flakes and crust, it does not treat what causes them.

> A genuine keratolytic at a genuinely therapeutic strength, and one of the simplest formulas in the category, eleven inactive ingredients, no fragrance, no dye listed on the label.[^dailymed] But be clear about what 3% [[salicylic-acid]] does on a scalp: it dissolves the cell-to-cell cement holding scale together so flakes and build-up wash away. It does not kill the Malassezia yeast that drives [[dandruff]] and [[seborrheic-dermatitis]], the job of an antifungal like [[nizoral-anti-dandruff-shampoo-ketoconazole-1|ketoconazole]], [[zinc-pyrithione]], or [[selenium-sulfide]], and it does not modify [[psoriasis]] itself. That is why dermatology routines often pair or rotate it with an antifungal shampoo: T/Sal clears the scale so other treatments can reach the scalp. The general salicylic acid evidence is owned by [[salicylic-acid]] and is not re-derived here.

## Summary

This is an FDA-regulated Drug Facts shampoo with a single active, salicylic acid 3% (30 mg per mL), distributed by Kenvue Brands LLC and marketed under OTC Monograph M032, the monograph covering dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis drug products.[^dailymed] As a monograph drug it was never FDA-approved through a New Drug Application with product-specific trials; it complies with a standing monograph that permits salicylic acid at this concentration for these scalp conditions without a fresh efficacy dossier.

One labeling detail worth reading closely: the purpose line names three conditions ("Anti-Dandruff, Anti-Seborrheic Dermatitis, Anti-Psoriasis") and the carton lists "dandruff, psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis," but the Drug Facts use statement itself claims only that the product "Controls the symptoms of dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis."[^dailymed] Either way the claim is symptom control, scale, flaking, build-up, not clearance of psoriatic plaques and not eradication of the yeast behind seborrheic flaking. For scalp [[psoriasis]] specifically, a keratolytic like this is an adjunct that thins scale; disease control comes from the treatments graded on that condition page.

Directions call for wetting hair thoroughly, massaging a liberal amount into the scalp, leaving the lather on for several minutes, then rinsing and repeating, "for best results, use at least twice a week or as directed by a physician."[^dailymed] Warnings are the standard monograph set: external use only, ask a doctor before use if the condition covers a large area of the body, avoid contact with the eyes and rinse thoroughly if contact occurs, and stop use and ask a doctor if the condition worsens or does not improve after regular use as directed.[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

Salicylic acid's ingredient-level case as a keratolytic, dissolving the intercellular cement that holds corneocytes together so scale detaches, is set out on [[salicylic-acid]]; no clinical trial of this specific T/Sal formulation was found in the sources reviewed here, and as an OTC monograph drug none was required. The product's regulatory basis is that its active (salicylic acid 3%) matches the concentration and dosage form the monograph allows for these scalp conditions.[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: the health claim on this label, controlling the flaking and build-up symptoms of dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis, rests on salicylic acid's long-standing monograph status and the ingredient evidence on [[salicylic-acid]], not on a trial of this shampoo. Expect descaling, not cure: comparative studies of scalp treatments belong to the antifungals and, for psoriasis, to the prescription tier covered on the condition pages.

That is a plain anionic/amphoteric surfactant base with conditioning polymers and pH adjusters; no fragrance, essential oil, or colorant appears anywhere on the label's ingredient list.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Clears and helps prevent flakes and scalp build-up" and "starts working after just one use."**[^dailymed] Both appear on the FDA-labeled carton itself. The first is consistent with the keratolytic mechanism; the second is a speed claim the Drug Facts panel neither supports nor quantifies, though softening scale in a single wash is plausible for a leave-on-several-minutes 3% salicylic acid lather.
- **"This dermatologist recommended scalp treatment is made with 3% salicylic acid and clears and helps prevent flakes and scalp build-up."**[^brand] The brand's own product-page copy. "Dermatologist recommended brand" is a survey-style marketing formula about the Neutrogena brand, not a clinical claim about this bottle.
- **"Treat itchy, crusty scalp."**[^brand] The brand's framing; the Drug Facts use statement is narrower, symptom control of dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis, and itch relief per se is not among this label's stated uses.[^dailymed]
