Trader Joe's Vitamin C Serum is a $9.99, 1 fl oz antioxidant serum sold only in Trader Joe's stores, whose own front label states 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% alpha-tocopherol (vitamin E), and 0.5% ferulic acid, the same three concentrations the SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic photoprotection studies were run at, a match that TikTok and lifestyle coverage frame as an under-$10 dupe of the $185 SkinCeuticals serum.[^1][^2][^3]

> A dropper-bottle antioxidant serum sold in Trader Joe's stores for $9.99 (1 fl oz / 29 mL), a small fraction of the roughly $185 SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic list price. Its own label states 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% alpha-tocopherol, and 0.5% ferulic acid, the exact three concentrations the SkinCeuticals photoprotection studies used, a fuller disclosed match than the other budget vitamin C dupes carry. No clinical trial or independent stability test of this product has been published, so the case for it rests on that label match rather than product-specific data; reviewers who tested it note the base differs in some respects (added sunflower seed oil, a different solubilizer), and cosmetic chemists quoted in press coverage say the texture and base formulation are not identical to SkinCeuticals' own. Trader Joe's separately sells a longer-running "Nourish" serum built on a vitamin C ester rather than L-ascorbic acid, a different product from the one graded here. The antioxidant case for vitamin C generally sits on [[vitamin-c]] and [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]].[^1][^2][^4][^5]

## Summary

Trader Joe's Vitamin C Serum is a leave-on morning antioxidant serum aimed at the same job as SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic: sitting under sunscreen to help neutralize everyday UV and pollution damage, alongside a gradual brightening effect. The active is [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]], the pure and best-studied form of vitamin C, mapped with its derivatives on [[vitamin-c]]. What distinguishes this product is not the active itself but price and disclosure: its front label states 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% alpha-tocopherol, and 0.5% ferulic acid, the same three concentrations [the SkinCeuticals photoprotection studies were run at](#the-evidence), in a bottle that retails for $9.99.[^1][^2]

The label also reads "Dermatologist Formulated"; press coverage reports the serum "was also formulated for the retailer by dermatologists, according to Trader Joe's," without naming them.[^3] The full ingredient declaration is reproduced identically across an independent ingredient database and a blogger's photographs of the packaging.[^1][^4] No clinical trial or independent stability test of this exact product has been published, so [the case for it](#the-evidence) rests on that disclosed percentage match to the reference formula rather than product-specific data. Reviewers who tested it alongside SkinCeuticals note the base differs in some respects: this serum adds sunflower seed oil and swaps in a different solubilizer, and Parade reports that "cosmetic chemists and skincare professionals have noted that the texture and base formulation differ from SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic," even though the three headline actives and their percentages match.[^3]

Trader Joe's separately sells a different, longer-running serum under its "Nourish" line, the Nourish Antioxidant Facial Serum, built primarily on [[vitamin-c|tetrahexyldecyl ascorbate]], a vitamin C ester, alongside retinol, retinyl palmitate, and resveratrol, without ferulic acid or sodium hyaluronate; that is a separate product from the plain "Vitamin C Serum" graded here.[^5]

NBC's Today.com reports that a patent covering the SkinCeuticals combination expired in March 2025 and ties that to the recent wave of lower-cost versions of the formula, including this one.[^2] Available only in Trader Joe's stores (the chain does not sell online), the serum lists for $9.99 for a 1 fl oz (29 mL) bottle, about a nineteenth of the SkinCeuticals list price.[^1][^2][^6]

## The Evidence

Topical L-ascorbic acid is a well-studied antioxidant with characterized roles defending skin against oxidative UV damage and supporting collagen synthesis; the vitamin C forms and their evidence are mapped on [[vitamin-c]], and the reference form's profile is [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]]. The fixed-ratio combination of L-ascorbic acid, vitamin E, and ferulic acid this product is compared against was developed and clinically tested by the inventors of [[skinceuticals-c-e-ferulic|SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic]], whose page carries that evidence and its limits. No clinical trial of this product itself has been published, and no independent laboratory has run the kind of accelerated stability test that formulator-run blogs have published for [[timeless-20-vitamin-c-e-ferulic-serum|Timeless]] and [[maelove-the-glow-maker|Maelove]].

On composition, a blogger's photographs of the packaging transcribe the front label as 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% alpha-tocopherol, and 0.5% ferulic acid.[^1] The 15% L-ascorbic acid figure also appears in NBC's Today.com and Parade's coverage, and both name the vitamin E and ferulic acid without stating their percentages, so the full 15% / 1% / 0.5% breakdown rests on the single label transcription rather than on independent agreement across sources.[^2][^3] That is a fuller disclosed match to the studied SkinCeuticals concentrations than the other budget vitamin C dupes carry: Timeless discloses only its 20% L-ascorbic acid figure and not its vitamin E or ferulic acid percentages, and Maelove discloses only its 15% L-ascorbic acid figure, leaving the other two undisclosed.[^1] A matching label is a composition fact, not a substitute for a trial of this product. NBC's Today.com quotes dermatologist Dr. Shasa Hu of the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine describing L-ascorbic acid's general profile, well-evidenced but prone to oxidation and requiring a low, potentially irritating pH, without testing or endorsing this product specifically, and Parade reports that "cosmetic chemists and skincare professionals have noted that the texture and base formulation differ from SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic" even though the headline actives match.[^2][^3]

> **Bottom line.** The label states the same three active percentages the SkinCeuticals photoprotection studies used: 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% vitamin E, 0.5% ferulic acid. That is a disclosed formula match, not a trial of this product; no clinical or independent stability study of this serum has been published.[^1][^2][^3]

One tester's account is consistent with a genuinely acidic, meaningfully concentrated L-ascorbic acid formulation rather than a milder derivative: a Today.com reporter who used the serum for several days reported that her sensitive, rosacea-prone skin developed stinging and red blotches by the third day, an anecdotal reaction rather than a controlled finding.[^2]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list as declared on the product packaging, reproduced identically by an independent ingredient database.[^1][^4] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the solvent, humectant, and preservative system.

Antioxidant actives, at the percentages stated on the front label:

- [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]] 15%, pure vitamin C[^1]
- [[tocopherol|alpha-tocopherol]] 1%, vitamin E[^1]
- [[ferulic-acid]] 0.5%, a plant antioxidant that stabilizes vitamin C and vitamin E in the reference formula[^1]
- [[hyaluronic-acid|sodium hyaluronate]], a humectant[^4]

Base, solvent, and preservative system: water, ethoxydiglycol (a penetration solvent), propylene glycol and [[glycerin]] (humectants), polyglyceryl-10 caprylate (a solubilizer), triethanolamine (pH adjuster), phenoxyethanol (preservative), sunflower (Helianthus annuus) seed oil, panthenol, and citric acid.[^4]

This product's declared base overlaps heavily with the SkinCeuticals ingredient list carried on [[skinceuticals-c-e-ferulic]] (water, ethoxydiglycol, propylene glycol, triethanolamine, phenoxyethanol, panthenol, sodium hyaluronate); against that reference list it swaps in polyglyceryl-10 caprylate as a solubilizer and adds sunflower seed oil.[^4]

This is a separate product from Trader Joe's Nourish Antioxidant Facial Serum, a longer-running item in the retailer's "Nourish" line built on tetrahexyldecyl ascorbate (a vitamin C ester) alongside retinol, retinyl palmitate, resveratrol, and coenzyme Q10, without ferulic acid or sodium hyaluronate among its declared ingredients.[^5]

Label and marketing language not established by the graded evidence above:
