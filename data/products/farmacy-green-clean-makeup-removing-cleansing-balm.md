Farmacy Green Clean is a papaya-and-botanical cleansing balm that melts from a solid balm into an oil, used as the first step of a [[double-cleansing|double cleanse]]. Farmacy's own site lists the 100 mL jar (branded "Original Green Clean," the formula's current full-size listing) at $36.00.[^farmacy]

> The removal job here runs on the same emollient-ester-and-emulsifier mechanism as any cleansing balm, with the evidence set out on [[double-cleansing]]. Its distinguishing feature is a large citrus and essential-oil panel (lime, bergamot, orange peel, ginger, ylang-ylang), confirmed as fragrance by the label's own allergen markers (citral, limonene, linalool), a plausible sensitizer for reactive skin; the "Green Clean" papaya-and-botanical framing is a natural-positioning marketing angle, not a demonstrated treatment benefit.

## Summary

Farmacy markets this as an "award-winning" balm "clinically proven to gently remove over 99% [of] longwear foundation, waterproof mascara + SPF," built on a "farm-grown super greens blend" of moringa, holy basil, and arugula (with papaya extract described separately).[^farmacy] The formula opens with cleansing esters and PEG-type emulsifiers, standard for the oil-to-milk balm category, then a run of botanical extracts (echinacea, papaya, moringa) and a cluster of citrus and essential oils, ending in a short preservative system. It is listed at $36.00 for the 100 mL jar at Farmacy's own site ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-28).[^farmacy]

## The Evidence

The product-specific claim worth checking is removal, and it is directionally sound for the format: as set out on [[double-cleansing]], an oil-based cleanser removes water-resistant sunscreen and long-wear makeup that a foaming water-based cleanser leaves largely behind. That controlled evidence is a single small study and is a property of the oil-balm format generally, not of this brand; no independently published trial of this exact product is cited here.

The formula's point of difference is its fragrance load and its "green," botanical marketing. It carries lime oil, bergamot fruit oil, orange peel oil, ginger root oil, and ylang-ylang (cananga) flower oil, and the ingredient tail lists citral, limonene, and linalool, the standard labeling markers for fragrance allergens. Those oils function as this product's fragrance even though no single "Fragrance" or "Parfum" entry appears on the list. Citrus and essential oils are common sensitizers, which matters here mainly as leave-behind residue and cumulative fragrance load rather than photosensitivity, since the oils rinse off with the balm. The "Green Clean" name and papaya-fruit-extract placement lean on a "natural"/botanical positioning, illustrated by Farmacy's own marketing image describing "papaya extract to cleanse, sunflower oil to nourish, and antioxidant-rich super greens."[^farmacy] Papaya fruit extract sits early in the list, just behind the five-ingredient cleansing base, but nothing here demonstrates a leave-on exfoliation or treatment effect from it; the "cleanse" framing is marketing language for a rinse-off product, not a substantiated enzymatic-exfoliation claim.

> **Bottom line.** A workable oil-to-milk cleansing balm whose removal job is the same evidence-backed mechanism as any balm in the category. Its distinguishing features are a large citrus and essential-oil panel that functions as fragrance, a plausible irritant for reactive skin, and a "green"/papaya marketing angle not backed by a product-specific efficacy claim.
