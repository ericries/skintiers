CeraVe Skin Renewing Retinol Serum is a 1 oz leave-on facial serum containing encapsulated retinol, three ceramides, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, and chicory root extract, sold for $24.99 both direct from the manufacturer and on Target.[^1][^2]

> A CeraVe encapsulated-retinol serum built on the brand's familiar ceramide-and-niacinamide base, marketed for fine lines, wrinkles, and radiance rather than for post-acne marks, which distinguishes it from the sibling [[cerave-resurfacing-retinol-serum]].[^1][^2] Topical retinol's wrinkle and pigment benefits are well established at the category level, covered on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]], but CeraVe does not print a retinol percentage anywhere on the packaging or ingredient list, so there is no way to know whether this serum's dose matches the concentrations those category trials actually tested, [the honest limitation set out under The Evidence](#the-evidence).

## Summary

CeraVe Skin Renewing Retinol Serum is a 1 oz serum priced at $24.99 on both CeraVe's own site and Target.[^1][^2] The manufacturer markets it as a "gentle, yet effective daily retinol serum" that improves the appearance of "fine lines, wrinkles, skin texture and radiance," and Target's listing corroborates the same anti-aging positioning, titling the product "for Fine Lines and Wrinkles."[^1][^2] That is a different pitch from the sibling [[cerave-resurfacing-retinol-serum]], which markets around post-acne marks and pore appearance; the two formulas also differ, this one adding chicory root extract, shea butter, tocopherol (vitamin E), and hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid,[^1][^2] and omitting the dipotassium glycyrrhizate (licorice root) in the Resurfacing formula,[^3][^4] confirming they are distinct SKUs rather than a relabeling.

The labeled active is [[retinol]] itself, not a milder retinyl ester, and retinol is a well-studied over-the-counter precursor with vehicle-controlled trial evidence for improving fine wrinkles and pigment, [set out in full on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]](#the-evidence). What CeraVe does not do is disclose how much: the ingredient list names "retinol" with no accompanying percentage, and none appears anywhere on the manufacturer's product page, so the serum's actual dose relative to the concentrations tested in the category trials is unknown, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence).

Around the retinol, the formula carries CeraVe's familiar barrier base: three [[ceramides]] (NP, AP, EOP), the fatty molecules that make up part of the skin's own barrier, and [[niacinamide]], a form of vitamin B3 with its own modest evidence for barrier support. The formula also adds hydrolyzed [[hyaluronic-acid]], a humectant, chicory root extract, and shea butter, and is free of added fragrance, confirmed independently by both the manufacturer and Target's listing.[^1][^2]

The manufacturer's own directions call for applying the serum in "small dots" after cleansing, for PM-only use, "gradually" increasing to daily use as skin adapts; Target's listing describes application "once or twice daily, alone or under makeup."[^1][^2]

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinol improves photoaged skin, and how it compares with prescription retinoids, is covered on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]: in short, vehicle-controlled trials support gradual improvement in fine wrinkles and pigment. No published trial tests this exact serum, or its specific combination with ceramides, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, and chicory root extract.

> **Bottom line.** The retinol case for this serum rests on the category-level evidence for the ingredient, not on a trial of the product itself, and because CeraVe does not disclose a retinol percentage, there is no way to know how its dose compares to the concentrations those category trials tested.[^1][^2]

The marketed claim that "encapsulated retinol... stabilizes and delivers the retinol throughout the skin gradually over time, so it helps minimize irritation" is a manufacturer/Target delivery-system claim; neither source reports an independent trial of this serum's release profile or its clinical effect.[^1][^2]

For the ceramides, the closest controlled test is a manufacturer-sponsored randomized trial of a *different* brand's ceramide-dominant cream, which improved a barrier measure (transepidermal water loss) but did not reduce eczema severity more than a plain vehicle; that trial, its industry funding, and its limits are set out on [[ceramides]]. For the niacinamide, the barrier evidence (raised stratum-corneum lipids and lower water loss in dry skin) comes from a single manufacturer research group; that work and its limits are on [[niacinamide]]. Neither active's percentage is disclosed in this formula.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), declared identically on the manufacturer's own product page and Target's retailer listing.[^1][^2] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

Actives and support ingredients, in the order declared:

- Water, glycerin (humectant), caprylic/capric triglyceride (emollient), potassium cetyl phosphate (emulsifier), hydrogenated palm glycerides (emollient), polysorbate 20 and PEG-40 stearate (emulsifiers), cyclopentasiloxane (silicone emollient), hydroxyethylcellulose (thickener), potassium phosphate (pH buffer)
- Three [[ceramides]] (ceramide NP, AP, EOP), plus cholesterol and phytosphingosine later in the list, the formula's barrier lipids
- Carbomer, a thickener
- [[niacinamide]], mid-list, a form of vitamin B3
- Isoceteth-10, dimethicone/vinyl dimethicone crosspolymer, triethanolamine, cetearyl alcohol, and behentrimonium methosulfate, emulsifiers and stabilizers
- Cichorium intybus root extract (chicory root extract)
- Lecithin, an emulsifier
- [[retinol]], no percentage disclosed
- Silica, sodium lauroyl lactylate, texture agents
- Phenoxyethanol, a preservative
- Tocopherol (vitamin E), an antioxidant
- Hydrolyzed [[hyaluronic-acid]], a humectant
- Butyrospermum parkii butter (shea butter), an emollient

Base, texture, and preservative system: alcohol, hydroxyacetophenone (preservative), citric acid (pH adjuster), pentylene glycol (humectant/preservative booster), xanthan gum (thickener), and ethylhexylglycerin.[^1][^2]

The formula is free of added fragrance, confirmed independently on both the manufacturer's and Target's listings.[^1][^2]
