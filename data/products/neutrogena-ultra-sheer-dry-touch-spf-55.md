Neutrogena Ultra Sheer Dry-Touch Sunscreen Broad Spectrum SPF 55 is a lotion-format face and body sunscreen built entirely on organic (chemical) UV filters, marketed for a fast-absorbing, non-greasy "Dry-Touch" finish.[^1]

> A Broad Spectrum SPF 55 sunscreen using an all-chemical filter set: avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene. Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; SPF 55 is a high FDA-tested UVB rating. Its UVA coverage rests on avobenzone alone, genuine once photostabilized but older and less photostable than the modern filters (Tinosorb, Mexoryl) sold abroad. The formula's octisalate and octocrylene are both at their US permitted maximum concentrations, and it lists Fragrance among its inactive ingredients.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Ultra Sheer Dry-Touch is a longstanding sunscreen line from Neutrogena, a Kenvue-owned US drugstore skincare brand. This SPF 55 version is a lotion sunscreen positioned for the face and body, built around Neutrogena's "Dry-Touch" finish claim and its "Helioplex" filter-stabilization technology.[^1] Its core purpose is the same as any sunscreen's: broad-spectrum UV protection is the best-evidenced way to slow photoaging and lower the risk of sun-related skin cancer, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

What distinguishes it from a plainer sunscreen is the filter loading and the cosmetic finish rather than a novel health mechanism. Per its FDA Drug Facts label, the actives are avobenzone 3%, homosalate 10%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 10%, an all-organic (chemical) filter set rated Broad Spectrum SPF 55.[^1] Avobenzone, at the US maximum concentration, is the formula's only dedicated UVA filter; [[octisalate]] and [[octocrylene]] are each at their own US permitted maximums, and octocrylene also serves to slow avobenzone's breakdown in sunlight ([the filter roles are detailed in The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1] The declared inactive ingredients include Fragrance, so this is not a fragrance-free formula.[^1] Target lists it at $9.89 for 3 fl oz ($3.30/fl oz) as of 2026-08-07, and corroborates the same four actives and percentages, water resistance up to 80 minutes, and non-comedogenic, dermatologist-tested marketing.[^2]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific product was located; the product-specific facts are its filter composition and FDA label rating.

The FDA Drug Facts label (revised November 2025) lists the actives as avobenzone 3%, homosalate 10%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 10%, at SPF 55, Broad Spectrum.[^1] SPF is a UVB (sunburn) number; the SPF 55 rating is FDA-tested for UVB strength and does not by itself certify UVA (photoaging) protection, a separate axis described on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

Three of the four filters, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene, are UVB absorbers; UVA coverage rests on avobenzone alone.[^1] [[avobenzone]] at its 3% US maximum provides genuine UVA coverage once photostabilized, but it degrades under sunlight unless a co-filter holds it stable. [[octocrylene]], present here at its own 10% US permitted maximum, serves that stabilizing role in addition to contributing its own UVB and short-wave UVA-II absorption. [[octisalate]] adds a weak, secondary UVB contribution at its 5% US maximum, and [[homosalate]] carries a UVB-only load below its 15% US ceiling. Measured against the global field, this remains the older US filter set: avobenzone is a weaker, less photostable UVA filter than the modern organic filters (bemotrizinol/Tinosorb S, bisoctrizole/Tinosorb M, the Mexoryl filters) standard in the EU, Korea, and Australia, a comparison detailed on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

> **Bottom line.** The FDA-tested SPF 55 Broad Spectrum rating reflects a genuine US chemical filter set at relatively high loading: homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene carry the UVB side, with octisalate and octocrylene at their US permitted maximums, and avobenzone, at its own permitted maximum and stabilized by octocrylene, carries the UVA side.[^1] That UVA coverage is real but older and less photostable than the modern-filter products sold outside the US.

The label directs to "apply liberally 15 minutes before sun exposure" and to reapply "after 80 minutes of swimming or sweating; immediately after towel drying," and at least every 2 hours.[^1] Target's listing separately states the product is "water resistant for up to 80 minutes," consistent with the label's reapplication direction.[^1][^2] How much protection any SPF delivers in practice depends on applying enough product, a point covered generally on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## What's In It

The active filters and inactive ingredients as declared on the FDA Drug Facts label.[^1] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

UV filters (Drug Facts actives):

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Dry-Touch" and "Helioplex."** Target's listing describes "Dry-Touch technology" absorbing fast for "an ultra-light feel and invisible finish," and "Helioplex technology" creating "a powerful shield of protection."[^2] These are the brand's own names for its finish and filter-stabilization approach, as relayed by Target's product listing; the graded UV-filter protection above rests on the FDA label's active percentages, not on these trade names.
- **PABA-free, oxybenzone-free, octinoxate-free.** Composition points about which UV filters are absent, not efficacy claims; the label's active filters are avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene.[^1][^2]
- **Non-comedogenic, dermatologist-tested.** Target's listing states these; they are testing claims, not part of the FDA-graded UV-filter protection above.[^2]
