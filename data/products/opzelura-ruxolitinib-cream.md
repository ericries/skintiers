OPZELURA is the brand name for ruxolitinib 1.5% cream, a prescription topical Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor made by Incyte, applied twice daily to treat two unrelated conditions: mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis (ages 2 and up) and nonsegmental vitiligo (ages 12 and up).[^dailymed] It carries the boxed warning shared by the entire JAK inhibitor class.[^dailymed]

> A genuinely effective drug for two conditions with limited topical options, backed by four randomized, vehicle-controlled trials, but not a cosmetic-aisle product. The label carries a boxed warning for serious infections, increased all-cause mortality, malignancy including lymphoma, major adverse cardiovascular events, and thrombosis, based on data from oral JAK inhibitors and reinforced by cases seen in OPZELURA's own trial program.[^dailymed] For atopic dermatitis it is meant for short-term, non-continuous use on limited body surface area, not as an everyday moisturizer substitute; for vitiligo, repigmentation is gradual and the drug does not work for everyone. This is a prescription-only decision to make with a dermatologist, not a self-directed purchase.

## Summary

Ruxolitinib inhibits JAK1 and JAK2, enzymes that relay signals from a range of inflammatory cytokines into the cell nucleus; by blocking that signaling, the cream dampens the inflammatory pathways involved in both atopic dermatitis and the autoimmune-driven melanocyte loss of vitiligo, though the label states the drug's pharmacodynamics and the relevance of inhibiting specific JAK enzymes to its therapeutic effect are not currently known.[^dailymed] Both approved uses are health claims, not cosmetic ones: this is a regulated drug for two diagnosed skin diseases, prescribed and monitored by a physician, not a wellness or anti-aging product.

For atopic dermatitis, OPZELURA is indicated for the topical short-term and non-continuous chronic treatment of mild-to-moderate disease in non-immunocompromised patients 2 years of age and older whose disease is not adequately controlled by topical prescription therapies, or when those therapies are not advisable, applied to up to 20% of body surface area.[^dailymed] For nonsegmental vitiligo, it is indicated for patients 12 and older, applied to up to 10% of body surface area.[^dailymed] The label explicitly advises against combining it with therapeutic biologics, other JAK inhibitors, or potent immunosuppressants such as azathioprine or cyclosporine.[^dailymed]

The drug's defining feature is its boxed warning, the FDA's strongest label warning, covering serious infections (including tuberculosis and opportunistic infections), higher all-cause mortality including sudden cardiovascular death, malignancies including lymphoma, major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), and thrombosis.[^dailymed] Those risks are drawn primarily from a large postmarketing safety study of an oral JAK inhibitor in rheumatoid arthritis patients 50 and older with a cardiovascular risk factor, not from OPZELURA's own topical trials, but the label applies the warning to the whole JAK inhibitor class, OPZELURA included, and the topical trials themselves reported serious lower respiratory tract infections.[^dailymed] See [What's In It](#whats-in-it) and [The Evidence](#the-evidence) below for the trial data and full warning detail.

## The Evidence

**Atopic dermatitis (TRuE-AD1 and TRuE-AD2).** Two identically designed, double-blind, randomized, vehicle-controlled trials enrolled 1,249 adult and pediatric patients 12 and older with mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis (IGA score 2-3, 3-20% affected body surface area), treated twice daily for 8 weeks.[^dailymed] The primary endpoint, IGA treatment success (a score of 0/clear or 1/almost clear with at least a 2-grade improvement from baseline) at Week 8, was reached by 53.8% of OPZELURA patients versus 15.1% on vehicle in TRuE-AD1 (treatment difference 38.9 percentage points, 95% CI 30.3-47.4), and 51.3% versus 7.6% in TRuE-AD2 (treatment difference 44.1 percentage points, 95% CI 36.2-52.0).[^dailymed] Among patients with baseline itch of at least 4 on a 0-10 scale, a 4-point or greater reduction in itch was reached by 52.2% and 50.7% of OPZELURA patients versus 15.4% and 16.3% on vehicle across the two trials.[^dailymed] A third, separately reported trial (TRuE-AD3) tested the same regimen in 330 children 2 to 11 years old and found IGA treatment success in 56.5% of OPZELURA patients versus 10.8% on vehicle (treatment difference 45.7 percentage points).[^dailymed] A pooled analysis of the two adolescent/adult trials also reported significant itch and skin-pain reductions within about 12 hours of the first application, and improvements in quality-of-life scores (DLQI, POEM) by Week 2 that were sustained through 52 weeks of as-needed use.[^simpson]

> Bottom line: against an inert vehicle cream, roughly half of patients reached clear or almost-clear skin within 8 weeks, versus under a fifth (and in one trial under a tenth) on vehicle.

**Nonsegmental vitiligo (TRuE-V1 and TRuE-V2).** Two identically designed, double-blind, randomized, vehicle-controlled trials enrolled 674 patients 12 and older with nonsegmental vitiligo (mean 14.8 years since diagnosis, mean 7.4% affected total body surface area), randomized 2:1 to OPZELURA or vehicle twice daily for 24 weeks, followed by 28 more weeks of open-label OPZELURA for all patients.[^dailymed] The primary endpoint, at least 75% improvement in facial repigmentation (F-VASI75) at Week 24, was reached by 29.9% of OPZELURA patients in both trials, versus 7.5% on vehicle in TRuE-V1 (treatment difference 22.5 points, 95% CI 14.2-30.8) and 12.9% on vehicle in TRuE-V2 (treatment difference 16.9 points, 95% CI 7.8-26.0).[^dailymed] A deeper response, F-VASI90 (at least 90% facial repigmentation), was reached by 15.5% and 15.4% of OPZELURA patients versus 2.2% and 1.9% on vehicle.[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: at 24 weeks, roughly 3 in 10 OPZELURA patients had at least 75% of their facial pigment back, versus under 1 in 10 on vehicle; the label notes phototherapy was not permitted during the trials, and repigmentation continued to build with longer treatment.[^dailymed]

The atopic dermatitis label restricts use to short-term, non-continuous treatment, not indefinite daily application, and both trial programs excluded immunocompromised patients and those with active or serious infections, populations the boxed warning is specifically written for.[^dailymed]

## What's In It

- **Active: ruxolitinib phosphate**, equivalent to 15 mg ruxolitinib per gram of cream (1.5%).[^dailymed]
- **Base and preservative system:** cetyl alcohol, dimethicone 350, edetate disodium, glyceryl stearate SE, light mineral oil, medium chain triglycerides, methylparaben, phenoxyethanol, phosphoric acid, polyethylene glycol 200, polysorbate 20, propylene glycol, propylparaben, stearyl alcohol, purified water, white petrolatum, and xanthan gum, as declared on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

**Boxed warning, quoted verbatim:** "WARNING: SERIOUS INFECTIONS, MORTALITY, MALIGNANCY, MAJOR ADVERSE CARDIOVASCULAR EVENTS (MACE), AND THROMBOSIS ... Serious infections leading to hospitalization or death, including tuberculosis and bacterial, invasive fungal, viral, and other opportunistic infections, have occurred in patients receiving Janus kinase inhibitors for inflammatory conditions. Higher rate of all-cause mortality, including sudden cardiovascular death have been observed in patients treated with Janus kinase inhibitors for inflammatory conditions. Lymphoma and other malignancies have been observed in patients treated with Janus kinase inhibitors for inflammatory conditions. Higher rate of MACE (including cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, and stroke) has been observed in patients treated with Janus kinase inhibitors for inflammatory conditions. Thrombosis, including deep venous thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, and arterial thrombosis, some fatal, have occurred in patients treated with Janus kinase inhibitors for inflammatory conditions."[^dailymed]

The label lists no contraindications and states the mortality, malignancy, MACE, and thrombosis signals were observed primarily in a large postmarketing safety study of an *oral* JAK inhibitor in rheumatoid arthritis patients 50 and older with a cardiovascular risk factor, extended to the topical drug as a class-wide warning; it separately notes serious lower respiratory tract infections were reported in OPZELURA's own clinical development program, and that no cases of active tuberculosis occurred in OPZELURA trials.[^dailymed] The most common adverse reactions reported at 1% or more of OPZELURA-treated patients and more often than on vehicle in the adolescent/adult atopic dermatitis trials were nasopharyngitis (3%), and, at 1% each, bronchitis, ear infection, increased eosinophil count, urticaria, diarrhea, folliculitis, tonsillitis, and rhinorrhea.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Not a steroid."** OPZELURA's own patient-facing materials emphasize the drug is a non-steroidal option, and the label confirms it is a JAK inhibitor, not a topical corticosteroid, so it avoids the skin-thinning risk of long-term steroid use.[^dailymed] That distinction is accurate, but it does not mean the drug is risk-free, it trades a topical-steroid risk profile for the boxed-warning risk profile above.
- **Framed as a fast-acting itch reliever.** A pooled patient-reported-outcomes analysis of the two adult/adolescent atopic dermatitis trials found significant itch and skin-pain reductions within about 12 hours of the first application.[^simpson] That finding comes from an Incyte-funded secondary analysis of the registration trials rather than an independent replication, though the underlying data are the same randomized trial population.[^simpson]

## Where to Buy

OPZELURA is available only by prescription; there is no over-the-counter purchase path. It is dispensed in 60 g and 100 g tubes, with dosing limits set by the label (no more than one 60 g tube per week or one 100 g tube per 2 weeks for atopic dermatitis and vitiligo in patients 12 and older; slower limits for children 2 to 11).[^dailymed] Pricing was not verified against a primary source for this entry and is left out rather than estimated; check with a pharmacy or Incyte's own patient-support program for current cost and copay-assistance options.
