Selsun Blue Itchy Dry Scalp is an over-the-counter medicated antidandruff shampoo built around pyrithione zinc 1%, the same active class as Head & Shoulders, and the gentler-base sibling to Selsun Blue's [[selsun-blue-selenium-sulfide-shampoo|selenium sulfide shampoo]].[^1]

> An OTC drug, not a cosmetic: the FDA Drug Facts label lists the active as "pyrithione zinc 1%" with the purpose "antidandruff."[^1] Pyrithione zinc is a long-established antidandruff active that works against the Malassezia yeasts behind dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis, and for seborrheic dermatitis it is used the same way as ketoconazole and selenium sulfide, covered on [[seborrheic-dermatitis]]. Its base here is notably milder than the selenium sulfide variant: no sulfate surfactants, plus added niacinamide and aloe.

## Summary

This is the pyrithione zinc member of the Selsun Blue line. It is regulated as an OTC drug: the Drug Facts label states the active as "pyrithione zinc 1%," the purpose as "antidandruff," and the use as fighting and helping prevent the recurrence of flaking and itching.[^1]

Pyrithione zinc is an antifungal and antimicrobial active against the Malassezia yeasts associated with dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis. Its evidence sits at the ingredient-class level: for seborrheic dermatitis it is one of the topical antifungals used the same way as [[ketoconazole]] and selenium sulfide, a comparison set out on [[seborrheic-dermatitis]]. No trial of this specific product was located, and unlike the [[selsun-blue-selenium-sulfide-shampoo|selenium sulfide sibling]], there is no on-site head-to-head study for the pyrithione zinc active, so the dandruff grade rests on its FDA monograph status plus that class evidence.

The off-label skin angle is weaker for this active than for selenium sulfide. Fungal acne is driven by the same Malassezia yeast, so pyrithione zinc is plausible as an off-label wash, but the [[fungal-acne]] evidence base centers on azole antifungals and selenium sulfide rather than pyrithione zinc, so that use is extrapolation from mechanism, not a documented result.

## The Evidence

Two health uses, graded above:

**Dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis (the labeled use).** This is what the product is approved and labeled for. Pyrithione zinc is an FDA OTC-monograph antidandruff active, and for seborrheic dermatitis it is grouped with ketoconazole and selenium sulfide as a first-line topical antifungal, with the class evidence and its limits set out on [[seborrheic-dermatitis]]. The caveat specific to this product is that no trial tested this exact formulation, and pyrithione zinc has less recent head-to-head data on this site than the selenium sulfide sibling, whose grade is supported by a direct randomized comparison against ketoconazole.

**Fungal acne (off-label, mechanistic).** Malassezia folliculitis responds to antifungals, and pyrithione zinc is antimicrobial against that yeast, so it is sometimes used off-label as a short-contact wash. But the documented [[fungal-acne]] options are azole antifungals and selenium sulfide; pyrithione zinc use rests on the shared mechanism rather than on trials for this active, and it is not on the product's label.

> **Bottom line.** An established pyrithione zinc antidandruff shampoo whose labeled dandruff and seborrheic-dermatitis use is well supported at the ingredient level, in a milder sulfate-free base than its selenium sulfide sibling.[^1] It has thinner product-specific and off-label evidence than that sibling, so it is a reasonable first-line antidandruff option rather than a documented fungal-acne treatment.

## What's In It

As an OTC drug the label separates the active from everything else. Active ingredient: "pyrithione zinc 1%."[^1]

Inactive ingredients, verbatim from the Drug Facts label: "water, cocamidopropyl betaine, glycol distearate, sodium methyl cocoyl taurate, glyceryl caprylate/caprate, sodium lauryl glucose carboxylate, lauryl glucoside, aloe barbadensis leaf juice, niacinamide, tocopheryl acetate, citric acid, polyacrylate crosspolymer-6, fragrance, hydroxyacetophenone, disodium EDTA, potassium hydroxide, blue 1."[^1]

The list is alphabetical, as OTC Drug Facts inactive lists are, so it does not convey concentration order. Unlike the selenium sulfide variant's ammonium lauryl and laureth sulfate base, this one uses milder non-sulfate surfactants (cocamidopropyl betaine, glucosides, and a cocoyl taurate) and adds [[niacinamide]], aloe, and vitamin E; it still contains added fragrance and a blue dye.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Fights and helps prevent recurrence of flaking and itching."** This matches the labeled antidandruff indication and is supported at the active level, not by a trial of this specific product.[^1]
- **Off-label "fungal acne" use.** Recommended online on mechanistic grounds, but not on the product's OTC label and not documented for pyrithione zinc on [[fungal-acne]], whose evidence centers on other antifungals.
