Avar-e Green is a prescription, green-tinted combination cream from Mission Pharmacal Company of San Antonio, TX, labeled on DailyMed with the same indication wording as its sister sulfacetamide/sulfur products: "This product is indicated for use in the topical control of acne vulgaris, acne rosacea and seborrheic dermatitis."[^dailymed] Unlike the NDA-approved Klaron, and like its sister Plexion, Avar-e Green's DailyMed listing carries the SPL marketing category "unapproved drug other" (code C73627), meaning it has not gone through FDA review as safe and effective and this labeling itself has not been FDA-approved.[^dailymed] The distinguishing feature is the tint: the cream is colored with chromium oxide green, marketed on the carton as a "color corrective emollient cream" intended to visually camouflage rosacea redness while the actives are applied.[^dailymed] Mission Pharmacal also sells Avar as a wash-off Cleanser, an untinted Avar-e Emollient Cream, a lower-sulfur Avar LS, and an Avar Gel; those are separate SKUs with their own DailyMed listings and strengths, not interchangeable with this tinted cream.[^dailymed]

> Avar-e Green shares both actives and its thin trial record with [[plexion-sodium-sulfacetamide-10-sulfur-5-cleanser|Plexion]], and its sulfacetamide with [[klaron-sodium-sulfacetamide-10-lotion|Klaron]]. Like Plexion, its DailyMed listing is filed as an unapproved drug (marketing category "unapproved drug other"), unlike the NDA-approved Klaron. The mechanism and the fuller ingredient-level evidence, including why this combination now sits behind metronidazole, azelaic acid, and ivermectin for rosacea, live on [[sodium-sulfacetamide]] and [[sulfur]] and are not re-derived here. See [[rosacea]] and [[acne]] for the broader conditions.

## Summary

Each gram of Avar-e Green contains 100 mg of sodium sulfacetamide and 50 mg of colloidal sulfur (true label strengths of 10% and 5%), "in a vehicle consisting of: benzyl alcohol, cetyl alcohol, chromium oxide green, disodium EDTA, fragrance, glyceryl stearate (and) PEG-100 stearate, magnesium aluminum silicate, PEG-150 distearate, phenoxyethanol, polyethylene glycol 400, purified water, sodium lauryl sulfate, sodium thiosulfate, stearyl alcohol and xanthan gum."[^dailymed] Directions per the label: "Cleanse affected areas. Apply a thin layer to the affected areas with light massaging, 1 to 3 times daily or as directed by a physician."[^dailymed] It is listed on DailyMed under NDC 0178-0460-02 (a 2 oz. / 57 g bottle), though the label's structured packaging data lists other configurations as well.[^dailymed]

This is a health claim, not a cosmetic one, for the sulfacetamide/sulfur actives: sodium sulfacetamide is a sulfonamide antibacterial acting, per the label, through "competitive antagonis[m] to para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA), an essential component for bacterial growth," while "the exact mode of action of sulfur in the treatment of acne is unknown, but it has been reported that it inhibits the growth of Propionibacterium acnes and the formation of free fatty acids."[^dailymed] The green tint itself is a cosmetic color-correction feature, not part of that mechanism. Fuller context on the actives is on [[sodium-sulfacetamide]] and [[sulfur]] and not re-derived here.

It is contraindicated in known or suspected hypersensitivity to any ingredient and in patients with kidney disease, and it carries warnings about rare but severe sulfonamide reactions, detailed under Warnings below.[^dailymed] It is prescription-only; see a prescriber for use and dosing, and see [[acne]] and [[rosacea]] for the broader conditions.

## The Evidence

The label reports no trial data for Avar-e Green itself, only pharmacology and the indication statement.[^dailymed] The one real randomized trial behind the combination is a 12-week, investigator-blinded study in which a different branded sodium sulfacetamide 10%/sulfur 5% cream with sunscreens (Rosac Cream) reduced inflammatory rosacea lesions more than metronidazole 0.75% cream (80% vs 72%, P=.04), with better tolerability in the metronidazole arm.[^torok] That trial did not test Avar-e Green and had no vehicle arm, so it establishes the combination as roughly metronidazole-class for rosacea rather than proving anything about this specific tinted product. Acne vulgaris and seborrheic dermatitis rest on older clinical tradition rather than a modern controlled trial; the fuller account, including a 2015 Cochrane review that found no pooled efficacy data for sodium sulfacetamide/sulfur among its main rosacea results, is on [[sodium-sulfacetamide]] and [[sulfur]] and not repeated here.

> **Bottom line.** Avar-e Green carries the same label indication as its sister sulfacetamide/sulfur products, but only rosacea has a randomized trial behind the combination, and that trial tested a different branded cream (Rosac Cream) against an active comparator with no vehicle arm.[^dailymed][^torok] Its own DailyMed listing is also filed as an unapproved drug, not an FDA-approved one. Treat it as an older, second-line, cosmetically-tinted option, not a proven leader for any of the three labeled uses.

## Warnings

Avar-e Green "is contraindicated in persons with known or suspected hypersensitivity to any of the ingredients of the product," and it "is not to be used by patients with kidney disease."[^dailymed] It carries the same sulfonamide class warning as its sister products: "Sulfonamides are known to cause Stevens-Johnson syndrome in hypersensitive individuals... Cases of drug-induced systemic lupus erythematosus from topical sulfacetamide also have been reported. In one of these cases, there was a fatal outcome."[^dailymed]

The label also notes that sodium sulfacetamide "is incompatible with silver preparations," a caution not carried on the Plexion or Klaron labels.[^dailymed] It warns that nonsusceptible organisms, including fungi, may proliferate with use; that reddening and scaling of the epidermis can occur; and that the product may darken on storage or discolor white fabric, though neither effect impairs safety or efficacy.[^dailymed] Pregnancy is listed as Category C, and safety under age 12 has not been established.[^dailymed] For external use only, not for ophthalmic use, avoid contact with eyes and mucous membranes, and keep out of the reach of children.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Color corrective" tinted cream for rosacea redness.** The chromium oxide green tint is real and distinguishes this SKU from plain Avar-e; it can visually offset erythema the way any green-tinted cosmetic corrector can, but that is a cosmetic camouflage effect layered on top of the drug, not evidence that the sulfacetamide/sulfur actives themselves reduce redness faster or better than in the untinted version.[^dailymed]
- **"Treats acne, rosacea, and seborrheic dermatitis in one product."** The label indication does span all three, but it sits on an unapproved-drug listing, and the controlled-trial support behind it is a single comparator trial in rosacea using a different branded cream (Rosac Cream), plus historical usage for acne and seborrheic dermatitis.[^dailymed][^torok]
