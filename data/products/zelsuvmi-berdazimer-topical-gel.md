ZELSUVMI is berdazimer topical gel 10.3%, FDA-approved under NDA 217424 by [[lnhc-pelthos-therapeutics|LNHC]] and the molluscum drug you mix and apply yourself.[^openfda][^spl] **Bottom line:** the convenience is real and the efficacy is thin. Its own label says "Efficacy was demonstrated in Trials 1 and 2", and reading the table behind that sentence shows one trial with a clearly positive primary result, a second whose confidence interval included zero, and a third that missed.[^spl] How molluscum treatments rank against each other is on [[molluscum-contagiosum]].

> **Three trials, one unambiguous primary result.** Complete clearance at Week 12 was 32.4% against 19.7% on vehicle in Trial 1, a difference of 12.8% (95% CI 7.1% to 18.6%); 30.0% against 20.3% in Trial 2, a difference of 9.2% (95% CI -0.04% to 18.4%); and "In Trial 3, the complete clearance rates at Week 12 were 26% versus 22% for ZELSUVMI and vehicle, respectively, with 95% confidence interval (-5%, 14%)."[^spl] Two of those three intervals cross zero.

## Summary

The product is a two-tube kit, and the mixing is the patient's job. The label directs you to "Dispense equal amounts from Tube A and Tube B per the dosing guide", mix, and "Apply once daily to each MC lesion for up to 12 weeks", with the warning "Do not premix or store mixed ZELSUVMI."[^spl] It is stored refrigerated until dispensed, kept at room temperature afterwards, and the label says "Discard 60 days after removal from refrigeration" and that the "Product contains alcohol and should be kept away from open flame."[^spl] Like cantharidin, its mechanism is unexplained: the label states "ZELSUVMI is a nitric oxide releasing agent. The mechanism of action for the treatment of molluscum contagiosum is unknown."[^spl] No price is quoted here; it is a prescription product.

## The Rubric

**Complete clearance at Week 12, the approved endpoint**
Graded minimal on mixed evidence. The three trials enrolled 891, 355 and 352 subjects, almost all of them children, with an average of 20.2 lesions at baseline and a range of 3 to 70.[^spl] Those are large, properly randomized, vehicle-controlled trials, so the problem is not trial quality. It is that only Trial 1 produced a primary result whose confidence interval excluded zero, and the drug's absolute clearance rate ran between 26% and 32.4% across the three trials against a vehicle rate between 19.7% and 22%.

**Clearing lesions by Week 8, the key secondary endpoint**
Graded minimal on solid evidence, and this is the more consistent finding. Week 8 clearance ran 19.6% against 11.6% in Trial 1 and 13.9% against 5.9% in Trial 2, differences of 7.5% and 7.8% with intervals of 3.0% to 12.0% and 1.8% to 13.8%.[^spl] Both clear zero. The oddity worth naming is that the secondary endpoint replicated where the primary one did not, which is the reverse of the usual pattern and is a reason to treat the Week 12 figures as the ceiling rather than the floor.

## The Evidence

**The vehicle arm is doing a lot of the work.** Around a fifth of children on the mixed placebo gel were completely clear at Week 12 in all three trials, 19.7%, 20.3% and 22%.[^spl] Molluscum resolves on its own, so a trial of any molluscum treatment is a race against spontaneous clearance, and the margin this drug wins by is roughly ten percentage points at best.

**Side effects are mostly stinging rather than blistering.** The commonest reactions at 1% or more are application site reactions: "pain (such as burning or stinging sensations, 18.7%), erythema (11.7%), pruritus (5.7%), exfoliation (5.0%), dermatitis (4.9%), swelling (3.5%), erosion (1.6%), discoloration (1.5%), vesicles (1.5%), irritation (1.2%), and infection (1.1%)."[^spl] Across the pooled safety population of 916 treated subjects, 1.7% had a severe treatment-emergent adverse event against 0.6% on vehicle.[^spl] That is a much gentler profile than the 97% local-reaction rate on [[ycanth-cantharidin-topical-solution]], which is the trade this product is offering.

**It is a daily chore for up to twelve weeks.** Cantharidin is applied by a clinician roughly every three weeks for up to four visits; this is mixed and applied by a parent every day for up to 84 days.[^spl] Neither label reports adherence, so nothing here says how many families complete the course. For a condition that often clears by itself within a year, the burden of the regimen is a legitimate part of the decision rather than a footnote to it.

**Approval age reaches lower.** The indication covers "adults and pediatric patients 1 year of age and older", against 2 years and older for cantharidin, though only 3% of trial subjects were under 2.[^spl] That 3% is a small base for a claim about the youngest patients.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. It is a prescription drug, it is for topical use only and not for ophthalmic, oral or intravaginal use, and the label tells patients to stop and seek medical attention if application site reactions last more than 24 hours.[^spl] Molluscum near the eye needs specialist assessment.

## Common Marketing Claims

**"ZELSUVMI is the FIRST and ONLY FDA-approved at-home, prescription treatment for molluscum contagiosum in adults and children 1 year of age and older."** The brand's own wording, and the at-home part is the genuine differentiator, since the other approved drug is applied in a clinic.[^brand] Every qualifier in that sentence is load-bearing: it is a claim about being first to a delivery format, not about working better.

**"Molluscum can finally be treated at home or on the go".** True as a statement about where you can use it.[^brand] What it does not say is how often treatment succeeds, which the label puts at about three patients in ten at Week 12, against about two in ten who clear on the vehicle gel.[^spl]

## Where to Buy

Prescription only, dispensed through a pharmacy and applied by the patient or a caregiver at home.[^spl] No retail price is quoted here. The brand site advertises patient support "including copay assistance", which is a manufacturer programme with its own conditions rather than a statement of cost.[^brand]
