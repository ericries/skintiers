Vanicream Gentle Facial Cleanser for Sensitive Skin (Vanicream) is a soap-free, fragrance-free facial cleanser positioned for dry, sensitive, and delicate skin.[^1]

> A very plain cleanser built to avoid common irritants rather than to treat anything: it is soap-free, sulfate-free, and fragrance-free, and it carries the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance.[^1][^2] No trial isolates this cleanser. The best-evidenced skincare intervention for eczema-prone skin is leave-on moisturizing, covered on [[moisturizing]], not cleansing.

## Summary

Vanicream Gentle Facial Cleanser is a soap-free facial wash made for dry, sensitive, and delicate skin, including people trying to avoid common cosmetic irritants.[^1] Its [ingredient list](#whats-in-it) is short: water, glycerin, two mild cleansing agents (coco-glucoside and sodium cocoyl glycinate), a texture agent, a preservative system, and a mica/titanium dioxide pair for opacity.[^1][^2] Target's listing confirms the same ingredient list and adds that the product is free of "sulfates, dyes, fragrance, masking fragrance, lanolin, parabens and formaldehyde releasers."[^2] What distinguishes it from a typical drugstore face wash is mainly what it leaves out rather than an added treatment ingredient: no fragrance, no essential oils, no botanical extracts, and no sulfate detergents.[^1][^2]

The cleanser has been awarded the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance and is described by the manufacturer as "dermatologist tested."[^1] That is a formulation-review designation and a manufacturer claim, not a clinical trial of this product; no product-specific trial is [cited below](#the-evidence). Sensitive, eczema-prone skin is a medical topic in its own right, covered on [[atopic-dermatitis]], where the best-evidenced skincare step is leave-on moisturizing rather than cleansing; that Cochrane-reviewed evidence and the role of humectants like [[glycerin]] are set out there and on [[moisturizing]].

It is inexpensive. Target lists the 8 fl oz bottle at $9.99 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^2] It is not a treatment product: it carries no drug active, and its positioning is tolerability (avoiding irritants) rather than a measured clinical benefit.

## The Evidence

No trial isolates this cleanser. The manufacturer states it was awarded the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance and describes it as "dermatologist tested," but no independent trial data, methodology, or review criteria for that designation are reported on the pages cited here.[^1] Sensitive and eczema-prone skin, and the best-evidenced skincare intervention for it, are covered on [[atopic-dermatitis]] and [[moisturizing]]. Because this is a rinse-off cleanser rather than a leave-on moisturizer, that general moisturizer evidence does not transfer directly to it. Glycerin, the second-listed ingredient, has solid evidence as a leave-on humectant for stratum corneum hydration and barrier function, graded on [[glycerin]]; whether a formula that is rinsed off deposits enough of it to matter has not been tested for this product.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer and matched on Target's listing.[^1][^2]

- water
- [[glycerin]], a humectant and the second-listed ingredient
- coco-glucoside, a mild non-ionic cleansing surfactant
- sodium cocoyl glycinate, a mild anionic cleansing surfactant
- acrylates copolymer (thickener/film former)
- caprylyl glycol (humectant and preservative booster)
- mica (opacifier/colorant)
- sodium chloride (thickener)
- 1,2-hexanediol (preservative booster)
- titanium dioxide (opacifier/colorant)
- sodium hydroxide (pH adjuster)
- disodium EDTA (chelator)

Not in the formula: no added fragrance or masking fragrance, no essential oils or botanical extracts, no sulfate detergents, no dyes, no lanolin, no parabens, and no formaldehyde-releasing preservatives.[^1][^2]
