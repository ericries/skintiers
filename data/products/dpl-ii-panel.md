A full-face LED panel cleared in 2017 for one thing: wrinkles. **Bottom line:** unusually for this category its file publishes the actual irradiance, 70.16 mW/cm2, and the only human testing in it is a 16-person usability study. Whether LED does anything for wrinkles is on [[home-led-light-therapy]], and the dose arithmetic is on [[led-dose-j-per-cm2-and-wavelength]].

> **It publishes the number most of its siblings do not.** The predicate comparison chart gives an Energy Level of "70.16 mW/cm²" over a "415 cm²" treatment area at "3 minutes per treatment".[^k171390] That is enough to do the arithmetic the rest of this category blocks: 70.16 mW/cm2 for 180 seconds works out at about 12.6 J/cm2 per session, which is this page's calculation from the file's own figures rather than a number the file states. The file also sets the regimen, "five days a week for eight weeks".[^k171390]

## Summary

Cleared 2017-08-03 as K171390, applicant LED Technologies, Inc. of Greenwood Village, Colorado, product code OHS under 21 CFR 878.4810, a Traditional 510(k) published as a Summary.[^openfda] The cleared indication, quoted in full from the Indications for Use form: "The dpl® II Panel system is an Over-the Counter (OTC) device intended for use in treating wrinkles."[^k171390] No body site, no severity limit, no skin-type restriction.

**Four wavelengths, no controls.** The file describes "four type of LEDs: 605nm amber, 630nm red, 660nm red, and 880nm infrared", with the panel unit, power supply, goggles and a storage case making up the system.[^k171390] Operation is deliberately minimal: "The treatment time is controlled by the user. There are no user settings or adjustments required.", and "The unit is applied directly to the skin to ensure consistent administration of light during each treatment."[^k171390]

**The predicate is its own smaller sibling.** Equivalence rests on a single device, "K141181 - dpl® Nuve for Wrinkles (LED Technologies, Inc.)", covered at [[dpl-nuve]].[^k171390] Both carry the same four wavelengths and the same three-minute treatment.

**No price is quoted here.** No brand storefront page was fetched for this panel and no retailer was consulted, so no price is asserted.

## The Rubric

**Wrinkles, the cleared indication (cosmetic)**
Graded modest on mixed evidence, inherited rather than earned. Red and infrared light for facial rejuvenation is the better-evidenced half of the LED case and is ranked on [[home-led-light-therapy]] and [[home-use-skincare-devices-by-evidence]]; this device adds nothing to it, because its file contains no clinical study. What the published irradiance does buy is the ability to check this device's per-session dose against the doses used in those trials, which is the exercise on [[led-dose-j-per-cm2-and-wavelength]].

**Anything this device itself demonstrated**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. "A Usability Study was conducted with 16 participants", and the two results reported are that "100% of the participants were able to demonstrate the light sensitivity test." and "100% of the participants were able to use the device successfully."[^k171390] Those are operability findings. The file says so itself: "The conclusions drawn from nonclinical tests demonstrate that the device is safe, as effective, and performs as well as the legally marketed devices."[^k171390]

## The Evidence

**The equivalence argument quotes the wrong device's output.** The predicate chart gives the subject device 70.16 mW/cm² and the predicate 62.07 mW/cm².[^k171390] The summary of technological characteristics on the next page then says the device "Has the same/similar output (i.e., 62 mW/cm²) as the predicate device", attaching the predicate's figure to the subject.[^k171390] Both numbers are in the same document two pages apart. It does not change what was cleared, and it is the sort of thing only reading the file surfaces.

**Same irradiance, fourteen times the area.** Treatment area runs "415 cm²" here against "30 cm²" for the predicate.[^k171390] Irradiance is power per unit area, so the dose delivered to any given patch of skin is comparable; what differs is how much skin gets it at once. A panel that treats the whole face in one three-minute session is a different proposition from a handheld that treats a patch, and the equivalence discussion lists treatment areas among the things compared before concluding "no significant differences exist".[^k171390]

**What was actually tested.** Functional performance testing, software validation and user safety testing, against IEC 60601-1 (2006), IEC 60601-1-2 (2007) and ISO 10993-10:2010 for irritation and delayed-type hypersensitivity.[^k171390] That is a competent safety and electrical package. None of it measures a wrinkle.

**It belongs on the disclosure ranking, and that ranking needed correcting.** [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]] previously said one LED Technologies filing out of fifteen supplied an irradiance figure. This is a second, and the join is exact: the later perioral filing K172662 lists three predecessor devices at 62.07, 70.16 and 61.59 mW/cm2 with areas of 30, 415 and 28 cm2, and the 70.16 over 415 cm2 entry is this device, confirmed here against its own file.[^k171390][^openfda]

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. The system ships with goggles and the file's one human study tested whether people could complete a light sensitivity check, so eye protection is part of using it as cleared. Wrinkle treatment is cosmetic; a changing lesion is a reason to see a dermatologist rather than to buy a panel.

## Common Marketing Claims

**No brand marketing copy is quoted here.** No storefront page was fetched for this device, so the only claims assessed are the regulated ones in the FDA file.

**The cleared wording is broad, and that is worth knowing in both directions.** Many LED clearances on this site are narrowed to periorbital wrinkles or to mild-to-moderate acne; this one says wrinkles, unqualified.[^k171390] A broader cleared indication is not evidence of a broader effect, since the file supporting it contains no clinical study at all.
