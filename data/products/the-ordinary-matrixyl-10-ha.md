The Ordinary Matrixyl 10% + HA is an inexpensive, water-based peptide serum combining palmitoyl tripeptide-1, palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7, and palmitoyl tripeptide-38 with sodium hyaluronate, sold for $10.90 for 30 ml.[^to]

> A budget serum that stacks two different Sederma "Matrixyl" trade-name peptide blends (Matrixyl 3000's palmitoyl tripeptide-1 + palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7, and Matrixyl synthe'6's palmitoyl tripeptide-38) plus hyaluronic acid. No trial tests this serum, and the peptides' own independent human evidence is thin, small, and largely run or funded by the ingredient supplier or a competing brand, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence). Judge it as a cheap, low-risk way to try signal peptides, not a proven collagen-building treatment.

## Summary

This is The Ordinary's dedicated Matrixyl serum: a lightweight, water-and-glycerin-based formula listing "Aqua (Water), Glycerin, Butylene Glycol" ahead of three named peptides, "Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1, Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7, Palmitoyl Tripeptide-38," followed by "Sodium Hyaluronate," then cyclodextrin, solubilizers, thickeners, and a phenoxyethanol/chlorphenesin preservative system.[^to] The brand describes it as "an advanced formulation that targets the look of fine lines and wrinkles, and promotes firmer, plumper skin," saying it uses "two generations of Matrixyl and hyaluronic acid" to "improve the appearance of facial areas prone to fine lines by strengthening the skin's support network."[^to]

Despite the product name, "Matrixyl" itself (palmitoyl pentapeptide-4, the original pal-KTTKS molecule) is not in this formula. The label lists three related but distinct peptides sold under two different Sederma trade names: palmitoyl tripeptide-1 combined with palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7 is Sederma's Matrixyl 3000 blend, and palmitoyl tripeptide-38 is the peptide behind Sederma's Matrixyl synthe'6 name.[^to] That trade-name history, and why it matters for reading the evidence, is set out on [[palmitoyl-pentapeptide-4-matrixyl]].

This is a cosmetic appearance claim (fewer visible fine lines, a firmer look), not a medical one; no health claim is made or evaluated here.

## The Evidence

No independent, controlled trial of this specific serum or its three-peptide combination was located. The case for it rests entirely on category-level evidence for its individual peptides, evidence that is graded in full on their own pages and only summarized here.

**Palmitoyl tripeptide-1 + palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7 (Matrixyl 3000 blend).** The only substantial human study of this exact two-peptide pairing is a supplier-authored "preliminary" study; the full record, including the supplier connection and the primary sources, is on [[palmitoyl-tripeptide-1]] and [[palmitoyl-tetrapeptide-7]].

**Palmitoyl tripeptide-38 (Matrixyl synthe'6).** The peptide's human data comes from a small, open-label, manufacturer-funded study with no control group, in which the peptide was combined with vitamin C, so no result isolates this peptide's own contribution; the details are on [[peptides]].

**Original Matrixyl (pal-KTTKS), not in this product.** The one substantial placebo-controlled trial in the Matrixyl evidence base tested the original pal-KTTKS molecule, not the peptides in this serum, and that trial's own result does not transfer to this formula, [full detail on [[palmitoyl-pentapeptide-4-matrixyl]]](#the-evidence).

> **Bottom line.** Every peptide in this serum has some human data, but none of it is an independent, placebo-controlled trial of the peptide alone, and none of it tests this product or this three-peptide combination.[^to]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Advanced formulation that targets the look of fine lines and wrinkles, and promotes firmer, plumper skin."**[^to] A cosmetic appearance claim; no trial of this serum was found, and the underlying peptides' own evidence is thin and largely supplier-connected (see [[palmitoyl-pentapeptide-4-matrixyl]] and [[palmitoyl-tetrapeptide-7]]).
- **"Two generations of Matrixyl."**[^to] Accurate as a description of the trade names on the label (Matrixyl 3000's peptides and Matrixyl synthe'6's peptide), but the original "Matrixyl" molecule itself is not in this formula.
- **"Strengthening the skin's support network."**[^to] A mechanistic framing for the peptides' proposed collagen-signaling action; this is a proposed mechanism, not a measured outcome in a trial of this product.
