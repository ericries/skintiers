The Braun Silk-expert Pro 5 IPL is an at-home intense pulsed light (IPL) hair-removal device, sold by Braun as an alternative to salon laser hair removal.[^braun]

> The realistic expectation is modest, temporary hair reduction with repeated sessions, not permanent removal. The best controlled evidence on laser and light-based hair removal found "little evidence" for IPL specifically, and no long-term hair removal documented for any device up to six months, and home devices like this one run at lower energy than the professional machines in that evidence base; see [[laser-hair-removal]]. IPL also works by targeting pigment in the hair, so it is built for light skin with dark hair and Braun states it is not suitable for very blonde, red, grey, or white hair.[^braun]

## Summary

The Silk-expert Pro 5 is Braun's flagship at-home IPL device, sold in several kits that vary by included attachments; the PL5347 kit ($469.99 as listed) ships with a standard cap, two precision heads, a wide cap, a Gillette Venus razor, and a vanity case.[^braun] It has 10 intensity levels, a skin-tone sensor that reads skin tone and adjusts each flash, three modes (normal, sensitive, extra sensitive), and a rated 400,000 flashes.[^braun] It is built for hair reduction, not removal: the underlying laser/light-based evidence base shows a short-term reduction effect, with "little evidence" specifically for IPL and no long-term (permanent) removal documented for any device studied, a caveat that applies with extra force to a lower-energy home unit (see [[laser-hair-removal]] and [The Evidence](#the-evidence)). Like all IPL, it depends on pigment contrast: Braun states its IPL is "suitable for a wide range of skin tones from light to medium dark skin tones, with hair ranging from natural blonde to dark brown or black," and is "least suitable on very blonde, red, grey or white hair."[^braun]

## The Evidence

The general evidence for laser and light-based hair removal is graded on [[laser-hair-removal]]: a Cochrane review of randomized trials found a short-term reduction of roughly 50% with alexandrite and diode lasers up to six months, "little evidence" of an effect for IPL specifically (along with Nd:YAG and ruby lasers), and no long-term hair removal documented for any device in the trials it could pool.[^cochrane] That review covered professional, in-office devices; no independent (non-Braun) clinical trial of the Silk-expert Pro 5 or a comparable at-home IPL device was found for this page. Home IPL units also operate at lower energy output than the salon and clinic devices used in that evidence base, which is a further reason to expect a smaller and more temporary effect than even the modest short-term numbers the Cochrane review reported for lasers.

IPL depends on the light being absorbed by melanin (pigment) in the hair rather than the surrounding skin, so it works best when there is strong contrast between dark hair and light skin, and is ineffective, and potentially unsafe, on dark skin tones or on light, grey, or red hair, which have too little pigment contrast for the device to target safely or effectively.[^braun] As with any light-based device, the manufacturer's own instructions carry the specific contraindications and precautions, which are not reproduced here.

## What It Is

The Silk-expert Pro 5 is a handheld IPL device with a skin-contact design (the brand says its integrated UV filter is why no safety glasses or goggles are needed) and a skin-tone sensor (Braun's SensoAdapt) that "continuously reads your skin and automatically adapts the power of every flash."[^braun] Per the PL5347 listing: 10 intensity levels, three modes (normal, sensitive, extra sensitive), and a rated life of 400,000 flashes.[^braun] The PL5347 kit includes a standard cap for underarms, a wide cap for larger areas such as the chest or upper legs, two precision heads for the face and bikini area, a Gillette Venus razor, and a vanity case.[^braun] Braun lists the PL5347 kit at $469.99; other kits in the same Silk-expert Pro 5 line vary the caps and accessories at different prices.[^braun]

## Common Marketing Claims

The following are Braun's own claims from company-run testing, not independent findings, and are separated here from the evidence above.

- **"2 year of smooth skin at home."** Braun's own headline claim, footnoted on its site as "following recommended regimen, individual results may vary."[^braun]
- **"Up to 95% hair reduction in 1 month."** Attributed by Braun to a clinical study "on lower legs 2022-2024," also with "individual results may vary."[^braun]
- **"Permanent reduction in hair regrowth."** Used in Braun's own product description; the controlled hair-removal evidence reviewed on [[laser-hair-removal]] has not established long-term (permanent) removal for any device, professional or at-home.
- **"Salon results without salon costs."** Braun markets a cost comparison of an $8,100 salon laser hair removal course against roughly $430 for one of its at-home IPL devices; this is a brand-published cost comparison, not an independent price study.[^braun]
- **"Dermatologically and gynecologically approved."** Braun's own product description, alongside "eye-safe: NO GLASSES NEEDED"; these are brand statements, not independent certifications cited here.[^braun]
