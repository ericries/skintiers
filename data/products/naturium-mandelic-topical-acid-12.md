Naturium Mandelic Topical Acid 12% is a water-based, leave-on exfoliating serum built around a labeled 12% mandelic acid, with a manufacturer-stated formula pH of 3.00 to 3.50, sold in a single 30 mL bottle listed at $20.[^1]

> A leave-on mandelic acid serum at a labeled 12%, a higher printed strength than The Ordinary's 10% [[the-ordinary-mandelic-acid-10-ha|Mandelic Acid 10% + HA]], at a stated pH (3.00 to 3.50) low enough for AHA exfoliation. Mandelic acid is the larger-molecule, slower-penetrating member of the AHA family, but its clinical evidence is thinner and more mixed than glycolic or lactic acid's; the case, and why no trial covers this product, is set out on [[mandelic-acid]] and below under [The Evidence](#the-evidence). It is listed at $20 for 30 mL on Naturium's own site, a price matched on Target's listing.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Mandelic Topical Acid 12% is Naturium's leave-on mandelic acid exfoliant: mandelic acid second on the ingredient list after water, at a labeled 12%, partially adjusted with aminomethyl propanol to a stated formula pH of 3.00 to 3.50, alongside an undisclosed amount of [[niacinamide]] and a blend of fruit and sugar-cane extracts.[^1] The general case for mandelic acid, modest and mixed evidence drawn almost entirely from small in-office peel trials at 30% to 45% for acne and hyperpigmentation, is set out on [[mandelic-acid]]; where it sits against the rest of the exfoliating-acid family is on [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]].

Against its closest category peer, [[the-ordinary-mandelic-acid-10-ha|The Ordinary's Mandelic Acid 10% + HA]], this serum prints a slightly higher strength (12% versus 10%) and states a lower, more clearly exfoliation-active pH (3.00 to 3.50 versus 3.5 to 4.5), at roughly two and a half times the price ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence) for what evidence covers, and doesn't cover, either product).

The label's claims, gentler exfoliation, brighter skin, and a reduced look of fine lines, are appearance (cosmetic) claims, and none is backed by a trial of this product; they are weighed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims). As an AHA product, the brand's own directions say to start a few times a week and "Always use SPF in the morning."[^1] It is listed at $20 for the single 30 mL size on Naturium's site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-17).[^1][^2]

## The Evidence

Mandelic acid's controlled evidence is smaller and more mixed than the evidence behind glycolic or lactic acid: almost all of it comes from small in-office peel trials at 30% to 45%, mostly for acne and hyperpigmentation, and the one trial that tested mandelic acid alone against a comparably gentle AHA, lactic acid, found lactic acid produced better pigment reduction, higher satisfaction, and less irritation; that full case, by use, is set out on [[mandelic-acid]]. Nothing specific to this product was found: no published trial tests Naturium's 12% leave-on formula, so its grades above rest on the general category evidence, all of it generated as in-office peels at concentrations two and a half to nearly four times this serum's 12%.[^1]

The product-specific facts are compositional. The formula is labeled 12% mandelic acid, listed second on the INCI after water, and Naturium states a formula pH of 3.00 to 3.50,[^1] a range at which an AHA is meaningfully exfoliation-active (mandelic acid's health-adjacent uses, acne and hyperpigmentation, were established at peel strengths, not at leave-on levels like this). The list also includes aminomethyl propanol, a pH adjuster that partially neutralizes the acid; no free-acid value is published.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its labeled 12% sits well below the 30% to 45% in-office peel concentrations that generated mandelic acid's own thin, mixed evidence, and no leave-on trial at any strength covers what this serum is sold to do. The evidence for what mandelic acid does, and does not do, is on [[mandelic-acid]].[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by a trial of this product; all are appearance (cosmetic) claims:

- **"Gently exfoliates."** No trial of this formula measures gentleness against a comparator; the one trial testing mandelic acid alone against lactic acid found irritation more frequent with mandelic acid, not less (see [[mandelic-acid]]).
- **"Brightens appearance of skin."** Mandelic acid's pigmentation evidence comes from in-office peels at 30% or more, where results favorable to mandelic acid used it combined with salicylic acid; tested alone it lost to lactic acid (see [[mandelic-acid]]).
- **"Helps reduce the look of fine lines and wrinkles."** The only texture and elasticity data for topical mandelic acid is a single 24-person, uncontrolled, open-label trial of a different, practice-developed formulation (see [[mandelic-acid]]).
- **"Contains a high concentration of mandelic and natural fruit extracts."** The 12% mandelic figure is manufacturer-declared; the bilberry, sugar cane, orange, lemon, and maple extracts carry no declared acid content, and no evidence in mandelic acid's trial base involves such extract blends.[^1]
