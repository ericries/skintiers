Aquaphor Healing Ointment is an over-the-counter skin-protectant ointment made by Beiersdorf Inc. under the FDA's Skin Protectant OTC monograph (M016), with petrolatum 41% as its sole active ingredient.[^1]

> Aquaphor is a petrolatum-based ointment, not pure petroleum jelly: it cuts the 41% petrolatum with mineral oil, lanolin alcohol, and glycerin, then adds panthenol and bisabolol as soothing extras. Petrolatum's own case as the reference occlusive, including a large RCT where plain petrolatum matched antibiotic ointment on post-surgical infection rates, is well established; see [[petrolatum]]. No published trial isolates this specific 41% blend, so its FDA-cleared uses (minor cuts, scrapes, burns, chapped skin, diaper rash) rest on the OTC skin-protectant monograph and the petrolatum-class evidence, not a product-specific study.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Aquaphor Healing Ointment is a multi-purpose ointment sold by Beiersdorf for dry, cracked, or irritated skin, chapped lips, and minor wounds and burns.[^1][^3] Unlike a plain jar of petroleum jelly, it is not 100% petrolatum: the FDA-regulated Drug Facts label lists petrolatum at 41% as the sole active ingredient, diluted with mineral oil, ceresin (a wax), lanolin alcohol, [[glycerin]], [[panthenol]], and [[alpha-bisabolol]] as inactive ingredients.[^1] It is regulated as an OTC drug under the FDA's Skin Protectant monograph (M016), the same regulatory category as plain petrolatum jelly, and its Drug Facts label states it "temporarily protects minor: cuts, scrapes, burns," "protects and helps relieve chapped or cracked skin and lips," "helps protect from the drying effects of wind and cold weather," and "helps treat and prevent diaper rash."[^1]

Petrolatum's own evidence base, including a large randomized trial where plain white petrolatum matched a topical antibiotic ointment on post-surgical wound infection rates while causing no allergic contact dermatitis, is set out in full on [[petrolatum]]; that general case, not a trial of this specific formula, underlies the grade below (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)). The brand markets it as the "#1 dermatologist recommended brand for dry, cracked skin," a claim from Beiersdorf's own product page rather than an independent survey.[^3]

## The Evidence

Petrolatum is the reference occlusive in dermatology: a mechanistic human study found it accelerates rather than impedes skin-barrier recovery, and a large randomized trial found plain petrolatum matched a topical antibiotic ointment on post-surgical wound infection rates while causing zero cases of allergic contact dermatitis. The full case, its mechanism, and its limits are set out on [[petrolatum]]; that page is the source for the health-use grade above.

No published clinical trial of Aquaphor Healing Ointment's specific 41%-petrolatum, mineral-oil, lanolin-alcohol, glycerin, panthenol, and bisabolol formula was identified. Its FDA-cleared uses rest on the Skin Protectant OTC monograph (petrolatum as the recognized active) rather than a product-specific efficacy trial, which is why the grade above is carried on the class-level petrolatum evidence rather than a dedicated study of this exact blend.[^1]

## What's In It

The Drug Facts label, as filed with the FDA, lists petrolatum as the sole active ingredient and the following inactive ingredients: mineral oil, ceresin, lanolin alcohol, [[panthenol]], [[glycerin]], and [[alpha-bisabolol]] (listed on the label as bisabolol).[^1]

- [[petrolatum]] (41%), the active ingredient and occlusive that slows water loss
- Mineral oil and ceresin (a petroleum-derived wax), additional occlusive/texturizing agents
- Lanolin alcohol, an emollient and emulsifier derived from wool grease
- [[glycerin]], a humectant
- [[panthenol]] and [[alpha-bisabolol]], soothing additives

## Tolerability

The Drug Facts label warns against use on deep or puncture wounds, animal bites, or serious burns, and against getting the product in the eyes; it directs users to stop use and consult a doctor if the condition worsens or symptoms persist beyond seven days or clear up and recur.[^1] Beiersdorf's own product page for the 14 oz jar describes it as preservative- and fragrance-free.[^3]
