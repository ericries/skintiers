The device at the root of the dpl chain, and it is an LED array cleared as an infrared heating lamp. **Bottom line:** every later dpl filing chains to the firm's own earlier products, so this 2005 clearance by a different company is the one link out of the family, and reading it shows the no-published-dose pattern starting here rather than with dpl. The line it predicates is on [[dpl-therapy-system]].

> **An LED device, under the infrared lamp regulation, for arthritis.** The Summary describes "a compact light source that delivers high intensity narrow band red and near infrared light to the skin surface" whose "light sources are narrow bandwidth LEDs mounted in an array".[^k051681] FDA's letter files it under regulation number "21 CFR 890.5500", regulation name "Infrared lamp", regulatory class "II" and product code "ILY", each printed on its own line of the letter.[^k051681] Which code a red-light device lands under is worked through on [[infrared-heat-lamps-vs-led-skin-devices]].

## Summary

Cleared 2005-07-27 as K051681, applicant Care Electronics, Inc. of Boulder, Colorado, product code ILY, Physical Medicine panel, filed with a Summary rather than a Statement.[^openfda]

**The cleared indication is the heating-lamp one.** The lamp is "intended for the relaxation of muscles and relief of muscle spasms, temporary relief of minor muscle and joint aches, pains and stiffness, temporary relief of minor pain and stiffness associated with arthritis, and to temporarily increase local blood circulation".[^k051681] That is the same indication the DPL Therapy System carried in 2008, which is what made the two substantially equivalent.

**Equivalence runs five ways, not one.** The Summary lists five predicate lamps rather than a single one, among them Anodyne Therapy Systems and an earlier Dermillume model.[^k051681]

**No price.** A 2005 device from a company with no current listing found.

## The Rubric

**Relaxing muscles and relieving minor aches, the cleared indication (health)**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. This is a physical-medicine pain claim and this site does not grade pain outcomes, so the grade is about scope rather than scepticism. It is worth recording what the file offers in place of data, which the next section does.

**Anything for skin**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence, because there is nothing to grade. The words wrinkle, acne and collagen appear nowhere in the document.[^k051681] A lamp that warms skin is not thereby a treatment for skin.

## The Evidence

**The dose is described as comparable to something else, with no number attached.** On performance the Summary says bench testing "substantiates that the irradiance at a practicable distance from the skin surface is comparable with the cited predicated lamps".[^k051681] That is the only occurrence of the word irradiance in the file, and no figure follows it. Checked by string search of the full extracted text, the document contains no occurrence of mW/cm, J/cm, nm or wavelength, so a device defined by emitting "narrow band red and near infrared light" never says which band.[^k051681] The same absence runs through the dpl filings that descend from it, ranked on [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]].

**One sentence asserts efficacy and cites nothing.** "There are no significant adverse reactions observed in clinical studies using this technology. The lamp is safe and efficacious."[^k051681] No study is named, no participant count is given, and the words randomised, placebo and sham appear nowhere in the file.[^k051681] The sentence is the submitter's own, written into its application, and [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] covers why clearance does not test such a claim.

**What this means for the family.** [[dpl-therapy-system]] inherited an equivalence argument whose outside anchor published no dose and no study. Whether the later skin devices in the line do any better is the question [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]] exists to answer, and mostly they do not.

## Manufacturer Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven unless it also appears in The Evidence above. These are the submitter's statements in a 510(k) Summary, which FDA does not verify.

- **"The lamp is safe and efficacious."** Discount. Stated flatly with no study, no numbers and no citation anywhere in the file.[^k051681]
- **"high intensity" light.** Not a specification. No irradiance figure appears, so intensity is claimed and never quantified.[^k051681]
- **Differences from the predicates "are minor and do not raise new questions of safety or efficacy."** This is the standard equivalence formula, and the Summary applies it while conceding "there are some differences in the source of the emitted light and output intensity".[^k051681]
