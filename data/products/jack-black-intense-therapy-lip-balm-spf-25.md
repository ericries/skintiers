An SPF lip balm is an OTC sunscreen drug, so unlike most lip products it has an FDA label you can read. **Bottom line:** this one declares three actives across two monograph purposes, and the most interesting thing about it is what its label does not claim. Lip context is on [[lip-care]].

> **It makes no broad-spectrum claim, and that is the claim that would matter.** The entire Use statement is "Helps prevent sunburn and chapped lips."[^label] This SPL contains no broad-spectrum designation, no water-resistance claim, no skin-cancer or early-skin-aging claim, and no reapplication interval. Under the US sunscreen monograph, the skin-cancer and aging claim is reserved for broad-spectrum products of SPF 15 or higher. So if you are buying an SPF lip balm because chronic sun exposure drives actinic damage on the lips, this label is not promising to address that.

## Summary

Marketing category "OTC Monograph Drug", application M20, NDC 66738-309.[^label] Three declared actives: "Avobenzone 3.0% Sunscreen", "Octinoxate 7.5% Sunscreen" and "Petrolatum 40.2% Skin Protectant".[^label] Directions are to "Apply liberally 15 minutes before sun exposure and as needed", with "Children under 6 months: Ask a doctor."[^label] No price is quoted here; no retailer was fetched and no metered lookup was spent.

## The Rubric

**Chapped lips**
- **Effect size: notable.** Petrolatum at 40.2% is declared as a Skin Protectant active, not an inactive filler.[^label] That is a high level of the single best-evidenced occlusive in skincare.
- **Evidence quality: solid.** The occlusive evidence is well established and is graded on [[petrolatum]], not repeated here. This is the claim on the tube most likely to be met.

**Preventing sunburn on the lips**
- **Effect size: notable.** Avobenzone 3.0% plus octinoxate 7.5% is a real filter pair, and the label's sunburn claim is the standard monograph one.[^label]
- **Evidence quality: mixed.** The monograph route means FDA recognised the filters for this use rather than reviewing this product's data, which [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] explains. Filter-level evidence is on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

**Reducing long-term UV damage to the lips**
- **Effect size: none that this label supports.** No broad-spectrum designation, no skin-cancer or early-skin-aging claim, no water-resistance claim, no stated reapplication interval.[^label] Those absences are the finding.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** Note the precise scope: this is about what the label claims, not a measurement of the product's spectral performance. Avobenzone does absorb in the UVA range, so the filters are not the issue; the missing item is the designation that would license a long-term-damage claim.

## The Evidence

**Two monograph purposes in one tube, which is unusual and worth knowing.** The actives list assigns a purpose to each entry: two are Sunscreen, one is Skin Protectant.[^label] So this is simultaneously a sunscreen drug and a skin-protectant drug, and the Use statement covers both outcomes in one sentence. That is why the chapped-lips half of the claim is better supported than the sun half.

**What the directions leave out.** "Apply liberally 15 minutes before sun exposure and as needed" has no interval.[^label] Face and body sunscreens under the monograph normally carry a reapplication instruction; lips are rubbed, licked and eaten off faster than any other site, so "as needed" is doing a lot of work. Nothing on the label tells a user how often that is.

**One composition note, stated factually.** Octinoxate is among the organic filters whose environmental and absorption profile has drawn regulatory attention in some jurisdictions. This page records that the product contains 7.5% of it and takes no position on harm, because no source consulted here establishes one.[^label]

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. A persistently rough, scaly or non-healing patch on the lip is a reason to see a clinician rather than a reason to buy a balm; that is covered on [[lip-care]].

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Helps prevent sunburn and chapped lips"**. Both halves are monograph claims and both are supported by the declared actives.[^label] It is also the whole of what the label promises.
- **The SPF 25 in the product name.** The number appears in the labelling rather than in the Use statement, and SPF describes sunburn protection only.[^label] Without a broad-spectrum designation it says nothing about UVA.
- **What is absent.** No broad spectrum, no water resistance, no skin-cancer claim, no reapplication interval.[^label] For a lip product bought for sun protection, that list is more informative than anything printed on the front.
