Head & Shoulders Classic Clean is the archetypal over-the-counter anti-dandruff shampoo: an FDA Drug Facts product from The Procter & Gamble Manufacturing Company with pyrithione zinc 1% as its single active, purpose line "Anti-dandruff," and the use statement "helps prevent recurrence of flaking and itching associated with dandruff."[^dailymed] The bottle calls itself "america's #1 dandruff shampoo" and promises "FLAKE FREE. UP TO 100% GUARANTEED," with asterisks doing real work on both claims.[^dailymed]

> The cheap default is also a genuinely medicated one. Pyrithione zinc 1% actually targets the Malassezia yeast behind [[dandruff]] and [[seborrheic-dermatitis]], which puts this bottle in a different category from a pure keratolytic like [[neutrogena-tsal-therapeutic-shampoo|salicylic acid 3%]] that only lifts scale. But be equally clear about the limits: this is management, not cure. The label itself claims prevention of recurrence with regular use, and flaking typically returns when you stop; many people also rotate it with [[nizoral-anti-dandruff-shampoo-ketoconazole-1|ketoconazole]] or [[selenium-sulfide]] shampoos for tolerance or when one active stops performing. The ingredient-level evidence for pyrithione zinc is owned by [[zinc-pyrithione]] and is not re-derived here.

## Summary

This is an OTC monograph drug, not an FDA-approved product with its own efficacy dossier: the active, pyrithione zinc 1% (1 g per 100 mL), matches the strength the long-standing dandruff monograph permits, and the DailyMed label lists it under product NDC 37000-071 in package sizes from travel bottles to multi-liter pumps.[^dailymed] The Drug Facts use statement is notably narrow, dandruff only: "helps prevent recurrence of flaking and itching associated with dandruff."[^dailymed] It does not name seborrheic dermatitis, even though pyrithione zinc is widely used there; that broader story belongs to [[zinc-pyrithione]] and the [[seborrheic-dermatitis]] page.

Directions are the monograph pattern plus a frequency push: "for best results use at least twice a week or as directed by a doctor," "for maximum dandruff control, use every time you shampoo," shake before use, "wet hair, massage onto scalp, rinse, repeat if desired."[^dailymed] That every-wash framing is consistent with how the active works, contact-based suppression of the yeast and its irritating byproducts rather than a one-time fix. Warnings are the standard set: external use only, avoid contact with eyes and rinse thoroughly if contact occurs, and stop use and ask a doctor if the condition worsens or does not improve after regular use as directed.[^dailymed]

One accuracy note for shoppers: the brand's current product page describes an "improved formula" with a "HydraZinc Complex" of zinc pyrithione, niacinamide, and glycerin, and its on-page ingredient list includes piroctone olamine and niacinamide, ingredients that do not appear anywhere on the February 2025 DailyMed label used for this page.[^brand][^dailymed] Bottles in circulation may carry either the classic formula documented below or the reformulated one; the declared active, pyrithione zinc 1%, is the same in both accounts.

## The Evidence

Pyrithione zinc's case as an antifungal antidandruff active, suppression of Malassezia and the flaking and itch it provokes, is graded on [[zinc-pyrithione]]; no clinical trial of this specific Classic Clean formulation was found in the sources reviewed here, and as an OTC monograph drug none was required. The regulatory basis is simply that the active and strength match what the monograph allows.[^dailymed] The brand does advertise "clinically proven" dandruff protection and a head-to-head value claim against ketoconazole, addressed under marketing claims below.[^brand]

> Bottom line: the health claim on this label, preventing the recurrence of dandruff flaking and itch with regular use, rests on pyrithione zinc's decades-old monograph status and the ingredient evidence on [[zinc-pyrithione]], not on a published trial of this bottle. It is a maintenance drug in shampoo form: effective while you keep using it, with relapse expected when you stop.

## What's In It

- **Active: Pyrithione zinc 1%** (1 g per 100 mL), as declared on the FDA Drug Facts label.[^dailymed] See [[zinc-pyrithione]] for the graded mechanism and ingredient-level evidence.
- **Inactive ingredients:** Water, sodium lauryl sulfate, sodium laureth sulfate, glycol distearate, sodium chloride, zinc carbonate, fragrance, cocamidopropyl betaine, sodium xylenesulfonate, dimethicone, sodium benzoate, guar hydroxypropyltrimonium chloride, magnesium carbonate hydroxide, blue 1, red 33, as declared on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

A conventional sulfate surfactant base with a pearlizer (glycol distearate), a conditioning silicone (dimethicone), fragrance, and two dyes; zinc carbonate is a stabilizer commonly paired with pyrithione zinc. As noted above, the brand's own page currently lists a partly different ingredient set for the reformulated version, so check the bottle in hand.[^brand]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"FLAKE FREE. UP TO 100% GUARANTEED" and "america's #1 dandruff shampoo."**[^dailymed] Both appear on the FDA-labeled bottle itself. "Up to 100%" is an upper bound, not a promise; the fine print ties flake-free to "visible flakes; with regular use," and the #1 claim is a sales-rank claim, not an efficacy ranking.
- **"As effective on flakes as 1% Ketoconazole Shampoo, at 1/3 the price."**[^brand] The brand's own comparison, footnoted to visible flakes with regular use and an MSRP-per-ounce math against "the leading 14oz 1% ketoconazole brand." No citation to a published head-to-head trial accompanies it on the page; note the comparator is [[nizoral-anti-dandruff-shampoo-ketoconazole-1|ketoconazole 1%]], not the stronger 2% strength.
- **"Treats the root cause of dandruff" with a "clinically proven formula" that "works 10 layers deep."**[^brand] Directionally fair in one sense, an antifungal active does address the microbial driver rather than just the flakes, but "root cause" oversells for a condition that relapses when treatment stops, and "10 layers deep" is imagery, not a measurement the page substantiates.
- **"America's #1 dermatologist recommended brand."**[^brand] A survey-style claim about the brand among US dermatologists, not a clinical claim about this bottle.

## Where to Buy

An over-the-counter product sold essentially everywhere shampoo is sold, in package sizes from a 10 mL sample pouch to a 1.28 L pump bottle per the DailyMed package listing (product NDC 37000-071); the labeled principal display panel documents the 12.5 fl oz (370 mL) bottle.[^dailymed] No durable list price was found in the sources reviewed for this page, so none is listed; it is consistently among the cheapest medicated options per ounce, which is much of its appeal.
