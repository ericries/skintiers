Ducray Kelual DS Soothing Cream is a 40 ml leave-on facial gel-cream from the French brand Ducray, formulated for skin irritated by [[seborrheic-dermatitis]], redness paired with scaling, and built around a patented combination of two anti-*Malassezia* actives, piroctone olamine and ciclopirox olamine.[^ducray]

> A real anti-fungal pairing, not a bland moisturizer: piroctone olamine and ciclopirox olamine each have independent evidence against the *Malassezia* yeast implicated in seborrheic dermatitis, [graded in full on their own pages](#the-evidence). But the cream-specific numbers Ducray cites, scale cut in half on first use and stronger redness and scale reduction by two weeks, come from two of the brand's own 57-subject studies that were not located published or peer-reviewed, so the combination's product-specific case is thinner than the ingredient-level science behind it.[^ducray]

## Summary

Ducray markets this as a "gel-cream" texture, 40 ml in a tube, intended for the face of teens and adults with irritated, scale-and-redness-prone skin, positioned squarely against facial seborrheic dermatitis rather than as a general moisturizer.[^ducray] The brand's own product page names the actives directly: "la synergie d'actif brevetée* PIROCTONE OLAMINE/CICLOPIROX OLAMINE limite la prolifération des Malassezias et rééquilibre efficacement le microbiome" (a patented active synergy of piroctone olamine and ciclopirox olamine limits *Malassezia* proliferation and effectively rebalances the microbiome).[^ducray] The product's own structured data confirms the same two actives, listed as "CPO/PO" (ciclopirox olamine/piroctone olamine).[^ducray]

Both actives are independently evidenced anti-*Malassezia* antifungals, [set out in full on [[piroctone-olamine]] and [[ciclopirox]]](#the-evidence); this is a cosmetic-tier combination, not a prescription antifungal, and its case rests on category-level evidence for those actives plus brand-run, unpublished cream-specific studies, not an independent peer-reviewed trial of this exact formula.

## The Evidence

Whether piroctone olamine and ciclopirox (as ciclopirox olamine) fight the *Malassezia* yeast implicated in seborrheic dermatitis and dandruff is covered at the ingredient level on [[piroctone-olamine]] and [[ciclopirox]]: ciclopirox has the stronger independent base, including a 2015 Cochrane review finding ciclopirox outperformed placebo for clearing seborrheic dermatitis with effectiveness similar to ketoconazole; piroctone olamine's cleanest published trial pairs it with a second antifungal and shows it roughly matching zinc pyrithione for dandruff. Neither ingredient page's evidence was generated using this specific cream.

Ducray's own product page cites two of its own clinical studies specific to this cream, both described as run on "57 sujets" (57 subjects), one application per day, in an adult/adolescent population: one reporting scale reduced by half from the first application, and a second reporting reduced redness and strongly reduced scale within two weeks of once-daily use, with recurrence prevention shown at eight weeks.[^ducray] No published paper, registry entry, or independent peer review of either study was located; both are cited here as the brand's own unpublished clinical claims, not as independently verified trial data.[^ducray]

> **Bottom line.** The anti-*Malassezia* mechanism behind this cream is real and independently evidenced at the ingredient level. The cream-specific redness and scale numbers Ducray reports come only from the brand's own unpublished 57-subject studies, not an independent trial of this formula.

## What's In It

Full INCI ingredient list, per [incidecoder](https://incidecoder.com/products/ducray-kelual-ds-cream) (an aggregator, cited here for the ingredient list and photo, not for efficacy claims):

- Water (Aqua), Caprylic/Capric Triglyceride, Dimethicone, Dicaprylyl Ether, Cetearyl Alcohol, base emollients and texture agents
- Lactamide MEA, Glycolic Acid, exfoliating/humectant agents
- Guanidine Carbonate, Glyceryl Stearate, Polymethyl Methacrylate, Ceteareth-33, texture and emulsifying agents
- [[alpha-bisabolol|Bisabolol]], Ethanolamine, Glycyrrhetinic Acid, soothing agents
- [[piroctone-olamine|Piroctone Olamine]], anti-*Malassezia* antifungal active
- Serenoa Serrulata (saw palmetto) Fruit Extract, botanical extract
- Sodium Chloride, Tetrasodium EDTA, [[tocopherol|Tocopherol]], texture, chelating, and antioxidant agents

Ciclopirox olamine, the second active Ducray names in its own marketing copy, does not appear by that name (or as "Ciclopirox") in incidecoder's 19-ingredient INCI listing for this product; this is noted as a discrepancy between the brand's marketing claim and the third-party aggregator's ingredient list rather than resolved here.[^ducray]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not fully established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Dès la 1ère application, les rougeurs sont apaisées et les squames diminués de moitié" (from the first application, redness is soothed and scale is cut in half).**[^ducray] A brand-cited result from Ducray's own 57-subject own study; no independent or published version of this study was located.
- **"La synergie d'actif brevetée PIROCTONE OLAMINE/CICLOPIROX OLAMINE... rééquilibre efficacement le microbiome" (the patented active synergy effectively rebalances the microbiome).**[^ducray] A patent covering the combination supports formulation novelty, not independently that this specific cream measurably rebalances the skin microbiome; the antifungal mechanism against *Malassezia* is separately evidenced at the ingredient level on [[piroctone-olamine]] and [[ciclopirox]].
- **"Prévient durablement leur réapparition" (durably prevents recurrence).**[^ducray] Sourced to the same unpublished 57-subject study reporting anti-recurrence results at 8 weeks; not independently verified.
