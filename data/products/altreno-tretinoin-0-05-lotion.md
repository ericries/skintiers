Altreno is a prescription-only [[tretinoin]] lotion, FDA-approved for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in patients 9 years of age and older, and the first tretinoin product formulated in a lotion vehicle rather than the usual cream or gel base.[^dailymed][^altreno]

> Altreno is not a different drug from plain tretinoin; it is [[tretinoin|tretinoin]] itself, [the trial-validated benchmark topical retinoid with solid, FDA-approved acne evidence](#the-evidence), carried in a lotion base with glycerin, sodium hyaluronate, and soluble collagen instead of a cream or gel. Unlike a typical reformulation, this exact lotion has its own registration-trial evidence: two 12-week, vehicle-controlled trials pooling 1,640 patients found significant lesion reduction versus vehicle, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence). No comparative trial against a conventional (non-lotion) tretinoin product is cited on the label, so the manufacturer's tolerability and hydration claims for the lotion vehicle are a formulation feature, not an independently proven advantage.[^dailymed][^tyring]

## Summary

Altreno (tretinoin) Lotion, 0.05% is a prescription topical retinoid manufactured by [[bausch-health|Bausch Health]] and marketed under its Ortho Dermatologics brand.[^dailymed][^altreno] It is dispensed "Rx only" and FDA-approved for "the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in patients 9 years of age and older."[^dailymed] The manufacturer's own site describes it as "the first and only tretinoin 0.05% in a lotion formulation for the treatment of acne vulgaris."[^altreno]

Tretinoin's general evidence, covered in full on [[tretinoin]], is solid for acne (a modest monotherapy effect, roughly matched by adapalene, strengthened when combined with benzoyl peroxide) and notable for photoaging (a well-replicated but gradual, partial improvement in fine wrinkles and mottled pigment). Acne is Altreno's FDA-approved indication, and unlike many reformulated retinoids, it carries its own registration-trial data rather than resting only on the molecule's general record ([the evidence](#the-evidence)).[^dailymed][^tyring]

What distinguishes Altreno from a plain tretinoin cream or gel is the delivery vehicle: a lotion base containing glycerin, sodium hyaluronate, and soluble collagen, which the manufacturer positions as "free of fragrance, silicone, and alcohol," able to be "applied morning or evening," and "worn under makeup."[^altreno] The FDA label lists benzyl alcohol among the inactive ingredients, so that "alcohol-free" claim refers to drying alcohols (such as SD or isopropyl alcohol) rather than to every alcohol-derived excipient.[^dailymed] No comparative trial against a conventional, non-lotion tretinoin product is cited on the label, so a claim that the lotion vehicle itself lowers irritation or improves hydration relative to plain tretinoin is not evaluated here ([what's in it](#whats-in-it)).[^dailymed]

Altreno requires a prescription and is not sold over the counter.[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

Tretinoin has solid, FDA-approved evidence for acne as a first-line topical, with a modest monotherapy effect roughly matched by adapalene, and separately has well-replicated, solid evidence for photoaging, an off-label use for this acne-indicated product; both are graded in full on [[tretinoin]]. The question specific to this product is Altreno's own registration-trial data.

Two double-blind, vehicle-controlled, 12-week, two-arm, parallel-group trials randomized "a total of 1640 patients, 9-58 years of age" to Altreno or vehicle for moderate-to-severe acne; the results from the two trials were pooled and analyzed together.[^tyring] At week 12, "tretinoin 0.05% lotion demonstrated statistically significant superiority to vehicle in reducing inflammatory and noninflammatory lesion counts (both P less than .001)," with "mean percent change in inflammatory and noninflammatory lesions" of 52% and 46% respectively, and "treatment success (a 2-grade improvement in EGSS and 'clear' or 'almost clear')" reported in 18% of patients.[^tyring] The same paper reports the trials as "very well tolerated with no substantive differences in cutaneous tolerability among treatment groups," with "no patients discontinued treatment because of adverse events."[^tyring]

The FDA label's own adverse-reaction table, pooling the same two trials (Altreno N=767, vehicle N=783), shows the most common reactions all more frequent on Altreno than vehicle: application-site dryness in 4% (29/767) versus <1% (1/783), pain (defined as stinging, burning, or pain) in 3% (25/767) versus <1% (3/783), erythema in 2% (12/767) versus <1% (1/783), irritation in 1% (7/767) versus <1% (1/783), and exfoliation in 1% (6/767) versus <1% (3/783).[^dailymed]

> **Bottom line.** Altreno's own two pooled registration trials (n=1,640) found significant lesion reduction versus vehicle at 12 weeks, with an 18% EGSS treatment-success rate, and the label's adverse-reaction data show low absolute rates of irritation-type reactions, all higher than vehicle but well tolerated overall. No head-to-head trial against a conventional (non-lotion) tretinoin product is cited on the label.[^dailymed][^tyring]

## What's In It

- **Active: tretinoin**, at a labeled 0.05% in "an opaque, pale yellow topical lotion."[^dailymed] This is the only ingredient with graded efficacy; its mechanism and full evidence are on [[tretinoin]].
- **Hydrating/base ingredients the manufacturer highlights:** [[glycerin]], sodium hyaluronate (a form of [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]]), and soluble collagen.[^dailymed][^altreno] The label's own drug-interaction warnings note Altreno "contains fish proteins" and advise telling a healthcare provider about a fish allergy, consistent with a marine-derived collagen source.[^altreno]
- **Full inactive-ingredient list, per the FDA label:** benzyl alcohol, butylated hydroxytoluene, carbomer copolymer type B (Pemulen TR-1), carbomer homopolymer type A (Carbopol 981), glycerin, methylparaben, mineral oil, octoxynol-9, purified water, sodium hyaluronate, soluble collagen, and trolamine.[^dailymed] These are formulation excipients; the label attributes no independent acne efficacy to them.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"The first and only tretinoin 0.05% in a lotion formulation" / "the only tretinoin in a water-based lotion formulation proven to treat acne."**[^altreno] A manufacturer formulation-positioning claim about being first-to-market with this vehicle; Altreno's own acne efficacy is supported by its cited registration trials, but the label does not include a controlled comparison against a conventional (non-lotion) tretinoin product, so a claim that the lotion vehicle itself outperforms plain tretinoin is not supported by anything cited here.
- **"Free of fragrance, silicone, and alcohol."**[^altreno] The FDA label lists benzyl alcohol among the inactive ingredients, so this claim is best read as excluding drying/denatured alcohol, not every alcohol-derived excipient.[^dailymed]
- **"The result of five years of research, development and testing."**[^altreno] An unverifiable manufacturer development-timeline claim, not addressed by anything cited here.

## Where to Buy

Altreno is dispensed by prescription only, not sold over the counter. The manufacturer lists an MSRP of about $60 for the 20 g size (roughly 45 days of use) and about $115 for the 45 g size (roughly three months).[^altreno]
