Cetaphil Gentle Skin Cleanser is a low-foaming, non-alkaline cream cleanser for normal to dry and sensitive skin, whose current US formula lists glycerin, niacinamide (vitamin B3), and panthenol (vitamin B5).[^1]

> A cheap, low-foaming cleanser that dermatologists have recommended for decades for sensitive, rosacea-, and acne-prone skin. A cleanser's health-relevant job is to remove dirt, oil, and makeup without stripping the skin barrier, and in the one published study of this exact product, two weeks of use did not raise water loss through the skin or cause irritation. Reviewers praise how gentle it is, and the same gentleness means it can leave heavy makeup and oily skin not fully cleaned. Keeping barrier-impaired skin moisturized is the better-evidenced routine, set out on [[moisturizing]].[^2][^4][^5]

## Summary

Cetaphil Gentle Skin Cleanser is a mild, low-foaming cream cleanser made for normal to dry and sensitive skin. Its job is a health one: lifting the day's dirt, oil, and makeup without stripping the skin barrier that holds moisture in and keeps irritants out. It is built on a single mild syndet (synthetic-detergent) surfactant, [sodium cocoyl isethionate](#whats-in-it), rather than a high-foaming sulfate or a true alkaline soap, and Cetaphil describes it as a "creamy formula" using "Micellar Technology."[^1] The dermatologist who ran the one published study of it calls it a "nonalkaline nonirritating cleanser."[^2]

Two questions decide how much that is worth. The first is a category question: does a gentle, non-alkaline cleanser protect the skin more than a harsher wash? On tolerability the category evidence leans yes. A study of 29 soaps and cleansers found that a product's irritation rose with its pH, and that syndet cleansers sat at the low-irritation end ([Baranda 2002](#the-evidence)).[^3] The best-evidenced routine for dry or barrier-impaired skin is leave-on moisturizing rather than a rinse-off wash, set out on [[moisturizing]] and [[skin-barrier-repair]].

The second question is whether this is a good example of a gentle cleanser, and the reputation is strong. Wirecutter writes that "for decades, dermatologists have recommended Cetaphil Gentle Skin Cleanser, especially for those with sensitive skin or acne."[^4] Allure, which calls it a "multiple-award-winning" formula, describes it as "dermatologist-gold-standard kind of gentle," and cosmetic chemist Ni'Kita Wilson credits the fatty alcohols in the formula, "which coat the skin to prevent the detergents from drying it out."[^5] The recurring criticism is the flip side of that gentleness: Allure notes that "because it's so gentle it doesn't always cut through heavy makeup,"[^5] and in Wirecutter's testing the cleanser "tended to trigger sensitivity and breakouts for our testers with sensitive skin" and did not leave skin "feeling especially sanitized."[^4]

It is inexpensive. Target lists the 16 fl oz bottle at $12.89, about $0.81 per fluid ounce ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-07-28).[^6] The one published study of this exact product, with its limits, is in [The Evidence](#the-evidence); the manufacturer's clinical-sounding label claims are separated out under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## The Evidence

A cleanser's health-relevant task is to remove dirt, oil, and makeup with as little disruption to the skin barrier as possible; keeping dry or barrier-impaired skin moisturized is one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines, set out on [[moisturizing]] and [[skin-barrier-repair]]. What is specific to gentle cleansing, and to this product, is narrower.

Baranda 2002 applied 8% emulsions of 29 soaps and liquid cleansers to the forearms of 30 people with sensitive skin for 5 consecutive days and scored the resulting irritation. "A significant correlation between pH and the IrIn [irritation index] of cleansers was found (P < 0.006)," and the low-irritation products were mostly syndet cleansers rather than true soaps.[^3]

> **Bottom line.** Across 29 cleansers, the more alkaline the product, the more it irritated sensitive skin, and gentle syndet cleansers clustered at the low-irritation end.[^3]

Draelos 2006 studied Cetaphil Gentle Skin Cleanser itself. Thirty patients with mild to moderate rosacea used it twice daily for 2 weeks, following a 2-week washout with Dove Sensitive Skin Bar and with no other rosacea treatment. "No significant increase in TEWL was demonstrated at any point during the study, indicating that the gentle skin cleanser did not damage the skin barrier," and "there were no increases in erythema, scaling, dryness, stinging, burning, or lack of smoothness in the skin." A post hoc analysis found a "significant reduction (P<.05) in investigator-assessed rosacea severity" on several facial areas compared with the end of the Dove washout. The study had no parallel control arm, enrolled 30 patients, and ran for 2 weeks.[^2]

> **Bottom line.** Over two weeks, this cleanser did not raise water loss through the skin or cause irritation in rosacea patients, and rosacea severity fell versus the end of a washout on a Dove bar. It was a small, short, single-arm study, and the rosacea-severity result came from a post hoc analysis.[^2]

The published study tested an earlier version of the formula. Allure's 2021 review describes an "eight-ingredient formula,"[^5] while the manufacturer's current US listing shows a ten-ingredient formula that adds niacinamide, panthenol, and glycerin.[^1]

## What's In It

The ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the surfactant, texture, and preservative system.

Humectants and skin-conditioning ingredients:

- [[glycerin]], a humectant and the second ingredient
- panthenol (provitamin B5), a humectant and skin-conditioning agent
- niacinamide (vitamin B3), which at leave-on concentrations has good evidence for supporting the skin barrier and evening out tone; see [[niacinamide]]

Cleansing, texture, and preservative system: water, cetearyl alcohol (an emollient fatty alcohol and thickener), pantolactone, xanthan gum (a thickener), sodium cocoyl isethionate (a mild syndet surfactant), sodium benzoate (a preservative), and citric acid (a pH adjuster).[^1]

The formula contains no added fragrance, no sulfate detergent such as sodium lauryl or laureth sulfate, and no drying alcohol (the cetearyl alcohol is an emollient fatty alcohol, not the drying kind).[^1] Because niacinamide and panthenol sit in a product that rinses off, their listed presence is a fact about the formula, not the same as the leave-on doses studied for those ingredients.

## Tolerability

In the one published study of the product, no patient showed an increase in TEWL or reported irritation over 2 weeks (Draelos 2006).[^2] Cetaphil labels the cleanser "fragrance free," states it "won't clog pores," and describes it as "Dermatologist tested and clinically proven to be gentle on sensitive skin."[^1] These are manufacturer label claims. Wirecutter reports a different experience for some users: in its testing the cleanser "tended to trigger sensitivity and breakouts for our testers with sensitive skin."[^4]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Clinically proven to provide continuous hydration to protect against dryness"** and **"clinically proven to leave skin hydrated after cleansing."** Manufacturer label claims.[^1] The one independent published study of the product measured skin barrier function (TEWL) and rosacea severity, not a hydration-duration endpoint (Draelos 2006).[^2]
- **"Formulated with Micellar Technology"** and a **"dermatologist-backed blend of niacinamide (vitamin B3), panthenol."** Composition and positioning claims.[^1] The niacinamide and panthenol appear in the ingredient list; their listed presence in a rinse-off product is a composition fact, not a measured clinical outcome.
- **"95% of users felt the product gently cleansed their skin."** A manufacturer consumer-survey claim.[^1]
- **"Dermatologist tested and clinically proven to be gentle on sensitive skin."** A label claim.[^1] Wirecutter's testers with sensitive skin reported sensitivity and breakouts.[^4]
