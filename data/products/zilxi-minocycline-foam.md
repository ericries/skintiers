Zilxi is a prescription-only 1.5% minocycline topical foam, the first topical minocycline the FDA approved for the inflammatory lesions of rosacea in adults, applied once daily to the whole face at least an hour before bedtime.[^dailymed] It is not a cosmetic product and it is a different drug from the 4% minocycline foam (Amzeeq) sold for acne: Zilxi's own label states its mechanism of action "is unknown," and its limitations-of-use statement notes the formulation "has not been evaluated in the treatment of infections."[^dailymed]

> A branded prescription rosacea drug with its own randomized-trial base. In two identical 12-week vehicle-controlled trials it brought roughly half of patients to clear or almost-clear skin, about 9 to 10 percentage points more than an inert vehicle achieved on its own, and cut inflammatory lesion counts by roughly 60%.[^gold][^dailymed] The propellant is flammable, and the most commonly reported adverse reaction was diarrhea in 1% of users versus 0% on vehicle.[^dailymed] No price data could be verified for this product, so [pricing is left to the retailer link below](#where-to-buy).

## Summary

Zilxi is the brand name for minocycline 1.5% topical foam, a prescription drug Journey Medical Corporation markets exclusively for the inflammatory lesions of rosacea, the papules and pustules of [[rosacea-papulopustular|papulopustular rosacea]].[^dailymed] It shares an active ingredient class, a tetracycline-class antibiotic, with the acne-indicated 4% foam and with oral minocycline capsules covered on [[minocycline-topical-oral]], but Zilxi is a distinct FDA approval at a lower concentration (1.5% vs 4%), tested only in rosacea, and that ingredient page's rubric does not carry over to this product; the trial base below is Zilxi's own.

The FDA approved Zilxi on the strength of two identically designed, 12-week, double-blind, vehicle-controlled phase 3 trials (Trial 1, NCT02601963, and Trial 2, NCT03142451) in a combined 1,522 subjects with an inflammatory lesion count of 15 to 75 and a baseline Investigator Global Assessment (IGA) of moderate or severe.[^dailymed] Once-daily Zilxi reached IGA treatment success, defined as clear or almost clear with at least a two-grade improvement, in 52.1% and 49.1% of subjects across the two trials, against 43.0% and 39.0% on vehicle, while cutting inflammatory lesion counts by a mean 61.3% and 60.2% versus 54.1% and 48.9% on vehicle ([the evidence](#the-evidence)).[^dailymed][^gold]

Systemic exposure was low in a companion pharmacokinetic study: after 14 days of once-daily use, mean plasma minocycline concentration peaked at 1.3 ± 0.9 ng/mL, with steady state reached by day 1 and no evidence of systemic accumulation.[^dailymed] Local tolerability at week 12 was comparable to or better than baseline, and comparable to vehicle in frequency and severity; the most common systemic adverse reaction was diarrhea, in 1% of Zilxi-treated subjects versus 0% on vehicle.[^dailymed] The label also carries the class-wide tetracycline warnings, tooth discoloration in children under 8, a flammable aerosol propellant, and a caution against use in pregnancy and breastfeeding, drawn largely from oral tetracycline data rather than topical Zilxi's own trials.[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

**Vehicle-controlled approval trials (Gold and colleagues, 2020).** Two identical, randomized, multicenter, double-blind, 12-week phase 3 trials (labeled FX2016-11 and FX2016-12 in the peer-reviewed report, and Trial 1/Trial 2 on the FDA label) compared once-daily minocycline 1.5% foam against an inert vehicle foam in adults with moderate to severe papulopustular rosacea.[^gold][^dailymed] Inflammatory lesion counts fell by a mean 17.6 and 18.4 with Zilxi versus 15.4 and 14.5 with vehicle (-17.57 vs -15.65, P=.0031 in the first trial; -18.54 vs -14.88, P<.0001 in the second), a mean percent reduction of 61.3% and 60.2% versus 54.1% and 48.9% on vehicle.[^gold][^dailymed] IGA treatment success reached 52.1% and 49.1% of Zilxi-treated subjects versus 43.0% and 39.0% on vehicle (P=.0273 and P=.0077), treatment differences of 9.0 and 10.2 percentage points.[^gold][^dailymed] No serious treatment-related adverse events occurred in either trial.[^gold]

> Bottom line: against an inactive vehicle foam, once-daily Zilxi lifted the IGA clear-or-almost-clear rate by 9 to 10 percentage points and cut inflammatory lesion counts by roughly 60%, a real but incremental gain over the vehicle's own substantial effect.[^dailymed]

**Pharmacokinetics.** A separate study in 20 adults with inflammatory rosacea lesions applying about 2 grams of Zilxi daily for 14 days found a mean maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) of 1.3 ± 0.9 ng/mL and an AUC0-24h of 22.5 ± 16.2 h·ng/mL on day 1, with steady state reached by day 1 and "systemic accumulation of minocycline was not evident."[^dailymed]

Neither trial compared Zilxi head-to-head against another rosacea drug, so this evidence establishes benefit over vehicle only, not superiority over [[soolantra-ivermectin-1-cream|ivermectin]], [[finacea-azelaic-acid-15-gel|azelaic acid]], or [[epsolay-benzoyl-peroxide-5-cream|encapsulated benzoyl peroxide]].

## What's In It

- **Active: minocycline 1.5%** as micronized minocycline hydrochloride. Each gram of Zilxi contains 15 mg minocycline, equivalent to 16 mg minocycline hydrochloride, in a yellow suspension foam.[^dailymed]
- **Inactive ingredients:** soybean oil, coconut oil, light mineral oil, cyclomethicone, cetostearyl alcohol, stearic acid, myristyl alcohol, hydrogenated castor oil, white wax (beeswax), stearyl alcohol, docosanol, as declared on the FDA label. The foam is dispensed from an aluminum can pressurized with a butane/isobutane/propane propellant.[^dailymed]

The label states the mechanism of action for treating rosacea's inflammatory lesions "is unknown," and separately warns Zilxi's formulation "has not been evaluated in the treatment of infections."[^dailymed] It is contraindicated in anyone with hypersensitivity to any tetracycline or to any ingredient in the formula.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand marketing page was reviewed for this product; the claims below come from the FDA label's own characterization rather than promotional copy.

- **"The first FDA-approved minocycline-based topical for rosacea."** This describes Zilxi's approval status, not a claim of superior efficacy; the trials that supported it showed benefit over vehicle, not superiority over other approved rosacea topicals.[^dailymed]
- **Framing minocycline foam as "safer" than oral minocycline because of low systemic exposure.** Supported specifically for Zilxi's own pharmacokinetic data (mean Cmax 1.3 ng/mL, no accumulation over 14 days), but oral minocycline's documented systemic risks are a different drug and route; see [[minocycline-topical-oral]] for that separate profile.[^dailymed]
