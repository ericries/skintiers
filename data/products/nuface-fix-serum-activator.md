## Summary

A $49 conductive serum sold to be used under a NuFACE microcurrent device.[^brand] **Bottom line:** the brand's own page calls it "an FDA-approved, full-face silky serum", and no FDA approval exists for a product like this. The page also carries an "FDA CLEARED" badge a few lines earlier, which is a different claim again, and openFDA returns no clearance of any kind naming a NuFACE serum.[^openfda]

> **The two regulatory words are not interchangeable, and neither fits.** Approval is what a drug gets. Clearance is what a device gets, and the firm does hold one for a conductive medium: K161654, the NuFACE Gel Primer, covered at [[nuface-gel-primer]].[^openfda] A search of the FDA 510(k) index for this applicant returns no device named as a serum.[^openfda] Whether this serum is sold under that gel clearance or as a plain cosmetic is not something the public record settles, and this page does not guess.

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: unknown, for the job it is sold for.** The brand says the serum "is designed to conduct microcurrent down to the muscle and helps boost device performance".[^brand] Conductivity is a measurable bench property, and no number is given: no conductivity value, no impedance figure, no comparison against the gel or against plain water. The claim that current reaches muscle is handled on [[nuface-gel-primer]], whose cleared indication concerns the electrode-to-skin interface rather than depth, and is not re-argued here.
- **Effect size: minimal, for the skincare benefits.** Three peptides lead the actives, at positions six, seven and eight: Palmitoyl Hexapeptide-52, Palmitoyl Heptapeptide-18 and N-Prolyl Palmitoyl Tripeptide-56 Acetate.[^brand] [[peptides]] is graded **weak** here on its own evidence. [[hyaluronic-acid]], graded strong, appears ninth as the hydrolysed form.
- **Composition finding: some of the effect is pigment.** The list ends Bismuth Oxychloride (CI 77163) and Iron Oxides (CI 77491, CI 77492), with Mica and Silica above them.[^brand] The brand does not hide this, describing "light-reflecting pigments" among what makes skin look "softer, smoother, and more radiant".[^brand] That is an optical finish, the same mechanism as a blurring primer, and it is worth separating from the peptide claims it sits beside. See [[iron-oxides]].
- **Record finding: unreplaced template text on a live product page.** Under the heading for the technology behind the product, the page opens with the instruction "Describe the technology behind this skincare product", going on to suggest covering what makes it work with microcurrent or the science behind the formulation.[^brand] That is a content-management placeholder instructing a copywriter, published as if it were copy.
- **What is not claimed.** No trial of this serum is cited anywhere on the page, and the "CLINICALLY PROVEN" badge is not attached to any study.[^brand]

## The Evidence

None specific to this product. Peptide evidence is on [[peptides]] and hyaluronic acid's is on [[hyaluronic-acid]]; the microcurrent question belongs to [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]] and [[nuface-plus]], where the filings are read.

What is specific to this product is a regulatory claim that does not survive a look at the register, and a formula in which the most immediately visible ingredients are pigments.

> **Bottom line.** If you own a NuFACE device and want a conductive medium, the gel is the one with a clearance behind it. This serum costs more, publishes no conductivity figure, and is described on its own page with a regulatory term that does not apply to it.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own product page and **not** endorsed here.

- **"FIX Serum Activator is an FDA-approved, full-face silky serum"**[^brand] FDA approval applies to drugs. A conductive cosmetic serum is not an approved drug, and no approval or clearance naming a NuFACE serum appears in the FDA device index.[^openfda]
- **"FDA CLEARED"**, as a badge on the same page.[^brand] The firm does hold a conductive-medium clearance, for the Gel Primer. This page carries both claims at once.
- **"Conducts microcurrent down to the muscle to optimize performance"**[^brand] Depth is the part that the cleared indication for the brand's gel does not cover; see [[nuface-gel-primer]].
- **"Brightens under-eyes"**[^brand] With mica, bismuth oxychloride and iron oxides in the formula, brightening under the eye is at least partly what a light-reflecting pigment does on contact.
- **"CLINICALLY PROVEN"**, badge, with no study named.[^brand]
