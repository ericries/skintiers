A prescription azole antifungal cream, and a genuinely short course: **once daily for one week** for jock itch and ringworm, two weeks for athlete's foot between the toes.[^label] Unlike most of the antifungals covered here it is an approved drug rather than an over-the-counter monograph product, cleared under NDA 204153.[^ndc] The molecule's general case is on [[luliconazole]].

**Bottom line:** the dosing is the selling point and the label supports it. The cure rates are the part worth reading before you expect much.

> **Approved, and still short of a cure for most adults.** Four weeks after a 14-day course for athlete's foot, complete clearance was "28 (26%)" and "15 (14%)" against vehicle's "2 (2%)" and "3 (3%)" in the two trials.[^label] That is a real drug effect and it is also roughly one adult in four at best. Mycological cure, meaning the fungus is gone, ran far higher at 62% and 56%. Clearing the organism is easier than clearing the rash.

## Summary

### What it treats, exactly

The indication is narrow and names the organisms: "interdigital tinea pedis, tinea cruris, and tinea corporis caused by the organisms Trichophyton rubrum and Epidermophyton floccosum".[^label] Three conditions, covered here as [[athletes-foot]], [[tinea-cruris-jock-itch]] and [[tinea-corporis-ringworm]].

Two limits are in that sentence. **Interdigital** means between the toes; the moccasin pattern across the sole and sides is not an approved use, the same gap [[lotrimin-ultra-butenafine-1-cream]] has. And the two named dermatophytes are not the only things that cause these rashes.

## The Rubric

**Dosing: the clearest advantage.** One week, once daily, for tinea cruris and tinea corporis; two weeks for interdigital tinea pedis, applied to the affected area "and approximately 1 inch of the immediate surrounding area(s)".[^label] A week of once-daily is a regimen people finish.

**Adult efficacy: statistically real, modest in absolute terms.** Jock itch, one trial of 256 subjects: complete clearance "35 (21%)" versus "4 (4%)", with mycological cure "129 (78%)" versus "41 (45%)".[^label] Athlete's foot as above. The drug beats its vehicle every time; it clears about a fifth to a quarter of people completely.

**The ringworm numbers come from children, and the comparator did unusually well.** The tinea corporis indication rests on a single trial "in 75 subjects age 2 to <18 years old", mean age 8.[^label] Complete clearance was "36 (71%)" on the drug against "5 (36%)" on vehicle. The drug arm looks far better than any adult arm here, but so does the vehicle arm, which cleared 36% where adult vehicle arms cleared 2 to 4%. The arms were also lopsided, 51 children on drug against 14 on vehicle, and 14 is a thin basis for a comparator. Read the 71% as a number from a different population under a different natural history, not as evidence the drug works better on ringworm than on jock itch.

**Tolerability: quiet.** "The most common adverse reactions observed in clinical trials were application site reactions, which occurred in less than 1% of subjects", across 616 subjects in three Phase 3 trials.[^label] Contraindications: "None."[^label] There are no human pregnancy data; the label says so plainly.[^label]

**Composition.** 10 mg luliconazole per gram in a vehicle of benzyl alcohol, butylated hydroxytoluene, cetostearyl alcohol, isopropyl myristate, medium-chain triglycerides, methylparaben, polysorbate 60, propylene glycol, purified water and sorbitan monostearate.[^label] Three of those are worth knowing if you react to creams: benzyl alcohol, propylene glycol and the sorbitan ester, a close relative of [[sorbitan-sesquioleate]].

**Price: not recorded.** It is a prescription product whose cost depends on insurance, and no reliable figure was sought.

## The Evidence

Four vehicle-controlled trials, all described on the label and all submitted in support of the approval: two in interdigital tinea pedis (423 subjects together), one in tinea cruris (256), and one in tinea corporis (75 children).[^label] No head-to-head against another antifungal appears on the label, so nothing here says whether it beats the over-the-counter options, only that it beats its own vehicle. Study-level detail for the molecule sits on [[luliconazole]].

The figures quoted above are the label's own tables. The two readings this page adds are arithmetic on those tables rather than label claims: that mycological cure runs well above complete clearance in every adult arm, and that the paediatric vehicle arm cleared roughly ten times the rate of the adult vehicle arms.

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand marketing copy was fetched for this page, so there are no promotional claims to quarantine. Everything above is from the FDA-approved label and the NDC directory. The one line on the carton that reads like a claim is a restriction: "For Topical Use Only / Not for ophthalmic, oral, or intravaginal use".[^ndc]

## Who it is for

Someone with a confirmed dermatophyte infection who wants the shortest course available and has a prescription. If you have not seen a clinician, the over-the-counter [[lotrimin-ultra-butenafine-1-cream]] offers a one-week regimen for two of the same three infections without one.

Two presentations are marketed, a 60 g tube and a carton of twelve 2 g tubes, both currently active.[^ndc]
