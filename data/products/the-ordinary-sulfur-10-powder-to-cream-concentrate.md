The Ordinary Sulfur 10% Powder-to-Cream Concentrate is a 5 g leave-on acne spot treatment from [[the-ordinary|The Ordinary]] that lists a disclosed 10% sulfur as its only active ingredient, priced at $9.90 on the manufacturer's US site.[^ord]

> A cheap, single-active sulfur spot treatment sold in an unusual powder-that-turns-to-cream format. Sulfur is a real but modest, decades-old keratolytic and antibacterial acne active whose own acne evidence a 2020 review called uncertain, well behind benzoyl peroxide, azelaic acid, and the retinoids; the full case is on [[sulfur]]. No trial isolates this product, so the grade rests on that ingredient-level evidence plus a straightforward, clearly disclosed 10% delivery, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence).[^ord]

## Summary

The Ordinary sells this as a 5 g tube of loose powder that, per the manufacturer, "becomes a light emulsion" when rubbed onto skin under finger pressure, for $9.90 on its US site (accessed 2026-09-17).[^ord] The ingredient panel names one active: "Sulfur (10%)," alongside 14 inactive ingredients including niacinamide, L-carnitine (as levocarnitine), kaolin clay, and a silica-based powder base.[^ord] The Ordinary describes the formulation as using "colloidal sulfur which has a smaller particle size to help provide effective delivery and minimize the visibility and odour of the ingredient," a longstanding complaint about topical sulfur.[^ord]

Sulfur is a real, long-used topical acne active with keratolytic and antibacterial action, but its acne-specific trial base is thin; a 2020 evidence review of six topical acne treatments stated plainly that reviewers "were uncertain about the effects of sulfur," placing it behind [[benzoyl-peroxide]], [[azelaic-acid]], and the [[retinoids]]; the fuller case, including sulfur's stronger rosacea evidence in combination with sodium sulfacetamide, is on [[sulfur]]. No published trial tests this exact product, and the disclosed 10% standalone concentration here is higher than, and formulated differently from, the 5% sulfur-plus-sulfacetamide combination cream behind the strongest sulfur trial data; [see The Evidence](#the-evidence).

The manufacturer's own directions describe once-to-three-times-daily spot application directly onto a blemish, starting with one application daily "because excessive drying of the skin may occur," and not rinsing it off.[^ord] The label also warns against combining it with another topical acne medication at the same time, against use on broken skin, and notes that sulfur "may react with silver surfaces," recommending jewelry be removed before application.[^ord] The Ordinary's own product page frames it as "a gentle approach to treating acne," positioned for sensitive skin, [a claim reported as marketing below](#common-marketing-claims).[^ord]

## The Evidence

No published trial of this product was located. Sulfur's own evidence base for acne is set out on [[sulfur]]: an abridged Cochrane-derived review covering azelaic acid, salicylic acid, nicotinamide, sulfur, zinc, and fruit acid concluded reviewers "were uncertain about the effects of sulfur and zinc."[^cochrane] The strongest controlled trial behind topical sulfur tested a combination sodium sulfacetamide 10%/sulfur 5% cream against metronidazole 0.75% cream for rosacea, not acne, and not sulfur alone; that trial found the combination reduced inflammatory lesions more than metronidazole (80% vs 72%, P=.04), with poorer tolerability, in 152 rosacea patients over 12 weeks.[^torok]

> **Bottom line.** This product supplies sulfur at a disclosed, higher-than-typically-tested 10% concentration, alone rather than paired with sodium sulfacetamide, in a spot-treatment format. Its case rests on sulfur's general, uncertain-graded acne evidence and a transparent active concentration, not on a trial of the product itself or of standalone 10% sulfur.[^cochrane][^torok]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Starts to treat pimples on application to reduce size and redness in under 1 hour," targeting a pimple's "height, diameter, redness."** A specific, fast efficacy timeframe stated on the product page, presented here as the brand's own claim.[^ord]
- **"A gentle approach to treating acne... ideal for those with sensitive skin."** A brand positioning claim. Sulfur is generally well tolerated, but it is not free of irritation risk: the comparative rosacea trial behind sulfur's strongest evidence recorded poor tolerance in 7 of 75 subjects on a sulfacetamide/sulfur cream,[^torok] and The Ordinary's own label warns of "excessive drying of the skin" and recommends starting with one application a day (see [[sulfur]] and the usage directions above).[^ord]
- **Sulfur "controls oil, unclogs pores, reduces inflammation, and fights bacteria."** FAQ copy on the product page describing sulfur's proposed mechanisms; these are the ingredient's traditionally cited actions ([[sulfur]] covers the pharmacology), not outcomes independently measured for this formula.[^ord]
