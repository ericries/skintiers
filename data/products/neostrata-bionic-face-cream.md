## Summary

Bionic Face Cream is NeoStrata's PHA-first emollient: a heavy, balm-like cream whose exfoliating and humectant actives are [[gluconolactone-pha]] (second on the ingredient list) and [[lactobionic-acid]] (fourth), declared together as "12% PHA."[^1] Its "Bionic Acid" branding for lactobionic acid appears throughout the product copy.[^1]

The general case for these actives, modest and preliminary evidence that lactobionic acid raises measured skin hydration at 6% to 30% and irritates less than glycolic acid in one head-to-head trial, is set out on [[lactobionic-acid]], with the closely related gluconolactone evidence on [[gluconolactone-pha]]. This product declares 12% PHA as a combined total of both actives without disclosing the split, so it's unclear whether either individually reaches the concentrations those trials tested. No published trial tests this specific formula, so the grades above rest on category evidence ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)).

Two practical notes. First, the brand's own page marks the product DISCONTINUED with no purchase option, at a last-listed price of $66.00 for 40 g, and names PHA Barrier Recovery Cream as the suggested replacement (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)).[^1] Second, the formula is fragrance free but does contain sodium sulfite and sodium bisulfite (antioxidant preservatives) and two dyes (yellow 5, red 33), which sensitive-skin buyers screening ingredient lists may want to know.[^1]

## The Evidence

Nothing product-specific was found: no published trial tests Bionic Face Cream or its exact 12% gluconolactone-plus-lactobionic-acid formula, so its grades rest on the category evidence for its two PHAs.[^1]

That evidence, laid out with its numbers and limits on [[lactobionic-acid]] and [[gluconolactone-pha]], is modest and preliminary: several small, unblinded trials found lactobionic acid raised corneometry-measured hydration at concentrations from 6% to 30%, and one 77-subject comparison found it produced less measured irritation and barrier disruption than glycolic acid. This product declares 12% PHA as a combined total of gluconolactone plus lactobionic acid; the label doesn't disclose the split between the two, so it's unclear whether either active individually reaches the concentrations those trials tested. The anti-aging half of the pitch is weaker: the line, discoloration, and matrix-metalloproteinase claims for bionic acids trace to a narrative review co-authored by NeoStrata-affiliated scientists, not a controlled skin trial.

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product, and its 12% PHA figure is an undisclosed-split total rather than a per-active concentration, so it's not clear either active individually reaches the range where lactobionic acid's small hydration and tolerability trials were run. The hydration and gentleness grades still carry over as category evidence, but only loosely; the anti-aging claims rest on manufacturer-affiliated review material and grade lower.

Actives and notable ingredients:

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by product-specific evidence:

- **"Helps to revitalize the look of stressed skin and improve texture."** Appearance language with no cited product trial; the supportable core is PHA-driven hydration and mild exfoliation, per the category evidence on [[lactobionic-acid]].[^1]
- **"Bionic Acid: a natural humectant that forms a water-binding matrix to help attract moisture to the skin barrier."** "Bionic Acid" is NeoStrata's own branding for lactobionic acid, and the "water-binding matrix" mechanism comes from manufacturer-affiliated review literature, not a controlled skin trial.[^1]
- **Reduces "the appearance of lines and wrinkles, discoloration, enlarged pores and roughness."** These bionic-acid claims trace to a narrative review by NeoStrata-affiliated authors; no controlled trial demonstrates them for this ingredient, let alone this product.[^1]
- **"Non-comedogenic."** A manufacturer testing claim with no published methodology or result on the product page.[^1]
