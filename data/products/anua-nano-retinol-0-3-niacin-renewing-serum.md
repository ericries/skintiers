## Summary

Anua Nano Retinol 0.3% + Niacin Renewing Serum pairs two evidenced actives, retinol and niacinamide, in a single water-based leave-on serum aimed at the same photoaging concerns as other over-the-counter retinol products.[^anua] At the category level, topical retinol has controlled trial evidence for increasing skin's own collagen-building activity (procollagen I and glycosaminoglycan), a measurable change in skin biology, and for improving fine-wrinkle appearance scores, a cosmetic effect; that evidence is set out in full on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]] and summarized with its source study in [the evidence](#the-evidence).[^kafi] No published trial tests this exact serum. Its own retinol is declared at 0.3% and sits low in the ingredient order, at the lower end of the concentrations used by comparator over-the-counter retinol serums such as The Ordinary Retinol 0.5% in Squalane and CeraVe Resurfacing Retinol Serum, and like every over-the-counter retinol it remains milder than prescription tretinoin, because retinol needs two enzymatic conversion steps in the skin before it becomes active.[^anua][^zasada][^huang]

What sets this serum apart from a plain retinol product is niacinamide, third on the ingredient list.[^anua] A double-blind, industry-run trial of niacinamide at a similar concentration reported, after 12 weeks, reduced fine lines and wrinkles, fewer hyperpigmented spots, less redness, and less sallowness, a cosmetic improvement in tone and texture rather than a measured health endpoint; see [the evidence](#the-evidence) for the study and [[niacinamide]] for the broader ingredient-level case.[^bissett]

The rest of the formula is a barrier-supporting ceramide, humectants (glycerin and hyaluronic acid), and antioxidant and soothing plant extracts, listed in [what's in it](#whats-in-it); the "BHA" on the ingredient list is the antioxidant butylated hydroxyanisole, not a beta-hydroxy exfoliating acid.[^anua] The manufacturer's own marketing describes a proprietary "nano" delivery system and cites a single-use, 730% hydration measurement and a cell-turnover claim; none of these is backed by independent, product-specific evidence, set out in [common marketing claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^anua]

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinol improves aged skin, and how the retinoid family ranks by strength and evidence, is covered on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]. No published trial tests this exact serum, so the relevant evidence sits at the ingredient level.

Kafi and colleagues (2007) ran the trial most often cited for over-the-counter retinol: a randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled study of a 0.4% retinol lotion applied up to three times a week to the arms of 36 elderly participants (mean age 87) for 24 weeks. They reported significant differences between retinol-treated and vehicle-treated skin for fine-wrinkling scores, and increased glycosaminoglycan and procollagen I in the treated skin.[^kafi]

> **Bottom line.** Topical retinol has controlled evidence for improving fine wrinkles, but the study that shows it used a higher concentration than this serum, a different base, and arm skin rather than the face.[^kafi]

Retinol is not the receptor-active molecule. The skin oxidizes it through two enzymatic steps to reach retinoic acid, and activity is lost at each step. Saurat and colleagues measured this ranking directly in skin, and Zasada and Budzisz describe retinol as weaker than retinoic acid for anti-wrinkle endpoints.[^saurat][^zasada] The trial-validated benchmark topical is prescription tretinoin, retinoic acid itself: a 2025 meta-analysis of eight randomized trials found tretinoin significantly better than vehicle for fine and coarse wrinkles.[^huang] This serum's retinol is declared at 0.3%, at the lower end of the retinol serums it competes with.[^anua]

> **Bottom line.** Like every over-the-counter retinol, this serum is milder than prescription tretinoin, because retinol has to be converted in the skin before it can act, and it is dosed at the low end of its category.[^anua]

The serum's other named active is niacinamide, third on the ingredient list.[^anua] Bissett, Oblong and Berge (2005) ran a double-blind, split-face trial of 5% niacinamide in 50 women with photoaging over 12 weeks and reported reductions in fine lines and wrinkles, hyperpigmented spots, red blotchiness, and skin sallowness; the work came from Procter and Gamble.[^bissett]

> **Bottom line.** Niacinamide is the ingredient that most distinguishes this serum from a plain retinol product, and a single industry-run trial of niacinamide at a similar concentration reported improvements in tone and fine lines.[^bissett] Broader niacinamide evidence is on [[niacinamide]].

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer for the current US product.[^anua] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

Actives and support ingredients:

- [[retinol]], declared at 0.3% and appearing low in the ingredient order, below the ingredients present above roughly one percent (see [[retinoids]] for where retinol sits in the family)
- [[niacinamide]], third on the list
- ceramide NP ([[ceramides]]), a barrier lipid
- [[glycerin]] and hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]), humectants
- panthenol, allantoin, adenosine, beta-glucan, squalane, and dipotassium glycyrrhizate, soothing and hydrating support ingredients

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, glycereth-26, caprylic/capric triglyceride, glyceryl citrate/lactate/linoleate/oleate, propanediol, ammonium acryloyldimethyltaurate/VP copolymer, cetyl ethylhexanoate, polyacrylate crosspolymer-6, methylpropanediol, butylene glycol, 1,2-hexanediol, pentylene glycol, polysorbate 20, carbomer, tromethamine, ethylhexylglycerin, disodium EDTA, hydrogenated lecithin, polyquaternium-51, dipropylene glycol, oleic acid, and maltodextrin.

The manufacturer's ingredient list contains no free salicylic acid or beta-hydroxy exfoliating acid. The "BHA" in the list is butylated hydroxyanisole, an antioxidant listed beside butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT), not a beta-hydroxy acid.[^anua]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Nano Retinol" and "delivers retinol deeper into skin layers with significantly less irritation."** A delivery claim about the vehicle. No independent trial of this product reports its clinical effect.[^anua]
- **"Skin moisture improved by 730% after 1 use."** A manufacturer instrumental measurement of single-use hydration, not a wrinkle or long-term outcome, and not independently reported.[^anua]
- **"Stimulates skin cell turnover to visibly improve the look of fine lines."** The retinol mechanism is real at the category level (see [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]), but no trial of this product is reported, and its retinol is at the low over-the-counter end.[^anua][^kafi]
