Beauty of Joseon Ginseng Essence Water is a Korean hydrating essence built on ginseng root water and niacinamide, layering four further ginseng-derived ingredients lower in the list, sold in a 150 ml bottle for $18.00.[^boj]

> The brand markets it as a "barrier-boosting" hydrating essence for glow and smoothness; niacinamide's evidenced pigment effect is for facial hyperpigmentation specifically, and no cited trial isolates ginseng in any of its five forms here, so the glow and brightening claims are cosmetic marketing rather than measured outcomes. The hydration case rests on standard, well-evidenced humectants (glycerin, propanediol, panthenol, sodium hyaluronate) that make up most of the formula, covered on [[moisturizing]].[^boj]

## Summary

Ginseng Essence Water is a lightweight, alcohol-free, fragrance-free essence from the Korean brand Beauty of Joseon, positioned for daily hydration and marketed for "nourishment, protection, balance."[^boj] It is designed to be applied after cleansing, morning and night, either pressed in by hand or swept on with a cotton pad, per the brand's own directions.[^boj]

The formula's aqueous base is standard humectants: butylene glycol, glycerin, and propanediol appear ahead of niacinamide, which sits fifth on the ingredient list.[^boj] That base gives the product a real, well-evidenced case for hydration, the humectant and moisturizing evidence behind glycerin and propanediol is set out on [[moisturizing]], but no published trial tests this exact formula.

The brand's naming ingredient is ginseng, present in five separate forms: Panax Ginseng Root Water (seventh on the list), plus a callus culture extract, root extract, berry extract, and a ginseng-root ferment filtrate lower in the list.[^boj] Ginseng's evidence in this product is thin: no cited trial isolates any of these five ginseng ingredients, and the brand's "deeply nourishes" and "supports moisture barrier" language for ginseng is unestablished marketing, quarantined under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

Niacinamide, the formula's other headline ingredient, does have an evidence base, covered in full on [[niacinamide]]: topical niacinamide has modest, mostly small-trial evidence for supporting the skin barrier and lowering water loss, and separately for lightening facial hyperpigmentation. This essence does not declare a niacinamide concentration, so that general, dose-specific case cannot be pinned to this undeclared-concentration formula, and the brand's "Smooth + Glow" and general brightening claims extend beyond the hyperpigmentation-specific evidence niacinamide actually has.

It is priced at $18.00 for 150 ml on the brand's own site, as of 2026-08-07.[^boj]

## The Evidence

No published trial tests this essence, its ginseng root water base, or any of its four other ginseng-derived ingredients.[^boj] The product's one active with a documented clinical case is niacinamide: topical niacinamide has modest, mostly small-trial evidence for skin barrier support and lowering water loss in dry skin, and separately for lightening facial hyperpigmentation, set out in full on [[niacinamide]]. This essence does not state how much niacinamide it contains, so the general, dose-specific niacinamide case on that page cannot be used to estimate this product's own effect size. The remaining hydration case rests on the formula's humectants (glycerin, propanediol, panthenol, sodium hyaluronate), whose general moisturizing evidence is set out on [[moisturizing]], rather than on any product-specific measurement.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by independent, product-specific evidence:

- **"A barrier-boosting essence packed with ginseng water and niacinamide for lasting hydration, antioxidant benefits, and a glow boost."** No cited trial isolates ginseng's contribution to hydration or antioxidant benefit in this formula; niacinamide's own barrier evidence is covered on [[niacinamide]] but this essence does not declare a niacinamide concentration.[^boj]
- **"Smooth + Glow: Apply daily for a complexion that looks smoother, brighter, and more even."** Niacinamide's evidenced pigment effect is for facial hyperpigmentation specifically, not a generic brightening or evening effect on already-even skin.[^boj]
