RoC Retinol Correxion Deep Wrinkle Night Cream is a 1 oz (30 ml) leave-on night cream from [[roc]] built around the brand's own "Patented Clinical Retinol," sold for $21.99 direct from the manufacturer ($19.79 with subscription).[^roc]

> A drugstore-tier retinol night cream whose central claims, "7x more powerful" than standard retinol and wrinkles reduced "by up to 50%," come only from RoC's own testing of its proprietary retinol complex, with no disclosed percentage and no independently published trial; a Target listing for what appears to be the same formula under a different name corroborates the price, size, ingredient order, and marketing figures, but does not independently verify them.[^roc][^target] Topical retinol's own fine-wrinkle and pigment benefits are well established at the category level on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]], [set out under The Evidence](#the-evidence).

## Summary

RoC Retinol Correxion Deep Wrinkle Night Cream is a 1 oz jar priced at $21.99 on RoC's own site, or $19.79 with a subscribe-and-save order.[^roc] That places it in the same range as other mass-market undisclosed-concentration retinol creams, such as [[cerave-resurfacing-retinol-serum]] at $21.99.

The manufacturer markets the cream as "developed with Dermatologists," describing "our award-winning advanced solution to smooth the look of deep wrinkles while you sleep," built on what it calls "RoC Patented Clinical Retinol," claimed to be "7x more effective than standard Retinol."[^roc] Neither the product page nor RoC's ingredient list discloses what percentage of retinol the formula actually contains, and the "7x" figure is credited to a study of the ingredient itself rather than a trial of this finished cream, so there is no way to weigh it against the specific retinol concentrations (0.1% to 0.4%) tested in the category-level trials covered on [[retinol]].[^roc]

The brand's own headline numbers, that "96%" of testers "had improvement in the look of deep wrinkles" after 12 weeks and that the cream reduces "the look of hard-to-treat wrinkles by up to 50%," are reported only on RoC's marketing page, with no published methodology or independent replication.[^roc] A Target listing for a RoC nighttime retinol moisturizer at the identical $21.99 price and 1 fl oz size, opening with the same ingredient order (water, dimethicone, glycerin, ethylhexyl hydroxystearate, isodecyl neopentanoate...) through "retinol," carries the same "96% ... in 12 weeks" and "up to 50%" figures under a different product name, "RoC Retinol Anti-Aging + Firming Night Face Moisturizer."[^target] That match corroborates the price, size, and marketing figures as genuinely RoC's own claims for this formula rather than a one-off marketing-page overstatement, but it is the same set of brand-authored numbers repeated on a retailer page, not an independent trial.

Retinol itself, the labeled active ingredient, is the most-studied over-the-counter retinoid, with vehicle-controlled trial evidence for improving fine wrinkles and pigment in aging skin, [covered in full on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]](#the-evidence). RoC's own directions call for applying the cream "at night," beginning "every other night" and increasing frequency gradually, alongside daytime SPF 30+, and note the cream is intended for consistent use "approximately 6-8 weeks," with slower reintroduction if irritation occurs.[^roc]

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinol improves photoaged skin is covered on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]: vehicle-controlled trials of retinol at 0.1% to 0.4% found real, gradual improvement in fine wrinkles and mottled pigmentation over 24 to 52 weeks, milder than prescription [[tretinoin]]. No published trial tests this cream, or the "Patented Clinical Retinol" complex it is built on, at any disclosed concentration.

> **Bottom line.** The wrinkle-reduction case for this cream rests on RoC's own unpublished figures (96% improvement at 12 weeks, up to 50% reduction, "7x" more powerful than standard retinol), corroborated in the sense that a Target listing for the same-priced, same-sized, same-ingredient-order formula repeats the identical figures, not in the sense of an independent trial confirming them.[^roc][^target]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), declared on RoC's own product page; the opening ingredients (through retinol) match a Target listing for what appears to be the same formula under a different product name.[^roc][^target]

- Water (Aqua/Eau), dimethicone (silicone emollient), glycerin (humectant), ethylhexyl hydroxystearate, isodecyl neopentanoate (emollients)
- Tetrahydroxypropyl ethylenediamine (pH adjuster/chelator), stearyl alcohol, cetearyl alcohol (emollient/thickening alcohols), glyceryl stearate, PEG-100 stearate, ceteareth-20, steareth-10 (emulsifiers)
- [[retinol]], no percentage disclosed, described by the brand as "RoC Patented Clinical Retinol"
- Dihydroxy methylchromone, magnesium aspartate, zinc gluconate, copper gluconate, andrographis paniculata leaf extract, ingredients the brand groups into its retinol complex, none independently quantified
- Butyrospermum parkii (shea) butter, an emollient
- [[glycolic-acid]] and [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|ascorbic acid]], no percentages disclosed
- Squalane (emollient), xanthan gum (thickener), [[tocopherol]] (antioxidant), propanediol (solvent/humectant)
- Methyl methacrylate crosspolymer, hydroxyethyl acrylate/sodium acryloyldimethyl taurate copolymer, sorbitan isostearate, polysorbate 20, polysorbate 60, tapioca starch, polymethylsilsesquioxane, texture agents
- Phenylpropanol, pentylene glycol, caprylyl glycol (preservative/humectant system), disodium EDTA (chelator), citric acid (pH adjuster)
- Fragrance (parfum), linalyl acetate, trimethylbenzenepropanol, tetramethyl acetyloctahydronaphthalenes, the fragrance and fragrance-adjacent ingredients

The formula contains added fragrance, unlike some competing retinol creams such as [[cerave-resurfacing-retinol-serum]].[^roc]
