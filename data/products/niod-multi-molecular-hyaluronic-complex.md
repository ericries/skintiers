NIOD Multi-Molecular Hyaluronic Complex (MMHC2) is a water-based hydration serum built around multiple forms of hyaluronic acid at differing molecular weights, paired with glycerin, sold in 15 mL and 30 mL sizes at $36.00 (15 mL) and marketed as a "complex of 15 hyaluronic technologies."[^1]

> A multi-weight hyaluronic-acid serum from [[the-ordinary|The Ordinary]]'s sister brand, both part of DECIEM. The general evidence for topical hyaluronic acid, reliable at raising a skin-hydration reading, with a fine-line benefit specific to lower-molecular-weight forms, is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. This formula lists five distinct hyaluronate ingredients spanning both the high- and low-molecular-weight tiers that evidence distinguishes, plus glycerin third on the list, ahead of any of them. No published trial tests this exact 63-ingredient formula or its stated 1.0% direct hyaluronic-acid figure.[^1]

## Summary

Multi-Molecular Hyaluronic Complex (MMHC2) is NIOD's hydration serum: a long, water-based formula whose first four functional ingredients after water are hydrolyzed yeast extract, [[glycerin]], and then hyaluronic acid and sodium hyaluronate, followed by three more hyaluronate forms further down the list.[^1] The general case for topical [[hyaluronic-acid]], a reliable rise in a skin-hydration measurement, and a fine-line benefit specific to lower-molecular-weight forms that can cross the stratum corneum, is set out on that ingredient's page. This product's declared INCI includes both ends of that spectrum: unspecified-weight "Hyaluronic Acid" and "Sodium Hyaluronate" near the top, and lower-molecular-weight forms (sodium butyroyl hyaluronate, hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate) further down.[^1]

What the brand adds beyond a plain hyaluronic-acid serum is a long tail of amino acids, a peptide (myristoyl nonapeptide-3), plant and algae extracts, and gums, none tested as a group in any published trial found for this analysis. NIOD states the product delivers 1.0% direct hyaluronic acid and describes it as supporting the skin's own hyaluronic-acid production and strengthening the barrier; those are manufacturer claims not backed by a cited independent trial, and are weighed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1] It is listed at $36.00 for 15 mL on NIOD's own site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^1]

## The Evidence

Topical hyaluronic acid reliably raises a skin-hydration reading in placebo-controlled testing, with a molecular-weight-dependent fine-line benefit; the full case, including the caveat that high-molecular-weight forms do not cross the stratum corneum, is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. Nothing specific to this product was found: no published trial tests NIOD's exact multi-hyaluronate, 63-ingredient formula, so its grades above rest on that general category evidence.[^1]

This formula's declared INCI is unusually explicit about spanning molecular weights: "Hyaluronic Acid," "Sodium Hyaluronate," "Sodium Butyroyl Hyaluronate," "Sodium Hyaluronate Crosspolymer," and "Hydrolyzed Sodium Hyaluronate" all appear as separate ingredients.[^1] The general evidence shows that only the lower-molecular-weight tier (roughly 20 to 300 kDa) has been shown to cross the stratum corneum and reduce wrinkle depth in the cited trials, while high-molecular-weight forms stay on the surface. No molecular-weight figures are published for any of this product's five hyaluronate ingredients, so it is not possible to say what share of the complex falls into the penetrating tier.[^1] Glycerin, third in the declared list ahead of every hyaluronate form, has its own solid, separately evidenced humectant effect.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its hydration grade rests on hyaluronic acid's and glycerin's general, solidly evidenced humectant case; its "15 technologies" and 1.0% direct-hyaluronic-acid framing, and any barrier-strengthening or HA-production claim, are unverified manufacturer statements.[^1]

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] The actives with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

Actives:

- [[hyaluronic-acid]] (as hyaluronic acid, sodium hyaluronate, sodium butyroyl hyaluronate, sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer, and hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate), five separate declared forms spanning high- and low-molecular-weight tiers

- Polyglucuronic acid, paired on-page with hydrolyzed yeast extract above, per that same NIOD ingredient callout[^1]
- Pseudoalteromonas ferment extract, labeled "Pseudoalteromonas Exopolysaccharides" in a separate NIOD on-page ingredient callout, described there as helping skin "achieve sustained hydration, reducing the look of dry fine lines, and improving the appearance of skin texture"[^1]
- Tremella fuciformis (snow mushroom) sporocarp extract, a plant-derived polysaccharide sometimes marketed as an HA alternative

The full declared list runs to 63 ingredients.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Complex of 15 hyaluronic technologies."** A manufacturer count of ingredients and forms included in the formula, not a measure of clinical benefit; the independent evidence on [[hyaluronic-acid]] covers hydration and fine-line effects by molecular weight, not a technology count.[^1]
- **"Enhancing skin's natural levels of hyaluronic acid" and "strengthening the barrier to keep moisture in."** No cited independent trial shows a topical hyaluronic-acid product increases the skin's own hyaluronic-acid production; the evidence on [[hyaluronic-acid]] finds no demonstrated mechanism for this beyond surface humectancy.[^1]
- **"MMHC2 also incorporates 1.0% of direct hyaluronic acid, for effective moisturisation."** A manufacturer-stated figure for the declared "Hyaluronic Acid" ingredient specifically, not independently verified and not broken out by the other four hyaluronate forms in the list.[^1]
- **"Visibly reduces wrinkles and fine lines... firms."** The independent fine-line evidence on [[hyaluronic-acid]] is molecular-weight-specific and comes from small trials with cosmetic-instrument endpoints; this product's own molecular weights are not published, so it cannot be matched to that evidence.[^1]
- **"Overtime" self-perception claims: "Enhances radiance (69%)," "Visibly smooths skin texture (61%)," "Firms skin (33%)," "Visibly reduces wrinkles and fine lines (-32%)."** A carousel of self-reported percentages on NIOD's own product page, shown with no published denominator, comparator group, or testing methodology; not independent clinical evidence.[^1]
