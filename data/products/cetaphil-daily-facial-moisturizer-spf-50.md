Cetaphil Daily Facial Moisturizer with Sunscreen Broad Spectrum SPF 50 is a lightweight daily face lotion from [[cetaphil]] that combines a moisturizer base with a chemical-plus-mineral sunscreen filter set, sold by Galderma in a 1.7 fl oz size.[^1][^2]

> A convenience "moisturizer with sunscreen" whose FDA drug label lists five active filters: octinoxate 7.5%, octisalate 5%, octocrylene 7%, oxybenzone 6%, and titanium dioxide 5.7%.[^1] Two of those, [[oxybenzone]] and [[octinoxate]], are the organic filters most often stripped from reformulated and "reef-safe" sunscreens, for the systemic-absorption and endocrine questions detailed on [[oxybenzone]] and the environmental debate on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Notably, Cetaphil's own [[cetaphil-sheer-mineral-sunscreen-stick-spf-50|mineral sunscreen stick]] advertises being free of both.[^3] Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case is well evidenced at the category level; no trial isolates this specific lotion, so its grades rest on that evidence and its filter chemistry.

## Summary

This is Cetaphil's daily facial moisturizer with built-in SPF 50. Its FDA drug label (via DailyMed) and a current Target retail listing both give the active ingredients as octinoxate 7.5%, octisalate 5%, octocrylene 7%, oxybenzone 6%, and titanium dioxide 5.7%.[^1][^2] The product is retail-labeled "Broad Spectrum SPF 50" and marketed as hypoallergenic, non-comedogenic, and "recommended for sensitive skin."[^2]

The health case for using any broad-spectrum sunscreen daily, slowing photoaging and reducing sun-related skin-cancer risk, has randomized-trial support and is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; that is where the value of this product lies, not in its moisturizer base.[^1] The moisturization itself comes from a glycerin-and-dimethicone lotion base and is a cosmetic, not a health, benefit.[^1]

The composition worth flagging is the filter set. It relies on [[octinoxate]], [[octisalate]], and [[octocrylene]] plus [[oxybenzone]] for the organic (chemical) filtering and a modest [[titanium-dioxide]] 5.7% for added coverage, with no [[avobenzone]], the most common long-UVA filter.[^1] [[oxybenzone|Oxybenzone]] is the filter absorbed most heavily into the body in the FDA's maximal-use trial and is estrogenic in laboratory assays, the reasons it and octinoxate have fallen out of favor and are the two most commonly removed from "reef-safe" reformulations; that evidence is set out on [[oxybenzone]] and [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Cetaphil's own mineral stick, by contrast, is marketed as free of both.[^3]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific lotion was located, so the product-specific facts available are its filter composition and its retail Broad Spectrum SPF 50 rating.

The active filters are confirmed by two independent sources that agree exactly: the product's FDA drug label on DailyMed (labeler Galderma, NDC 0299-4930) and a current Target listing both list octinoxate 7.5%, octisalate 5%, octocrylene 7%, oxybenzone 6%, and titanium dioxide 5.7%.[^1][^2] Octinoxate, octisalate, and octocrylene are UVB-weighted organic filters; oxybenzone adds shorter-UVA (UVA2) reach; titanium dioxide 5.7% is a mineral filter contributing broad but modest coverage. The set contains no [[avobenzone]], the organic filter that supplies most long-wave UVA1 protection in modern chemical sunscreens, so its long-UVA coverage rests on oxybenzone, octocrylene, and a single-digit-percent mineral filter rather than a dedicated UVA1 absorber.[^1] The label still carries an FDA Broad Spectrum claim, which requires passing a critical-wavelength test, so this is a note on the filter mix, not a claim that the product fails its rating.

The label's own "Uses" statement is narrow: it says the product "Helps prevent sunburn," and its Skin Cancer/Skin Aging Alert follows the standard FDA wording.[^1] The moisturizer base, water, propylene glycol, glycerin, and dimethicone lead the inactive list, delivers the "daily moisturizer" positioning, but that is cosmetic hydration; the evidenced benefit of the product is the UV protection, covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^1]

> **Bottom line.** A retail-labeled Broad Spectrum SPF 50 daily moisturizer whose filter set, confirmed by both its FDA label and a current retailer, still includes oxybenzone 6% and octinoxate 7.5%, the two filters most often removed from reformulated "reef-safe" sunscreens over the systemic-absorption and endocrine questions detailed on [[oxybenzone]], and no avobenzone.[^1][^2] The sunscreen is the reason to use it; the composition is the reason to compare it against filter sets like the brand's own [[cetaphil-sheer-mineral-sunscreen-stick-spf-50|mineral stick]].[^3]

Base, texture, and [[preservatives|preservative system]]: water, propylene glycol, [[glycerin]], [[dimethicone]], VP/eicosene copolymer, cyclohexasiloxane, stearic acid, potassium cetyl phosphate, dimethiconol, glyceryl stearate, PEG-100 stearate, aluminum hydroxide, disodium EDTA, [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), phenoxyethanol, ethylparaben, chlorphenesin, cetyl alcohol, acrylates/C10-30 alkyl acrylate crosspolymer, methylparaben, xanthan gum, and sodium hydroxide.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Recommended for sensitive skin" / "hypoallergenic."** Positioning claims on the Target listing and Cetaphil's marketing; no sensitivity or patch-test data for this specific SKU is presented there, and hypoallergenic is not an FDA-defined term.[^2]
- **"Non-comedogenic."** A brand/retailer claim not backed by a published comedogenicity test for this profile.[^2]
- **"No synthetic fragrance" / "phthalate-free."** Composition descriptors from the Target listing; the ingredient list carries no added fragrance.[^1][^2]
- **"Dermatologist tested."** A brand testing claim on the Target listing with no methodology attached.[^2]
