## Summary

CeraVe's third cleanser format, $15.99, sitting between the cream and the foam.[^brand] **Bottom line:** the surfactant system is genuinely mild and sulfate-free, which is the real reason to pick it. The two claims worth discounting are a slow-release delivery promise on a product you rinse off, and a "100% of women" figure that comes from a satisfaction survey.

> **If you arrived here looking for a salicylic acid cleanser, this is not one.** Salicylic acid does appear, twenty-ninth of fifty-nine ingredients on CeraVe's own list.[^brand] The brand does not name it among this product's ingredients anywhere on the page, lists the cleanser as "Made For: Normal to Dry Skin; Including Sensitive Skin" and says it "Helps With: Cleanses & Removes Makeup".[^brand] For an acne cleanser from the same brand, see [[cerave-acne-control-gel]] and the ranked options on [[best-cleansers-for-oily-acne-prone-skin]].

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: modest, for cleansing.** The detergents are Sodium Methyl Cocoyl Taurate, Coco-Betaine and Sodium Cocoyl Isethionate at positions three, four and five, with no sulfate anywhere in the list.[^brand] That is the mild end of the surfactant shelf, and it is consistent with the brand describing an "Amino acid-based surfactant" that "transforms the cream face wash into a delicate foam".[^brand] No trial of this product was found.
- **Effect size: minimal, for the barrier claim.** Ceramide NP, AP and EOP sit fifteenth to seventeenth, cholesterol fortieth, phytosphingosine fifty-second, sodium hyaluronate thirty-seventh.[^brand] [[ceramides]] is graded **mid** here and [[hyaluronic-acid]] **strong**, but both grades come from leave-on use. A cleanser is on the skin for under a minute and then removed.
- **The delivery claim is the one that does not fit the format.** CeraVe says "MVE technology encapsulates ceramides to ensure efficient delivery within the skin's barrier and slow release over time", and that this supports the barrier "long after you've finish applying".[^brand] The general version of this claim is quarantined on [[cerave-moisturizing-cream]], where no independent trial of MVE's clinical effect is found. What is specific here is that the promise is being made for a wash-off product.
- **The headline number is a survey.** "100% of women* agree that it instantly removes dirt, oil, pollution and facial makeup", with the asterisk resolving to "*Consumer study".[^brand] Agreement is not measurement, and the comparison group is absent.
- **The label disclosures are real and worth having.** Fragrance-free, and the brand also states "Non-comedogenic, fragrance-free, allergy-tested and pH balanced" and "Suitable for contact lens wearers".[^brand] Fragrance-free is checkable against the ingredient list and holds. Non-comedogenic is an unregulated term.
- **For once the two published lists agree.** incidecoder's entry for this product matches CeraVe's own list in content and order, including salicylic acid at the same position, which is not what happened on the last two non-US products covered here.

## The Evidence

None specific to this product. The ingredient evidence is on [[ceramides]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]], and the question of whether cleanser choice matters much at all is handled on [[best-gentle-cleansers-sensitive-skin]].

Against its own siblings, the honest difference is texture rather than performance: [[cerave-hydrating-facial-cleanser]] does not foam, [[cerave-foaming-facial-cleanser]] is built for oily skin, and this one is the brand's attempt to give the foaming sensation to people with dry or sensitive skin. Nothing on the page measures that against either.

> **Bottom line.** A mild, fragrance-free, sulfate-free cleanser at a normal price, which is a reasonable thing to buy. Just not for the salicylic acid, and not for the ceramides staying behind after the rinse.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own product page and **not** endorsed here.

- **"MVE technology encapsulates ceramides to ensure efficient delivery within the skin's barrier and slow release over time."**[^brand] A delivery claim with no independent trial behind it, made here for a rinse-off.
- **"100% of women* agree that it instantly removes dirt, oil, pollution and facial makeup"**, and "More than 98% of women* find their skin clean, smooth, soft, refreshed and feeling healthy without feeling stripped", both footnoted "*Consumer study".[^brand] Satisfaction scores, not outcomes.
- **"Developed with dermatologists"**[^brand] A positioning statement, handled the same way on [[cerave-moisturizing-cream]].
- **"Non-comedogenic"**[^brand] No regulatory definition and no required test.
