Twyneo is a prescription-only topical cream combining [[tretinoin]] 0.1% and [[benzoyl-peroxide|benzoyl peroxide]] 3%, FDA-labeled for "the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in adults and pediatric patients 9 years of age and older."[^dailymed]

> Two individually well-evidenced acne actives in one once-daily cream: tretinoin is a first-line topical retinoid and benzoyl peroxide is a first-line oxidizing antimicrobial, and network meta-analysis data show pairing a retinoid with benzoyl peroxide beats either alone (see [[tretinoin]] and [[benzoyl-peroxide]] for that evidence).[^dailymed] Its formulation twist is that tretinoin and benzoyl peroxide normally degrade each other when mixed directly; the manufacturer describes keeping the two apart with a "microencapsulation technology" until the cream is applied, a claim we could confirm only from the brand's own site, not an independent stability study.[^twyneo] This exact product has also been tested directly, in two phase 3 vehicle-controlled trials that found it significantly outperformed vehicle at week 12 (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^jaad2023]

## Summary

Twyneo pairs [[tretinoin]] 0.1%, a first-line topical retinoid, with [[benzoyl-peroxide|benzoyl peroxide]] 3%, a first-line oxidizing antimicrobial, in a single once-daily cream.[^dailymed] Both actives have their own general evidence set out on their ingredient pages; this page does not repeat those trial numbers. What distinguishes the formula is chemistry rather than a novel active: tretinoin and benzoyl peroxide are known to oxidize and degrade each other on contact, which is why they are normally sold and applied separately. Twyneo's own site describes a "microencapsulation technology" meant to keep the two actives physically apart in the jar so they combine only after application; this is the brand's own description, and no independent stability or degradation study of the product was located.[^twyneo]

The FDA label lists Twyneo's labeler as Galderma Laboratories, L.P. (NDC 0299-5945), while a SingleCare pricing page lists Mayne Pharma as the product's manufacturer; we could not resolve that discrepancy from the sources fetched here, so both are noted.[^dailymed][^singlecare]

Twyneo's own FDA indication, "the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in adults and pediatric patients 9 years of age and older," gives this exact combination a regulatory sign-off, and two phase 3 vehicle-controlled trials (n=858 total) found it significantly reduced lesion counts and raised clear/almost-clear (IGA) rates versus vehicle at week 12 (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence) for the numbers).[^dailymed][^jaad2023] SingleCare lists a regular cash price of $1,295.99 for a 30 g pump, with a SingleCare coupon lowering that to $451.96 at some pharmacies (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^singlecare]

## The Evidence

Tretinoin 0.1% is a first-line topical retinoid for mild-to-moderate acne with solid, if modest-effect, evidence, strongest when paired with benzoyl peroxide rather than used alone; see [[tretinoin]]. Benzoyl peroxide 3% is a first-line topical antimicrobial for acne with a modest effect and mixed-quality evidence; see [[benzoyl-peroxide]]. A network meta-analysis restricted to mild-to-moderate acne (Mavranezouli et al., 2022), drawing on 90 of its 179 pooled RCTs (41 treatment classes, 17,260 observations), found that combining a topical retinoid with benzoyl peroxide raised the effect to 26.16% lesion reduction versus placebo (95% CrI 16.75-35.36), compared with 18.27% for retinoid monotherapy (95% CrI 10.28-26.14), the combination category outperforming either active alone.[^tretinoin]

Beyond that class-level evidence, Twyneo itself has been tested directly. Two identical phase 3, multicenter, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials (n=858 total, ages 9 and up with moderate-to-severe acne, 12 weeks; Del Rosso et al., 2023) compared Twyneo against vehicle.[^jaad2023] Per the FDA label's Clinical Studies table, IGA treatment success (clear or almost-clear with a 2-grade improvement from baseline) was reached by 33.9% of Twyneo patients versus 14.3% on vehicle in trial 1, and 26.8% versus 15.1% in trial 2; inflammatory lesion counts fell by -21.6 versus -14.8 lesions in trial 1 and -16.2 versus -14.1 in trial 2; noninflammatory lesion counts fell by -29.7 versus -19.8 in trial 1 and -24.2 versus -17.4 in trial 2.[^dailymed] The peer-reviewed publication of the same two trials reports the same lesion-count figures but slightly different IGA success percentages (38.5% and 25.4% for Twyneo versus 11.5% and 14.7% for vehicle, P < .001 and P = .017); we could not resolve why the label's and the paper's IGA percentages differ from the sources checked here, so both are noted.[^jaad2023] On every endpoint, trial 1 showed a substantially larger effect than trial 2. The grade above rests on this product-specific trial evidence together with the two actives' separate, well-established evidence and the product's own FDA indication.

> **Bottom line.** Tretinoin and benzoyl peroxide are each solidly first-line for acne on their own, and combining a retinoid with benzoyl peroxide beats either alone in cross-trial data. Twyneo itself was tested in two phase 3 vehicle-controlled trials and beat vehicle on IGA success and lesion counts in both, more convincingly in trial 1 than trial 2 (see above). The manufacturer's microencapsulation claim, meant to prevent the two actives degrading each other, is sourced only to the brand's own site.[^dailymed][^twyneo][^jaad2023]

## What's In It

Active ingredients, per the FDA label:

- [[tretinoin]] 0.1%[^dailymed]
- [[benzoyl-peroxide|benzoyl peroxide]] 3%[^dailymed]

Inactive ingredients, per the same label: "anhydrous citric acid, butylated hydroxytoluene, carbomer homopolymer type C, cetrimonium chloride, cetyl alcohol, cyclomethicone, edetate disodium, glycerin, hydrochloric acid, imidurea, (S)lactic acid, macrogol stearate, mono and di-glycerides, polyquaternium-7, purified water, silicon dioxide, sodium hydroxide, squalane, tetraethyl ortho silicate and white wax."[^dailymed]

The manufacturer's site describes the tretinoin and benzoyl peroxide as microencapsulated so the two actives, which normally inactivate each other on direct contact, stay separated until the cream is applied to skin; this description comes only from the brand's own site, not an independent source.[^twyneo]
