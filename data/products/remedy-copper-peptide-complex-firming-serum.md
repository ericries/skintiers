REMEDY's Copper Peptide Complex Advanced Firming Serum is a 1 fl oz serum built on copper tripeptide-1 (GHK-Cu), from the dermatologist-founded brand on [[remedy-science]]. The brand describes it as "A dermatologist-created serum built with pure GHK-Cu Copper Peptides, multi-weight hydrators and barrier-supporting ingredients to support collagen production, smooth texture, and promote a firmer, radiant look."[^brand]

> **Bottom line.** A well-built hydrating serum whose headline active is the weakest part of the case. Copper tripeptide-1 is declared **7th of 31** with no percentage given, and the evidence for copper peptides generally is thin, as [[copper-peptides]] sets out. Worth noting against the brand's own standard: REMEDY publishes three third-party studies with report numbers for its azelaic serum, and **publishes none here**.[^brand] What this formula reliably does is hydrate. Judge it on that and treat the firming as unproven.

## Summary

The declared ingredient list runs water, pentylene glycol, isopentyliol as the brand spells it, 1,2-hexanediol, glycerin, ectoin, **copper tripeptide-1**, polyglutamic acid, allantoin, biosaccharide gum-1, butylene glycol, caprylyl glycol, centella asiatica extract, citric acid, turmeric root extract, ethylhexylglycerin, **acetyl hexapeptide-8**, hydroxyacetophenone, an acrylates copolymer, lactobacillus ferment, **palmitoyl tripeptide-5**, phenoxyethanol, two polysorbates, sodium citrate, sodium hyaluronate, sorbitan isostearate, superoxide dismutase, tocopherol, trisodium ethylenediamine disuccinate and tromethamine.[^brand]

One structural point matters more than any single ingredient. **From roughly the eleventh position the list runs close to alphabetical**, which is the conventional signal that those ingredients sit at or below 1%, where labelling rules allow any order. So positions in that tail carry no information about amount. That cuts both ways here: it means acetyl hexapeptide-8 at 17th is not meaningfully "ranked" below turmeric at 15th, and it also means none of the tail can be assumed present in a working quantity.

Copper tripeptide-1 at 7th sits above that line, so it is plausibly present at a real concentration. The brand does not state one.

## The Evidence

No trial of this serum is cited here, so the case rests on the ingredients, and they split cleanly.

**The hydration half is sound and unremarkable.** Glycerin fifth, [[polyglutamic-acid|polyglutamic acid]] eighth, biosaccharide gum-1 tenth and sodium hyaluronate in the tail make this a humectant-rich serum, and humectants reliably raise a short-term hydration measurement. That is why the hydration grade is the only `solid` one on this page, and also why it is not a reason to choose this product over any other well-made hydrating serum; see [[hyaluronic-acid]].

**The firming half is where the claim outruns the evidence.** The general case for copper peptides, including what the trials do and do not show, is on [[copper-peptides]] and is not repeated here. What this page adds is product-specific: no stated concentration, no trial of the finished serum, and no brand testing published, which is conspicuous given the same brand published pH-metry and ex vivo gene-expression studies with report numbers for [[remedy-15-azelaic-acid-complex-serum]]. A brand that documents one product and not another is telling you something about which case it can make.

**Two further peptides do not add much.** Acetyl hexapeptide-8 and palmitoyl tripeptide-5 both have their own small literatures, discussed on [[peptides]], and both sit in the unordered tail here at undisclosed amounts.

## Common Marketing Claims

Claims quoted from the brand's own store page; treat each as unproven unless it also appears under The Evidence.

- **"A dermatologist-created serum built with pure GHK-Cu Copper Peptides, multi-weight hydrators and barrier-supporting ingredients to support collagen production, smooth texture, and promote a firmer, radiant look."**[^brand] "Support" and "promote a firmer, radiant look" are appearance claims rather than measured outcomes, and no trial of this serum is cited for any of them. The "dermatologist-created" framing is accurate as a statement about the founder, not about testing.
- **"GHK-Cu is one of the most extensively researched peptides in skincare."**[^brand] Defensible in relative terms, which is the trap. Being the most researched peptide is a low bar, and it does not establish that the research supports a firming benefit at a cosmetic concentration; see [[copper-peptides]].
- **"Counterintuitively, in its tripeptide form it acts as an antioxidant, so it supports skin without the free-radical concern people expect from copper."**[^brand] This addresses a reasonable question about free copper, and no evidence is cited here either for the antioxidant claim or for the concern it answers.
- **"Copper peptides layer well with most actives. They pair well with retinoids."**[^brand] A compatibility claim, not an efficacy one. Nothing here measures whether layering changes the result.

Worth crediting: the brand footnotes individual supporting ingredients to named papers, such as Choi 2015 on polyglutamic acid as a skin-hydrating agent and Graf 2008 on ectoine for skin protection.[^brand] Both ingredients have their own pages here, [[polyglutamic-acid]] and [[ectoin]]. Those are real citations for ingredient-level properties, not for this product's firming claim.

## Who It Is For

Someone who wants a comfortable hydrating serum and is curious about copper peptides without expecting much. If firmness is the actual goal, the graded options are on [[skin-firmness-elasticity]], where the evidence-led answers are sunscreen and retinoids rather than peptides. At $30 for 1 fl oz it is priced in line with the brand's other serums.[^brand]
