DHS Tar Gel Shampoo is an over-the-counter medicated shampoo from Person and Covey with coal tar 0.5% as its FDA-monograph active, labeled to control the symptoms of psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis, and dandruff.[^dailymed]

> A generic-style coal tar 0.5% shampoo, functionally identical in active and strength to the archetypal [[neutrogena-tgel-therapeutic-shampoo-coal-tar|Neutrogena T/Gel Therapeutic Shampoo]]. Coal tar's evidence is real but modest and dated, [covered in full on [[coal-tar]]](#the-evidence). The label carries the same photosensitivity and hair-discoloration warnings as every coal tar shampoo, and this product has no published trial of its own; the case rests entirely on the monograph active.[^dailymed]

## Summary

DHS Tar Gel is a gel-format shampoo whose sole active is coal tar, declared on the FDA structured product label at 0.005 g per 1 g of product, that is, 0.5%.[^dailymed] The label's own purpose statement reads "Controls symptoms of Psoriasis, Seborrheic Dermatitis and Dandruff," and the indications section states it "helps prevent recurrence of scalp itching and flaking associated with Psoriasis, Seborrheic Dermatitis and Dandruff."[^dailymed] It is marketed and FDA-regulated as an OTC Monograph Drug, not a cosmetic.[^dailymed]

This is a health claim, not a cosmetic one: coal tar's antipruritic and antiscaling action on inflamed, flaking scalp skin is a recognized FDA monograph use, with the full mechanism and cross-product trial base graded on [[coal-tar]]. There is no evidence specific to this exact formulation beyond its regulatory listing; the case for using it rests on coal tar being the active, at the same 0.5% strength long established in the monograph and matching the reference product it is functionally interchangeable with, [[neutrogena-tgel-therapeutic-shampoo-coal-tar]].[^dailymed]

The base formula is a simple sulfate shampoo: triethanolamine lauryl sulfate as the primary surfactant, cocamidopropyl hydroxysultaine as a secondary surfactant, sodium chloride for thickening, PEG-8 distearate, hypromellose, citric acid, and purified water.[^dailymed] The label lists two package sizes, a 249.6 g bottle and a 20.8 g bottle.[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

Coal tar's antiscaling, antipruritic, and mild antiproliferative action on the inflamed, flaking scalp skin of dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis is set out in full, including the trial base and the settled question of cancer risk from topical use, on [[coal-tar]]. That evidence base supports coal tar as a monograph OTC active generally; no published clinical trial of this specific DHS-branded shampoo was located. The defensible, product-specific facts are the ones the FDA label itself states: the active is coal tar at 0.5%, the labeled uses are dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and scalp psoriasis, and directions call for a liberal application massaged into a lather, left on the scalp for several minutes, then rinsed and repeated, used at least twice a week or as a physician directs.[^dailymed]

> **Bottom line.** This shampoo carries the same monograph active, at the same concentration, as the reference coal tar shampoos; its case for effectiveness is the category-level coal tar evidence on [[coal-tar]], not a trial of this product.

The label's warnings match what every coal tar product carries: for external use only, avoid the eyes, use caution in sun exposure because it "may increase your tendency to sunburn for up to 24 hours after application," avoid combining with other psoriasis therapies such as ultraviolet light or prescription drugs unless a physician directs it, and a note that gray, blond-tinted, or bleached hair may occasionally discolor.[^dailymed] Anyone with psoriasis or seborrheic dermatitis covering a large body area is told to ask a physician before use, and to stop and consult a physician if the condition worsens or does not improve with regular use.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Controls symptoms of Psoriasis, Seborrheic Dermatitis and Dandruff."** The FDA label's own purpose statement, not brand marketing copy; it is the monograph claim coal tar 0.5% is entitled to make, and it rests on the category-level evidence on [[coal-tar]], not a trial of this shampoo.[^dailymed]
- **A shampoo "gel" format as a distinguishing feature.** No efficacy claim is tied to the gel format on the label; it is a formulation and texture difference from a standard liquid shampoo, not an evidence-backed advantage.
