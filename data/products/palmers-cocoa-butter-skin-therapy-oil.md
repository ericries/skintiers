Palmer's Cocoa Butter Skin Therapy Oil (Rosehip with Vitamin E) is a lightweight, non-greasy cosmetic body-and-face oil built on cocoa butter, argan oil, and vitamin E, marketed to improve the appearance of scars and stretch marks, sold at about $13.49 for 5.1 fl oz (151 mL).[^brand]

> A pleasant, non-greasy moisturizing oil whose headline case is weak where it counts. As a plain emollient it softens dry skin like any good body oil. But its scar and stretch-mark claims rest on cocoa butter, the ingredient a placebo-controlled trial found did not prevent stretch marks (see [[stretch-marks]]), and Palmer's own "93% saw a reduction in scars" numbers come from a self-reported home-user survey, not a controlled study. Judge it as a nice body oil, not a proven scar or stretch-mark treatment.[^brand]

## Summary

This is the oil format in Palmer's Cocoa Butter Formula line, a sibling to the [[palmers-cocoa-butter-massage-lotion-stretch-marks|Cocoa Butter Massage Lotion for Stretch Marks]]. The brand describes it as "an advanced multi-purpose skin perfection product" for "scars, stretch marks, dry, damaged skin, uneven skin tone, aging skin and fine lines and wrinkles," built on cocoa butter and vitamin E, argan oil and other natural oils, and a proprietary "Cetesomate-E Complex," with a rosehip fragrance.[^brand]

The problem is the evidence, which lives on [[stretch-marks]]: cocoa butter is the topical a double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT found did no better than placebo at preventing pregnancy stretch marks, and a 2016 review concluded cocoa butter "did not demonstrate any effect."[^brand] The oil's other named ingredients (argan oil, vitamin E, rosehip) are emollients and antioxidants without controlled evidence for erasing scars or stretch marks either.

## The Evidence

No independent, controlled trial of this oil was located. What Palmer's reports is its own company-run consumer study: "after 8 weeks, 94% of women tested saw more even skin tone, 93% of women tested saw a reduction in the appearance of scars, 92% of women tested saw an improvement in the appearance of stretch marks," which the brand footnotes as "based on responses in an 8-week home user trial by 200 female panelists."[^brand] Those are self-reported satisfaction figures with no control group, not evidence the oil outperforms simply moisturizing.

Its defensible value is the same as any emollient body oil: cocoa butter, argan oil, and vitamin E soften and moisturize dry skin, and a well-hydrated stretch mark or scar can look temporarily less obvious. That is a moisturizer effect, not the scar- or stretch-mark-clearing the marketing implies; established stretch marks respond poorly to any topical, as set out on [[stretch-marks]].[^brand]

> **Bottom line.** A cosmetically nice, non-greasy cocoa butter body oil that moisturizes well and may make marks look temporarily softer.[^brand] Treat the "#1 Stretch Marks brand," "93% saw a reduction in scars," and stretch-mark claims as marketing built on cocoa butter, the ingredient shown not to prevent stretch marks in a controlled trial; see [[stretch-marks]] for what topicals can and cannot do.

## What's In It

The brand names the functional ingredients rather than printing a full INCI list on the page: cocoa butter and vitamin E, argan oil and "natural oils," and a proprietary "Cetesomate-E Complex" delivery blend, in a rosehip-fragranced oil base.[^brand] It is marketed as free of mineral oil, parabens, phthalates, and fragrance allergens, and as hypoallergenic and non-comedogenic.[^brand]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"93% of women saw a reduction in the appearance of scars" / "92%... improvement in stretch marks."** Palmer's own 8-week home-user trial of 200 female panelists, self-reported and uncontrolled, not an independent study.[^brand]
- **"#1 Stretch Marks brand."** A sales-ranking claim (footnoted to IRI retail unit-sales data), not evidence the product works.[^brand]
- **"Dermatologist & OBGYN approved," "hypoallergenic."** Tolerability and marketing claims, not efficacy for scars or stretch marks.[^brand]
