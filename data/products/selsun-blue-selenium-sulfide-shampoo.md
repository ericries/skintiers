Selsun Blue's selenium sulfide shampoo is an over-the-counter medicated antidandruff shampoo built around selenium sulfide 1%, sold in "Normal to Oily" and "Moisturizing" variants (and historically as "Medicated"), and used intermittently rather than as a daily shampoo.[^1]

> An OTC drug, not a cosmetic: the FDA Drug Facts label lists the active as "selenium sulfide 1%" with the purpose "antidandruff."[^1] Selenium sulfide is a long-established antidandruff antifungal, and a 2026 randomized double-blind trial found a selenium disulfide shampoo statistically matched 2% ketoconazole for dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis.[^2] The reason this shampoo appears on a skincare site is its off-label use as a short-contact wash for fungal acne (Malassezia folliculitis), a use covered on [[fungal-acne]].

## Summary

This is a drugstore selenium sulfide antidandruff shampoo. It is regulated as an OTC drug: the Drug Facts label states the active ingredient as "selenium sulfide 1%," the purpose as "antidandruff," and the use as fighting and helping prevent the recurrence of dandruff.[^1]

Selenium sulfide (the label name for the compound also called selenium disulfide) is an antifungal that works against the Malassezia yeasts associated with dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis. The class evidence is real and recent: a randomized, double-blind, 6-week trial in 87 people with moderate-to-severe dandruff found a 0.6% micronized selenium disulfide shampoo reduced Total Scale Score by 77.9% versus 69.5% for 2% ketoconazole, with no significant difference between the two on scaling, symptoms, or itch, detailed on [[farris-2026-beyond-efficacy-0-6-selenium-disulfide-shampoo-matches-2-k]].[^2] That trial used a specific 0.6% micronized formulation rather than this product's 1% concentration, and no trial of this exact Selsun Blue product was located, so the dandruff grade rests on the active's monograph status plus that class evidence.

The reason a dandruff shampoo shows up here is the off-label use. Because fungal acne is driven by the same Malassezia yeast, selenium sulfide is one of the topical antifungals used off-label to treat it, typically as a short-contact wash left on the skin for a few minutes; that evidence, and the caution that it is off-label, lives on [[fungal-acne]] and the related [[seborrheic-dermatitis]] hub.

## The Evidence

Two health uses, graded above:

**Dandruff (the labeled use).** This is what the product is approved and labeled for. Selenium sulfide is an FDA OTC-monograph antidandruff active, and the strongest recent head-to-head evidence for the ingredient class is the 2026 Farris trial, where a selenium disulfide shampoo matched 2% ketoconazole on efficacy (Total Scale Score down 77.9% vs 69.5%, no significant between-group difference) and was preferred on hair cosmesis.[^2] The important caveat is that this trial used a 0.6% micronized formulation, not this product's 1%, so it supports the active rather than this exact SKU.

**Fungal acne (off-label).** Malassezia folliculitis responds to antifungals rather than to the benzoyl peroxide or retinoids used for ordinary acne, and selenium sulfide is one of the topical options with reported benefit, applied off-label as a short-contact wash. The regimen detail and its evidence sit on [[fungal-acne]]; this is not a use on the product's OTC label.

> **Bottom line.** An established selenium sulfide antidandruff shampoo whose labeled dandruff use is well supported at the ingredient level, with recent trial evidence that selenium disulfide matches ketoconazole for dandruff, though that trial used a lower 0.6% micronized formulation.[^1][^2] Its skincare relevance is the off-label short-contact use for fungal acne, covered on [[fungal-acne]].

## What's In It

As an OTC drug the label separates the active from everything else. Active ingredient: "selenium sulfide 1%."[^1]

Inactive ingredients (Normal to Oily variant), verbatim from the Drug Facts label: "water, ammonium lauryl sulfate, ammonium laureth sulfate, cocamidopropyl betaine, magnesium aluminum silicate, fragrance, cocamide DEA, DMDM hydantoin, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, citric acid, sodium citrate, sodium chloride, titanium dioxide, blue 1."[^1]

The list is alphabetical, as OTC Drug Facts inactive lists are, so unlike a cosmetic INCI list it does not convey concentration order. The base is a conventional detergent shampoo (ammonium lauryl and laureth sulfate surfactants) and it contains added fragrance and a blue dye.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Fights and helps prevent recurrence of dandruff."** This matches the labeled antidandruff indication and is supported at the active level, not by a trial of this specific product.[^1][^2]
- **Off-label "fungal acne" use.** Widely recommended online and plausible given the shared Malassezia mechanism, but this use is not on the product's OTC label and the supporting evidence is for the ingredient, not this SKU; see [[fungal-acne]].
