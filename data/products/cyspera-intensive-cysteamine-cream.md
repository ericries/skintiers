Cyspera Intensive is a once-daily, short-contact rinse-off cream built around [[cysteamine|cysteamine]], made by [[scientis|Scientis]], the Geneva, Switzerland company whose own product page currently lists the cream at 7% cysteamine.[^cyspera]

> Cysteamine's melasma evidence, set out in full on [[cysteamine]], beats placebo but has not been shown to clearly outperform [[hydroquinone|hydroquinone]] 4% in any single head-to-head trial.[^mansouri][^nguyen][^mawu] Unusually for a product page, the placebo-controlled and head-to-head trials behind that evidence were funded by this cream's own manufacturer, Scientis, and one lists a Scientis-affiliated co-author, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence).[^mansouri][^nguyen]

## Summary

Cyspera Intensive is a rinse-off cream sold by Scientis, marketed for melasma, post-inflammatory dark spots, and uneven skin tone.[^cyspera] Its manufacturer's own current product page lists the concentration as "Cysteamine 7%," while a separate retailer selling a product under the same name lists it as "5% Cysteamine," a 50 g jar for $175.00; the manufacturer's own site lists Cyspera Intensive at $180.00 without stating a size.[^cyspera][^skintruth] Both sources describe the same short-contact application: applied once daily, starting at about 5 minutes of contact time and gradually increasing to about 15 minutes before rinsing off, used daily for roughly 16 weeks and then twice weekly for maintenance.[^cyspera][^skintruth]

[[cysteamine|Cysteamine]] is a well-studied non-hydroquinone depigmenting agent for melasma: it beats placebo in a randomized trial, and in a pooled meta-analysis of 7 RCTs it did not differ significantly from hydroquinone 4%, though no single head-to-head trial has shown it clearly ahead of hydroquinone, [set out in full on [[cysteamine]]](#the-evidence). What makes this particular product page unusual is that two of the trials behind that evidence are directly tied to Cyspera's manufacturer: a placebo-controlled trial lists a co-author affiliated with Scientis Pharma SA, and a separate head-to-head trial against hydroquinone was funded by Scientis, [reported in full below](#the-evidence).[^mansouri][^nguyen] Neither trial states the Cyspera brand name; the placebo-controlled trial tested a cysteamine 5% cream (per its title) while the head-to-head trial's abstract does not state a concentration, so the trial evidence does not cleanly match the 7% the manufacturer's own current product page states, [detailed under What's In It](#whats-in-it).

Cysteamine's characteristic sulfur odor and its short-contact, wash-off application (rather than a leave-on cream) are distinctive features of this category, covered at the ingredient level on [[cysteamine]]; a head-to-head trial against hydroquinone rated hydroquinone cream better tolerated overall.[^nguyen]

## The Evidence

Whether cysteamine works for melasma, and how it compares with hydroquinone, is covered in full on [[cysteamine]]: in short, cysteamine 5% cream beats placebo, and pooled trial data put it close to, but not significantly better or worse than, hydroquinone 4%.[^mansouri][^mawu] Two of the trials establishing that evidence carry a direct link to this product's manufacturer.

A double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT randomized 50 patients with epidermal melasma to cysteamine 5% cream or placebo, applied once daily at bedtime for 4 months. Melasma Area and Severity Index (MASI) scores at 4 months were lower with cysteamine than placebo (7.2 ± 5.5 vs 11.6 ± 7.9, P=0.02).[^mansouri] The trial's published author affiliations include "Scientis Pharma SA, Geneva, Switzerland," this product's manufacturer.[^mansouri]

> **Bottom line.** Cysteamine 5% cream outperformed placebo on melasma severity in a trial with a manufacturer-affiliated co-author.[^mansouri]

A separate randomized, double-blinded trial in Australia compared cysteamine cream against hydroquinone cream over 16 weeks. Of 20 patients enrolled, 14 completed (5 cysteamine, 9 hydroquinone). Modified MASI fell 21.3% with cysteamine versus 32% with hydroquinone, a difference the authors reported as not statistically significant (P=0.3), and rated hydroquinone cream "generally better tolerated."[^nguyen] The published grant support for this trial is listed as "Scientis Pharma, Geneva, Switzerland."[^nguyen]

> **Bottom line.** In the one head-to-head, Scientis-funded trial, hydroquinone's point estimate for pigment reduction was larger than cysteamine's, in a small trial that did not reach statistical significance.[^nguyen]

A 2024 meta-analysis pooling these two trials with five other RCTs found cysteamine 5% cream more effective than placebo on MASI (SMD -0.84, P<0.00001) and not significantly different from hydroquinone 4% (SMD 0.16, P=0.42); further detail, including the trial-quality caveats, is on [[cysteamine]].[^mawu]

Neither the placebo-controlled trial nor the head-to-head trial names the Cyspera brand. The placebo-controlled trial tested a cysteamine 5% cream (stated in its title), while the head-to-head trial's published abstract does not state a concentration. Scientis's own current product page for Cyspera Intensive states "Concentration: Cysteamine 7%," while a retailer selling a product under the same name, Cyspera Intensive, states "5% Cysteamine."[^cyspera][^skintruth] So the manufacturer link to the trial evidence is direct and well documented, but the trial evidence does not cleanly match every version currently sold under the Cyspera Intensive name.

## What's In It

Two different ingredient lists are attached to products sold under the Cyspera Intensive name, consistent with the concentration difference above.

Scientis's own current product page lists: water, cysteamine HCl, isobionic-amide (a proprietary ingredient the brand does not independently substantiate), mineral oil, glycolic acid, potassium cetyl phosphate, glycerin, isopropyl myristate, cetyl alcohol, sodium ascorbyl phosphate, arachidyl alcohol, shea butter, ascorbyl palmitate, xanthan gum, and further emollients, fragrance, and preservatives.[^cyspera] [[glycolic-acid|Glycolic acid]] and the two vitamin C forms, [[sodium-ascorbyl-phosphate|sodium ascorbyl phosphate]] and [[ascorbyl-palmitate|ascorbyl palmitate]], appear alongside cysteamine but are not the product's studied active.

The retailer listing for the same product name lists a different formula: water, mineral oil, cysteamine HCl, [[niacinamide|niacinamide]], shea butter, glyceryl stearate, lecithin, isopropyl myristate, cetyl alcohol, and further emollients, fragrance, and preservatives, without glycolic acid.[^skintruth]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Reduce the appearance of dark spots in as little as 4 weeks."**[^cyspera] The cited placebo-controlled and head-to-head trials of cysteamine measured outcomes at 4 months and 16 weeks, not 4 weeks, and no trial tested this exact branded product.
- **"Isobionic-Amide," a named proprietary ingredient the manufacturer pairs with cysteamine.**[^cyspera] Isobionic-Amide has no independent supporting trial, alone or combined with cysteamine.
