Round Lab 1025 Dokdo Cream is a South Korean facial moisturizer from [[round-lab]], part of the brand's "1025 Dokdo" line named for Dokdo island, formulated around sea water and marketed with hyaluronic acid and ceramides; Target's US listing describes it as "packed with hyaluronic acid and ceramides, designed to deeply hydrate and nourish normal skin types."[^target]

> Moisturizing itself is well-evidenced: a Cochrane review found that regular emollient use reduces eczema flares and lowers the topical-corticosteroid dose needed to control them, covered on [[moisturizing]]. This cream's own hydrating work rests mainly on glycerin, a well-evidenced humectant, and a standard emollient/occlusive base; a manufacturer safety data sheet obtained via Target shows the marketed ceramides and hyaluronic acid are each present at trace concentrations, from about 0.05 ppm/50 ppb (ceramides) down to well under 1 part per billion (sodium hyaluronate).[^sds]

## Summary

Round Lab 1025 Dokdo Cream is marketed as a lightweight, unscented daily face cream for normal skin, built around sea water from Dokdo island and positioned with hyaluronic acid and ceramides as its headline ingredients.[^target] The category claim, that moisturizing helps skin, has strong trial support and is covered on [[moisturizing]]; this page is about how the product itself is formulated.

The safety data sheet COSMAX (the contract manufacturer) filed for this SKU lists the full formula by weight.[^sds] The largest ingredients are water (about 60.6%), glycerin (10%), dipropylene glycol and caprylic/capric triglyceride (4% each), and cetearyl alcohol (3.25%), the standard humectant/emollient base of a lightweight cream. Sea water, the ingredient the "Dokdo" line is named for, appears far down the list at about 0.001%. The ceramides and hyaluronic acid Target's listing calls out by name are present at [trace concentrations](#the-evidence), each well below 0.001% and some down to parts per billion, levels far lower than typical leave-on ceramide or hyaluronic-acid formulas.

Target lists the 80 ml size at $35.99 as of the date below.[^target]

## The Evidence

Whether moisturizing works is a well-evidenced, separate question from this product's formulation; see [[moisturizing]] for the Cochrane-review evidence that regular emollient use reduces eczema flares and lowers the corticosteroid dose needed to control them.

No study of this specific product was found. What can be checked is the formula itself. COSMAX's safety data sheet for the 80 ml SKU lists ingredient concentrations by weight.[^sds] Glycerin, a well-evidenced humectant (see [[glycerin]]), is the second-largest ingredient at 10%. The emollient/occlusive base, cetearyl alcohol (3.25%), caprylic/capric triglyceride (4%), shea butter (1%), and related fatty esters and waxes, does the remaining moisturizing work.

By contrast, the six named ceramide-family ingredients (Ceramide NP, Ceramide AS, Ceramide NS, Ceramide EOP, Ceramide AP, and phytosphingosine) are each listed at 5.0E-6% (about 0.05 ppm, or 50 ppb), and the three hyaluronic-acid forms (hyaluronic acid, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, and sodium hyaluronate) run from 8.3E-7% down to 8.3E-9% (as low as roughly 1 part in 12 billion).[^sds] See [[ceramides]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]] for what those ingredients are evidenced to do at typical leave-on concentrations; the concentrations disclosed here are far below that range.

> **Bottom line.** The safety data sheet for this cream shows a conventional glycerin-plus-emollient moisturizing base doing most of the work, while the ceramides and hyaluronic acid featured in its marketing are each present at trace, parts-per-million-or-lower concentrations.[^sds]
