## Summary

A cordless handheld blue-light device cleared over the counter for acne in April 2013.[^k124042] **Bottom line:** it is the rare home LED whose FDA file actually tells you the dose, which makes it the one device in this category you can genuinely compare against another. What the file does not contain is a single clinical result.

> **The number everyone else withholds.** The clearance's comparison table gives a peak wavelength of "414 nm ± 6", a dose rate of 22.4 mW/cm2 and a dose of 12 J/cm2, and states those are unchanged from its predecessor.[^k124042] Set that beside the home LED files covered here that publish wavelengths and no irradiance at all, such as [[omnilux-new-u]]. A dose you can read is the difference between a device you can assess and one you cannot.

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: unknown.** The Indications for Use read: "The Tanda Mini Skincare System is generally indicated to treat dermatological conditions. Specifically, blue light modules are indicated to treat mild to moderate inflammatory acne."[^k124042] Mild to moderate inflammatory acne is the whole of it. Comedonal acne is not named, and neither is any anti-ageing use.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal, because the file contains no study.** Performance Data lists four non-clinical tests only: electrical safety (IEC 60601-1:2005), electromagnetic compatibility (IEC 60601-1-2:2007), software verification and validation (IEC 62304:2006) and biocompatibility (IEC 10993), concluding that "in all instances, the Tanda Mini Skincare System functioned as intended".[^k124042] Functioning as intended is an engineering statement, not an efficacy one. The efficacy argument is that the device "delivers energy at the same dose and dose rate as the predicate device for the same indication and therefore no new questions of efficacy are raised".[^k124042]
- **Composition note: the safety features are specified, which is also unusual.** The device is described as cordless and handheld with a single on/off push button, delivering blue light at 414 nm through LEDs, with a skin contact sensor and a temperature sensor plus sound indicators carried over from the predecessor, and a textured treatment surface and vibration indicator added.[^k124042]
- **Record finding: no clearance is named Clear.** This product is widely discussed under retail names including Tanda Clear. A search of openFDA's 510(k) index for applicant or device name "tanda" returns 8 records across three applicants, and none of them carries the word Clear; the acne-indicated devices are the Tanda Mini Skincare System (K124042) and the Tanda Skincare System lineage it descends from.[^openfda] This page therefore covers the cleared device and declines to assert that any particular retail unit is it, exactly as [[revive-light-therapy-lux-spot]] does for its family.

## The Evidence

None specific to this device. What stands in for it is a chain of substantial equivalence running back through the firm's own products, and the file names the chain: the predicate is "Syneron Beauty Inc.'s Tanda Skincare System (K080591)", and the company is identified in the comparison table as "Syneron Beauty Inc (Formerly Pharos Life Corp)".[^k124042] That corporate succession is visible in the index too, where the eight Tanda clearances are split between Pharos Life Corporation, Syneron Beauty and Tanda Beauty Canada.[^openfda]

One small internal inconsistency is worth noting for anyone checking the file themselves: the Technological Characteristics section cites the predicate as K080951 while the Predicate Device heading and the comparison table both give K080591.[^k124042] K080591 is the number that resolves in openFDA, to the Tanda Skincare System cleared to Pharos Life Corporation on 2008-11-14.[^openfda]

Category evidence for blue light in acne is on [[home-led-light-therapy]], and the condition is on [[acne]]. Nothing on those pages is evidence about this unit, but unlike most of its competitors this unit at least publishes the numbers that would let someone try.

## Common Marketing Claims

There are none to quarantine here. No current brand storefront for the Tanda skincare line could be found: tanda.com now serves an unrelated workforce-management product under the page title "Employ with confidence", with no skincare content on it.[^domain] Any promotional language a reader encounters will come from third-party sellers, and the cleared indication against which to check it is mild to moderate inflammatory acne.[^k124042]
