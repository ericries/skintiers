Weleda Skin Food Original Ultra-Rich Cream is a fragrance-containing, lanolin-and-beeswax-based emollient balm for dry, rough skin on the face and body, first launched by the Swiss brand [[weleda|Weleda]] in 1926.[^weleda]

> A traditional occlusive, emollient-rich balm rather than a treatment product with a specific proven active: its ingredients (lanolin, beeswax, sunflower and almond oil) work by the same mechanism as other rich moisturizers, slowing water loss and softening dry skin, the general case set out on [[moisturizing]]. No independent, controlled trial of this exact cream is published; the brand's own figures come from an uncontrolled in-use survey.[^weleda]

## Summary

Skin Food Original Ultra-Rich Cream is Weleda's best-selling product, sold by the brand as its original 1926 formula and marketed for dry or rough skin on the face, hands, feet, and elbows, as well as off-label uses such as a makeup primer.[^weleda] It is a thick, water-based balm built on lanolin, beeswax, sunflower seed oil, and sweet almond oil, the same class of occlusive-plus-emollient ingredients (not the identical ingredient) that give rich moisturizers like [[petrolatum|petrolatum]]-based ointments their barrier-slowing effect; the general evidence that occlusive, emollient moisturizers reduce water loss and improve dry skin is set out on [[moisturizing]].

No independently published, controlled trial of this specific formula is cited by the brand. Weleda instead reports its own uncontrolled in-use survey of 129 people, finding that most participants agreed the cream hydrated skin and improved the feel of dry, flaky skin ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)); that is brand-reported, single-arm evidence, not the kind that supports a strong grade, and it is reported separately under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims). The formula carries a botanical extract blend (rosemary, chamomile, calendula, pansy) that Weleda calls its "Phyto-Infusion," and it is not fragrance-free: the ingredient list includes fragrance (parfum) and several fragrance allergens, though Weleda states the fragrance is not synthetic ([see Tolerability](#tolerability)).[^weleda] Weleda's own US site lists the 2.5 fl oz tube at $22.99, with smaller travel and mini sizes also sold, as of 2026-08-23 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)).[^weleda]

## The Evidence

Occlusive and emollient moisturizers are a well-evidenced way to slow transepidermal water loss and improve the feel of dry skin, the mechanism-level case made on [[moisturizing]]. Skin Food's base is not petrolatum (the strongest-evidenced single occlusive) but a mix of lanolin, beeswax, sunflower seed oil, and sweet almond oil, traditional occlusive and emollient ingredients that work by the same general mechanism rather than a specific ingredient tested in the trials cited on that page.

No independently published or controlled trial of Skin Food itself is cited here. Weleda's own product page reports an in-use survey of 129 subjects: 93% agreed the cream "instantly hydrates skin" after a single use, 93% agreed it "gives skin longer lasting hydration" after daily use for two weeks, and 90% agreed it "visibly improves the feel of dry, flaky skin" after daily use for four weeks.[^weleda]

> That survey has no control group and is not independently published, so it is brand-reported perception data rather than evidence a grade can rest on; it is treated here as a marketing claim, not a clinical result.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on Weleda's own product page.[^weleda] The ingredients with a functional role are described below; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

Occlusive and emollient base:

- Lanolin, beeswax (cera alba), sunflower (helianthus annuus) seed oil, and sweet almond (prunus amygdalus dulcis) oil, the balm's main occlusive and emollient ingredients
- [[glycerin]], a humectant
- Hydrolyzed beeswax and polyglyceryl-3 polyricinoleate, texture and emulsion aids

Botanical extracts (Weleda's "Phyto-Infusion"): rosemary (rosmarinus officinalis) leaf extract, chamomile (chamomilla recutita) flower extract, calendula officinalis flower extract, and viola tricolor (pansy) extract.[^weleda]

Base, preservative, and fragrance system: water, alcohol, sorbitan olivate, arginine, zinc sulfate, fragrance (parfum), and the fragrance allergens limonene, linalool, geraniol, citral, and coumarin.[^weleda]

Weleda states the formula is certified natural to NATRUE standards and is free of synthetic preservatives, synthetic fragrance, parabens, and phthalates.[^weleda]

## Tolerability

The formula is not fragrance-free: its ingredient list includes fragrance (parfum) and five fragrance allergens (limonene, linalool, geraniol, citral, coumarin), a consideration for fragrance-avoidant or reactive skin, even though Weleda states the fragrance itself is naturally derived rather than synthetic.[^weleda] Weleda describes the product as "dermatologist approved," a brand-stated claim with no cited independent test or panel size given.[^weleda]
