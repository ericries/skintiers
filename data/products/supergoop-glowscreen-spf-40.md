Glowscreen SPF 40 is Supergoop's tinted, dewy-finish sunscreen and makeup primer, sold in four shades (Sunrise, Dawn, Golden Hour, Sunset) and built around a chemical UV filter set with niacinamide and hyaluronic acid worked into the base.[^1][^2]

> A broad-spectrum SPF 40 sunscreen using an all-chemical filter set, avobenzone, octisalate, octocrylene, and (in three of its four shades) homosalate. Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Its avobenzone-based UVA coverage is genuine once photostabilized by octocrylene, though older and less photostable than the modern filters (Tinosorb, Mexoryl) sold abroad. The niacinamide and hyaluronic acid behind its "all-day hydration" marketing are listed with no disclosed percentage, so that claim cannot be independently checked against the concentration-dependent evidence on [[niacinamide]]. It is priced at $19 for 1.7 fl oz and $48 for 2.5 fl oz on Supergoop's own site.[^1]

## Summary

Glowscreen SPF 40 is a facial sunscreen from Supergoop, a US suncare brand built around cosmetically elegant formulas. It differs from a plain sunscreen mainly in finish and positioning: a tinted, illuminating lotion sold in four shades that doubles as a makeup primer, rather than a novel health mechanism.[^1] The core purpose is the same as any sunscreen's: broad-spectrum UV protection is the best-evidenced way to slow photoaging and lower the risk of sun-related skin cancer, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

Per its FDA Drug Facts label, the Dawn/Golden Hour/Sunset shades carry avobenzone 3%, homosalate 4%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 8%, rated Broad Spectrum SPF 40.[^2] The Sunrise shade uses a different filter mix on Supergoop's own site: avobenzone 3%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 10%, with no homosalate.[^1] Avobenzone is the formula's only dedicated UVA filter in every shade; octocrylene helps keep it from degrading in sunlight ([the filter breakdown is in The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1][^2]

What distinguishes it from Supergoop's own plainer, clear Unseen Sunscreen line, and from a mineral tinted sunscreen like [[la-roche-posay-anthelios-mineral-tinted-spf-50|La Roche-Posay's Anthelios Mineral Tinted SPF 50]], is the tint, the shade range, and the added niacinamide and sodium hyaluronate in the base.[^1][^2] Neither ingredient's concentration is disclosed, so the "clinically tested" all-day hydration claim rests on Supergoop's own testing, not a published, concentration-matched trial (weighed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)). It is listed at $19 for the standard 1.7 fl oz size and $48 for a 2.5 fl oz jumbo size on Supergoop's own site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)).[^1]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific product was located; the product-specific facts are its filter composition and FDA label rating.

The FDA Drug Facts label for the Dawn shade (revision date January 2026, filed with DailyMed) lists the actives as avobenzone 3%, homosalate 4%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 8%, at SPF 40, Broad Spectrum; the Sunset shade's label matches.[^2] Supergoop's own product page lists a different active set for the Sunrise shade: avobenzone 3%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 10%, with no homosalate.[^1] SPF is a UVB (sunburn) number; the SPF 40 rating is FDA-tested for UVB strength and does not by itself certify UVA (photoaging) protection, a separate axis described on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

[[octisalate]] and [[homosalate]] (where present) are weak-to-moderate UVB absorbers; [[octocrylene]] contributes UVB and short-wave UVA-II absorption and also stabilizes [[avobenzone]], the formula's sole dedicated UVA filter, which degrades under sunlight unless a co-filter holds it stable.[^2] Measured against the global field, this remains the older US filter set: avobenzone is a weaker, less photostable UVA filter than the modern organic filters (bemotrizinol/Tinosorb S, bisoctrizole/Tinosorb M, the Mexoryl filters) standard in the EU, Korea, and Australia, a comparison detailed on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

On the cosmetic side, [[niacinamide]] and sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]) both appear on the inactive-ingredient list, but the label discloses no percentage for either.[^1][^2] Niacinamide's barrier-support and hydration evidence, summarized on [[niacinamide]], was generated at roughly 2% to 5%; with no disclosed concentration here, this product's hydration claim cannot be checked against that evidence.

> **Bottom line.** The FDA-tested SPF 40 Broad Spectrum rating reflects a genuine, if not top-tier, US chemical filter set: octisalate, octocrylene, and (in most shades) homosalate carry the UVB side, and avobenzone, stabilized by octocrylene, carries the UVA side.[^1][^2] The niacinamide/hyaluronic acid "hydration" claim is undisclosed in concentration and untested independently of the brand's own marketing.

Directions on the label call to "apply generously and evenly 15 minutes before sun exposure" and to reapply "at least every 2 hours."[^2] How much protection any SPF delivers in practice depends on applying enough product, a point covered generally on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## What's In It

The active filters and inactive ingredients as declared on the FDA Drug Facts label for the Dawn shade (the Sunset shade matches; the Sunrise shade uses a different filter set, noted below).[^2] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

UV filters (Drug Facts actives, Dawn/Golden Hour/Sunset shades):

- [[avobenzone]] 3%, the formula's only dedicated UVA filter
- [[homosalate]] 4%, a UVB filter
- [[octisalate]] 5%, a weak, secondary UVB filter
- [[octocrylene]] 8%, a UVB and short-wave UVA-II filter that also stabilizes avobenzone

The Sunrise shade instead lists avobenzone 3%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 10%, with no homosalate, per Supergoop's own product page.[^1]

Other functional/notable ingredients: [[niacinamide]] and sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]), both with no disclosed concentration; [[titanium-dioxide]] and mica (tint/pigment, with iron oxides and, in some shades, bismuth oxychloride, for shade-matching); [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E) and [[ferulic-acid]] as antioxidants, both listed near the end of the inactive-ingredient list.[^2]

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, propanediol and glycerin (solvents/humectants), butyloctyl salicylate and C12-15 alkyl benzoate (emollients), polymethylsilsesquioxane and isododecane (silicone/texture agents), glyceryl stearate citrate, glyceryl stearate, cetyl phosphate and related esters (emulsifiers), caprylic/capric triglyceride (emollient), diisopropyl sebacate, isodecyl neopentanoate, lauryl lactate, and coco-caprylate (additional esters/emollients), silica and tin oxide (texture and mattifying agents), triethoxycaprylylsilane (surface-treatment agent), arginine (pH adjuster), butylene glycol, 1,2-hexanediol, caprylyl glycol, chlorphenesin and hydroxyacetophenone (preservative system), acrylates/C10-30 alkyl acrylate crosspolymer (thickener), trisodium ethylenediamine disuccinate (chelator), and phospholipids, pantothenic acid, sea lavender (Limonium Gerberi) extract, Leuconostoc/radish root ferment filtrate, and cocoa (Theobroma Cacao) seed extract (additional conditioning ingredients).[^2]

Not in the formula: no fragrance or parfum on the FDA-filed inactive-ingredient list for the Dawn shade.[^2]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Clinically tested to deliver all-day hydration."** Supergoop's own product page makes this claim without a linked published trial, sample size, or peer review; the niacinamide and hyaluronic acid behind it carry no disclosed concentration on the label.[^1][^2]
- **"Visibly evens tone" and glow/luminosity finish.** A cosmetic, tint-and-mica-driven finish claim, not a graded skin-health outcome.[^1]
- **"Doubles as a primer."** A texture/wear claim about layering under makeup, separate from the graded UV-filter protection above.[^1]
- **"Helps prevent the visible signs of aging" framing tied to the SPF number.** Broad-spectrum UV protection genuinely slows photoaging (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)), but the marketing framing extends beyond the FDA label's own Uses statement (helps prevent sunburn; decreases skin cancer and early skin aging risk when used as directed with other sun protection measures).[^2]
- **"PA+++" printed on the package label.** The DailyMed-filed label carries "Broad Spectrum Sunscreen SPF 40 PA+++" on its front-of-pack text, outside the FDA-regulated Drug Facts fields; PA is a Japanese/Korean UVA-protection-grade system, not an FDA-tested rating, so it is not independently verified the way the SPF 40 Broad Spectrum designation is.[^2]
