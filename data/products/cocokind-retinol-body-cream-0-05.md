Cocokind Retinol Body Cream is a fragrance-free body moisturizer built around a manufacturer-declared 0.05% retinol, packaged in an 8 fl oz (236 ml) tube alongside shea butter, squalane, and ceramide NP, and marketed to "firm skin's appearance and improve the look of uneven tone and texture."[^1]

> [[retinol]] has genuine, if modest, trial evidence for improving fine wrinkles and pigment, drawn from facial and inner-arm/forearm testing at 0.1% to 0.4%, not from a body cream like this one. No cited trial tests trunk or leg skin, or a concentration as low as this product's 0.05%.[^retinol] Cosmetic chemist Javon Ford has separately warned against DIY-mixing raw retinol into a body lotion at home, on stability grounds, calling pre-formulated retinol body creams like this one the more sensible route to the same goal.[^ford]

## Summary

This is Cocokind's plain retinol body moisturizer: a fragrance-free cream built around [[retinol]] at a manufacturer-declared 0.05%, in a base of shea butter, squalane, ceramide NP, and a rice ferment filtrate, sold in a single 8 fl oz tube.[^1] The general case for topical retinol, real but modest vehicle-controlled trial evidence for fine wrinkles and pigment, is set out on [[retinol]]. The cited retinol trials tested facial and inner-arm/forearm skin, not trunk or leg skin, and neither tested a concentration as low as this product's 0.05%; the two landmark trials used 0.1% and 0.4%.[^retinol]

No published trial isolates this product, its 0.05% strength, or retinol's effect on trunk or leg skin specifically, so its grade below rests entirely on facial and inner-arm/forearm evidence at higher doses, extrapolated to broader body skin and a lower dose. Cosmetic chemist Javon Ford, reviewing DIY retinol-in-lotion mixing, called ready-made retinol body creams the more practical option over home-mixing raw retinol into an open-air container that will not preserve it, without commenting on this product's efficacy.[^ford] It is listed at $21 for the single 8 fl oz size on Cocokind's own site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-15).[^1]

## The Evidence

Topical retinol has vehicle-controlled trial evidence for improving fine wrinkles, texture, and pigment, with a full accounting of the trials, their populations, and limits set out on [[retinol]]. Nothing specific to this product was found: no cited trial tests trunk or leg skin at any concentration, and none tests a concentration as low as Cocokind's 0.05% formula, so the grade below rests on facial and inner-arm/forearm evidence at higher doses (0.1% to 0.4%) extrapolated to broader body skin at a lower dose.[^retinol]

The two controlled trials behind retinol's evidence, cataloged in full on [[retinol]], used 0.4% on the inner arm of elderly subjects over 24 weeks and 0.1% mainly on the face over 52 weeks, with a smaller forearm sub-study at that same 0.1% dose and duration.[^retinol] This product is printed at 0.05%, below both tested concentrations, and applied across broader trunk and limb skin rather than the face or the specific inner-arm/forearm test sites used in the trials. Whether a lower dose applied more broadly across the body produces a comparable effect has not been tested.

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product, its 0.05% concentration, or retinol's effect when applied across trunk or leg skin. Its grade rests on retinol's modest, real, facial and inner-arm/forearm evidence, extrapolated downward in dose and outward in application site.[^retinol]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Firm skin's appearance and improve the look of uneven tone and texture."** Retinol's controlled trial evidence for texture and pigment improvement comes from facial and inner-arm/forearm testing at 0.1% to 0.4%, not from the trunk or leg skin this product targets, and none of it used a 0.05% concentration.[^retinol]
- **"Youthful body skin treatment."** A positioning phrase, not a tested claim; no cited trial measures retinol's anti-aging effect on trunk or leg skin, or at this product's 0.05% concentration.
- **The 0.05% concentration as a meaningful strength marker.** No cited trial establishes a dose-response relationship for retinol at any concentration, let alone one below the lowest tested facial dose (0.1%).[^retinol]
