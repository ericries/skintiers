Clinique Take The Day Off Cleansing Balm is a solid-to-oil cleansing balm, used to dissolve makeup and sunscreen as the first step of a [[double-cleansing|double cleanse]]. Clinique lists the 3.8 oz jar at $44.00 (a 1 oz size at $15.00 and a 6.7 oz size at $58.00 are also sold).[^clinique]

> A cleansing balm earns its place by how much it removes that a water-based cleanser leaves behind, and the format evidence for that job is set out on [[double-cleansing]]. This formula is a short list of emollient esters and safflower oil plus two rinse-off emulsifiers, nothing more. Its one real, list-verifiable advantage over many other balms, including [[banila-co-clean-it-zero-cleansing-balm-original|Banila Co's Clean It Zero]], is that it contains no added fragrance, which matters for reactive skin even though fragrance-free is a formulation fact, not a clinical claim on its own.

## Summary

Clinique markets Take The Day Off Cleansing Balm as its "iconic makeup remover," calling it the "#1 Face Cleanser in the U.S." by dollar sales, and states it delivers "over 95% removal of face makeup, sunscreen, and pollutants," a figure the brand attributes to average removal of longwearing foundation, hybrid sunscreen, and carbon powder.[^clinique] It is massaged onto dry skin, where Clinique says it "transforms from a solid balm into a silky oil," then rinsed off with water.[^clinique]

The formula leads with ethylhexyl palmitate and safflower seed oil as the cleansing oils, followed by caprylic/capric triglyceride, then sorbeth-30 tetraoleate and PEG-5 glyceryl triisostearate, the emulsifiers that let an oil balm rinse clean, plus polyethylene for texture, water, tocopherol (vitamin E), and a phenoxyethanol preservative: nine ingredients total, which the brand calls a "minimal formula" of "less than 10 ingredients."[^clinique] It is listed at $44.00 for the 3.8 oz jar at Clinique ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-26).[^clinique]

## The Evidence

The product-specific claim worth checking is the removal one, and it is directionally right for the format: as set out on [[double-cleansing]], an oil-based cleanser removes water-resistant sunscreen that a foaming water-based cleanser leaves largely behind, so an oil balm is a sound first step on heavy-sunscreen or long-wear-makeup days. That controlled evidence is a single small study, and no independently published trial of this exact product is cited here; the brand's own "over 95% removal" figure comes from its internal testing description, not a cited independent trial.[^clinique] The formula carries no active treatment ingredients: no retinoid, no acid exfoliant, no vitamin C. Its one genuinely differentiating, list-verifiable fact is that the ingredient list has no Fragrance or Parfum entry, unlike [[banila-co-clean-it-zero-cleansing-balm-original]] and other cleansing balms on the market that add fragrance; Clinique's own "Free From" list names fragrance among the excluded ingredients.[^clinique]

> **Bottom line.** A simple, competently formulated oil-to-emulsion cleansing balm whose real value is dissolving makeup and sunscreen, a job the double-cleanse evidence supports. It is a cleanser, not a treatment, and its "moisture balance" and pore-related claims are the brand's own framing; the fragrance-free formulation is its clearest, verifiable edge for reactive skin.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), as decoded by incidecoder from the product's packaging and confirmed on the brand listing.

Ethylhexyl Palmitate, Carthamus Tinctorius (Safflower) Seed Oil, Caprylic/Capric Triglyceride, Sorbeth-30 Tetraoleate, Polyethylene, PEG-5 Glyceryl Triisostearate, Water, Tocopherol, Phenoxyethanol.

Notable: ethylhexyl palmitate and safflower seed oil are the emollient cleansing oils, with caprylic/capric triglyceride adding a third; sorbeth-30 tetraoleate and PEG-5 glyceryl triisostearate are the emulsifiers that let it rinse off with water; polyethylene sets the balm's solid texture; tocopherol is vitamin E, an antioxidant; phenoxyethanol is the preservative; and there is no Fragrance or Parfum entry anywhere on the list.
