## Summary

A $175 pen-shaped microcurrent wand for the eye and mouth area, sold on instant results lasting eight hours.[^brand] **Bottom line:** the gap between what this device is cleared to do and what it is sold to do is the widest on any device covered here. Its FDA clearance permits "facial stimulation" and nothing more specific; its product page promises reduced fine lines, plumper lips and depuffed under-eyes, with a "CLINICALLY PROVEN" badge backed by a questionnaire filled in two minutes after use. Microcurrent's own evidence is argued on [[nuface-mini-microcurrent-device]] and is not re-run here.

> **The entire cleared indication is one sentence.** K182424's Indications for Use form reads: "The NuFACE FIX™ Device is intended for facial stimulation and is indicated for over-the-counter cosmetic use."[^k182424] That is the whole of it. No line, wrinkle, puffiness, lip or lift claim appears anywhere in it. The FDA's own letter in the same file names the regulation it was cleared under as "Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulator For Pain Relief", 21 CFR 882.5890, and the clearance was signed out of the Division of Neurological and Physical Medicine Devices.[^k182424]

## The Rubric

- **Evidence quality: anecdotal, and the badge says otherwise.** The product page carries a standing banner reading "FDA CLEARED CLINICALLY PROVEN".[^brand] The only figures supporting it on that page are "97%* saw an instant reduction in fine lines around the eyes" and "93%* saw instantly plumper-looking lips", footnoted "*Results from a consumer post-treatment questionnaire 2 minutes post-treatment".[^brand] A questionnaire completed two minutes after treatment, with no published sample size, is self-reported perception. It is not a clinical proof of anything, and the word clinical should not be doing work that a questionnaire did.
- **Effect size: unknown, and deliberately temporary.** The brand's own framing is that the effect expires: it "delivers instant visible volume at the skin's surface that lasts up to 8 hours".[^brand] Volume at the surface lasting eight hours describes transient swelling or fluid shift, not a structural change, and the page is unusually honest in saying so. Anyone reading "Reduces fine lines and wrinkles" in the Key Benefits list next to it should carry the eight-hour qualifier across.[^brand]
- **Composition note: three minutes, three modes, one small tip.** It is described as a pen-like design allowing "precise application around the eyes and lips exactly where you need it" in three minutes, powered by microcurrent with "three targeted treatment modes".[^brand] Treatment-area size is the real difference from the full-face devices, and it is the reason this page exists separately rather than being folded into them.
- **Record finding: FIX is a product family, not one clearance.** openFDA returns 15 clearances to Carol Cole Company and its NuFACE trading name, two of which are FIX devices: K182424 for the "NuFACE FIX Skin Toning Device" in December 2018 and K240564 for "NuFACE® FIX+" in June 2024.[^openfda] The word MicroWand appears in none of them. This page covers the 2018 clearance and does not assert that the unit currently sold under the MicroWand name is either of them.

## The Evidence

There is no trial of this device in the clearance file, which contains an Indications for Use form and a substantial-equivalence letter. For the modality, [[nuface-mini-microcurrent-device]] and [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]] carry the argument and the grades, and [[nuface]] lists the family.

One regulatory detail is worth stating precisely rather than as a gotcha, because two different names attach to the same code. The FDA letter gives the Regulation Name for 21 CFR 882.5890 as "Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulator For Pain Relief".[^k182424] openFDA's classification record for the product code actually assigned, NFO, gives the device name as "Stimulator, Transcutaneous Electrical, Aesthetic Purposes" under the same regulation number, Class II, with a medical specialty of Neurology.[^openfda] So the aesthetic category exists and this device sits in it; the regulation that category lives under is the pain-relief one. Both are true, and neither is a wrinkle indication.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from NuFACE's own product page and **not** endorsed here.

- "Depuff, plump, and smooth the look of fine lines in real-time."[^brand] Note "the look of", which is doing real work.
- "Reduces fine lines and wrinkles" and "Results last up to 8 hours", listed together under Key Benefits.[^brand] The second qualifies the first.
- "97%* saw an instant reduction in fine lines around the eyes" and "93%* saw instantly plumper-looking lips".[^brand] Consumer questionnaire at two minutes, no sample size.
- The site banner "FDA CLEARED CLINICALLY PROVEN".[^brand] FDA cleared is accurate and means substantially equivalent to an existing device for facial stimulation. Clinically proven is not supported by anything on the page.
