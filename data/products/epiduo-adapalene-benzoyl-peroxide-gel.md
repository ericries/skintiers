Epiduo is a fixed-dose gel combining [[adapalene]] 0.1%, a synthetic [[retinoids|retinoid]], with [[benzoyl-peroxide|benzoyl peroxide]] 2.5% in a single once-daily application, FDA-labeled as "indicated for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in patients 12 years of age and older."[^epiduo-rx]

> Two individually well-evidenced acne actives in one gel rather than a novel ingredient: adapalene is a solidly evidenced topical retinoid and benzoyl peroxide is a solidly evidenced first-line topical, and pairing a retinoid with benzoyl peroxide is a well-established combination strategy (see [[adapalene]] and [[benzoyl-peroxide]] for that evidence).[^epiduo-rx] The original branded gel is dispensed by prescription, and as of May 2026 the identical 0.1%/2.5% formula is also FDA-approved for over-the-counter sale under the Differin Epiduo name, the label on that carton describing it as the "First FDA-approved over-the-counter dual active ingredient acne treatment."[^epiduo-otc]

## Summary

Epiduo is a leave-on gel applied once daily over the whole affected area, not a spot treatment. The over-the-counter Differin Epiduo label gives the directions as: "use once daily. Clean the skin gently and pat dry before applying the product. Cover the entire affected area with a thin layer... If going outside, apply sunscreen after using this product."[^epiduo-otc]

The formula's distinguishing feature is that it pairs two separately proven [[acne]] actives in one product rather than requiring the two to be bought and layered separately: [[adapalene]], a solidly evidenced topical retinoid, and [[benzoyl-peroxide|benzoyl peroxide]], a solidly evidenced first-line topical that also suppresses the antibiotic resistance a retinoid alone does not address. Both ingredient pages carry the general evidence for each active; this page does not repeat those trial numbers.

Epiduo has existed as a prescription drug for years, and its FDA prescribing information (DailyMed, labeler Physicians Total Care, marketed by Galderma) confirms it is "Rx only."[^epiduo-rx] The same active combination and concentrations also cleared the FDA as an over-the-counter switch under the Differin Epiduo name, with an initial marketing start date of May 22, 2026, and a "First FDA-approved over-the-counter dual active ingredient acne treatment" claim printed on its carton.[^epiduo-otc] That OTC version is the one currently listed on Target, priced from $19.99 for a 15 g/0.5 oz tube to $34.99 for a 45 g/1.6 oz tube.[^target]

## The Evidence

Adapalene 0.1% is a well-evidenced topical retinoid for acne, level with benzoyl peroxide and tretinoin in head-to-head data; see [[adapalene]]. Benzoyl peroxide 2.5% is a well-evidenced first-line topical acne treatment whose combination with a retinoid outperforms either agent alone; see [[benzoyl-peroxide]]. Both DailyMed labels for this exact combination, the original prescription version and the 2026 over-the-counter switch, carry the same indication, "for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in patients 12 years of age and older" on the prescription label and "For the treatment of acne" on the OTC Drug Facts box, so the fixed-dose product has its own FDA sign-off rather than resting purely on the two ingredients' separate evidence.[^epiduo-rx][^epiduo-otc]

## What's In It

Active ingredients, per the Differin Epiduo Drug Facts label:

- Adapalene 0.1% ([[adapalene]]), a synthetic retinoid[^epiduo-otc]
- Benzoyl peroxide 2.5% ([[benzoyl-peroxide]])[^epiduo-otc]

Inactive ingredients, per the same label: "acrylamide/sodium acryloyldimethyltaurate copolymer, docusate sodium, edetate disodium, glycerin, isohexadecane, poloxamer 124, polysorbate 80, propylene glycol, purified water, and sorbitan oleate."[^epiduo-otc]
