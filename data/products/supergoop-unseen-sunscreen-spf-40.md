Supergoop! Unseen Sunscreen SPF 40 is a clear, gel-like face sunscreen built entirely on organic (chemical) UV filters, formulated to leave no visible cast and to double as a makeup primer.[^1]

> A broad-spectrum SPF 40 sunscreen using an all-chemical filter set (avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, octocrylene). Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; SPF 40 is a high UVB rating, but the protection an SPF number promises depends on applying enough, which most people do not do. Its avobenzone-based UVA coverage is genuine once photostabilized by octocrylene, though older and less photostable than the modern filters (Tinosorb, Mexoryl) sold abroad. It contains no fragrance/parfum on its FDA label. Supergoop's own site no longer sells this exact formulation, having replaced it with a reformulated Unseen Sunscreen SPF 50.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Unseen Sunscreen is a facial sunscreen from Supergoop, a US suncare brand built around clear, cosmetically elegant formulas. This SPF 40 version is a completely oil-free, gel-cream sunscreen designed to sink in without a white cast or greasy finish and to sit under makeup as a primer. Its core purpose is the same as any sunscreen's: broad-spectrum UV protection is the best-evidenced way to slow photoaging and lower the risk of sun-related skin cancer, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

What distinguishes it from a plainer sunscreen is the filter combination and the cosmetic finish rather than a novel health mechanism. Per its FDA Drug Facts label, the actives are avobenzone 3%, homosalate 8%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 4%, an all-organic (chemical) filter set rated Broad Spectrum SPF 40.[^1] Avobenzone, at the US maximum concentration, is the formula's only dedicated UVA filter; octocrylene helps keep it from degrading in sunlight ([the filter breakdown is in The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1] The formula carries no fragrance or parfum on its FDA-filed ingredient list.[^1]

A notable finding while researching this profile: Supergoop's own current product page no longer sells this SPF 40 formula. It has been replaced there by a reformulated Unseen Sunscreen SPF 50, whose Drug Facts panel lists different active percentages (avobenzone 3%, homosalate 7%, octisalate 5%, octocrylene 9%), and the brand's own site frames it as the successor to "the original Unseen Sunscreen formula."[^2] The SPF 40 version profiled here remains on file with the FDA (label revised August 2024) under the same "Unseen Sunscreen" branding and cosmetic positioning, invisible, weightless, and doubling as a makeup primer, that the brand now applies to the SPF 50 formula.[^1][^2]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific product was located; the product-specific facts are its filter composition and FDA label rating.

The FDA Drug Facts label (revised August 2024) lists the actives as avobenzone 3%, homosalate 8%, octocrylene 4%, and octisalate 5%, at SPF 40, Broad Spectrum.[^1] SPF is a UVB (sunburn) number; the SPF 40 rating is FDA-tested for UVB strength and does not by itself certify UVA (photoaging) protection, a separate axis described on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

Three of the four filters, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene, are UVB absorbers; UVA coverage rests on avobenzone alone.[^1] [[avobenzone]] at its 3% US maximum provides genuine UVA coverage once photostabilized, but it degrades under sunlight unless a co-filter holds it stable. [[octocrylene]] serves that stabilizing role here, in addition to contributing its own UVB and short-wave UVA-II absorption. Measured against the global field, this remains the older US filter set: avobenzone is a weaker, less photostable UVA filter than the modern organic filters (bemotrizinol/Tinosorb S, bisoctrizole/Tinosorb M, the Mexoryl filters) standard in the EU, Korea, and Australia, a comparison detailed on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

> **Bottom line.** The FDA-tested SPF 40 Broad Spectrum rating reflects a genuine, if not top-tier, US chemical filter set: homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene carry the UVB side, and avobenzone, at its permitted maximum and stabilized by octocrylene, carries the UVA side.[^1] That UVA coverage is real but older and less photostable than the modern-filter products sold outside the US.

Directions on the label call to "apply generously and evenly 15 minutes before sun exposure," and to reapply "after 40 minutes of swimming or sweating, immediately after towel drying, at least every 2 hours."[^1] How much protection any SPF delivers in practice depends on applying enough product, a point covered generally on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## What's In It

The active filters and inactive ingredients as declared on the FDA Drug Facts label.[^1] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

UV filters (Drug Facts actives):

- [[avobenzone]] 3%, the formula's only dedicated UVA filter, at the US permitted maximum
- [[homosalate]] 8%, a UVB filter
- [[octisalate]] 5%, a weak, secondary UVB filter
- [[octocrylene]] 4%, a UVB and short-wave UVA-II filter that also stabilizes avobenzone

Other functional/notable ingredients: Boswellia Serrata (frankincense) resin extract, jojoba esters, shea butter (Butyrospermum Parkii), and tocopherol (vitamin E, an antioxidant).[^1]

Base, texture, and preservative system: isododecane, dimethicone crosspolymer, dimethicone/bis-isobutyl PPG-20 crosspolymer, polymethylsilsesquioxane, isohexadecane, dicaprylyl carbonate, medium-chain triglycerides, polyester-7, neopentyl glycol diheptanoate, meadowfoam estolide, Lithothamnion calcareum extract (algae extract), mannitol, lecithin (sunflower), microcrystalline cellulose, diatomaceous earth, zinc sulfate, and silicon dioxide.[^1]

Not in the formula: no fragrance or parfum on the FDA-filed inactive-ingredient list.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"100% invisible, weightless, scentless" and "doubles as a primer."** Supergoop's current product page for the Unseen Sunscreen line, now selling the reformulated SPF 50 version, describes the formula this way and positions it to be worn on its own or as a makeup primer.[^2] The SPF 40 formula profiled here carried the same "Unseen" name and cosmetic positioning before being discontinued on the brand's own site; the cosmetic finish is a texture claim, separate from the graded UV-filter protection above.
- **Oxybenzone- and octinoxate-free.** A composition point about which UV filters are absent, not an efficacy claim; the label's active filters are avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene.[^1]
