RINVOQ is AbbVie's oral extended-release upadacitinib tablet, approved under NDA 211675 on 16 August 2019 as a new molecular entity.[^drugsfda] It now carries nine separate FDA indications, and exactly one of them names a disease of the skin: refractory moderate to severe [[atopic-dermatitis]] in patients 12 years and older.[^label] The other eight are arthritides and inflammatory bowel disease.[^label]

> **Most of this product is not a dermatology drug.** Nine labelled indications, one of which names a skin disease. Three tablet strengths, of which dermatology uses two. And a sibling oral solution, RINVOQ LQ, that shares some of the tablets' indications but is not approved for eczema at all. The clinical case for the molecule is argued on [[upadacitinib]]; what this page adds is which RINVOQ a dermatology patient is actually handed, and which parts of the boxed warning were measured on this drug rather than borrowed from another one.

## Summary

The marketed tablet comes in three strengths: 15 mg (NDC 0074-2306), 30 mg (0074-2310) and 45 mg (0074-1043), all extended-release, in 30-count bottles except the 45 mg, which is sold in 28.[^ndc] For atopic dermatitis the label uses only the first two. Patients 12 and over weighing at least 40 kg, and adults under 65, start at "RINVOQ 15 mg orally once daily" and may go up to 30 mg if the response is inadequate; adults 65 and older are held at 15 mg, as are patients with severe renal impairment.[^label] The 45 mg strength appears nowhere in the eczema dosing. It is the induction dose for ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease, taken for 8 and 12 weeks respectively before dropping back.[^label] A dermatology patient will not be prescribed the largest tablet in the range.

The indication itself is written as a last resort rather than an option. It covers patients "whose disease is not adequately controlled with other systemic drug products, including biologics, or when use of those therapies are inadvisable", so by the label's own wording, a failure of something like [[dupilumab]] comes first.[^label]

RINVOQ LQ is a separate product on a separate application: NDA 218347, approved 26 April 2024 as a new dosage form, a 1 mg/mL oral solution.[^drugsfda][^ndc] The label grants it psoriatic arthritis and polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis alongside the tablets, phrasing those indications "RINVOQ/RINVOQ LQ".[^label] The atopic dermatitis indication names RINVOQ alone.[^label] The cartons of both products say the same thing in a box: "RINVOQ and RINVOQ LQ are NOT substitutable".[^carton]

## The Rubric

**Refractory moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (oral, prescription)**

- **Effect size: strong.** Graded on [[upadacitinib]], which holds tier good on a 2026 real-world meta-analysis; those figures are not repeated here. What the product adds is the ceiling the label puts on them: this is the drug you reach for when the systemic options before it have failed, not the one you reach for first.
- **Evidence quality: established.** FDA approval for this indication rests on randomized controlled trials, and the efficacy claim is not the uncertain part of this product. The uncertain part is how much of the boxed warning applies to an eczema patient, and that is treated below.

## The Evidence

The efficacy evidence sits on [[upadacitinib]] and is not repeated here. The product-level question this section answers is a different one: what the boxed warning rests on. Its five headings are not evidenced the same way, and the label is explicit about which is which.[^label]

For serious infections, the warning is about this drug: "Patients treated with RINVOQ/RINVOQ LQ are at increased risk for developing serious infections that may lead to hospitalization or death". Thrombosis is attributed to the class including this drug: thromboses "have occurred in patients treated for inflammatory conditions with JAK inhibitors, including RINVOQ". Malignancy is split: "Lymphoma and other malignancies have been observed in patients treated with RINVOQ", followed by a comparative rate drawn from elsewhere.

The mortality heading is the one worth reading twice. It opens: "In a large, randomized, postmarketing safety study in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) patients 50 years of age and older with at least one cardiovascular risk factor comparing another Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor to tumor necrosis factor (TNF) blockers, a higher rate of all-cause mortality, including sudden cardiovascular death, was observed with the JAK inhibitor".[^label] Another drug, another disease, an older population selected for cardiovascular risk. The major-adverse-cardiovascular-events heading rests on the same study and says so in the same words.

None of that makes the warning ignorable; the FDA applied it to the class deliberately. But a 25-year-old prescribed this for eczema and a 60-year-old smoker prescribed it for rheumatoid arthritis are reading one warning built from two different evidence bases, and only the label distinguishes them.

## What's In It

One active, upadacitinib, in an extended-release tablet; 15, 30 or 45 mg.[^ndc] There is no cosmetic component and nothing about this product is topical. The oral solution is 1 mg/mL in a 180 mL bottle supplied with an adapter and a reusable 10 mL syringe.[^carton]

## Common Marketing Claims

AbbVie's consumer page for the eczema indication leads with four claims, and the striking thing is how few numbers are in them.[^brand] "Itch relief 48 hours after the first dose and significantly less itch at 16 weeks." "Reduction in skin pain at 16 weeks." Neither carries a figure, a comparator or a proportion of patients.

The one number given is "75% skin clearance as early as 2 weeks and 16 weeks."[^brand] A reader has no way to tell from that page whether it means three-quarters of patients got clear skin or a 75% reduction in severity for some unstated share of them. Those are two claims of very different size. The page never resolves it: the word EASI, the scale on which a figure like that is conventionally reported, does not appear anywhere in it.[^brand]

The five-year claim is better handled, and the brand deserves the credit. Beneath "In another study of RINVOQ, patients had clearer skin and less itch at 5 years" sits AbbVie's own asterisk: "In a less rigorous study where patients chose to stay on therapy, everyone knew RINVOQ was used which may have increased results at 5 years."[^brand] That is an accurate description of an open-label extension's weaknesses, printed by the company making the claim.

The page's safety summary also states the boxed-warning point in plain English ("Increased risk of death in people age 50+ with at least 1 heart disease risk factor"), which is closer to the label's actual evidence than the warning's own all-caps heading is.[^brand]
