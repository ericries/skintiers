Multi-Peptide Advanced Serum is Naturium's peptide-led face serum: a water-based, fragrance-free formula built on a propanediol, squalane and shea butter base with a multi-peptide blend (encapsulated copper peptides, Argireline, and a further palmitoylated peptide), plus ferulic acid and hydrolyzed collagen, sold in a 1.0 fl oz / 30 ml size for $25.[^1]

> A peptide-and-antioxidant serum whose individual actives are each real ingredients with their own separately documented, but modest, evidence bases. Copper peptides grade weak: their cosmetic evidence, set out on [[copper-peptides]], leans on discoverer-authored reviews and an unresolved skin-permeation question, with no independent trial isolating a benefit. Argireline's wrinkle-reduction data comes mostly from the peptide's own developers, with one small independent trial. Ferulic acid appears without a declared concentration and without the vitamin C and vitamin E partners its best-documented antioxidant role depends on. No trial tests this specific formula. It is listed at $25 for 1.0 fl oz / 30 ml on Naturium's own site.[^1]

## Summary

Multi-Peptide Advanced Serum is the serum in Naturium's Multi-Peptide collection, marketed as an "advanced multi-peptide serum with encapsulated copper peptides and Argireline® Amplified to improve texture and reduce the look of fine lines, plus ferulic acid and collagen."[^1] The brand's fuller description adds that the serum is "formulated with an advanced multi-peptide blend, including encapsulated copper peptides and Argireline® Amplified peptide, to improve texture and reduce the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles" and that "encapsulated ferulic acid and collagen help firm skin's appearance to support a more youthful-looking complexion."[^1]

The ingredient list names three peptides, copper palmitoyl heptapeptide-14 (a copper peptide), acetyl hexapeptide-8 (the generic name for Argireline), and palmitoyl sh-hexapeptide-13 serine SP amide, alongside ferulic acid, arginine, and hydrolyzed collagen, all riding on a base led by water, propanediol, caprylic/capric triglyceride, and squalane; no concentration is declared for any active.[^1]

The general case for each named active is set out on its own ingredient page: topical peptides as a class on [[peptides]], the copper-binding tripeptide specifically on [[copper-peptides]], the "topical Botox" peptide on [[argireline-acetyl-hexapeptide-8]], and the antioxidant on [[ferulic-acid]] ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)). None of those pages, and no trial found here, tests this Naturium formula itself.

At $25 for 1.0 fl oz / 30 ml, it is comparable in price to [[cosrx-6-peptide-skin-booster-serum]] at $25 for 150 ml, though the COSRX product is a far larger volume; [[the-ordinary-multi-peptide-copper-peptides-serum]] is a lower-cost comparator in the same copper-peptide-plus-multi-peptide category.

## The Evidence

Topical peptides are well-tolerated ingredients with a modest, mostly preliminary measured effect on the appearance of aging; that class-level case is set out in full on [[peptides]]. This serum's peptide blend combines three chemically distinct peptides, and each has its own, separately weaker, evidence base rather than a pooled or product-specific trial.

Copper palmitoyl heptapeptide-14 is a copper-binding peptide of the same family covered on [[copper-peptides]], where its cosmetic evidence and its limits (discoverer-authored reviews plus a skin-permeation question) are set out; no independent trial isolates this ingredient's benefit here.

Acetyl hexapeptide-8 is Argireline, covered in full on [[argireline-acetyl-hexapeptide-8]]. Its foundational wrinkle-depth reduction figure comes from the peptide's own developers' study; an independent, smaller randomized trial found a measurable but more modest effect on peri-orbital wrinkles.[^1]

Palmitoyl sh-hexapeptide-13 serine SP amide is a signal-type peptide of the kind covered generally on [[peptides]], and no independent trial of this specific peptide was found.

Ferulic acid's best-documented topical role, set out on [[ferulic-acid]], is as the third antioxidant in a fixed combination with 15% L-ascorbic acid and 1% alpha-tocopherol, where it both stabilizes and roughly doubles the pair's measured photoprotection. This serum contains none of that vitamin C or vitamin E pairing, and ferulic acid appears here without a declared concentration, so the ingredient's strongest evidence base does not transfer to this formula.[^1]

No published trial tests Naturium's Multi-Peptide Advanced Serum as a finished product. What the formula reliably delivers is closer to its base: squalane, shea butter, and phosphatidylcholine as emollients and glycerin as a humectant are established moisturizing ingredients, a hydration benefit independent of the peptide and antioxidant claims.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its texture and fine-lines grade rests on separately weak or preliminary class evidence for three different peptides; its antioxidant grade is undercut by ferulic acid's undosed, unpaired appearance here; its hydration grade rests on ordinary but reliable emollients and humectants. All of its claimed benefits are cosmetic; none is a health claim.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and page claims not established by product-specific evidence:

- **"Improve texture and reduce the look of fine lines."** Rests on the general, separately weak-to-preliminary class evidence for the formula's three peptides ([[peptides]], [[copper-peptides]], [[argireline-acetyl-hexapeptide-8]]); no trial of this product was found.[^1]
- **"Encapsulated ferulic acid and collagen help firm skin's appearance."** Ferulic acid's documented role is as a co-antioxidant alongside vitamin C and vitamin E ([[ferulic-acid]]), a pairing absent from this formula; hydrolyzed collagen appears without a declared concentration or a cited firming trial.[^1]
- **"Dermatologist Tested."** A manufacturer testing claim with no methodology, sample, or result published.[^1]
