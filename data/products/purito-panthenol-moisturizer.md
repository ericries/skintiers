Purito Mighty Bamboo Panthenol Cream is a South Korean facial moisturizer from [[purito]] built around 10% panthenol as its headline ingredient, sold in a 100 ml size for $23.00 on the brand's own site.[^purito]

> Panthenol (provitamin B5) is a well-studied soothing and barrier-support ingredient rather than a treatment active in its own right: controlled trials show topical dexpanthenol speeds barrier repair and eases irritation-induced redness compared with a plain vehicle, and a separate dose-ranging trial found panthenol at 1% and 5% cut water loss, covered in full on [[panthenol]]. This cream's 10% panthenol concentration sits above every level directly tested in that evidence.

## Summary

Purito Mighty Bamboo Panthenol Cream is marketed with the tagline "10% Panthenol – Blemishes out. Glow on!" and described by the brand as a formula that helps strengthen the skin barrier, alongside "Blemish recovery" and "Glow boost" framing and a "Bamboo Extract resembling body moisture."[^purito] It comes in a 100 ml / 3.38 fl. oz. bottle priced at $23.00.[^purito]

Panthenol at 10% is the product's single named, quantified active. The category claim, that topical panthenol supports barrier repair and eases irritation, has controlled-trial support and is covered in full on [[panthenol]]; that page also covers a separate, smaller body of evidence for panthenol as a plain [[moisturizing]] humectant, tested at 1% and 5%. This product runs at twice the highest concentration in that moisturizing trial, so its hydrating effect at 10% is not itself established by that data, only inferred from a lower-dose trend.

Beyond panthenol, the declared ingredient list includes squalane and butylene glycol in the emollient/humectant base, niacinamide, a bamboo extract (Bambusa Vulgaris Extract) at about 3,497 ppm, sodium hyaluronate, four named centella asiatica triterpenes (madecassoside, asiaticoside, asiatic acid, madecassic acid), and copper tripeptide-1, none of which the brand discloses a concentration for.[^purito]

## The Evidence

Whether panthenol supports the skin barrier and eases irritation is a well-evidenced, separate question from this specific product's formulation; see [[panthenol]] for the controlled-trial evidence, including a sodium-lauryl-sulphate irritation model in which a dexpanthenol cream produced significantly less redness than a vehicle, and a dose-ranging trial in which two lower cosmetic concentrations of panthenol significantly reduced transepidermal water loss.

No published trial of this specific product was found. What the brand discloses is the formula itself: Panthenol is listed second in the ingredient list, immediately after water, at a declared 10%, the largest named functional ingredient in the formula.[^purito] That concentration is double the highest level (5%) tested in the moisturizing trial cited on [[panthenol]], and the ingredient here is declared simply as "Panthenol," not specifically as the dexpanthenol form used in the barrier-repair and wound-healing trials on that page. Niacinamide, sodium hyaluronate, the four centella asiatica triterpenes, and copper tripeptide-1 are all present in the declared list, but the brand does not disclose their concentrations, so their individual contribution cannot be assessed from this listing.[^purito]

> **Bottom line.** Topical panthenol has controlled-trial support for barrier repair, irritation recovery, and hydration at concentrations up to 5%; this cream declares 10% panthenol as its headline active, a higher dose than that evidence directly covers, alongside several other named but undisclosed-concentration actives.[^purito]
