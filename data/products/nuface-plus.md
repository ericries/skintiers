## Summary

The NuFACE Plus is a discontinued 2011 facial toning device, and it matters only because of what points at it. **Bottom line:** it is the predicate the [[nubody-skin-toning-device]] was measured against, and its own clearance rests on an electrical safety test, an electromagnetic emissions test and a risk analysis. No clinical study appears anywhere in the file.

> **Read this before reading "FDA cleared" on any microcurrent device.** The NuFACE Plus 510(k) summary contains a sentence saying microcurrent "has the ability to increase facial contour and firm the skin and muscles".[^k103472] That sentence is the company's own, in the section describing the technology. FDA's decision was that the device is substantially equivalent to the company's earlier device, not that the sentence is true. The document's own testing section lists compliance with electrical safety and emissions standards and nothing else.[^k103472]

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: unknown, because the cleared indication contains no effect.** The indication reads in full: "The NuFACE® Plus Facial Toning Device is intended for facial stimulation and is indicated for over-the-counter cosmetic use", followed by "The anatomical site for application of the NuFACE® Plus is the face."[^k103472] Facial stimulation is a description of what the current does, not a result it produces. This is the same shape as the indication on [[nuface-fix-microwand]], which is also stimulation plus over-the-counter cosmetic use.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal, and the filing says so by omission.** Under COMPLIANCE DATA the summary reports testing against FDA's performance standards in 21 CFR 898, against IEC 60601-1-2 for radiated and power line conducted emissions, and against IEC 60601-1 for electrical safety.[^k103472] Under SUBSTANTIAL EQUIVALENCE it reports a risk analysis used to identify hazards and failure modes.[^k103472] There is no clinical section, no subject count and no outcome measure in the document.
- **Record finding: it was an Abbreviated 510(k), and its predicate is older than the queue entry that sent me here assumed.** openFDA records K103472 as clearance type Abbreviated, decision Substantially Equivalent, decided 7 November 2011 on a submission received 24 November 2010.[^openfda] The summary names the predicate as the company's own earlier NuFACE device, cleared under **K072260** in 2007.[^k103472] So the NuFACE Plus is a link in the chain rather than its root.
- **Record finding: the retail name and the cleared name are different, and the filing that says so is a later one.** The K103472 summary never uses the word Trinity; it calls the device the NuFACE® Plus throughout.[^k103472] But the company's 2014 Mini filing, looking back at this clearance, describes its predicate as "the NuFACE® Trinity device, cleared as the NuFACE®D Plus under 510(k) K103472".[^k2014] So the hardware sold at retail as the Trinity is the hardware cleared as the NuFACE Plus. That is worth knowing before matching a box to a clearance number: the later [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]] carries its own separate clearance, K181008, from 2018.
- **Record finding: this is a neurology-panel device.** Product code NFO is "Stimulator, Transcutaneous Electrical, Aesthetic Purposes", class II, regulation 21 CFR **882.5890**, and 882 is the neurological devices part; the summary gives the classification name as "Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulator (21 CFR 882.5890)".[^nfo][^k103472] A facial toning gadget is regulated as a nerve stimulator with an aesthetic product code, which is why these clearances say stimulation and not firming.
- **What it was, physically.** Two chrome-plated spherical probes, user-adjustable from 0 to 400 microamps, powered by four rechargeable AA nickel-metal hydride batteries, and requiring a conductive gel to work.[^k103472] That last requirement is the reason [[nuface-gel-primer]] exists as a separately cleared product.

## The Evidence

None specific to this device. The 510(k) summary is the only document consulted, and it contains no study.

What can be said precisely is how it performs relative to its descendant, because a later filing printed both columns. The NuBODY comparison table lists the NuFACE Plus predicate at a maximum output current of "400 µA @ 500Ω", net charge of "24 µC" per pulse, maximum average power density of 1.12 mW/cm², maximum output current density of "0.419 mA/cm2" and maximum phase charge of "0.48 mC/Burst", against roughly double on each for the body device, with maximum output voltage "28 VDC" and frequency "Approximately 8.3 Hz" identical between them.[^k171588] Those figures are set out in full, with the column order confirmed, on [[nubody-skin-toning-device]].

The general question of whether home microcurrent does anything measurable is not this page's to answer and is not settled by this file. [[home-use-skincare-devices-by-evidence]] ranks the device categories.

> **Bottom line.** A device you cannot buy, whose main present-day function is to be the thing other NuFACE clearances were compared with. Its file is worth knowing about because it shows exactly how little a microcurrent 510(k) establishes: safety and emissions testing, a risk analysis, and a firming sentence that the sponsor wrote and FDA did not assess.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the company's own 510(k) summary and **not** endorsed here. These are unusual in that they sit inside a regulatory document, which is exactly why they are easy to mistake for findings.

- Microcurrent "is an aesthetic modality providing electric current in millionths of an ampere and has the ability to increase facial contour and firm the skin and muscles by supplying 80 - 400" microamps.[^k103472] An efficacy claim with a dose range attached, in a filing whose testing section measured only electrical safety and emissions. Nothing in the document supports the contour or firming half of the sentence.
- "Facial Toning Device" as the common name.[^k103472] Toning is the marketing word; the cleared indication is stimulation.

## Where to Buy

Nowhere. The brand's own storefront feed, read on 2026-11-03, returns 34 products, and the NuFACE Plus is not among them; the current facial line is the TRINITY+ and MINI+ families, with the FIX MicroWand alongside.[^feed]
