Danessa Myricks Beauty's Yummy Skin 10/10 Barrier Boost Hyaluronic Acid + Ectoin Plumping Serum is a milky leave-on face serum built around six declared forms of hyaluronic acid plus ectoin, listed at $42 with no size stated on the brand's own site, and marketed for barrier support and "multi-depth plumping hydration."[^1]

> A multi-form hyaluronic-acid serum whose hydrating case rests on the general, solid evidence that topical hyaluronic acid across molecular weights raises measured skin hydration, and the thinner, molecular-weight-dependent evidence for wrinkle appearance, both set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. The formula's own headline figures, a brand-run 34-person, 2-week study reporting "100%" results on fine lines, pores, and a "55.6%" hydration increase, are unpublished and not independently corroborated.[^1] No size (mL or oz) is stated on the brand's product page, and no independent retailer listing could be reached to cross-check the ingredient list, so this profile rests on a single manufacturer source.[^1]

## Summary

Yummy Skin 10/10 Barrier Boost Hyaluronic Acid + Ectoin Plumping Serum is Danessa Myricks Beauty's hyaluronic-acid serum, a "weightless milky serum" the brand markets under its "10/10" name for "10 molecular weights of hyaluronic acid."[^1] The declared ingredient list names six distinct hyaluronate forms, not ten: sodium hyaluronate, sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, hyaluronic acid, sodium acetylated hyaluronate, and hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate, listed eleventh through sixteenth of thirty-eight total ingredients. Ectoin, the other named active, sits seventeenth, just after the hyaluronate block.[^1]

The general case for topical hyaluronic acid, solid evidence for raising measured hydration across molecular weights, and thinner, molecular-weight-dependent evidence for wrinkle appearance, is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. This formula's six declared hyaluronate forms split evenly by INCI name: three unmodified (sodium hyaluronate, sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer, hyaluronic acid) and three hydrolyzed or chemically modified (hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, sodium acetylated hyaluronate, hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate); no independent source classifies each by actual molecular weight ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)).

Ectoin is marketed to "protect, strengthen + nourish the skin's moisture barrier," but this profile does not carry an ingredient page for ectoin and does not independently grade that claim here; it is treated as a marketing claim below rather than graded evidence ([see Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)). The brand's own "100%" fine-line, pore, and hydration figures come from a single unpublished, brand-run 34-person, 2-week study with no published methodology ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1]

It is listed at $42 on Danessa Myricks Beauty's own site, with no size in mL or fl oz stated on the product page (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-15).[^1]

## The Evidence

Topical hyaluronic acid reliably raises a measured skin-hydration reading across molecular weights in placebo-controlled testing, with a thinner, molecular-weight-dependent case for reducing the appearance of fine lines; the full case, its trials, and its limits are set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. Nothing specific to this product was found: no published trial tests this exact six-form hyaluronate plus ectoin combination, so its grades above rest on the general category evidence for hyaluronic acid.[^1]

This formula's six declared forms split evenly by INCI naming convention: sodium hyaluronate, sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer, and plain hyaluronic acid appear unmodified; hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, sodium acetylated hyaluronate, and hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate carry hydrolysis or other chemical modification in their INCI names.[^1] The product page does not state molecular weight for any of these forms, and no independent source was found that classifies each by actual molecular weight, so this profile does not assign specific named ingredients to the higher- or lower-molecular-weight tiers discussed in the general evidence on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. The brand's "10 molecular weights" marketing language names only six distinct INCI ingredients.[^1]

The brand's product page separately reports a brand-run clinical claim: a 2-week study in 34 participants in which "100% saw a reduction in fine lines" at every timepoint, "100% showed visible pore reduction," and "100% showed immediate increase in hydration by 55.6%."[^1] No methodology, control arm, statistical detail, or peer review is published alongside these figures, and no independent source corroborates them.

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its hydration grade rests on hyaluronic acid's general, solid category evidence; its wrinkle-appearance grade rests on the thinner, molecular-weight-dependent case that applies generally to hydrolyzed and chemically modified hyaluronate forms, though no independent source classifies this product's specific named ingredients by molecular weight. The brand's own "100%" brand-run figures are unpublished and uncorroborated.
