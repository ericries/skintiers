Lotrimin AF Antifungal is clotrimazole 1% cream from Bayer. **Bottom line:** it shares a brand with an FDA-approved product and is not one itself, and the AF on its carton is attached to a different active elsewhere in the same range. Active-ingredient evidence is on [[clotrimazole]].

> **Same brand, same shelf, two different regulatory footings.** [[lotrimin-ultra-athletes-foot-cream]] is butenafine and is **FDA-approved** under NDA021307. This one reaches the market as an **OTC Monograph Drug**, recorded in its label under application M005.[^label][^ultra] A monograph product has not had a product-specific FDA efficacy review; it relies on the agency having already recognised the active for that use. Neither is wrong, but only one of the two can accurately be called FDA-approved, and the names give no hint which.

## Summary

Clotrimazole 1%, distributed by Bayer HealthCare LLC, marketing category "OTC Monograph Drug" (code C200263) under M005, NDC 11523-6549.[^label] The Uses section reads: "cures most ringworm relieves itching, redness, irritation and discomfort which accompany this condition".[^label] Directions are to "apply a thin layer over affected area twice daily (morning and night)" and to "use daily for 4 weeks".[^label] The printed display panel shows a 12 g tube.[^label] No price is quoted here; no retailer was fetched and no metered lookup was spent.

**"Lotrimin AF" does not tell you which drug is in the tube.** This carton reads "LOTRIMIN ® AF ANTIFUNGAL clotrimazole cream".[^label] Yet in FDA's product directory, every record whose brand name is Lotrimin AF lists miconazole nitrate 20 mg/g instead, and a combined query for clotrimazole under the brand name Lotrimin AF returns nothing at all; this NDC is filed under the plain brand name Lotrimin.[^brands] Both things are true at once: the AF suffix is printed on a clotrimazole carton and is registered against miconazole products. Across the range the Lotrimin name covers at least four actives, clotrimazole and miconazole here, butenafine under Ultra and tolnaftate under Daily Prevention, and "Lotrimin Antifungal" is itself registered against both clotrimazole and miconazole.[^brands] Read the active-ingredient line, because neither the brand nor the AF tells you what you are buying.

## The Rubric

**Ringworm**
- **Effect size: notable.** It is a cure claim, "cures most ringworm", not a symptom-relief claim.[^label] Most, not all. Condition context is on [[tinea-corporis-ringworm]].
- **Evidence quality: mixed.** Two things pull it below the NDA-backed sibling. The monograph route means the agency recognised the active for this use rather than reviewing this product's own data, which [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] explains. And clotrimazole is an azole; the head-to-head evidence favouring allylamines over azoles is graded on [[clotrimazole]] and is not repeated here.

**Athlete's foot and jock itch**
- **Effect size: none that this label supports.** This is the finding worth carrying away. The product is branded "AF", yet the Uses section of this SPL claims ringworm and nothing else, and the display panel is coded "Lotrimin RW 6549".[^label] Compare the butenafine sibling, whose label explicitly claims athlete's foot between the toes and jock itch.[^ultra]
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** Scoped strictly: other Lotrimin products, including the miconazole AF range, carry different claims. What is verified is that the SKU at NDC 11523-6549 does not, and [[athletes-foot]] covers what that condition needs.

**Scalp or nails**
- **Effect size: none, by the label's own statement.** "this product is not effective on the scalp or nails".[^label]
- **Evidence quality: solid for the exclusion.** It is the manufacturer saying so in the Directions.

## The Evidence

**Four weeks, twice daily, with a built-in stop rule.** The course here is "use daily for 4 weeks", against the butenafine sibling's option of twice daily for one week.[^label][^ultra] The label also tells you when to give up: stop use and ask a doctor if "there is no improvement within 4 weeks".[^label] That is a useful instruction to have in writing, because the common failure mode with topical antifungals is indefinite reapplication without reassessment.

**Age limit.** "Do not use on children under 2 years of age unless directed by a doctor", and the Directions add "supervise children in the use of this product".[^label] The butenafine sibling's floor is higher, at 12 years.[^ultra]

**Composition.** Inactive ingredients are "benzyl alcohol, cetyl alcohol, cetyl esters wax, octyldodecanol, polysorbate 60, sorbitan monostearate, stearyl alcohol, water".[^label] Benzyl alcohol and the fatty alcohols are the entries most relevant to anyone with a known contact sensitivity.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The label's own escalation rule applies: if there is no improvement in four weeks, that is the point to see a doctor rather than keep applying.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"cures most ringworm"**. Supported as a monograph claim, and narrower than the brand name implies.[^label]
- **"relieves itching, redness, irritation and discomfort which accompany this condition"**. A symptom claim attached to ringworm specifically, not a standalone efficacy claim.[^label]
- **The "AF" in the name.** It reads as athlete's foot, but this label claims only ringworm.[^label] That is the single most useful thing a shopper could know about this tube.
- **"this product is not effective on the scalp or nails"**. The opposite of a marketing claim, and worth reading before buying for a nail or scalp problem.[^label]

## Where to Buy

Not priced here. It is a widely stocked drugstore product; this label records NDC 11523-6549 with one package code and a 12 g tube on the printed panel.[^label]
