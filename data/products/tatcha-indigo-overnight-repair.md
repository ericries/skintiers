Tatcha Indigo Overnight Repair is a silicone- and ceramide-containing night cream sold in a 50 mL / 1.7 fl. oz. full size (with a mini also offered), positioned by the brand as a "Redness Reducing Barrier Cream."[^1] Despite "serum" appearing in the product's web address, Tatcha's own product page names and markets it as a cream, not a serum; this page uses the brand's current name and category.[^1]

> A premium barrier-repair night cream built on dimethicone, a small amount of ceramide and hyaluronate, and a proprietary blend of botanical extracts including the indigo plant (Polygonum tinctorium) the product is named for. The barrier and hydration ingredients have modest, general category evidence set out on [[ceramides]], [[hyaluronic-acid]], and [[dimethicone]]; none of it is specific to this formula, and the concentrations of the ceramide and hyaluronate are undisclosed and listed well down the INCI. The headline "calms visible redness in 1 night" claim rests on the brand's own unpublished testing, not an independent trial.[^1] It is listed on Tatcha's own site as a "Sale price $94" for the 50 mL full size.[^1]

## Summary

Indigo Overnight Repair is Tatcha's ceramide-and-silicone barrier cream, formulated around [[dimethicone]] (the second-listed ingredient), a proprietary blend of botanical extracts led by Polygonum tinctorium (the indigo plant the line is named for), and smaller amounts of [[ceramides|Ceramide Ng]] and [[hyaluronic-acid|Sodium Hyaluronate]] further down the ingredient list.[^1] The general, modest evidence for ceramide- and hyaluronic-acid-containing moisturizers improving measured barrier function and surface hydration is set out on [[ceramides]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]]; none of it is specific to this product, and neither active's exact concentration is disclosed.

The brand's central claim, that the cream "calms visible irritation & redness in 1 night" and that "100% showed an improvement in skin radiance, softness, smoothness & texture," is the brand's own testing, disclosed as n=40 panelists (bio-instrumentation for the separate skin-barrier stat, an undescribed "clinical study" for the redness/radiance stats), but with no control arm, comparator, or peer review.[^1] No independent trial of this formula, or of its named botanical actives (indigo leaf, Salvia miltiorrhiza root, Camellia sinensis leaf), was found. Those claims and the rest of the label's language are weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims). It carries a "Sale price $94" for the 50 mL full size on Tatcha's own site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^1]

## The Evidence

No published, independent trial of Indigo Overnight Repair as a finished product was found; nothing here is specific to this formula, and the grades above rest on general category evidence for its individually evidenced actives.[^1]

[[Dimethicone]] is the second-listed INCI ingredient, ahead of every named botanical or humectant, which suggests it is present at a functionally meaningful concentration; it is a well-characterized silicone occlusive and emollient, with a moisturizing and softening effect described in dermatology reviews. [[Ceramides|Ceramide Ng]] and [[hyaluronic-acid|Sodium Hyaluronate]] appear much further down the ingredient list, after several botanical extracts and a clay-based thickener, consistent with smaller, supporting-level amounts; category evidence for both is modest, drawn mostly from placebo-controlled trials on barrier and hydration measurements rather than clinical outcomes. [[Tocopherol]] is listed third from the end, ahead of the two-ingredient preservative system (ethylhexylglycerin, phenoxyethanol), consistent with a trace antioxidant-preservation amount; its own evidence page finds the antioxidant mechanism well described but controlled clinical benefit sparse.

The product's namesake ingredient, Polygonum tinctorium (indigo) leaf/stem extract, along with Salvia miltiorrhiza (a plant used in traditional Chinese medicine, sometimes called danshen) root extract and Camellia sinensis (green tea) leaf extract, are declared on the INCI but have no dedicated evidence page here and no independent published trial was found testing them in this formula or at this concentration. The brand's redness- and radiance-related claims for the finished product rest on its own unpublished testing.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its evidence rests on general, modest, category-level findings for [[dimethicone]], [[ceramides]], and [[hyaluronic-acid]], none of them at a disclosed concentration; the brand's own "1 night" redness and radiance claims are unpublished company testing, not independent evidence.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer, in the order given.[^1] Actives with a functional role and an evidence page are linked; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

Actives and functional ingredients:

- [[dimethicone]], a silicone occlusive and emollient, second on the list
- Saccharomyces/rice ferment filtrate, a fermented-yeast extract, fourth on the list
- Ophiopogon japonicus root extract, Polygonum tinctorium (indigo) leaf/stem extract, Sargassum vulgare extract, a second saccharomyces ferment filtrate, Salvia miltiorrhiza root extract, Camellia sinensis (green tea) leaf extract, and Cladosiphon okamuranus (a brown seaweed) extract, a proprietary botanical blend with no independent trial found for this formula or these ingredients individually on this profile
- Sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]), phytosterols, [[ceramides|ceramide NG]], and hydrogenated lecithin, a barrier-lipid group listed after the botanicals
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant, listed ahead of the two-ingredient preservative system (ethylhexylglycerin, phenoxyethanol)

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, diglycerin and glycerin (humectants), diphenylsiloxy phenyl trimethicone and PEG-10 dimethicone (silicone emulsifiers/texturizers), butylene glycol and propanediol (solvents), neopentyl glycol diethylhexanoate and diisostearyl malate (emollient esters), disteardimonium hectorite (a clay thickener), palmitic acid and linoleic acid (fatty acids), lauryl PEG-9 polydimethylsiloxyethyl dimethicone and cetyl alcohol (emulsifiers), maltodextrin (a texture agent), magnesium sulfate (a salt, often used to help thicken silicone bases), and ethylhexylglycerin and phenoxyethanol (the preservative system).[^1]

The declared list runs to 32 ingredients; no fragrance is separately declared.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Calms visible irritation & redness in 1 night."** The brand's own testing found "95% showed an improvement in visible redness" after one night, disclosed as n=40 panelists and labeled only "a clinical study" (no control arm, comparator, or peer review).[^1] No independent trial of this formula's botanical blend for redness was found.
- **"100% showed an improvement in skin radiance, softness, smoothness & texture" after one night.** The brand's own consumer-perception result, disclosed as n=40 panelists ("a clinical study"), reported without a control arm, comparator, or peer review.[^1]
- **"Empowers skin's natural immune defenses."** A mechanism-style claim not tied to any cited trial on this product page.[^1]
- **"Redness Reducing Barrier Cream" as a category name.** The barrier-support half has general, modest category evidence via [[dimethicone]] and [[ceramides]]; the redness-reducing half rests on the brand's own testing above, not an independent trial of redness or rosacea outcomes.
