Eucerin Original Healing Cream is a fragrance-free, dye-free body cream built on water, petrolatum, and mineral oil, sold by [[eucerin|Eucerin]] as a 16 oz jar.[^pdp][^target]

> A plain occlusive body cream led by petrolatum and mineral oil, both confirmed on Target's declared ingredient list for this SKU and named among Eucerin's own "key ingredients" for the product.[^pdp][^target] Neither source shows a Drug Facts Active Ingredient panel, so the formula is built as a cosmetic rather than a regulated OTC skin protectant, unlike petrolatum-led ointments sold with a Drug Facts label such as [[cerave-healing-ointment]].[^pdp][^target] Petrolatum's occlusive evidence, independent of this specific cream, is set out on [[petrolatum]].

## Summary

Eucerin's own product page, which titles the item "Original Healing Rich Cream" but describes it in body copy as "Eucerin Original Healing Cream," calls it "a time-tested formula that helps heal and protect extremely dry, sensitive and compromised skin."[^pdp] Target's listing for the same 16 oz jar carries the retail name "Eucerin Original Healing Cream" and describes it as a "Body Moisturizer for Extremely Dry, Compromised Skin" with "a thick, rich formula that helps prevent moisture loss."[^target] Both sources agree the cream is fragrance-free, dye-free, and non-comedogenic.[^pdp][^target]

Target's declared ingredient list for the jar, in order, is water, petrolatum, mineral oil, ceresin, lanolin alcohol, phenoxyethanol, piroctone olamine.[^target] Eucerin's own page declares the same full ingredient list and additionally highlights water, petrolatum, and mineral oil as its "key ingredients."[^pdp] Neither source discloses a Drug Facts Active Ingredient panel or a stated percentage for petrolatum, so the cream is formulated and marketed as a cosmetic moisturizer rather than a regulated OTC skin protectant.[^pdp][^target] Petrolatum's own well-established occlusive evidence, independent of this specific cream, is set out on [[petrolatum]]; the general case for moisturizing dry and barrier-impaired skin is set out on [[moisturizing]].

Directions on both sources are the same: "smooth this Eucerin dry skin cream onto your skin daily" (Eucerin) / "to use, smooth this Eucerin body moisturizer for dry skin onto your skin daily" (Target).[^pdp][^target] Neither source's cached page text confirms a specific price for this jar (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)).

## The Evidence

No independently published trial of this exact cream was located. Eucerin's own product page claims it "provides 24-hour moisture" and "relieves dry skin associated with eczema, psoriasis, or drying medications," but discloses no study design, sample size, or methodology behind either claim.[^pdp] The product-specific fact available is its declared composition, led by water, petrolatum, and mineral oil, confirmed on Target's listing and matching the "key ingredients" named on Eucerin's own page.[^pdp][^target]

Petrolatum's own occlusive mechanism and its effect on transepidermal water loss are well established and set out on [[petrolatum]], including a mechanistic human study showing it accelerates barrier recovery rather than impeding it. The category-level evidence that moisturizers generally reduce eczema flares and spare topical steroid use is a Cochrane-review-anchored finding also set out on [[petrolatum]] and [[moisturizing]]; that evidence covers moisturizers as a class, not this cream's specific combination of petrolatum, mineral oil, ceresin, and lanolin alcohol tested head to head. [[cerave-healing-ointment]], by contrast, discloses petrolatum at a stated percentage under an FDA Drug Facts panel, a formulation difference from this cosmetic-labeled cream.

> **Bottom line.** No independently reported study isolates this exact cream's own effect. Its case rests on petrolatum and mineral oil, both confirmed but undisclosed at a stated percentage[^pdp][^target], which fits the well-evidenced general pattern for occlusive moisturizing covered on [[petrolatum]] and [[moisturizing]]; Eucerin's own eczema- and psoriasis-related claims carry no disclosed data specific to this cream.

## What's In It

The full declared ingredient list (INCI), as shown on Target's listing for the 16 oz jar; Eucerin's own product page names the same first three ingredients as its "key ingredients" without listing the full formula.[^target][^pdp]

Notable/functional ingredients:

- [[petrolatum|Petrolatum]], the second-listed ingredient after water, named among Eucerin's own "key ingredients"; not disclosed at a stated percentage and not presented in a Drug Facts panel on either source
- Mineral oil, the third-listed ingredient, also named among Eucerin's own "key ingredients"
- Ceresin, a mineral wax, and lanolin alcohol, an emollient, both in the declared ingredient list
- Piroctone olamine, in the declared ingredient list, typically used as a preservative/antimicrobial in cosmetic formulas
- Phenoxyethanol, the preservative

Claims from Eucerin's own product page, not accompanied by a published study of this cream:

- **"Relieves dry skin associated with eczema, psoriasis, or drying medications."**[^pdp] No cited data specific to this cream; the general moisturizer-eczema evidence, which is category-level rather than product-specific, is covered on [[moisturizing]].
