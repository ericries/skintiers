Paula's Choice Clear Back and Body Acne Spray is a leave-on, spray-format acne treatment sold in a 118 ml (4 fl oz) bottle, with an FDA-regulated Drug Facts panel listing salicylic acid 2.0% as its sole active ingredient.[^dailymed] The label lists its purpose plainly as "Acne Treatment" and its stated use is treatment of acne, with no claim restricted to the face; the spray format itself is what targets the hard-to-reach back and chest, where [[body-acne|body acne]] most often appears.[^dailymed]

> An FDA-monograph OTC acne treatment at 2% salicylic acid, the top of the concentration range the monograph allows, delivered as a spray so it can reach the back without hands. Salicylic acid has real but second-tier evidence for acne generally, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence); nothing about the spray delivery format itself has been tested in a published trial, and the label itself warns against use "on large areas of the body."[^dailymed]

## Summary

The product's FDA-filed Drug Facts label states the active ingredient as "Salicylic Acid 2.0%" with the purpose "Acne Treatment."[^dailymed] Its Uses section reads "For the treatment of of Acne" (a typo present on the filed label itself) and "Penetrates pores to eliminate most acne blemishes, blackheads, and whiteheads."[^dailymed] [[salicylic-acid|Salicylic acid]] is an oil-soluble beta-hydroxy acid recognized in the US OTC acne drug monograph at concentrations from 0.5% to 2%; at 2.0%, this spray sits at the top of that range, [set out in full on [[salicylic-acid]]](#the-evidence).

The spray format is the product's distinguishing feature relative to typical leave-on salicylic acid treatments, which are usually gels or lotions applied by hand to the face. The label's own Directions instruct users to "Hold container 4 to 6 inches from the skin to apply" and specifically warn "Do not spray directly into face. Spray on hands, then apply to face," making clear the spray is designed primarily for the body, where hand application is awkward across the back.[^dailymed] The Warnings section also states "Do not use... on large areas of the body," a caution against blanket-spraying the whole trunk rather than the affected patches.[^dailymed]

The health case rests on salicylic acid itself, a guideline-recognized but second-tier OTC acne active, [[body-acne|not a novel body-specific mechanism]]; the cosmetic case is the delivery convenience of a spray for a surface area that is hard to treat by hand.

## The Evidence

Whether topical salicylic acid clears acne, and how it compares with benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids, is covered on [[salicylic-acid]] and not repeated here; in short, it is a genuine monograph acne active with weaker trial support than those first-line options. [[body-acne|Body acne]]'s underlying disease process is identical to facial acne, so that ingredient-level evidence applies to the trunk as well as the face.

No published clinical trial isolates this exact spray, its 2% salicylic acid dose in this specific inactive-ingredient base, or its spray delivery on the back and chest.[^dailymed]

> **Bottom line.** The active ingredient case is real and monograph-established at this concentration; the spray delivery format for the trunk has not itself been trial-tested.

## What's In It

The active and full inactive ingredient lists, quoted verbatim from the FDA-filed Drug Facts label on DailyMed.[^dailymed]

**Active ingredient:** Salicylic Acid 2.0%, purpose "Acne Treatment."[^dailymed]

**Inactive ingredients:** Water (Aqua), Dipropylene Glycol, Butylene Glycol, Polysorbate 20, Pentylene Glycol, Panthenol, Sodium Hyaluronate, Dipotassium Glycyrrhizate, Glycyrrhiza Glabra (Licorice) Root Extract, Berberis Vulgaris (Barberry) Root Extract, Lauric Acid, Allantoin, Glycerin, PEG/PPG-17/6 Copolymer, Methyl Gluceth-20, Glycereth-26, Sodium Hydroxide, Sodium Metabisulfite, Disodium EDTA, Potassium Sorbate.[^dailymed] None of these carries an independent acne claim on the filed label; [[panthenol]], [[allantoin]], and sodium hyaluronate are included in the base for their general skin-conditioning roles rather than as acne actives.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing language not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Penetrates pores to eliminate most acne blemishes, blackheads, and whiteheads."**[^dailymed] This is the FDA-filed label's own Uses language for the monograph acne indication; the underlying salicylic acid evidence is real at the category level (on [[salicylic-acid]]), but no trial of this specific spray or its penetration claim on the back and chest was found.
- **Spray delivery as a body-acne-specific advantage.** The format is a genuine practical convenience for covering the back, a surface most people cannot reach well by hand, but this is a delivery-mechanism argument, not a claim that the spray clears acne any better than an equivalent-strength gel or lotion applied by hand.
