SK-II Facial Treatment Essence (FTE) is a watery leave-on essence built around Galactomyces ferment filtrate (branded Pitera), sold by Procter & Gamble's SK-II line in four sizes as the brand's original, flagship essence.[^1]

> A long-running, high-priced essence whose entire case rests on one fermentation byproduct, Pitera, examined in full on [[galactomyces-ferment-filtrate]]. Nearly every study of that ingredient was run or funded by P&G, and no independent placebo-controlled trial isolates it. One P&G-run study did use this exact product, a facial treatment essence, but only as one of three SK-II products applied together for a year with no untreated comparison group, so it cannot show what this bottle alone does. At $99 for 2.5 oz, it is an expensive way to get a modest, preliminary, mostly self-comparison case.[^1]

## Summary

Facial Treatment Essence is SK-II's original product and best-known seller: a fragrance-light, water-thin liquid the brand says contains "more than 90% PITERA™," its name for [[galactomyces-ferment-filtrate|Galactomyces ferment filtrate]] (GFF), a byproduct of yeast fermentation.[^1] The general evidence for GFF, mostly preliminary and mostly generated or funded by P&G, is set out in full on [[galactomyces-ferment-filtrate]].

This product is unusual among the essence's category peers in that one published study did test it directly, up to a point: an 86-woman, 12-month open-label study used this essence alongside SK-II Cellumination Essence and SK-II Skin Signature Cream, all applied together, comparing wrinkles, spots, and roughness to baseline and to the same 86 women's own measurements taken 11 years earlier, in 1999, with no untreated control group at either time point.[^gff] Because three products were used at once with no control arm, the result cannot be credited to this essence specifically, and the study's authors were P&G Innovation GK employees or a paid P&G consultant.[^gff] The rest of the evidence behind Pitera, cell-culture gene expression work and open-label pore/redness imaging studies, is general to the ingredient and not specific to this formula ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)).

The brand's own marketing claims, "clinically proven" redness reduction, dark-spot reduction, and cell-turnover renewal, are weighed against the cited evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims). It is listed at $99.00 for the smallest 2.5 oz size on SK-II's own site, with three larger sizes offered at unlisted prices (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^1]

## The Evidence

The full case for Pitera/GFF, its cell-culture gene-expression work, open-label pore and redness imaging studies, and one independent combination-serum trial for post-acne marks, is set out with sourcing on [[galactomyces-ferment-filtrate]]. Nothing here repeats that; this section covers what is specific to this product.

One study used this exact product formula, but confounded (mixed together with two other products, so no single one's effect can be isolated) with two others. Miyamoto and colleagues (P&G Innovation GK and Kyushu University, with dermatologist Masutaka Furue disclosed as "a consultant of P&G Innovation GK") had 86 women apply three GFF-containing SK-II products, SK-II Facial Treatment Essence, SK-II Cellumination Essence, and SK-II Skin Signature Cream, together twice daily for 12 months, and reported "significant reversal" of facial wrinkle, pigmented-spot, and roughness scores against baseline and the same 86 women's own measurements taken 11 years earlier, in 1999, alongside rising hydration and falling water loss.[^gff] There was no untreated or placebo arm at either the 2023 or 1999 measurement, and because three multi-ingredient products were used together, none of the reported improvement can be attributed to this essence rather than the other two products or a year of daily moisturizing.[^gff]

The remaining GFF evidence, in-vitro gene-expression changes in cultured keratinocytes and open-label pore/roughness/redness imaging over 4 weeks, was generated with Pitera generally, not with this specific 90%+ formula, and carries the same limits (small, company-run or company-funded, largely without control arms) detailed on [[galactomyces-ferment-filtrate]].[^gff]

> **Bottom line.** One P&G-run study did use this product by name, but only as part of a three-product regimen with no control group, so it cannot isolate this essence's effect. Everything else cited for it is general Pitera evidence, preliminary and mostly company-funded, not a trial of this bottle. [[galactomyces-ferment-filtrate]] has the full picture.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Proven to reduce redness."** SK-II ties this to redness from dryness on its own product page without a linked, published, controlled trial of this product; the closest published evidence is an open-label imaging study of general Pitera use with no untreated comparison group.[^1][^gff]
- **"Stimulates skin's natural reproduction of Hyaluronic Acid and Natural Moisturizing Factors" and "improves surface cell turnover renewal."** Mechanistic-sounding claims on the product page with no cited published trial measuring hyaluronic acid production, NMF levels, or cell turnover rate for this product.[^1]
- **Reduces the appearance of dark spots.** The only relevant published trial for GFF and pigmentation tested a different, three-active combination serum (GFF plus dexpanthenol and centella asiatica), not this essence, in one small independent RCT (details on [[galactomyces-ferment-filtrate]]); the 12-month study that did use this product tracked "pigmented spots" only as part of a three-product regimen with no control arm.[^gff]
- **A result timeline of 2 weeks for fine lines, 1 month for radiance, and 1 year for "visible aging damage."** Brand-stated schedule on the product page, not tied to a published, controlled trial with those specific checkpoints.[^1]
- **"Miracle water" positioning and the sake-brewers'-hands origin story.** A P&G-published account, not an independently verified history (full discussion on [[galactomyces-ferment-filtrate]]).
