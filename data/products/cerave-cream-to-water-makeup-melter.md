A $14.99 cream makeup remover that turns watery on the skin and wipes off without rinsing, 12.7 cents a millilitre. **Bottom line:** a sensible oil-phase remover at a drugstore price, and the brand's own website credits it with an ingredient the formula does not contain.

> **Hyaluronic acid is listed as a key ingredient and is not in the product.** The brand page's Key Ingredients block names three for this item: Ceramides, Hyaluronic Acid and Squalane Oil.[^brand] The declared list runs 25 entries and contains neither hyaluronic acid nor sodium hyaluronate.[^brand] The tube is the honest one: it claims only "With 3 essential ceramides & squalane".[^photo]

## Summary

CeraVe sells it at **$14.99**, and the tube gives the size as 4 FL OZ / 118 ml.[^brand][^photo] The brand describes it as combining "squalane oil and three essential ceramides to cleanse without disrupting the skin barrier" and says it "delivers hydration for up to 48 hours".[^brand]

The declared list opens "AQUA / WATER / EAU • GLYCERIN • ISOPROPYL PALMITATE • C15-19 ALKANE • COCO-CAPRYLATE/CAPRATE • POLYGLYCERYL-2 ISOSTEARATE/DIMER DILINOLEATE COPOLYMER • SQUALANE • CERAMIDE NP • CERAMIDE AP • CERAMIDE EOP".[^brand]

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: modest, and uncontroversially so.** Positions three through seven are emollient esters and alkanes, the things that dissolve makeup. No trial is needed for that and none is offered.
- **Evidence quality: solid for the mechanism, absent for the product.** [[ceramides]] and [[squalane]] carry their own evidence; nothing specific to this tube is cited anywhere on the page read.
- **Record finding: the website adds an ingredient the formula lacks.** Covered in the pull quote above. Worth being precise about where the error sits: the packaging claim is accurate, the Key Ingredients module on the product page is not, and the two other mentions of hyaluronic acid on that page are site navigation for different products.[^brand]
- **Record finding: one product, two names.** The page title and the tube both say **Makeup Melter**. The site navigation and the page's own FAQ call it "CeraVe Cream to Water Makeup Remover".[^brand] Searching either name should find it, which is the practical reason to record both.
- **The tube and the website disagree about who it is for.** The tube reads "For Normal to Sensitive Skin"; the page says "Made For: All skin types".[^brand][^photo] A small gap, but the narrower claim is the one printed on the thing you buy.
- **An unusual inclusion.** [[piroctone-olamine]] sits seventeenth. It is an antifungal more familiar from dandruff shampoo, used here in a preservative role, and it is the only ingredient in the list that would surprise anyone reading a makeup remover.

## The Evidence

None specific to this product. The barrier-lipid arguments live at [[ceramides]], [[cholesterol]] and [[phytosphingosine]], and the emollient case at [[squalane]]; none is restated here. A wipe-off product has limited contact time regardless of what is in it, which is the honest ceiling on any barrier claim made for a cleanser.

> **Bottom line.** Does the job it is sold for, at a fair price, with a website that oversells it by exactly one ingredient.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own page and the tube, and **not** endorsed here.

- "delivers hydration for up to 48 hours"[^brand] No study is cited, and the product is designed to be wiped off.
- "Stronger skin barrier in just 1 use"[^brand] A single-use barrier claim with no measurement offered.
- "DOUBLE CLEANSING EFFICACY" on the tube, and "the transformative double cleansing power" on the page.[^brand][^photo] The instructions say no water is needed and a second cleanser is optional, so the double cleansing is a description of the texture change rather than of two products.
- "It helps remove long-wear and some waterproof makeup, but a dedicated remover may be needed for heavy waterproof products."[^brand] The brand's own FAQ, and a more measured claim than the front of the tube.
