Banila Co Clean It Zero Tea-Tree EX Pore Clarifying Cleansing Balm is the oily and combination-skin version of the brand's [[banila-co-clean-it-zero-cleansing-balm-original|Clean It Zero]] oil-to-milk balm: the same first-step [[double-cleansing|oil cleanse]] that dissolves makeup and sunscreen, with tea tree and a little salicylic acid added and an oily-skin, pore-clarifying pitch. Banila Co lists it at $22.00.[^banila]

> This is a cleansing balm first and a "pore" product second. Its evidence-backed job is the same as any oil cleanser, removing makeup and water-resistant sunscreen that a water wash leaves behind (see [[double-cleansing]]). The additions that justify the "pore clarifying" name, tea tree and salicylic acid, sit low on the ingredient list in a product that rinses off after a minute, so it does not deliver the leave-on pore or acne benefit a working-strength [[salicylic-acid]] treatment provides. Like the Original, it contains added fragrance where many balms are fragrance-free.

## Summary

Banila Co markets this as the pore-clarifying member of the Clean It Zero line: "The Pore Clarifying cleansing balm removes waterproof makeup, impurities, dead skin cells, and excess sebum for oily & combination skin."[^banila] It is massaged onto dry skin, emulsified with water into a milk, then rinsed.

The formula leads with two emollient esters (cetyl ethylhexanoate and ethylhexyl stearate) as the cleansing oils, then PEG-20 glyceryl triisostearate and PEG-10 isostearate, the emulsifiers that let an oil balm rinse clean, plus synthetic wax and jojoba oil. What differs from the Original is a cluster of oily-skin-marketed ingredients lower down: tea tree extract and leaf oil, salicylic acid, capryloyl salicylic acid (a lipophilic salicylic-acid derivative), and [[gluconolactone-pha|gluconolactone]], alongside hot-spring water and botanical extracts. Fragrance is present. It is listed at $22.00 at Banila Co ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-26).[^banila]

## The Evidence

The removal claim is the one that holds up, and it is the same case as every oil cleanser: an oil-based first cleanse removes water-resistant sunscreen and long-wear makeup a foaming wash leaves behind, the trial evidence set out on [[double-cleansing]]. Nothing about this product's removal is specific to oily skin; that framing is positioning.

The "pore clarifying" and "excess sebum" story rests on the tea tree and salicylic acid. [[salicylic-acid]] and its derivative [[capryloyl-salicylic-acid-lha|capryloyl salicylic acid]] do have real evidence for unclogging pores, but as leave-on treatments at working concentrations, not sitting low on the list of a balm that is on the skin for under a minute and then rinsed away. Tea tree oil has some evidence for acne as a leave-on, but again is a rinse-off trace here. So this cleanses as well as the Original and no better for pores; if oily or acne-prone skin is the actual concern, the treatment belongs in a leave-on step (see [[blackheads-comedones-congestion]] and [[oily-skin]]), not a cleanser.

> **Bottom line.** A competent oil-to-milk cleansing balm for oily and combination skin whose real value, like the [[banila-co-clean-it-zero-cleansing-balm-original|Original]], is removing makeup and sunscreen. The tea tree and salicylic acid make the marketing, not the results, in a rinse-off format, and like the Original it contains added fragrance where many balms are fragrance-free.
