La Roche-Posay Anthelios UVMune 400 SPF50+ is a French pharmacy face sunscreen sold internationally; this listing is from La Roche-Posay's Middle East regional site.[^1] It's built around Mexoryl 400 (MCE), an L'Oreal filter the brand markets as extending UV protection into "ultra long UVA" up to 400nm.[^1]

> An SPF50+ broad-spectrum face sunscreen carrying eight organic UV filters, layering avobenzone, bemotrizinol, ecamsule, drometrizole trisiloxane, and diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate with the newer Mexoryl 400 (MCE), which La Roche-Posay describes as reaching further into the UVA band (up to 400nm) than older filters.[^1] Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. The filter set is not part of the FDA's sunscreen ingredient list, and the product is not stocked by Target in the US.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Anthelios UVMune 400 SPF50+ is a face sunscreen from La Roche-Posay, the French dermatological brand owned by L'Oreal. It is sold through the brand's international sites (this listing is from its Middle East regional site) as an "invisible fluid," positioned as non-greasy, non-sticky, and free of white cast.[^1] Its core purpose is the same as any sunscreen's: broad-spectrum UV protection is the best-evidenced way to slow photoaging and lower the risk of sun-related skin cancer, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

What sets this formula apart from a plainer sunscreen is its filter count and its newest filter. Per the brand's own product page, the formula carries eight organic UV filters: ethylhexyl salicylate, bemotrizinol, ethylhexyl triazone, avobenzone, drometrizole trisiloxane, diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate, ecamsule, and Mexoryl 400 (MCE), the last introduced by the brand as a new addition that the marketing describes as protecting against "ultra long UVA" reaching up to 400nm, beyond the range most other organic filters absorb.[^1] That long-UVA extension is a genuine formulation difference from single-UVA-filter (avobenzone-only) sunscreens common on US shelves, [detailed below](#the-evidence), though the specific 400nm claim currently rests on the manufacturer's own description rather than an independent test.

None of Mexoryl 400 (MCE), bemotrizinol, drometrizole trisiloxane, or diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate is part of the US FDA's list of permitted sunscreen actives, and the product does not appear in Target's US catalog.[^1][^2] The brand's page does not list a price.[^1]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific product was located; the product-specific facts available are its filter composition and the manufacturer's own label claims.

La Roche-Posay's own product page lists the full INCI ingredient panel, from which the active UV filters are: ethylhexyl salicylate, bis-ethylhexyloxyphenol methoxyphenyl triazine ([[bemotrizinol]]), ethylhexyl triazone, butyl methoxydibenzoylmethane ([[avobenzone]]), methoxypropylamino cyclohexenylidene ethoxyethylcyanoacetate (marketed by the brand as [[mexoryl-400-mce]]), diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate, drometrizole trisiloxane, and terephthalylidene dicamphor sulfonic acid (ecamsule).[^1] That is eight organic filters in one formula, more than most US sunscreens carry.[^1]

Of those, ethylhexyl salicylate, ethylhexyl triazone, and bemotrizinol are primarily UVB-active; avobenzone, ecamsule, drometrizole trisiloxane, diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate, and Mexoryl 400 (MCE) are UVA-active.[^1] The brand's marketing for the product singles out Mexoryl 400 (MCE) as "a new exclusive UV filter that protects against even the most insidious UV rays: ULTRA LONG UVA* up to 400nm."[^1] That is a longer wavelength than avobenzone (the sole UVA filter in most US chemical sunscreens) is generally understood to cover well, but the 400nm claim is the manufacturer's own characterization; no independently published test of this specific filter combination was located.

> **Bottom line.** The formula pairs a labeled SPF50+ rating with an unusually large stack of UVA-active filters, including a newly introduced one (Mexoryl 400/MCE) the brand markets for reaching further into the UVA band than older filters.[^1] That combination is a genuine formulation difference from the single-filter (avobenzone-only) UVA coverage typical of US chemical sunscreens, though the specific long-UVA extension claim is currently manufacturer-sourced rather than independently verified, and none of the newer filters (Mexoryl 400/MCE, bemotrizinol, drometrizole trisiloxane, diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate) is on the FDA's list of permitted sunscreen actives, so the product is not sold in the US. The brand's page doesn't disclose which SPF test method (e.g. ISO/COLIPA) was used; that it's an EU-style rating rather than an FDA test is an inference from the non-US market and filter set, not a sourced fact.[^1][^2]

The brand's page also states dermatological testing on sensitive, reactive, and intolerant skin, ophthalmological testing on sensitive eyes and contact-lens wearers, and allergy testing, plus figures from a brand-run use test ("99.1% Skin feels comfortable," 113 subjects, immediately after use).[^1] These are manufacturer-reported comfort measures, not independently corroborated.
