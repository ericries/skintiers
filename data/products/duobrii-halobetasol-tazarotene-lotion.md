Duobrii is the brand name for a fixed-combination lotion of halobetasol propionate 0.01% and tazarotene 0.045%, made by Bausch Health (marketed through Ortho Dermatologics) and FDA-approved for "the topical treatment of plaque psoriasis in adults."[^dailymed]

> A genuinely clever pairing: tazarotene's anti-proliferative effect is thought to offset the skin-thinning that limits how long a potent steroid can be used, so this combination is positioned for longer courses than a standalone potent corticosteroid.[^stl] It beat vehicle by a wide margin in both of its own registration trials.[^dailymed] But it is still built on halobetasol, which a vasoconstrictor assay places in the high to super-high potency range among topical corticosteroids,[^dailymed] and the retinoid does not eliminate the steroid's usual cautions, only extends the runway. The edge here is duration and tolerability, not a dramatic jump in how completely it clears skin. General steroid and retinoid evidence is owned by [[topical-corticosteroids]] and [[tazarotene]]; the broader condition is [[psoriasis]].

## Summary

Duobrii combines two actives already used separately against plaque psoriasis: [[topical-corticosteroids|halobetasol propionate]] 0.01%, a potent corticosteroid, and [[tazarotene]] 0.045%, a receptor-selective retinoid.[^dailymed] The rationale is that halobetasol here is dosed at one-fifth the strength of standalone 0.05% halobetasol products, and tazarotene's effect on keratinocyte turnover is thought to counteract corticosteroid-induced atrophy, letting the pair be used for longer stretches than a full-strength steroid alone could tolerate.[^stl] The label's indication, quoted above, carries no pediatric approval; safety and effectiveness under 18 have not been evaluated.[^dailymed] It is dosed once daily as a thin layer to affected areas, for external use only, not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use.[^dailymed] Duobrii is contraindicated in pregnancy: "DUOBRII Lotion is contraindicated in pregnancy," because tazarotene is teratogenic, and the label requires a negative pregnancy test within two weeks before starting therapy plus effective contraception during use.[^dailymed] The general profiles for each active, including how this fits among other corticosteroid and retinoid options, are owned by [[topical-corticosteroids]] and [[tazarotene]]; the broader disease context is [[psoriasis]].

The product-specific case rests on two vehicle-controlled phase 3 trials, described in full under [The Evidence](#the-evidence). Across 418 adults with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis covering 3 to 12% body surface area, treatment success (at least a 2-grade IGA improvement to clear or almost clear) at Week 8 was 36% on Duobrii versus 7% on vehicle in the first trial, and 45% versus 13% on vehicle in the second.[^dailymed] That is a wide, consistent gap over an inert vehicle lotion, though more than half of treated patients in both trials still fell short of clear or almost clear.

The most common adverse reactions were contact dermatitis (7%), application-site pain (3%), folliculitis (2%), skin atrophy (2%), and excoriation (2%).[^dailymed] This is a prescription drug; see a prescriber for use and dosing, and see [[psoriasis]] for the broader condition context.

## The Evidence

The general case for corticosteroid potency classes and for tazarotene as a receptor-selective retinoid, including how each behaves on its own, is covered on [[topical-corticosteroids]] and [[tazarotene]]; this section covers only what the Duobrii label itself reports for this fixed combination.

Two identically designed, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled phase 3 trials enrolled a combined 418 adults 18 and older with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis covering 3 to 12% body surface area.[^dailymed] Subjects applied Duobrii or vehicle lotion once daily. The primary endpoint was treatment success at Week 8, defined as at least a 2-grade improvement from baseline in Investigator's Global Assessment (IGA) score to a score equating to clear or almost clear.[^dailymed]

| | Trial 1 (NCT02462070) | Trial 2 (NCT02462122) |
|---|---|---|
| Duobrii treatment success[^dailymed] | 36% (N=135) | 45% (N=141) |
| Vehicle treatment success | 7% (N=68) | 13% (N=74) |

Both trials showed a wide, consistent gap over vehicle at Week 8.[^dailymed]

> **Bottom line.** Duobrii beat vehicle by roughly a fivefold margin on treatment success in both of its own trials, a strong and consistent effect, but a majority of treated patients in each trial still did not reach clear or almost clear at Week 8.[^dailymed]

## What's In It

The active and inactive ingredients as declared on the FDA-approved prescribing information.[^dailymed]

Active:

- [[topical-corticosteroids|Halobetasol propionate]] 0.01% (0.1 mg per gram), a potent topical corticosteroid, at one-fifth the strength of standalone 0.05% halobetasol products.[^dailymed]
- [[tazarotene]] 0.045% (0.45 mg per gram), a prescription topical retinoid.[^dailymed]

Inactive ingredients: carbomer copolymer type B, carbomer homopolymer type A, diethyl sebacate, edetate disodium dihydrate, light mineral oil, methylparaben, propylparaben, purified water, sodium hydroxide, sorbitan monooleate, and sorbitol solution 70%, in a white to off-white lotion base.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **A steroid-sparing combination that can be used longer than a potent steroid alone.** The rationale, tazarotene offsetting corticosteroid-induced atrophy, is a real and plausible pharmacologic mechanism reflected in the lower 0.01% halobetasol strength versus standalone 0.05% products,[^stl] but the label itself does not report a head-to-head trial proving longer safe duration versus a standalone potent steroid; treat the "extended duration" framing as mechanistic reasoning, not its own tested claim.[^dailymed]
