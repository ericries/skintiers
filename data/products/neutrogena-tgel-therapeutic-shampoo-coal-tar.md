Neutrogena T/Gel Therapeutic Shampoo is the classic coal tar medicated shampoo, built around "Neutar" solubilized coal tar extract at a strength equivalent to coal tar 0.5%, for dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and scalp psoriasis.[^brand]

> The reference coal tar shampoo. Its active is coal tar 0.5%, an FDA-monograph treatment for the scaling of dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis, with genuine but modest and dated evidence covered on [[coal-tar]]. Two honest caveats: coal tar stains, smells, and raises sun sensitivity, and Neutrogena has discontinued the UK T/Gel Therapeutic line, though equivalent coal tar 0.5% shampoos remain widely sold and FDA-labeled.[^brand][^dailymed]

## Summary

This is the original therapeutic member of the T/Gel range. The brand states the active as "Neutar Solubilised Coal Tar Extract 20 mg/ml (equivalent to 5mg/ml Coal Tar)," i.e. coal tar 0.5%, and markets it as an "effective treatment for dandruff, scalp psoriasis, and seborrhoeic dermatitis of the scalp."[^brand] Coal tar's evidence lives on [[coal-tar]] and in the ranking on [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]]: it is a recognized FDA over-the-counter active, but modest and largely superseded by newer antifungals, and it stains and increases photosensitivity.

Neutrogena lists the UK T/Gel Therapeutic as discontinued.[^brand] The identical active and labeled use remain available in the US market: a store-brand coal tar 0.5% shampoo on DailyMed carries the same "Anti-dandruff, anti-seborrheic dermatitis, anti-psoriasis" purpose and is explicitly sold to "compare to the active ingredient of Neutrogena T/Gel Therapeutic Shampoo."[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

No independent trial of this exact shampoo was located beyond the brand's own "clinically proven" statements. The defensible facts are its active (coal tar 0.5%) and the FDA-monograph standing of coal tar for these scalp conditions, set out on [[coal-tar]].[^dailymed] The magnitude is modest and the modern high-quality trial base is thin relative to azole antifungals and selenium sulfide, which is why coal tar sits below them on [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]] and on [[seborrheic-dermatitis]].

The practical trade-offs are coal tar's, not this formula's: it can stain skin, hair, and fabric, has a tar odor, and "may increase your tendency to sunburn for up to 24 hours after application," so daytime sun protection matters during use.[^dailymed] The cancer worry that surrounds anything coal-derived has been tested directly and is addressed on [[coal-tar]].

> **Bottom line.** The archetypal coal tar 0.5% medicated shampoo, genuinely effective at the FDA-monograph level for dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and scalp psoriasis, but modest, messy, and photosensitizing.[^brand][^dailymed] A reasonable option for tar-responsive scalps; for most people the better-evidenced first-line choices are the antifungals ranked on [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]].

## What's In It

The active is Neutar solubilized coal tar extract, equivalent to coal tar 0.5%.[^brand] The base is a standard fragranced sulfate shampoo; the brand's listed other ingredients include a laureth sulfate surfactant, cocamidopropyl betaine, preservatives, EDTA, citric acid, fragrance, and water, and exact inactive lists vary by market and version.[^brand] The FDA-labeled US equivalent lists water, sodium laureth sulfate, cocamide MEA, cocamidopropyl betaine, laureth-4, fragrance, polysorbate 20, citric acid, sodium chloride, disodium EDTA, phenoxyethanol, methylchloroisothiazolinone, methylisothiazolinone, and colorants.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Clinically proven to treat psoriasis, seborrhoeic dermatitis and dandruff."** A brand claim; it aligns with coal tar's FDA-monograph standing for these conditions, but rests on the active's recognition, not a published trial of this shampoo.[^brand]
- **"Developed with dermatologists."** A brand statement, not an efficacy result.[^brand]
