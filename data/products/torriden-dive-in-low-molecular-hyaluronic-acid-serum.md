Torriden Dive-In Low Molecular Hyaluronic Acid Serum is a South Korean humectant face serum built around several low-molecular-weight forms of hyaluronic acid, sold in a 50 mL / 1.69 fl oz size.[^torriden]

> A low-molecular-weight hyaluronic-acid serum whose hydrating case rests on the well-evidenced general role of hyaluronic acid and glycerin as humectants (see [[hyaluronic-acid]] and [[moisturizing]]); glycerin, a higher-tier humectant on [[moisturizing]]'s own evidence ranking, is listed third in the ingredient list, ahead of every hyaluronate form. [No published trial has tested this exact serum](#the-evidence); the brand's own percentage hydration figures are not corroborated on an independently fetchable source and are quarantined below.

## Summary

Torriden Dive-In Low Molecular Hyaluronic Acid Serum is a K-beauty humectant serum that the brand markets as delivering "lightweight hydration for glowing skin" using what it calls "5D multi low-molecular hyaluronic acid."[^torriden] It is sold in one 50 mL size, and Torriden's own US site lists it at $24.00.[^torriden]

Its declared ingredient list opens water, butylene glycol, glycerin, dipropylene glycol, 1,2-hexanediol, and [[panthenol]], then five distinct hyaluronate forms: sodium hyaluronate, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, sodium acetylated hyaluronate, sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer, and hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate.[^torriden][^yesstyle] Two things follow from that order. First, [[glycerin]], a longer-established and higher-tier humectant on [[moisturizing]]'s own evidence ranking, sits ahead of every hyaluronate form, so glycerin is likely doing at least as much of the hydrating work as the headline low-molecular hyaluronic acid. Second, several of the named hyaluronate forms (hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, sodium acetylated hyaluronate, hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate) are the lower-molecular-weight, more processed kind that the general evidence on [[hyaluronic-acid]] shows can actually cross the stratum corneum, unlike the higher-molecular-weight hyaluronic acid used in many serums that stays on the skin's surface. Further down the list, [[ceramides|ceramide NP]], cholesterol, beta-glucan, and the soothing agents madecassoside and madecassic acid appear eighteenth through twenty-third of thirty-three total ingredients, a minor share of the formula relative to the humectant block above them.[^torriden][^yesstyle]

No published trial has tested this exact serum. Torriden's own product page cites several brand-run percentage figures for skin-moisture improvement, but these are not corroborated on an independently fetchable second source, so they are reported as brand marketing claims rather than established findings ([see Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^torriden] Its case instead rests on the general evidence for hyaluronic acid and glycerin as humectants ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence), and [[hyaluronic-acid]], [[glycerin]], [[moisturizing]]).

## The Evidence

Hyaluronic acid and glycerin, applied topically as humectants, reliably raise a measured skin-hydration reading in controlled testing; the case for hyaluronic acid, including the molecular-weight distinction that determines whether it penetrates past the skin's outer layer, is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]], and glycerin's case is set out on [[glycerin]] and [[moisturizing]], where glycerin sits in a higher evidence tier than hyaluronic acid. This serum's declared ingredient list places glycerin third overall, ahead of every one of its five hyaluronate forms, so glycerin's well-evidenced humectant effect is at least as relevant to this formula as the headline low-molecular-weight hyaluronic-acid claim.[^torriden][^yesstyle]

No published trial has tested this exact serum. What is specific to this formulation is its use of several lower-molecular-weight and chemically modified hyaluronate forms (hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, sodium acetylated hyaluronate, hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate) alongside standard sodium hyaluronate, which is consistent with the brand's own description of the product as a "5D multi low-molecular hyaluronic acid" blend.[^torriden] That composition is directionally consistent with the general finding on [[hyaluronic-acid]] that only lower-molecular-weight forms cross the stratum corneum, but no independent study isolates this specific multi-form blend's own effect.

Torriden's own product page reports several percentage figures from small brand-run tests, including an "88.87% improve outer-skin moisture" and a "10.09% improve inner-skin moisture" reading (20 people, immediately after use), a "48-hour" moisture-retention claim (20 people), a "14.94% improve moisture in lower stratum corneum layer" reading (22 people, 4 weeks), and a non-irritation claim from a 50-person, 6-week human repeat insult patch test.[^torriden] None of these figures, sample sizes, or methods are published in a peer-reviewed or independently verifiable source; YesStyle's retail listing repeats the brand's general "clinically proven" and moisturizing-effectiveness language but does not corroborate any of the specific percentages or sample sizes.[^torriden][^yesstyle] They are reported below as manufacturer marketing claims, not graded evidence.

> **Bottom line.** No independent study has isolated this serum's own effect. Its case rests on the general evidence for hyaluronic acid and glycerin as humectants, applied to a formula that leads with glycerin and layers in several low-molecular-weight hyaluronate forms; the brand's own percentage hydration figures are single-sourced, brand-run results, not independently corroborated findings.

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on Torriden's own product page and independently matched on YesStyle's retail listing.[^torriden][^yesstyle] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

Hydration and humectant ingredients:

- [[glycerin]], butylene glycol, dipropylene glycol, and propanediol, humectant solvents
- [[panthenol]], a humectant and skin-conditioning agent
- Sodium hyaluronate, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, sodium acetylated hyaluronate, sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer, and hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate, five forms of [[hyaluronic-acid]]

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, 1,2-hexanediol, pentylene glycol, glyceryl acrylate/acrylic acid copolymer, PVM/MA copolymer, polyglyceryl-10 laurate, xanthan gum, tromethamine, carbomer, ethylhexylglycerin, Scutellaria baicalensis root extract, and Paeonia suffruticosa root extract.[^torriden][^yesstyle]
