Vichy Minéral 89 Hydrating & Plumping Daily Booster is a fragrance-free hyaluronic-acid and polyglutamic-acid hydrating serum, sold in 30 mL, 50 mL, and 75 mL sizes, built around Vichy's mineralizing volcanic water.[^vichy]

> A hydrating gel serum whose headline case rests on hyaluronic acid, a well-evidenced humectant (see [[hyaluronic-acid]]), paired with polyglutamic acid, a newer humectant with only preliminary, non-human evidence so far (see [[polyglutamic-acid]]). [No independent study of this exact formula was located](#the-evidence); the 93%/19% hydration figures are the brand's own small, self-run tests, not independently published research.

## Summary

Vichy markets this as the current version of its best-selling Minéral 89 booster line, describing it on its own US product page as "powered by the Moisture Acid Twins [Hyaluronic Acid and Polyglutamic Acid] and Vichy Volcanic Water," in a "non-sticky, fast-absorbing gel texture."[^vichy] The brand states the formula was "clinically tested by 325 dermatologists on 3,900+ people worldwide," is fragrance-free, and is "accepted by the National Eczema Association."[^vichy] It reports two of its own small tests: a self-assessment in 53 subjects found 93% felt instant hydration, and a separate clinical test in 42 subjects over two months found 19% longer-lasting plumpness.[^vichy] Both are company-run and not independently published, so they are reported here as the brand's own claims, not verified third-party research.

Hyaluronic acid is a well-studied topical humectant that reliably raises measured skin hydration; that general evidence, including the molecular-weight caveat for how far it penetrates the skin, is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. Polyglutamic acid, the second named active, has a much thinner evidence base: the strongest primary study located tested it in cultured skin cells and a lab-grown skin model, not in living human skin, and its own authors call for human trials before drawing conclusions; that is set out on [[polyglutamic-acid]]. No independent, published study of this specific Vichy formula was found ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)).

Vichy identifies this product on its current US site as a reformulation, distinct from an earlier "Minéral 89" hyaluronic-acid-only serum sold under a similar name; that earlier product's original US retail page no longer resolves.[^vichy] This page profiles the version Vichy currently sells and markets as its best seller.[^vichy] Vichy does not sell the product through a direct checkout on its own US site; its "Where to Buy" links to Amazon, so no independently verified retail price is asserted here.[^vichy]

## The Evidence

Hyaluronic acid, applied topically as a humectant, reliably raises a measured skin-hydration reading in controlled testing, and only its lower-molecular-weight forms cross the stratum corneum; that evidence is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. Polyglutamic acid's case is far thinner: the most skin-relevant primary study located tested a high-molecular-weight strain in cultured keratinocytes and a reconstructed human-skin model, not living human skin, and the study's own authors state that in vivo human data are absent and needed; a separate formulation study found commercial polyglutamic acid gave a hydration effect only "comparable" to, not exceeding, hyaluronic acid and glycerin. That evidence is set out in full on [[polyglutamic-acid]].

No independent, published study of this Vichy formula was located. What Vichy reports on its own product page are two small, company-run tests: a self-assessment in 53 subjects found 93% "feel instant hydration," and a separate clinical test in 42 subjects over two months found 19% "long lasting plump."[^vichy] Neither test's methodology, control condition, or statistical detail is disclosed beyond the subject counts, and neither appears to be independently published, so they are reported here as the brand's own claims rather than verified evidence. Vichy separately states the formula was "clinically tested by 325 dermatologists on 3,900+ people worldwide" and is "accepted by the National Eczema Association," claims that describe dermatologist and safety testing rather than an efficacy trial.[^vichy]

> **Bottom line.** No independent study has isolated this exact serum's own effect. Its case rests on the well-evidenced humectant role of hyaluronic acid, the much thinner, non-human evidence for polyglutamic acid, and the brand's own small, unpublished self-assessment and clinical-test figures for instant and longer-lasting hydration.

## What's In It

Vichy's own product page names three "Key Ingredients" for this formula: hyaluronic acid, polyglutamic acid, and Vichy Volcanic Water.[^vichy] Vichy describes hyaluronic acid as drawing "moisture into the skin to provide instant hydration," polyglutamic acid as locking "in moisture" and providing "a visible plumping effect... especially when paired with Hyaluronic Acid," and its volcanic water as strengthening and repairing "your skin's moisture barrier."[^vichy] Vichy's US page for this SKU does not display the full INCI list inline on the main product page (it is one click away via a "Full ingredient list" link), so the base, solvent, and preservative system beyond these three named ingredients is not documented here.
