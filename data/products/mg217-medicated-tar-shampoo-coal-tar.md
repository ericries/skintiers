MG217 Psoriasis Medicated Conditioning Shampoo is an over-the-counter medicated shampoo from Wisconsin Pharmacal Company, labeled under the FDA's OTC coal tar monograph at coal tar 3% (from Coal Tar Topical Solution, USP 20%) for the temporary relief of itching, scaling, flaking, redness, and scalp irritation of psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis, or dandruff.[^dailymed] It is not a disease-modifying drug: the label claims symptom relief and recurrence prevention, not plaque clearance, and it carries a California Proposition 65 cancer warning for coal tar.[^dailymed]

> An OTC monograph coal tar shampoo, not a branded innovation with its own clinical trials. Coal tar has decades of use as a keratolytic and antipruritic for [[psoriasis]], [[seborrheic-dermatitis|seborrheic dermatitis]], and dandruff, and this product's own label restricts its claims to temporary symptom relief and helping prevent recurrence, not to clearing plaques or slowing disease progression.[^dailymed] The full evidence base for coal tar as an ingredient is graded on [[coal-tar]], not repeated here; this page covers what is specific to this product, its strength, formulation, and its own label language. One accuracy note up front: MG217 also sells a separate, lower-strength coal tar shampoo marketed as "0.5%," an "introductory level" strength according to CVS's retail listing for that product, distinct from the 3% strength on this product's DailyMed label; the two should not be confused.[^cvs]

## Summary

This is an FDA-regulated Drug Facts shampoo with an active of coal tar 3%, derived from Coal Tar Topical Solution, USP 20%.[^dailymed] It is regulated as an OTC monograph drug, meaning it is not FDA-approved through a New Drug Application with product-specific trials; instead it complies with the long-standing coal tar monograph that permits marketing without a fresh efficacy dossier for each product.

The label's purpose line reads "Psoriasis, Seborrheic Dermatitis, Dandruff Treatment," and the uses section states the product is "For the temporary relief of these symptoms associated with psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis or dandruff: itching, scaling, flaking, redness, irritation of the scalp. Helps prevent recurrence."[^dailymed] That is a health claim, temporary symptom relief for three named scalp conditions, not a cosmetic one, and it is honest in scope: the label does not claim to clear plaques, reduce lesion counts, or alter the underlying disease course of psoriasis. Coal tar's mechanism and the wider evidence for its keratolytic and antipruritic effects are graded on [[coal-tar]]; this page does not repeat that ingredient-level case.

Directions call for shaking well, wetting hair, massaging a liberal amount into the scalp, leaving it on for several minutes, then rinsing thoroughly, "for best results, use at least twice a week or as directed by a physician."[^dailymed] The label also carries a Proposition 65 warning that the product "can expose you to chemicals including Coal Tar, which is known to the State of California to cause cancer," and instructs users to avoid contact with the eyes, use caution in sun exposure for up to 24 hours after application because of increased sunburn risk, and not combine it with other psoriasis therapies such as ultraviolet radiation or prescription drugs unless a physician directs otherwise.[^dailymed] The brand's own FAQ notes that this warning is a California-specific requirement and that "an expert advisory panel of the US FDA has recognized coal tar to be a safe and effective treatment for psoriasis when used as directed," a framing consistent with the cancer-safety data reviewed on [[coal-tar]].[^pharmacalway]

## The Evidence

Coal tar's category-level evidence as a keratolytic and antipruritic for psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis, and dandruff is set out on [[coal-tar]]; no product-specific clinical trial of this MG217 shampoo formulation was found in the sources reviewed here. As an OTC monograph drug, it was not required to run one: FDA OTC monograph compliance rests on the ingredient (coal tar 3%, from a 20% USP topical solution) matching the concentration and formulation type the monograph allows, not on a per-brand efficacy trial.[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: the health claim on this label, temporary symptom relief for psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis, and dandruff, rests on coal tar's long-standing OTC monograph status and the ingredient-level evidence graded on [[coal-tar]], not on a trial of this specific shampoo.

## What's In It

- **Active: Coal Tar 3%** (from Coal Tar Topical Solution, USP 20%), as declared on the FDA Drug Facts label.[^dailymed] See [[coal-tar]] for the graded mechanism and ingredient-level evidence base.
- **Inactive ingredients:** Deionized Water, Sodium Lauryl Ether Sulfate, Cocamidopropyl Betaine, Lauramidopropyl Hydroxysultaine, Palmitic Betaine, Guar Hydroxypropyl-trimonium Chloride, Hydrolyzed Collagen, Phenoxyethanol, Methyl Paraben, n-Butyl Paraben, Ethyl Paraben, Isobutyl Paraben, Propyl Paraben, Fragrance, FD&C Blue #1, as declared on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

The label warns for external use only, to ask a doctor before use if the condition covers a large area of the body, and to stop use and ask a doctor if the condition worsens or does not improve after regular use.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Helps prevent recurrence."**[^dailymed] This is drawn directly from the product's own FDA Drug Facts label, so it is a regulated OTC monograph claim rather than unregulated marketing copy; it is not accompanied on the label by trial data quantifying how much recurrence is reduced or over what time period.
- **"help slow the rapid growth of skin cells on the scalp" and "coal tar is the only non-prescription active ingredient that works by slowing down excessive cell reproduction."**[^pharmacalway] This is a brand mechanism claim; it is directionally consistent with coal tar's known antiproliferative action described on [[coal-tar]], but "only non-prescription active" overstates the field somewhat, since other OTC actives (like salicylic acid) also address scaling through a different, keratolytic mechanism rather than by slowing cell turnover.
- **"Leaves hair clean and manageable"**[^cvs] and **"conditioning formula"**[^pharmacalway]. A cosmetic, non-health claim about the shampoo base rather than the coal tar itself; it is not addressed by the FDA Drug Facts panel and it is not attested anywhere on the DailyMed label.
