Nivea Creme is a fragranced, water-mineral oil-petrolatum cream sold by NIVEA (Beiersdorf) in its original blue metal tin (8.4 oz), available at major retailers including Target.[^niveausa][^target]

> A century-old occlusive cream, not a modern actives formula: its case rests on mineral oil and petrolatum, both confirmed on the declared ingredient list but undisclosed at a stated percentage, which fits the well-evidenced general pattern for occlusive moisturizing rather than any product-specific trial.[^niveausa][^target] NIVEA's own "nourishes and protects" language carries no disclosed data specific to this formula.[^niveausa]

## Summary

NIVEA traces the cream to 1911, when Dr. Oscar Troplowitz, head of Beiersdorf, used a new emulsifier called Eucerit to stabilize a water-and-oil cream in Hamburg; Beiersdorf's own brand page says this "white cream was named NIVEA and revolutionized skin care by making daily care easy, smooth and reliable."[^beiersdorf] Since 2024 the tin itself has contained "at least 80% recycled aluminum," per the same page.[^beiersdorf]

NIVEA's US product page (for the 1 oz travel tin, same formula) describes it as "the rich, creamy formula" that, "enriched with Provitamin B5," "intensively moisturizes, nourishes and protects the skin from that rough, dry, feel," for use "to face, hands, and body."[^niveausa] Target's listing for the 8.4 oz tin gives the same use case ("Moisturizing Cream, For Normal & Dry Skin, Whole Body Application") and directions to "smooth over skin and apply daily for best results."[^target]

The declared ingredient list on NIVEA's own US site, in order, is water, mineral oil, petrolatum, glycerin, microcrystalline wax, lanolin alcohol, paraffin, magnesium sulfate, decyl oleate, octyldodecanol, aluminum stearates, fragrance, panthenol, citric acid, magnesium stearate, sodium anisate.[^niveausa] Target's listing for the 8.4 oz tin matches this list but does not show the last ingredient, sodium anisate.[^target] Neither source discloses a percentage for mineral oil or petrolatum, or a Drug Facts Active Ingredient panel, so the cream is formulated and marketed as a cosmetic moisturizer rather than a regulated OTC skin protectant. The general evidence for occlusive moisturizing is set out on [[petrolatum]] and [[moisturizing]].

## The Evidence

No independently published trial of this exact formula was located. NIVEA's own site claims the cream "intensively moisturizes, nourishes and protects the skin," but discloses no study design, sample size, or methodology behind that claim.[^niveausa] The product-specific fact available is its declared composition, led by water, mineral oil, and petrolatum on both sources cited here.[^niveausa][^target]

Mineral oil and petrolatum's occlusive mechanism, and their effect on transepidermal water loss, are well established and set out on [[petrolatum]]. That evidence covers occlusive ingredients as a class and plain occlusive creams generally, not this specific combination of mineral oil, petrolatum, microcrystalline wax, and lanolin alcohol tested head to head against a vehicle or a comparator cream. [[cerave-healing-ointment]], by contrast, discloses petrolatum at a stated percentage under an FDA Drug Facts panel, a formulation and disclosure difference from this cosmetic-labeled, fragranced cream.

> **Bottom line.** No independently reported study isolates this exact cream's own effect. Its case rests on mineral oil and petrolatum, both confirmed but undisclosed at a stated percentage[^niveausa][^target], which fits the well-evidenced general pattern for occlusive moisturizing covered on [[petrolatum]] and [[moisturizing]]; NIVEA's own "nourishes and protects" claim carries no disclosed data specific to this formula.

## What's In It

The full declared ingredient list (INCI), as shown on NIVEA's own US product page; Target's listing for the 8.4 oz tin matches this list except for omitting the final ingredient, sodium anisate.[^niveausa][^target]

Notable/functional ingredients:

- [[petrolatum|Petrolatum]] and mineral oil, the second- and third-listed ingredients after water on NIVEA's own list; neither is disclosed at a stated percentage or shown in a Drug Facts panel
- [[glycerin|Glycerin]], a humectant
- Microcrystalline wax and paraffin, structuring/thickening waxes that give the cream its firm, whipped texture
- Lanolin alcohol, an emollient derived from sheep's wool wax
- Fragrance, undisclosed by component; the product is scented, not fragrance-free
- Panthenol, a conditioning agent (provitamin B5), named in NIVEA's own marketing copy

## Common Marketing Claims

Claims from NIVEA's own product page, not accompanied by a published study of this cream:

- **"Intensively moisturizes, nourishes and protects the skin from that rough, dry, feel."**[^niveausa] NIVEA's own headline claim; no accompanying study design or data.
- **"Enriched with Provitamin B5."**[^niveausa] A formulation claim; panthenol is confirmed on the declared ingredient list, but no product-specific data on its contribution is disclosed.
- **"Dermatologist Tested."**[^target] Target's listing carries this claim; no underlying test report, sample size, or methodology is disclosed on either source cited here.
