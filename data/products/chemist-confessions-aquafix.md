Chemist Confessions Aquafix Hydrating Water Gel is a fragrance-free, oil-free hydrating gel from [[chemist-confessions|Chemist Confessions]], the skincare-education brand founded by cosmetic chemists [[victoria-fu|Victoria Fu]] and [[gloria-lu|Gloria Lu]], sold in one 50 mL size.[^cc]

> A glycerin-led humectant water gel: the brand's own declared actives list leads with a "9.5% Smart Glycerin Complex," a well-evidenced humectant (see [[glycerin]] and [[moisturizing]]), well ahead of the 2% sodium PCA and the 0.2% hyaluronic acid that the product's hydration positioning evokes. [No published trial has tested this exact product](#the-evidence); the brand's "clinically tested" claims for its glycerin complex and proprietary CityStem ingredient rest on one manufacturer page with no independently fetchable corroboration and are quarantined below, and the brand does not publish a full ordinal INCI list on its own product page.

## Summary

Aquafix Hydrating Water Gel is marketed by Chemist Confessions as "essential high-performance soothing hydration for all skin types," described as a 100% oil-free water gel "formulated to maximize hydration without the weight," and positioned as "the ultimate safety net for your skin barrier."[^cc] It is sold in one 50 mL size for $38.[^cc] The brand's directions describe it as "the first step after cleansing" applied "1-2 pumps... day and night," before actives or moisturizers.[^cc]

Rather than a full ordinal INCI declaration, Chemist Confessions' own product page discloses named actives with percentages under an "Ingredients List & Actives Profiles" section: a "9.5% Smart Glycerin Complex" it calls an "upgraded glycerin system featuring clinically tested, synergistic hydrators," 2% sodium PCA, a proprietary "2% CityStem" (described as a "bioengineered Marrubium vulgare extract clinically tested to enhance skin's defense"), 1% panthenol (which the brand elsewhere names, along with madecassoside and allantoin, as part of its "Barrier Calm Complex"), 0.5% madecassoside and asiaticoside (paired together in the percentage breakdown, though asiaticoside is not part of that named complex), 0.5% allantoin, and 0.2% hyaluronic acid.[^cc] Two things follow from that order. First, glycerin, at nearly five times the concentration of the next-highest named active, is doing the bulk of the declared humectant work, and glycerin is a well-evidenced, first-tier barrier humectant on [[glycerin]]'s own ranking. Second, the hyaluronic acid the product's name evokes is the lowest-concentration named active on the list, at 0.2%, with no molecular-weight specification given.

No published trial has tested this exact product, and the brand's "clinically tested" language for its glycerin complex and CityStem extract is not corroborated on an independently fetchable second source ([see Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^cc] Its case instead rests on the general evidence for glycerin, panthenol, and centella-derived triterpenes ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence), and [[glycerin]], [[moisturizing]], [[panthenol]], [[centella-asiatica]]).

## The Evidence

Glycerin, applied topically as a humectant, has an unusually clean mechanistic case and vehicle-controlled human data for raising stratum corneum water content and supporting barrier function; that general evidence, and where it sits relative to other humectants such as urea and hyaluronic acid, is set out on [[glycerin]] and [[moisturizing]]. This product's declared actives list places its "9.5% Smart Glycerin Complex" first and at the highest concentration of any named ingredient, well ahead of the 2% sodium PCA and the 0.2% hyaluronic acid, so glycerin's well-evidenced humectant/barrier effect is the strongest-supported part of this formula's hydration case.[^cc]

Panthenol has controlled-trial support for easing irritation and speeding short-term barrier recovery, covered on [[panthenol]]; it is declared here at 1%, within the dose range that trial evidence covers, and the brand names it, along with madecassoside and allantoin, as part of its "Barrier Calm Complex." Madecassoside and asiaticoside, paired together at a combined 0.5% in the brand's percentage list (though asiaticoside is not named as part of that complex), are centella-derived triterpenes with a reproducible collagen-stimulating and anti-inflammatory mechanism in cell studies but a thinner controlled human record, covered on [[centella-asiatica]]; allantoin is declared separately at 0.5%. The proprietary "2% CityStem," which the brand describes as a Marrubium vulgare extract "clinically tested to enhance skin's defense," has no independently fetchable study or corroborating source beyond that brand claim.[^cc]

No published trial has tested this exact formulation, and Chemist Confessions' own product page does not cite a specific study, sample size, or method for its "clinically tested" claims about the glycerin complex or CityStem; it states only the ingredient names and percentages.[^cc]

> **Bottom line.** No independent study has isolated this product's own effect. Its strongest-evidenced component is its glycerin complex, the highest-concentration named active, backed by the general humectant/barrier case for glycerin; the centella-derived soothing ingredients and panthenol are supporting players at lower declared concentrations, and the brand's "clinically tested" language for the glycerin complex and its proprietary CityStem extract is not corroborated beyond its own product page.

## What's In It

Chemist Confessions' own product page does not publish a full ordinal INCI declaration; instead it discloses these named actives with percentages under an "Ingredients List & Actives Profiles" section.[^cc]

- "9.5% Smart Glycerin Complex," built on [[glycerin]], described as containing "clinically tested, synergistic hydrators"
- 2% sodium PCA, a humectant component of skin's natural moisturizing factor
- "2% CityStem," a proprietary Marrubium vulgare (horehound) extract
- 1% [[panthenol]], which the brand names, along with madecassoside and allantoin, as its "Barrier Calm Complex"
- 0.5% madecassoside and asiaticoside (paired together in the brand's percentage list, though asiaticoside is not part of the named complex), [[centella-asiatica]]-derived triterpenes
- 0.5% [[allantoin]]
- 0.2% [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]], with no molecular weight stated

The formula is described by the brand as fragrance-free, essential-oil-free, vegan, and water-based.[^cc]
