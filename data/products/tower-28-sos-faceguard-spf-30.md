Tower 28's SOS FaceGuard SPF 30 is a Broad Spectrum, 100% mineral facial sunscreen from [[tower-28]], built on a single active, non-nano [[zinc-oxide]], and sold at about $32 for 50 mL (1.69 fl oz).[^brand] It is fragrance-free, oxybenzone-free, and marketed for sensitive and acne-prone skin, with a universal tint to avoid white cast.[^brand]

> A gentle, all-mineral SPF 30 with a genuinely barrier-supportive base (ceramides, cholesterol, phytosphingosine, squalane, and hyaluronic acid) and three sensitive-skin acceptance seals.[^brand] The sun-protection case is the general one for mineral broad-spectrum sunscreen, on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; the honest limits here are that the brand does not disclose the zinc oxide percentage, the rating is SPF 30 rather than the SPF 50 many facial sunscreens now offer, and the efficacy claims are consumer perception, not clinical outcomes.

## Summary

This is Tower 28's dedicated mineral face sunscreen, a "whipped, creamy" SPF 30 whose single UV active is non-nano zinc oxide, formulated for reactive, eczema-, rosacea-, and acne-prone skin.[^brand] Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health value, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; being 100% zinc oxide, it avoids the organic filters (including oxybenzone) some people prefer to skip.[^brand]

What distinguishes it from a bare mineral sunscreen is the base. Alongside the zinc oxide, the formula carries a full barrier-lipid set, [[ceramides]] (AP, EOP, and NP), [[cholesterol]], and [[phytosphingosine]], plus [[squalane]], [[hyaluronic-acid|sodium hyaluronate]], and the soothing agents [[allantoin]] and alpha-bisabolol.[^brand] That is a legitimately sensitive-skin-friendly, barrier-supportive vehicle, and the product carries the National Eczema Association, National Rosacea Society, and National Psoriasis Foundation acceptance seals, which attest to tolerability on those conditions rather than to treating them.[^brand]

The honest caveats are on the label. The brand states the active as "Zinc Oxide" without a percentage, so the actual filter load, and therefore how strong the protection is, cannot be read off the label.[^brand] And the rating is SPF 30, below the SPF 50 that many facial sunscreens now provide, a point one reviewer raised directly.[^brand]

## The Evidence

Regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing skin-cancer burden; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent, product-specific study of this sunscreen was located, so its grades rest on category-level UV-filter evidence plus what the label discloses.

The label discloses one active, non-nano zinc oxide, a mineral filter with genuine broad-spectrum and long-UVA reach, but not its concentration, which is the single most useful number for judging a mineral sunscreen's strength and is unusual to omit.[^brand] The iron oxides in the formula give a slight universal tint; iron oxides can add visible-light protection that matters for [[melasma]] and [[hyperpigmentation]], but that benefit tracks with tint depth, and a "no-coverage" universal tint is at the light end of that range.[^brand]

The brand's efficacy-adjacent numbers are consumer-perception data, not clinical endpoints: "96% agree that this sunscreen did not break them out" and "100% agreed" it did not sting the eyes, both "based on a third party test with 30 people."[^brand] Those speak to tolerability and cosmetic acceptability (real strengths for a sunscreen people will actually reapply), not to measured sun protection.

> **Bottom line.** A well-formulated, gentle, all-mineral SPF 30 with a barrier-supportive base and strong tolerability credentials, a reasonable pick for sensitive or acne-prone skin that wants a fragrance-free, oxybenzone-free option.[^brand] Just note it is SPF 30 with an undisclosed zinc percentage, and the "clinically-tested" claims are about comfort and wear, not protection level; the sun-protection case is the category one on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## What's In It

The single UV active, from the brand's ingredient list, is non-nano [[zinc-oxide]] (percentage not stated).[^brand]

Barrier and soothing base: [[ceramides]] (ceramide AP, EOP, NP), [[cholesterol]], [[phytosphingosine]], [[squalane]], [[hyaluronic-acid|sodium hyaluronate]], [[allantoin]], alpha-bisabolol, glycerin, caprylic/capric triglyceride, and [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), with iron oxides for the universal tint.[^brand] The formula is fragrance-free, alcohol-free, and oxybenzone-free, and the brand lists it as non-comedogenic.[^brand]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by product-specific trial evidence:

- **"96% agree it did not break them out" / "100% agreed" no eye sting.** Consumer-perception results from a third-party test of 30 people, reported by the brand; tolerability and comfort data, not measured sun protection or clinical outcomes.[^brand]
- **NEA, NRS, and NPF acceptance seals.** Third-party seals for tolerability and suitability on eczema-, rosacea-, and psoriasis-prone skin, not efficacy certifications.[^brand]
- **"Clinically-tested," "dermatologist approved."** Brand testing and endorsement language (including a quote from a dermatologist on the brand's own medical advisory board), not published efficacy trials.[^brand]
