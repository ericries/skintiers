Medik8 Crystal Retinal 3 is a 30 ml (1 fl. oz.) leave-on night serum whose active is encapsulated retinaldehyde at 0.03%, aimed at [[sun-damage-photoaging|photoaging]] (fine lines, texture), the second-lowest rung of a six-strength Crystal Retinal ladder that runs from 0.01% to 0.24%, priced at $66.00 as sold by [[medik8]], Amazon, and Dermstore.[^wirecutter][^dermstore]

> Retinaldehyde sits one enzymatic step from the receptor-active acid, closer than [[retinol]]'s two steps, and NYT Wirecutter, which tested 35 over-the-counter retinoid products with brand names concealed, calls it "considered to be the most effective retinoid sold over the counter," naming this serum its "Best for adjustable potency" pick.[^wirecutter] No published trial tests this exact serum; its case rests on the category-level evidence for retinaldehyde, covered on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]], and on a six-strength ladder that lets a user move up in dose without switching formulas.[^wirecutter][^dermstore] Like every over-the-counter retinoid it remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin]].

## Summary

Medik8 Crystal Retinal 3 is a 30 ml (1 fl. oz.) leave-on night serum from [[medik8]], a British skincare brand, priced at $66.00 direct from the manufacturer and matched by Amazon's and Dermstore's US retail listings.[^wirecutter][^dermstore] Its active is retinaldehyde (retinal), declared at 0.03% in the product name and independently confirmed on the Dermstore and Space NK retailer pages, encapsulated in what Wirecutter describes as a lipid that "encases the retinaldehyde to help regulate absorption, so it's less apt to irritate skin."[^wirecutter][^dermstore][^spacenk]

What sets it apart from a single-strength retinol serum is the ladder. Crystal Retinal is sold in six strengths, and Dermstore's own product photography names each rung and its marketed user level: Crystal Retinal 1 (0.01% retinaldehyde, "for sensitive skin"), this Crystal Retinal 3 (0.03%, "for new vitamin A users"), Crystal Retinal 6 (0.06%, "for regular vitamin A users"), Crystal Retinal 10 (0.10%, "for advanced vitamin A users"), Crystal Retinal 20 (0.20%, "for experienced vitamin A users"), and Crystal Retinal 24 (0.24%, "for expert vitamin A users"), letting a user move up in dose within one product line rather than switching formulas.[^dermstore] Wirecutter, describing that same range as running "from 0.01% for sensitive skin all the way up to 0.24% for longtime users," reports its testers "tried, and praised, the 0.03% version."[^wirecutter]

Retinaldehyde sits one enzymatic step from the receptor-active retinoic acid, closer than retinol's two steps; Wirecutter reports it "needs only one step to convert to retinoic acid" and "has been linked to quicker, more visible results, in comparison with retinol."[^wirecutter] Retinaldehyde's category-level evidence is the strongest among over-the-counter retinoid forms, [set out in full on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]](#the-evidence), but no published trial tests this exact serum.[^wirecutter] Like every over-the-counter retinoid it remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin]], which is retinoic acid itself.

The main catch, per Wirecutter, is price: "At more than $60 an ounce, Medik8 Crystal Retinal 3 is expensive. And the cost creeps up along with the concentration level."[^wirecutter] Against comparators already covered: [[skinmedica-retinol-complex-1-0]] is a pricier ($96) retinol serum whose own percentage is undisclosed by its manufacturer; [[trader-joes-retinol-night-serum]] (about $10) and [[anua-nano-retinol-0-3-niacin-renewing-serum]] are both retinol serums declared at 0.3%, a form that needs an extra conversion step to reach retinoic acid; [[differin-adapalene-0-1-gel]] is an FDA-cleared over-the-counter retinoid with real acne trials, about $15; and [[tretinoin-0-025-cream]] is prescription retinoic acid, the benchmark every over-the-counter retinoid, including this one, remains milder than.

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinaldehyde improves aging skin, and how it compares with retinol and prescription retinoids, is covered on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]: in short, one vehicle-controlled trial plus two independent concentration-comparison trials converge on measurable improvement in fine wrinkles, roughness, and texture, the strongest over-the-counter retinoid evidence, ahead of retinol's thinner data, though not shown to match prescription [[tretinoin]]. No published trial tests this exact serum, and no cited trial isolates the 0.03% concentration.[^dermstore]

NYT Wirecutter's own testing, which the outlet describes as 14 panelists using brand-concealed products over months, is an editorial and tester-experience review rather than a controlled trial. Its verdict on this serum was favorable: it called the active "considered to be the most effective retinoid sold over the counter," and its testers "tried, and praised, the 0.03% version." Testers singled out how it applied ("Fresh out of the pump, it looks like lotion, but then it massages into a sheer, silky layer") and its scent, noting it "has no added synthetic fragrance" yet was "lauded for its nice, subtle aroma."[^wirecutter] Wirecutter named price the main drawback: "At more than $60 an ounce, Medik8 Crystal Retinal 3 is expensive. And the cost creeps up along with the concentration level."[^wirecutter]

> **Bottom line.** Wirecutter's editorial pick and its testers' reports are a credible outside endorsement, but they are testimonial rather than clinical evidence.[^wirecutter]

Among over-the-counter retinoids, retinaldehyde's single enzymatic step to retinoic acid puts it a step ahead of the retinol forms, which need two, such as [[trader-joes-retinol-night-serum]] and [[anua-nano-retinol-0-3-niacin-renewing-serum]]; the FDA-cleared adapalene [[differin-adapalene-0-1-gel]] works by a different route, and it remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin-0-025-cream]] (retinoic acid itself).

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Retinal... works 11x faster than traditional retinol" / "more effective and 11x faster-working than retinol."** The retailer listings attribute this multiplier to three references, including the 1998 Creidi retinaldehyde-versus-retinoic-acid trial also cited on [[retinaldehyde]], but the specific "11x" figure does not appear in that trial's own reported results. The retinaldehyde evidence review already discounts this class of claim: the peer-reviewed data support that retinaldehyde ranks closer to active retinoic acid than retinol does, not a specific "times faster" multiple.[^dermstore][^spacenk]
- **"Clinically proven wrinkle reduction in 7 days," a claimed 20% reduction in dark spots, and an 11% increase in firmness.** Sourced by the retailer to "an independent clinical study on the Crystal Retinal ladder tested on 33 participants over 18 weeks," a small, unpublished study run across the whole six-strength ladder rather than isolating the 0.03% strength reviewed here.[^dermstore]
- **"94% agreed Crystal Retinal promoted visibly clearer skin" and "100% agreed Crystal Retinal was gentle on their skin."** Self-reported consumer-perception results from the same 33-participant ladder study, not a controlled trial outcome.[^dermstore]
