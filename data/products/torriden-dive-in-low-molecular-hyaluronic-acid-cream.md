Torriden Dive-In Low Molecular Hyaluronic Acid Cream is a South Korean humectant face moisturizer built around several low-molecular-weight forms of hyaluronic acid, sold in an 80 mL / 2.70 fl oz size.[^torriden]

> A low-molecular-weight hyaluronic-acid moisturizer whose hydrating case rests on the well-evidenced general role of hyaluronic acid and glycerin as humectants (see [[hyaluronic-acid]] and [[moisturizing]]); glycerin, a higher-tier humectant on [[moisturizing]]'s own evidence ranking, is listed third in the ingredient list, ahead of every hyaluronate form. [No published trial has tested this exact cream](#the-evidence); the brand's own percentage figures are not corroborated on an independently fetchable source and are quarantined below.

## Summary

Torriden Dive-In Low Molecular Hyaluronic Acid Cream is a K-beauty humectant moisturizer, the cream counterpart to the brand's [[torriden-dive-in-low-molecular-hyaluronic-acid-serum|Dive-In Serum]], sold in one 80 mL size. Torriden describes it as "a comfortable, daily moisturizing cream that balances moisturization and hydration without tightness using 5D complex low-molecular hyaluronic acid."[^torriden] Torriden's own US site lists it at $18.40, discounted from a $23.00 regular price, as of 2026-08-23.[^torriden]

Its declared ingredient list opens water, butylene glycol, glycerin, dipropylene glycol, caprylic/capric triglyceride, and 1,2-hexanediol, then five distinct hyaluronate forms: sodium hyaluronate, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, sodium acetylated hyaluronate, sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer, and hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate.[^torriden] As with the serum, two things follow from that order. First, [[glycerin]], a longer-established and higher-tier humectant on [[moisturizing]]'s own evidence ranking, sits ahead of every hyaluronate form, so glycerin is likely doing at least as much of the hydrating work as the headline low-molecular hyaluronic acid. Second, several of the named hyaluronate forms (hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, sodium acetylated hyaluronate, hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate) are the lower-molecular-weight, more processed kind that the general evidence on [[hyaluronic-acid]] shows can actually cross the stratum corneum, unlike the higher-molecular-weight hyaluronic acid used in many products that stays on the skin's surface. Further down the list, three plant oils (jojoba, macadamia, and argan) and, below those, [[ceramides|ceramide NP]], beta-glucan, and the soothing agents madecassoside and madecassic acid appear well below the humectant block, a minor share of the formula relative to the ingredients above them.[^torriden]

No published trial has tested this exact cream. Torriden's own product page cites brand-run percentage and agreement figures for moisture and non-irritation, but these are not corroborated on an independently fetchable second source, so they are reported as brand marketing claims rather than established findings ([see Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^torriden] Its case instead rests on the general evidence for hyaluronic acid and glycerin as humectants ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence), and [[hyaluronic-acid]], [[glycerin]], [[moisturizing]]).

## The Evidence

Hyaluronic acid and glycerin, applied topically as humectants, reliably raise a measured skin-hydration reading in controlled testing; the case for hyaluronic acid, including the molecular-weight distinction that determines whether it penetrates past the skin's outer layer, is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]], and glycerin's case is set out on [[glycerin]] and [[moisturizing]], where glycerin sits in a higher evidence tier than hyaluronic acid. This cream's declared ingredient list places glycerin third overall, ahead of every one of its five hyaluronate forms, so glycerin's well-evidenced humectant effect is at least as relevant to this formula as the headline low-molecular-weight hyaluronic-acid claim.[^torriden]

No published trial has tested this exact cream. What is specific to this formulation is its use of several lower-molecular-weight and chemically modified hyaluronate forms (hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, sodium acetylated hyaluronate, hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate) alongside standard sodium hyaluronate, the same "5D multi low-molecular hyaluronic acid" complex the brand uses across its Dive-In line, plus an emollient layer of jojoba, macadamia, and argan oils that the serum does not contain.[^torriden] That composition is directionally consistent with the general finding on [[hyaluronic-acid]] that only lower-molecular-weight forms cross the stratum corneum, but no independent study isolates this specific multi-form blend's own effect.

Torriden's own product page reports a "16.48% improved moisture content in the lower layer of the stratum corneum (inner moisturizing)" result from a 22-person clinical test, average results measured 4 weeks after use, a "100% agreed feels gentle/moisture/conditioning on the skin" figure from the self-assessment portion of that same 22-person test, and a non-irritation claim from a 50-person, 6-week human repeat insult patch test.[^torriden] None of these figures, sample sizes, or methods are published in a peer-reviewed or independently verifiable source. They are reported below as manufacturer marketing claims, not graded evidence.

> **Bottom line.** No independent study has isolated this cream's own effect. Its case rests on the general evidence for hyaluronic acid and glycerin as humectants, applied to a formula that leads with glycerin and layers in several low-molecular-weight hyaluronate forms plus a modest plant-oil emollient blend; the brand's own percentage and agreement figures are single-sourced, brand-run results, not independently corroborated findings.
