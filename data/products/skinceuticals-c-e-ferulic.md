SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic is a leave-on morning vitamin C serum, a fixed blend of 15% L-ascorbic acid (pure vitamin C), 1% alpha-tocopherol (vitamin E), and 0.5% ferulic acid in a low-pH base.[^1]

> A daily morning antioxidant serum. Vitamin C is a well-evidenced antioxidant, and this is one of the oldest and most-studied ways to wear it: used under sunscreen it helps defend skin against everyday UV and pollution, and over months it supports brighter, more even, firmer-looking skin. Skin doctors rate it highly and reviewers call it the original of its kind; the recurring criticism is the price, since cheaper serums use the same actives. The antioxidant case is set out on [[vitamin-c]], and the value question in [Cheaper Alternatives](#cheaper-alternatives).[^8][^9]

## Summary

C E Ferulic is a morning antioxidant serum. Its job is to sit under sunscreen and neutralize the free radicals that everyday UV and pollution generate, a protective, health-oriented role rather than a quick cosmetic fix. The active doing the work is [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]], the pure and best-studied form of vitamin C: a well-evidenced antioxidant that also helps even out tone and supports collagen, mapped with the other forms on [[vitamin-c]]. What sets this product apart is the delivery rather than a special active. It is one of the oldest and most-studied vitamin C formulas, a fixed 15% vitamin C, 1% vitamin E, and 0.5% ferulic acid blend that [the published photoprotection studies](#the-evidence) actually tested.

Wirecutter describes applying a vitamin C serum "every morning" and "after cleansing and before moisturizer and sunscreen."[^9] The antioxidant is meant to work alongside daily sunscreen rather than in place of it: the antioxidant and the UV filter do different jobs, so the serum is meant to help sunscreen work harder while the sunscreen blocks the rays. The brightening and firming benefits are gradual, building over months of consistent use.

On reputation, it is a genuine favorite among skin doctors. Forbes Vetted names it the overall best vitamin C serum and "a tried-and-true favorite in the industry," recommended by board-certified dermatologist Dr. Dendy Engelman.[^8] Wirecutter calls it a "category-defining classic," and the cosmetic formulators at Chemist Confessions call it "the original patented formula."[^9][^6] The recurring criticism is the price. ## The Evidence

Topical L-ascorbic acid is a well-studied antioxidant with characterized roles as a collagen-synthesis cofactor and, paired with vitamin E, in defending skin against oxidative UV damage; the vitamin C forms and how well each is evidenced are mapped on [[vitamin-c]], and the reference form's profile is [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]]. What is specific to this product is the ferulic-acid-stabilized combination of vitamins C and E and the photoprotection studies behind it.

Pinnell 2001 established the formulation rules the product follows. In percutaneous absorption studies, "L-ascorbic acid must be formulated at pH levels less than 3.5 to enter the skin," the maximal concentration for optimal absorption was 20%, tissue levels were saturated after three daily applications, and the tissue half-life was about 4 days.[^2]

> **Bottom line.** For L-ascorbic acid to penetrate skin, the serum has to be acidic (below pH 3.5). Pinnell found optimal absorption around 20%; this product uses 15%.[^2]

Lin 2005 tested the combination on pig skin. Adding 0.5% ferulic acid to a solution of 15% L-ascorbic acid and 1% alpha-tocopherol "doubled photoprotection to solar-simulated irradiation of skin from 4-fold to approximately 8-fold as measured by both erythema and sunburn cell formation," and the three-antioxidant formulation "efficiently reduced thymine dimer formation." The study used a small number of weanling Yorkshire pigs (swine skin). Its disclosure states that Sheldon Pinnell was a consultant for SkinCeuticals, the work was supported by a US National Institutes of Health small-business grant, and the stabilized formula is covered by a Zielinski and Pinnell patent.[^3]

> **Bottom line.** In pig skin, adding ferulic acid roughly doubled the measured protection against solar-simulated light and blocked a UV DNA marker. The study was small and was run by the formula's inventors, who disclosed a commercial tie to SkinCeuticals.[^3]

Murray 2008 applied the same 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% alpha-tocopherol, and 0.5% ferulic acid formula (labeled CEFer) to human skin for four days before solar-simulated UV exposure. "CEFer provided significant and meaningful photoprotection for skin by all methods of evaluation" (erythema, sunburn cells, thymine dimers, p53, and cytokines), and the authors reported it was "particularly effective for reducing thymine dimer mutations known to be associated with skin cancer." The listed limitation was that "the number of patients evaluated was relatively small." Sheldon Pinnell of Duke University was the senior author.[^4]

> **Bottom line.** A small human study found the formula reduced the same UV surrogate markers in living skin. It measured redness, sunburn cells, and DNA markers, not long-term photoaging or skin cancer, and shared the inventor's authorship.[^4]

These endpoints are health-oriented: UV-induced redness reflects inflammation and sunburn, sunburn cells are UV-damaged keratinocytes, and thymine dimers are a form of UV DNA damage the authors tied to skin-cancer risk.[^3][^4] For the product's cosmetic anti-aging and brightening positioning, the published studies measured UV-protection markers rather than appearance; the general evidence that topical vitamin C can brighten and support collagen sits on [[vitamin-c]], not in a trial of this formula.

## What's In It

The active antioxidants declared by the manufacturer, and the base as listed in the ingredient declaration.[^1] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the solvent, humectant, and preservative system.

Antioxidant actives:

- [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]] 15%, pure vitamin C
- [[tocopherol|alpha-tocopherol]] 1%, vitamin E
- [[ferulic-acid]] 0.5%, a plant antioxidant that stabilizes the vitamin C and vitamin E[^3]

Base, solvent, and preservative system: water, ethoxydiglycol (a penetration solvent), [[glycerin]] and propylene glycol (humectants), laureth-23 (a surfactant), phenoxyethanol (preservative), triethanolamine (pH adjuster), panthenol, and sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]).[^1]

Two composition facts matter for this product. The serum is acidic by design: L-ascorbic acid only penetrates skin below about pH 3.5, and the research formula's vehicle was set to pH 3.[^2][^3] The manufacturer's current US version is an "enhanced" formula that it states is "now boosted with 0.3% Carnosine and 1.0% Endophytol"; the published photoprotection studies used the classic combination without these two additions.[^1][^3][^4]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"8x" or "advanced environmental protection."** The roughly 8-fold figure is from pig skin (Lin 2005); a small human study (Murray 2008) reported protection on surrogate markers. Both were run by the formula's inventors, and neither measured photoaging or skin cancer.[^3][^4]
- **A manufacturer anti-aging positioning and a stated "41% reduction in oxidative damage."** These are performance claims; the independent published endpoints are UV surrogate markers, not clinical aging outcomes.[^1][^3][^4]
- **"Enhanced formula boosted with 0.3% Carnosine and 1.0% Endophytol."** A manufacturer composition and marketing claim.[^1] The photoprotection studies used the classic 15% C, 1% E, 0.5% ferulic acid formula.[^3][^4]
