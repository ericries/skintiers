A silicone gel that dries into a thin film over raw skin after a resurfacing procedure. **Bottom line:** there are two small trials, the better one is randomised and does show less redness than Aquaphor, and the headline marketing claim is not the thing either trial measured. For the aftercare decision this sits inside, see [[post-ablative-laser-aftercare]].

> **The product is FDA-listed, not FDA-cleared, and that is normal here rather than sinister.** Stratacel is listed with FDA under product code NAD, "Dressing, Wound, Occlusive", by Stratpharma AG of Switzerland.[^listing] That code sits at 21 CFR 878.4020, which is class I and "exempt from the premarket notification procedures in part 807, subpart E of this chapter subject to the limitations in § 878.9".[^cfr] So there is no 510(k) file to read. Searches of openFDA's 510(k) index for applicant "Stratpharma" and for device name "Stratacel" both return no matches.[^listing] No regulator reviewed any claim on this page.

## Summary

**What it is.** The brand calls it "an advanced, flexible, film-forming primary wound dressing for faster recovery of damaged or compromised skin", for use after fractional ablative and non-ablative resurfacing, microneedling and superficial chemical peels.[^brand] Application is "Apply a very thin layer and allow to dry for 5–6 minutes."[^brand]

**What is in it.** Stratpharma states the composition only in general terms, not as an ordered INCI list: "All Stratpharma products are 100% silicones in the general forms as stated in our packaging. For example, Stratacel contains polydimethylsiloxanes, siloxanes and alkylmethyl silicones. Stratacel contains no alcohols, parabens or fragrances and it is odorless."[^faq] Asked whether it contains sun protection, the brand answers plainly: "No."[^faq] Incidecoder does not index it, so no ordered ingredient list was obtainable.

**No price.** Stratpharma's shop gates every product behind a shipping-country selection and returned no figure, and no third-party listing was fetched. The page ships without a price rather than with a guessed one.

## The Rubric

**Reducing redness in the first week after fractional microneedling or laser resurfacing (health)**
Graded modest on preliminary evidence. The 2019 trial is the good one: randomised, single-blinded, crossover, 20 subjects, Stratacel against Aquaphor after full-face fractional radiofrequency microneedling, with "Erythema at each study visit was significantly reduced with the use of the novel wound dressing (P < 0.001)."[^gold] Modest rather than notable because one 20-person single-site trial is where this evidence begins and ends.

**"Reduces downtime by half", the brand's headline claim (health)**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. This is the one quantified promise on the product's front page, and following the brand's own footnote leads to the 2019 microneedling trial, which measured erythema and patient-rated product feel.[^brand][^gold] An erythema score is not a downtime duration, and no halving appears in either abstract.

**Bacteriostatic protection of the healing surface (health)**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. The claim is a bullet with nothing behind it on the brand's own page, and neither trial looked at infection.

## The Evidence

Two trials, and both are small.

**The randomised one, 2019.** Gold, Biron and Thompson ran a randomised, single-blinded crossover study in 20 subjects after full-face fractional radiofrequency microneedling, comparing Stratacel with Aquaphor applied immediately after the procedure and daily, assessed at baseline, 2, 3 and 7 days.[^gold] Erythema was significantly reduced at every visit (P < 0.001), and "A statistically significant difference in favor of the innovative wound dressing also emerged with respect to the patient-rated product properties (P = 0.008), such as feel on skin, drying time and stickiness."[^gold] All patients healed without adverse reactions to either product.[^gold] The authors' affiliation is the Tennessee Clinical Research Center in Nashville; the abstract carries no funding or conflict statement, and only the abstract was read, as the full text is paywalled.[^gold]

**The five-person one, 2018.** A split-face pilot after full-face fractional CO2 resurfacing, Stratacel against spring thermal water plus Vaseline, with instrument surrogates rather than healing time, five volunteers, and a single author who directs the private clinic where it ran. It is set out in full on [[post-ablative-laser-aftercare]] and not repeated here.[^marini]

**One thing worth noticing about who ran them.** Stratacel's own website carries endorsements attributed to "Dr. Micheal Gold" of Nashville, TN and to "Dr. Leonardo Marini" of Trieste, Italy.[^brand] Those are the surnames and cities of the first authors of the 2019 trial and the 2018 pilot respectively.[^gold][^marini] The brand states no payment and none was found; what is verifiable is that the entire published evidence base for this product comes from two investigators who also appear in its marketing. That does not make the randomised result wrong. It does mean there is no independent replication of it.

**What the general case rests on.** Whether moist occlusive dressing beats open-air petrolatum after resurfacing at all is a question about the category, not this product, and it belongs to [[post-ablative-laser-aftercare]] and [[fractional-co2-laser-resurfacing]].

## Manufacturer Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven unless it also appears in The Evidence above. All six benefit bullets below are quoted verbatim from Stratacel's own site and are the brand's claims, not findings.[^brand]

- **"Reduces downtime by half"**. Discount as stated. It is the only bullet carrying a reference marker, and the reference is the 2019 microneedling trial, which measured erythema and patient-rated product feel rather than downtime duration.[^brand][^gold]
- **"Reduces inflammation, redness and heat sensation"**. Partly supported, for redness only. Erythema was significantly reduced against Aquaphor at every visit in the 2019 trial (P < 0.001).[^gold] Inflammation and heat sensation were not separately measured in either trial abstract.
- **"Bacteriostatic wound dressing"**. Discount. No reference marker, no entry on the brand's reference page, and no microbiological endpoint in either trial.
- **"Can be used immediately post-procedure"**. Consistent with how both trials applied it, immediately after the procedure and then daily.[^gold][^marini]
- **"Can be covered with sunscreen or cosmetics"**. The brand's own instruction is to wait until the film is dry, and the brand separately confirms the product itself contains no sun protection.[^brand][^faq]
- **"Easy to use"**. Not an efficacy claim. The 2019 trial does report patient-rated properties including feel on skin, drying time and stickiness favouring it over Aquaphor (P = 0.008).[^gold]
