Dr.G Green Mild Up Sun+ SPF50+ PA++++ is a South Korean daily facial sunscreen from Dr.G (Gowoonsesang Cosmetics) built on a single mineral UV filter, zinc oxide, with no titanium dioxide or organic filters in the formula.[^yesstyle]

> A single-filter mineral (zinc oxide) sunscreen rated SPF50+ PA++++, and broad-spectrum protection is the best-evidenced way to lower photoaging and skin-cancer risk (see [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]). Zinc oxide alone gives it real, photostable UVB and UVA coverage, but as the sole filter it is unlikely to match the very top independent UVA scores that modern organic- or hybrid-filter Korean sunscreens reach. The SPF50+ and PA++++ figures are Korean-regulatory label claims, not FDA-tested, and the product reaches US buyers as an import.[^yesstyle][^stylevana]

## Summary

Green Mild Up Sun+ is a daily facial sunscreen from the Korean brand Dr.G, marketed for sensitive skin. Its core purpose is health rather than cosmetics: broad-spectrum UV protection is the best-evidenced way to reduce photoaging and lower the risk of sun-related skin cancer, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

What distinguishes this formula is its filter set. The ingredient list shows zinc oxide as the only UV filter, with no titanium dioxide and none of the modern organic UVA filters (bisoctrizole, ethylhexyl triazone, and similar) found in some other Korean sunscreens.[^yesstyle] Zinc oxide is a well-characterized mineral filter with genuine broad-spectrum coverage and photostability, covered in full on [[zinc-oxide]]; as a single filter it is a real but not the strongest UVB absorber, and its UVA reach, while the broadest of the mineral filters, typically falls short of the top scores modern organic UVA filters achieve (the filter comparison is in [The Evidence](#the-evidence)).

The label pairs SPF50+, the top of the capped Korean/EU UVB scale, with PA++++, the top tier of the graded UVA (PPD) scale.[^yesstyle][^stylevana] No independent lab test of this specific product was found, so those ratings are Korean-regulatory label claims rather than FDA-tested measurements, and the product reaches US buyers as an import rather than an FDA-reviewed OTC drug.[^yesstyle]

The marketed formula also lists [[centella-asiatica]] and Houttuynia cordata extracts, positioned by the retailer listing as calming ingredients for sensitive or irritated skin.[^yesstyle] It is sold in the US mainly through Korean-import retailers; a 2-pack (50 ml x2) is listed at Stylevana for $57.99, and a set (50 ml + 20 ml) is listed at YesStyle for $32.00 before discount, both accessed 2026-08-07, putting a single 50 ml unit at roughly $29.[^stylevana][^yesstyle]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and for reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and PA/UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. What is specific to this product is its single-filter mineral composition and its Korean label ratings. It is a cosmetic sunscreen sold in Korea and imported to the US, not an FDA-reviewed OTC drug.[^yesstyle]

The ingredient list carries only one UV filter, zinc oxide, with water as the base and no titanium dioxide or organic UV filter of any kind.[^yesstyle] Zinc oxide's own UVB and UVA performance, covered in full on [[zinc-oxide]], is real but not top-of-class in either band: it is "a real UVB absorber, though titanium dioxide and the organic UVB filters are more efficient in that band," and while it is "the broadest, most photostable single mineral filter in long-wave UVA," a single mineral filter's absolute UVA protection depends on particle size, loading, and dispersion, and the modern organic UVA filters used in some other Korean sunscreens (bemotrizinol, bisoctrizole, the Mexoryls) can reach higher UVA protection in a cosmetically lighter formula. No independent lab test (such as the Consumer Reports comparison run on other Korean sunscreen imports) of this specific SKU was found in the sources fetched for this page.

> **Bottom line.** SPF50+ PA++++ is a Korean-regulatory label pairing of a top UVB rating and the top UVA (PPD) grade; the formula earns that rating from zinc oxide alone, a real and photostable but not top-tier mineral UV filter, with no product-specific independent lab test found to confirm the label figures.[^yesstyle]

This product's single-filter, zinc-oxide-only approach differs from the hybrid and organic-filter formulas of other Korean sunscreens on the site: [[beauty-of-joseon-relief-sun-rice-probiotics-spf50]] pairs a high SPF with two modern organic UVA filters (bisoctrizole and diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate) and scored a top independent UVA rating in a Consumer Reports lab test, while [[eltamd-uv-clear-spf-46]] uses a 9% zinc oxide load in a US-market formula graded modest for UVA on that page.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded UV evidence above:

- **"Antipollution + electronic protection complex," protection "against blue light from electrical appliances such as Smartphones and monitors."** A Stylevana listing for the product makes this claim.[^stylevana] The randomized clinical-endpoint evidence on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] concerns ultraviolet radiation, not visible or blue light, so this is unproven for the photoaging and skin-cancer outcomes graded there.
- **"100% organic UV protection... using a mild mineral filter."** The same Stylevana listing pairs the word "organic" with a description of the product as mineral-filtered; the ingredient list confirms a single mineral filter, zinc oxide, and no organic UV filter, so "organic" here does not describe the filter chemistry.[^stylevana][^yesstyle]
- **Calming/sensitive-skin positioning ("hypoallergenic," "mild yet powerful," "calm irritated skin," "supports weak skin," "skin irritation test completed").** A YesStyle listing attributes this framing to centella asiatica, Houttuynia cordata, and pycnogenol in the formula and to a completed skin-irritation test.[^yesstyle] Centella asiatica's own evidence for soothing and barrier support is covered on [[centella-asiatica]].
- **"Non-sticky," "matte finish without any white cast," "soft cream texture with a moisturizing finish."** Texture and finish claims from the YesStyle listing, not independently verified here.[^yesstyle]
