The reVive Light Therapy Wrinkle and Acne LED Device is a small over-the-counter LED unit from LED Technologies, Inc. of Largo, Florida, cleared by the FDA in December 2022 under 510(k) number K223482. Its cleared indication, quoted in full from the FDA's own Indications for Use form, is that it "is intended for treatment of wrinkles and mild to moderate inflammatory acne".[^fda] The device category and its evidence are on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

> **The colours are the right ones. The clearance is not evidence that this device works.** Its acne mode runs 415nm and 630nm, which is the blue plus red combination that a 2026 GRADE review of home devices placed at Level B with a Grade I recommendation, the best grade any home acne device received.[^home] But read what this particular clearance contains. It is a **Special 510(k)**, its two predicates are the company's **own earlier devices**, and the summary states that "the only modification introduced is the updated software and associated hardware" with all other features "identical to that of the cleared devices".[^fda] The testing section describes **software validation**, not a clinical trial.[^fda] So "FDA cleared" here means this unit is equivalent to the maker's previous unit. It does not mean anyone measured a wrinkle or counted a pimple.

## Summary

This is a handheld over-the-counter LED unit with two modes: a wrinkle mode at 605nm, 630nm, 660nm and 880nm, and an acne mode at 415nm and 630nm.[^fda] It is placed in contact with the skin over a 7 cm2 treatment window for 3 minutes per placement, runs on a battery or USB, and its housing is ABS plastic.[^fda] Its FDA-cleared indication covers wrinkles and mild to moderate inflammatory acne.[^fda] What the clearance does NOT contain is any clinical efficacy study, because it is a Special 510(k) against the company's own earlier devices whose only change was software.[^fda] The evidence that applies to it is therefore category-level evidence about blue and red LED, which is set out on [[home-led-light-therapy]] rather than repeated here.

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate inflammatory acne**
- **Effect size: modest, and the grade belongs to the light, not to this box.** The acne mode's 415nm and 630nm is exactly the blue-red LED combination that the 2026 GRADE review of home-use devices graded Level B, "moderate quality evidence", with "favorable safety profiles and Grade I recommendations".[^home][^fda] That is the strongest grade in that review's acne section. It is a judgement about a modality studied across many devices, and nothing in this clearance tests this one.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary for the device, mixed at the category level.** The clearance contains no efficacy study.[^fda] Behind the category grade sits a literature that the Cochrane review of light for acne called small and conflicted, a caveat carried on [[home-led-light-therapy]] rather than repeated here.

**Wrinkles**
- **Effect size: minimal.** LED reached Level B for facial rejuvenation in the same review, but behind home fractional nonablative lasers at "high-quality evidence (Level A)".[^home] The device offers 605nm, 630nm, 660nm and 880nm in its wrinkle mode.[^fda] No source consulted here reports what those wavelengths achieved on this device.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** Same gap as above, with the added problem that "treatment of wrinkles" is a broad claim and the clearance quantifies nothing.

**Covering a whole face**
- **Effect size: not applicable; this is a form-factor limit, not an efficacy question.** The cleared treatment area is **7 cm²**, the method is "Place device directly on the skin", and the treatment time is **3 minutes**.[^fda] That is a spot-treatment footprint, roughly a large postage stamp, and it is applied in contact rather than held at a distance. Anyone picturing a mask that covers the face in one 3-minute session is picturing a different product.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable; these are the manufacturer's own declared specifications in the 510(k) summary.**

## The Evidence

**What a Special 510(k) actually is.** The FDA letter says the agency "determined the device is substantially equivalent (for the indications for use stated in the enclosure) to legally marketed predicate devices", and openFDA records the clearance type as Special and the decision as "Substantially Equivalent".[^fda][^openfda] The two predicates named are K210965 and K210968, both listed as "The reVive Light Therapy® Essentials (LED Technologies, Inc.)".[^fda] A clearance of this shape asks whether a changed device matches a device already on the market. It does not ask whether either of them works.

**The FDA says so itself, in the letter.** The same page states that "FDA's issuance of a substantial equivalence determination does not mean that FDA has made a determination that your device complies with other requirements of the Act", and closes the paragraph with "We remind you, however, that device labeling must be truthful and not misleading".[^fda] That is the agency drawing the line between clearance and endorsement, in its own words.

**What was tested.** The summary's entire testing section reports that the device software "was tested and validated in accordance with" the FDA guidance on software contained in medical devices, and that this testing "confirmed that modifications made to the device were correctly implemented and that the device performs as well as the legally marketed devices".[^fda] Performing as well as the predicate is a comparison between two devices, not a measurement on skin.

**The wavelengths are the genuinely encouraging part.** The comparison chart separates the modes: wrinkle treatment at 605nm, 630nm, 660nm and 880nm, acne treatment at 415nm and 630nm.[^fda] Blue light sits around 415nm and red around 630nm, so the acne mode is the blue-red pairing rather than red alone, and red alone is the configuration the GRADE review dropped to "low-quality evidence (Level C)".[^home] Whatever this clearance fails to show, the device is at least built in the better-evidenced configuration.

**One documentation oddity, recorded for anyone checking the source.** The 510(k) Summary document is headed "K223842", while the clearance letter, the Indications for Use form and the FDA database all use **K223482**.[^fda][^openfda] The digits are transposed in the summary's own title. Search the database for K223482.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The cleared indication stops at mild to moderate inflammatory acne; acne that is scarring, nodular or severe is a reason to see a dermatologist rather than to buy a device.

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand marketing copy could be retrieved for this page, because the brand domain no longer serves the brand (see Where to Buy). The claims below are therefore the regulatory language itself and the one phrase that travels with every device in this category. Each is recorded here with what the FDA documents actually support.

- **"FDA cleared"**. The most common claim made for home LED devices, and the most misunderstood. For this device it means the FDA "determined the device is substantially equivalent (for the indications for use stated in the enclosure) to legally marketed predicate devices", where the predicates were the company's own earlier units.[^fda] The agency states in the same letter that this "does not mean that FDA has made a determination that your device complies with other requirements of the Act".[^fda] Clearance is a market-entry decision. Whether the device works is a separate question, and this package does not answer it.
- **"Treatment of wrinkles and mild to moderate inflammatory acne"**. This is the cleared indication verbatim, so it is legitimate labelling.[^fda] It describes what the device may be sold for, not a measured result, and the qualifier "mild to moderate" is doing real work.
- **Claims about specific wavelengths**. The wavelengths are documented in the clearance and are accurate as specifications.[^fda] A wavelength is an engineering fact, and quoting one is not the same as showing a clinical outcome.

## Where to Buy

**No price is stated on this page because none was verified, and the cleared device does not map cleanly onto anything currently sold.**

The manufacturer is trading. LED Technologies, Inc., the applicant named on the clearance, sells reVive Light Therapy products at ledtechnologies.com, which returned a live storefront when fetched on 2026-10-14. It is the separate domain revivelighttherapy.com that no longer serves the brand; that one returned an unrelated parked page in Polish.

What could not be confirmed is this device. The current reVive skincare line is built around the Lux Collection plus two spot treatments, and the only product carrying the predicate's "Essentials" name is **Essentials Acne**, described on the brand's own site as a blue-light acne spot treatment. That matches the acne-only predicate rather than the dual wrinkle-and-acne device cleared under K223482, whose wrinkle mode adds 605nm, 660nm and 880nm.[^fda] No current product was found under the cleared device's name.

So treat the clearance as evidence about a device, not as a guide to what is on sale. If you are buying, match the product's own stated wavelengths and indication against the clearance above rather than trusting the brand name alone.
