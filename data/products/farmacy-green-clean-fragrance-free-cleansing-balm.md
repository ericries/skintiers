The Farmacy Green Clean Fragrance-Free Cleansing Balm is the fragrance-free version of the brand's [[farmacy-green-clean-makeup-removing-cleansing-balm|Green Clean balm]], an oil-to-milk first cleanse aimed at sensitive skin. Farmacy lists the 100 ml jar at $39.00.[^farmacy]

> This is the same oil-to-milk cleansing balm as the original Green Clean, with the one change that matters for reactive skin: it drops the citrus and essential-oil panel that functions as fragrance in the original. Cleansing esters and sunflower oil dissolve long-wear makeup and water-resistant sunscreen, and polyglyceryl emulsifiers let it rinse off. The general evidence that an oil-based first cleanse removes what a water wash leaves behind is a single small study, set out on [[double-cleansing]], and no published trial covers this exact product; the fragrance-free formula is its real advantage over the original.

## The Evidence

There is no independently published trial of this specific product. Its job, dissolving oil-based makeup and water-resistant sunscreen as a first cleanse, is the oil-cleansing mechanism whose controlled evidence (a single small study) is set out on [[double-cleansing]]; it cannot exceed what that format has shown. Farmacy's "clinically proven to gently remove over 99% longwear foundation, waterproof mascara + SPF" is the brand's own clinical-test result, not an independently published trial, and "over 99% removal" is a cleansing-efficiency figure, not a skin-health outcome.[^farmacy]

What is specific and verifiable here is the fragrance-free reformulation. The original Green Clean carries a citrus and essential-oil panel (lime, bergamot, orange peel, ginger, ylang-ylang) that functions as fragrance and is a plausible sensitizer; this version omits it, which is its sole difference from the cheaper, more widely stocked original and the reason a fragrance-free version exists: reactive, eczema-prone, or eye-area-sensitive skin.

> **Bottom line.** The fragrance-free Green Clean (100 ml, $39.00).[^farmacy] It cleanses like the original oil-to-milk balm, on evidence that is a single small study for the format and a brand clinical-test claim for this product; the one real difference is that it drops the essential-oil fragrance, the fragrance-free alternative for reactive, eczema-prone, or eye-area-sensitive skin. The general oil-cleansing case is on [[double-cleansing]].

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), as decoded by incidecoder: Cetyl Ethylhexanoate, Helianthus Annuus (Sunflower) Seed Oil, Polyglyceryl-6 Dicaprate, Helianthus Annuus (Sunflower) Seed Wax, Polyglyceryl-10 Dioleate, Stearyl Behenate, C10-18 Triglycerides, Polyhydroxystearic Acid, Moringa Oleifera Seed Oil, Ocimum Sanctum Leaf Extract, Eruca Sativa Leaf Extract, Carica Papaya (Papaya) Fruit Extract, Linum Usitatissimum (Linseed) Seed Oil, Brassica Campestris (Rapeseed) Seed Oil, Sorbitan Sesquioleate, Glyceryl Laurate, Tocopherol, Lecithin.

Notable: cetyl ethylhexanoate (an emollient ester) and sunflower seed oil are the makeup-dissolving base; the polyglyceryl entries (polyglyceryl-6 dicaprate, polyglyceryl-10 dioleate) are the emulsifiers that let the balm rinse off. The plant extracts are the brand's "super greens," including moringa seed oil, holy basil (Ocimum sanctum), arugula (Eruca sativa), and papaya fruit extract. Crucially, there is no fragrance, parfum, or citrus and essential-oil panel of the kind in the fragranced [[farmacy-green-clean-makeup-removing-cleansing-balm|original]], consistent with the fragrance-free claim.
