Paula's Choice Pro-Collagen Multi-Peptide Booster (formerly Peptide Booster) is a fragrance-free, water-based serum built on a genuinely high concentration of eight signal and carrier peptides, marketed to firm skin and soften fine lines and wrinkles.[^inci]

> This is the honest version of a "multi-peptide" serum: eight peptides really do sit high on the ingredient list, fourth through eleventh, including the Matrixyl 3000 pair, so it is not a fairy-dusted trace formula like many peptide products.[^inci] What limits it is the ingredient class, not the formulation. Topical peptides have only modest, mostly small-study evidence for wrinkles and firmness, and skin penetration is the recurring question (covered on [[peptides]]). Judge it as a well-built peptide serum whose ceiling is set by how much topical peptides can actually do, and treat "eight peptides" as formulation, not proof.

## Summary

The Booster's formula is unusually peptide-dense. After water, glycerin, and a silicone slip agent, the next eight ingredients are peptides: tripeptide-1, palmitoyl tripeptide-1, palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7 (the last two together are the trademarked Matrixyl 3000), palmitoyl hexapeptide-12, myristoyl hexapeptide-16, myristoyl pentapeptide-17, hexanoyl dipeptide-3 norleucine acetate, and azelaoyl bis-dipeptide-10.[^inci] It also carries glycoproteins, phospholipids, [[adenosine]], soothing dipotassium glycyrrhizate, a spread of free amino acids, [[ectoin]], and ginseng extract, and is formulated alcohol-free and fragrance-free.[^inci]

Where this differs from trace-peptide serums (for example [[the-ordinary-argireline-solution-10|The Ordinary's single-peptide Argireline serum]]) is that the peptides are clearly a large share of the formula, not a label flourish. What it shares with every topical peptide product is the evidence ceiling: the case for peptides softening wrinkles and firming skin is real but modest, built largely on small and often manufacturer-linked studies, and complicated by how little of a large peptide molecule crosses intact skin. That general evidence lives on [[peptides]] and is not repeated here.

## The Evidence

No independent, controlled trial of this specific serum was located; its case rests on its peptide content plus the general peptide evidence.

**Fine lines, wrinkles, and firmness.** The formulation is a genuine strength: eight peptides high on the list is far more than the single, often trace, peptide in most "peptide" serums, and the Matrixyl 3000 pair is the most-studied cosmetic peptide combination.[^inci] But more peptides is not the same as more proven benefit. The topical-peptide evidence base, set out on [[peptides]], is modest and penetration-limited, and no data shows that combining eight peptides produces an additive or synergistic wrinkle effect over a well-chosen one or two. The supporting cast (glycerin, ectoin, amino acids, dipotassium glycyrrhizate) makes it a comfortable, hydrating, soothing serum regardless.[^inci]

> **Bottom line.** One of the more legitimately peptide-loaded serums on the market, well formulated and gentle, whose real-world effect on lines and firmness is bounded by what topical peptides can do at all, which is modest.[^inci] A reasonable pick if you want a peptide serum and prefer a dense multi-peptide formula; not evidence that eight peptides beat one. The full peptide evidence picture is on [[peptides]].
