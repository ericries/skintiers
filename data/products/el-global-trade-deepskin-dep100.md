A home microcurrent handheld cleared in May 2026, made by the contract manufacturer behind several shelf brands.[^openfda] **Bottom line:** its cleared indication is narrower than anything a microcurrent device is usually sold on, and its own file says plainly that no clinical testing was done. The applicant's other work on this site is at [[el-global-trade]] and [[currentbody-skin-rf]].

> **The word wrinkle appears nowhere in it.** Checked by string search of the full ten-page summary, the document contains no occurrence of wrinkle, and none of lifting, toning or firming either. What it claims is that "The DEP100 device is indicated for facial stimulation for over-the-counter cosmetic use", and separately that it is "intended for the stimulation of healthy facial skin".[^k252621] Microcurrent devices are advertised on lift and contour. This one is cleared to stimulate.

## Summary

Cleared 2026-05-08 as K252621 under product code NFO, which openFDA's classification index names "Stimulator, Transcutaneous Electrical, Aesthetic Purposes" at regulation 882.5890, Class II.[^openfda][^class] The filing describes "an OTC, home-use hand-held battery-powered device used with FDA cleared conductive gel to stimulate the face with microcurrents", non-invasive and non-ablative, built from a user interface, a programmable logic controller, a microcurrent power module, a power supply and microcurrent electrodes.[^k252621] Note the gel: the device is described as used WITH a separately cleared conductive gel, which is a running cost and a second product the file does not name. No price is given here.

## The Rubric

**Facial stimulation for cosmetic use**
- **Effect size: unknown.** That phrase is the whole of the cleared claim. The filing contains no wrinkle, lifting, toning or firming claim, and no outcome measure of any kind.[^k252621] A clearance to stimulate healthy skin permits the device on the market; it says nothing about what the stimulation does to the skin.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** The document states it directly under its own Clinical Performance Data heading: "No clinical testing was gathered for the subject device, as it is substantially equivalent to the predicate device, and thus requires no additional clinical data to prove its safety and efficacy".[^k252621] That is how substantial equivalence works, and it is also the reason a 2026 clearance can carry no evidence at all.

**What was measured on people, and what it measured**
- **Effect size: not applicable.** The only human study is a self-selection and human factors validation, in which "18 subjects who successfully self-selected themselves as eligible for treatment with the device, performed treatment", reaching "a validation rate of 100% success rate for all critical tasks, with no critical issues raised during use".[^k252621] The stated conclusion is that the design "is safe for use by the intended lay users".[^k252621]
- **Evidence quality: not applicable.** A 100% success rate here means eighteen people could follow the instructions. It is a usability result and the filing presents it as one.

**A claim the bench tests cannot carry**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a wording problem in the file.** The bench-test section says its data were provided "in support of the performance, safety, and efficacy of the DEP100 device".[^k252621] Bench testing can support performance and safety. Efficacy on a human face is not something a bench test is able to show, and the same document later confirms no clinical testing was gathered.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable.**

## The Evidence

**Its single predicate belongs to another company.** The file names one: "Facial Spa device by Nu Skin Enterprises Inc. Cleared under 510(k) number K122711 on September 17th, 2013".[^k252621] Note Nu Skin Enterprises, which is not the same company as NuFACE, whose device is covered at [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]]. A 2026 device resting its case on a 2013 clearance from an unrelated firm is the outward-anchored pattern this site documents across home devices.

**No animal testing, and sensitization testing was done.** The filing states "No animal testing was performed with the subject device", and records that sensitization tests were performed under its cleaning and biocompatibility heading.[^k252621] Those are the right tests for a device held against skin, and they speak to tolerability rather than benefit.

**Why it is indexed here.** Not because it is notable, but because it is ordinary. It is a current, fully documented example of a cleared home cosmetic device whose file contains a usability study, a bench-test list and an explicit statement that no clinical data exists. Read it next to [[silkn-blue]], whose 2013 filing did publish a clinical result, and the contrast is in what manufacturers choose to measure rather than in what regulators require.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The cleared indication is cosmetic stimulation of healthy facial skin, so nothing here applies to a skin condition, and the device is described as used on healthy skin with a separately cleared gel.

## Common Marketing Claims

> No brand copy is quoted on this page, because this clearance is the manufacturer's statement to the FDA rather than a consumer advertisement.

That gap is the thing worth watching. The filing claims facial stimulation and nothing else, while microcurrent devices reach shoppers through claims about lifting and contour. Where a shelf brand built on this clearance makes a firmer claim than the clearance does, the clearance is the document that was reviewed.
