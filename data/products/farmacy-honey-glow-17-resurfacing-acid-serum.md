The Farmacy Honey Glow 17% Resurfacing Acid Serum (formerly sold as Honeymoon Glow) is a leave-on alpha-hydroxy-acid night serum built around a 17% acid blend led by glycolic acid. Farmacy lists the 30 ml bottle at $60.00.[^farmacy]

> This is a glycolic-acid-led exfoliating serum: glycolic acid is the second ingredient, with lactic acid close behind, so its resurfacing works by the standard alpha-hydroxy-acid mechanism set out on [[glycolic-acid]]. Two honest notes: the "BHA" the brand pairs with the AHAs is willow bark extract, not true [[salicylic-acid]], and the "clinically proven to reduce fine lines and pores" claim is Farmacy's own testing, not an independent trial of this product. The honey, propolis, and royal jelly it is named for sit low in the formula and carry little standalone evidence.

## Summary

Farmacy markets this as "an AHA/BHA night serum that is clinically proven to reduce the look of fine lines and pores" that "reveals smooth, glowing skin by morning," at a stated 17% acid concentration.[^farmacy] The general evidence for alpha-hydroxy-acid exfoliation, and how to use acids without overdoing it, lives on [[glycolic-acid]] and [[lactic-acid]]; this page covers what is specific to this product. It ships as a 30 ml (1 oz) bottle for $60.00 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-10-02).[^farmacy]

## The Evidence

There is no independently published trial of this specific product. Its resurfacing job rests on the alpha-hydroxy acids high in the formula: glycolic acid (second on the list) and lactic acid, whose evidence for smoothing texture, exfoliating, and improving the look of pigmentation and fine lines is set out on [[glycolic-acid]] and [[lactic-acid]]. At a stated 17% total acid concentration it is a fairly strong leave-on exfoliant, so the usual acid cautions (irritation, sun sensitivity, not stacking with other strong actives) apply as covered on those pages.[^farmacy]

Three product-specific points are worth naming. First, the "BHA" in the AHA/BHA marketing is willow bark (Salix alba) extract, a plant source of salicin that is often marketed as a natural beta-hydroxy acid but is not the same as, and is weaker and less proven than, true [[salicylic-acid]]. Second, Farmacy's "clinically proven to reduce the look of fine lines and pores" is the brand's own claim, not an independent trial, and "reduce the look of" is an appearance claim. Third, the honey, propolis, and royal jelly behind the product's name sit low on the ingredient list and have little standalone human evidence; the work is done by the acids.[^farmacy]

> **Bottom line.** A $60, 30 ml glycolic-acid-led AHA exfoliating night serum (stated 17% acid).[^farmacy] It resurfaces by the well-evidenced alpha-hydroxy-acid mechanism on [[glycolic-acid]], but its "BHA" is willow bark rather than [[salicylic-acid]], its fine-line and pore claims are the brand's own testing, and the honey branding is cosmetic. Use it as you would any strong leave-on acid, with sunscreen by day.

## What's In It

The ingredient list (INCI), from Farmacy's own product page, begins: Water, Glycolic Acid, Vitis Vinifera (Grape) Seed Oil, Propanediol, Saccharomyces Ferment, Lactic Acid, C13-15 Alkane, Arginine, Inositol, Potassium Hydroxide, Carthamus Tinctorius (Safflower) Oleosomes, Isopentyldiol, Cetearyl Alcohol, Opuntia Ficus-Indica Stem Extract, Squalane, Salix Alba (Willow) Bark Extract, Vitis Vinifera (Grape) Fruit Extract, Honey Extract, Hyaluronic Acid, Propolis Extract, Royal Jelly Extract, Glycerin, Ubiquinone, Tocopherol, and others.[^farmacy]

Notable: the exfoliating actives are [[glycolic-acid]] (second) and lactic acid; arginine and potassium hydroxide partially neutralize the acids to buffer the pH. Willow bark extract is the marketed "BHA" (a natural salicin source, not true [[salicylic-acid]]). Squalane, hyaluronic acid, glycerin, ubiquinone (CoQ10), and tocopherol are supporting humectants and antioxidants, and the honey, propolis, and royal jelly are low-level botanicals behind the branding.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms attributed to the brand:

- **"Clinically proven to reduce the look of fine lines and pores."**[^farmacy] A brand clinical claim, not an independent trial; it is an appearance ("look of") claim, and the mechanism behind it is the alpha-hydroxy-acid exfoliation covered on [[glycolic-acid]].
- **"AHA/BHA" / "17% acid."**[^farmacy] The AHAs (glycolic, lactic) are real and high in the list; the "BHA" is willow bark extract rather than salicylic acid, and the 17% total-acid figure is the brand's stated concentration.
- **Honey, propolis, and royal jelly branding.**[^farmacy] These give the product its "honey" identity but sit low in the formula and are not the reason it resurfaces.
