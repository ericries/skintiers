Caudalie Vinopure Acne Serum is a French brand's leave-on acne treatment built around 0.5% salicylic acid in an essential-oil-scented formula, sold in a single 30 mL bottle for $49.[^1]

> The serum's only FDA-recognized acne active, salicylic acid, sits at 0.5%, the low end of the OTC monograph range (0.5% to 2%), and the American Academy of Dermatology recommends salicylic acid only conditionally for acne, ranking benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids above it (general evidence on [[salicylic-acid]]).[^1] The brand's headline "86% saw acne reduced after 7 days" claim comes from a small, company-run satisfaction survey, not a controlled trial (see [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^1]

## Summary

Vinopure Acne Serum (its current official name on Caudalie's site is "Vinopure Acne Serum to Treat and Prevent Blemishes") is a lightweight, essential-oil-scented leave-on treatment aimed at oily, blemish-prone skin.[^1] Its Drug Facts inactive ingredients are listed alphabetically, so their order does not indicate concentration; they include alcohol, a set of essential oils (lemongrass, lavandin, peppermint, melissa, geranium, rosemary) and their fragrance-allergen components (citral, citronellol, geraniol, limonene, linalool), sunflower seed oil, [[gluconolactone-pha|gluconolactone]] (a PHA), [[niacinamide]], Palmitoyl Grape Seed Extract, and [[sodium-hyaluronate|sodium hyaluronate]]; the declared active is salicylic acid at 0.5%.[^1]

Salicylic acid has genuine, guideline-recognized evidence as an acne treatment, but it is a second-tier option: the American Academy of Dermatology recommends it only conditionally, and the one randomized trial Cochrane could pool found it performed no better than [[tretinoin]], while guidelines rank benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids above it (general evidence on [[salicylic-acid]]). This product sits at the bottom of the FDA's approved 0.5%-to-2% concentration range, so it delivers a lower dose of that already second-tier active than a 2% product such as [[paulas-choice-skin-perfecting-2-bha|Paula's Choice Skin Perfecting 2% BHA Liquid Exfoliant]] ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)). No published trial isolates this exact formula; the brand's "86% saw acne reduced after 7 days" figure is a self-reported satisfaction result from 43 volunteers, not a placebo-controlled study ([see Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^1]

It is listed at $49 for the single 30 mL size on Caudalie's own US site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-23).[^1]

## The Evidence

No published trial was found that tests this product or its exact formula; the grades above rest on the general, category-level evidence for salicylic acid and niacinamide.

Salicylic acid's evidence as a topical acne active is set out on [[salicylic-acid]]: it is an FDA OTC-recognized active at 0.5% to 2%, with a genuine but second-tier case, the American Academy of Dermatology recommends it only conditionally, and the one randomized controlled comparison Cochrane could pool found it performed no better than [[tretinoin]] on participants' global improvement rating.[^1] This product uses 0.5%, the lowest concentration in the approved range, whereas the comparator product, [[paulas-choice-skin-perfecting-2-bha|Paula's Choice Skin Perfecting 2% BHA Liquid Exfoliant]], uses 2%, the top of the range. Neither product has its own published trial; the difference between them is concentration within the same evidenced active, not a difference in evidence base.

Niacinamide's general evidence, on [[niacinamide]], supports a modest cosmetic effect on skin tone and texture; this product does not disclose a concentration for it, and no product-specific trial exists.

> **Bottom line.** This serum delivers the FDA's own acne active at the low end of its approved range, an active the American Academy of Dermatology recommends only conditionally and ranks below benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids. No trial isolates this product; its 43-volunteer satisfaction survey is not a controlled comparison.[^1]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on the manufacturer's product page, in order.[^1] The actives with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest is the base, solvent, and fragrance system.

- Alcohol, listed first, the solvent base for this leave-on formula
- Butylene Glycol, a humectant, listed third
- A cluster of essential oils, Cymbopogon Citratus (Lemongrass) Leaf Oil, Lavandula Hybrida (Lavandin) Oil, Mentha Piperita (Peppermint) Oil, Melissa Officinalis Leaf Oil, Pelargonium Graveolens (Geranium) Flower Oil, and Rosmarinus Officinalis (Rosemary) Leaf Oil, plus the associated declared fragrance allergens Citral, Citronellol, Geraniol, Limonene, and Linalool
- [[gluconolactone-pha|Gluconolactone]], a polyhydroxy acid (PHA)
- [[niacinamide]], for skin tone and texture
- Palmitoyl Grape Seed Extract, a proprietary grape-derived extract tied to Caudalie's vinotherapy branding; no dedicated independent evidence page exists for it here
- Rosa Damascena (Rose) Flower Water
- Sodium Hyaluronate, a humectant, a salt form of [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]]
- **Active: Salicylic Acid 0.5%**, declared as the FDA OTC acne-treatment active

Base and preservative system: Aqua/Water/Eau, Citric Acid, Sodium Citrate, Sodium Hydroxide, Sodium Phytate, Triethyl Citrate, [[tocopherol|Tocopherol]], Xanthan Gum.[^1]

The declared list includes five separately named fragrance-allergen components (citral, citronellol, geraniol, limonene, linalool) from the essential-oil blend.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"86% saw acne reduced after 7 days."** The product page attributes this to a clinical trial measuring "% of satisfaction" among 43 volunteers, a self-reported satisfaction survey rather than a placebo-controlled, objectively graded acne-clearance trial.[^1]
- **"5% BHA and PHA complex."** The declared active salicylic acid (a BHA) is listed at 0.5%; the "5%" figure describes a combined complex with gluconolactone (a PHA) whose individual concentrations are not disclosed.[^1]
- **"Treats and prevents all types of acne."** A broad efficacy claim not tied to a linked, controlled trial on the product page; general acne evidence for salicylic acid is graded conditionally by the American Academy of Dermatology (see [[salicylic-acid]]).[^1]
