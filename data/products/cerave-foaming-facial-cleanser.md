## Summary

CeraVe Foaming Facial Cleanser is a fragrance-free foaming cleanser made for normal to oily skin. Its job is a health one: removing the oil, dirt, and makeup of the day without stripping the skin barrier that keeps moisture in and irritants out. It is built on mild amphoteric and anionic surfactants, with [no sulfate detergents and no added fragrance](#whats-in-it), the ingredients most often blamed for the tight, stripped feeling a harsher cleanser can leave.[^1] It also carries [niacinamide, a little ceramide, and hyaluronic acid](#whats-in-it), the barrier and humectant ingredients CeraVe builds its moisturizers around. The niacinamide is what most distinguishes it from the plainer [[cerave-hydrating-facial-cleanser|CeraVe Hydrating Facial Cleanser]], which has none.[^1]

Two separate questions decide how much that is worth. The first is a category question: does a gentle, barrier-ingredient-carrying cleanser do more for the skin than any mild cleanser would? That is answered at the category level rather than by this product. Keeping a dry or barrier-impaired complexion moisturized is one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines, but that case rests on leave-on moisturizers, not rinse-off washes; the general evidence is on [[moisturizing]], and for the barrier lipids on [[ceramides]]. Niacinamide, left on the skin, has modest evidence for raising ceramide production and lowering water loss in dry skin, mapped by route and use on [[niacinamide]]; whether a cleanser that mostly rinses down the drain deposits enough of it to matter is not something a trial has [tested for this product](#the-evidence).

The second question is whether this is a good example of a gentle cleanser, and here the answer is affirmative. It is an inexpensive, fragrance-free, sulfate-free wash that skin doctors recommend by name. Quoted in NewBeauty, New York City dermatologist Julia Tzu says, "I love this cleanser because it's gentle to my skin but effectively removes the dirt and oils so that my face feels clean."[^3]

It is inexpensive. The manufacturer lists it at $15.99 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-07-28).[^1] It is not a treatment: it delivers no active drug, and the manufacturer's positioning that it "cleanses & removes oil without disrupting the protective skin barrier" and gives "12-hour oil control" is a [label claim](#common-marketing-claims) the product-specific evidence does not establish.

## The Evidence

Keeping dry, barrier-impaired, and eczema-prone skin moisturized reduces flares and spares topical corticosteroids, one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines; that general case, including the role of barrier lipids like [[ceramides]], is set out on [[moisturizing]] and in the [[van-zuuren-2017-emollients-moisturisers-eczema|2017 Cochrane review]], which found most moisturizers gave some benefit for eczema and that no single one was shown to beat another. That evidence is for leave-on moisturizers rather than rinse-off cleansers, and the closest test of a ceramide wash comes from a regimen study of a different brand.

Spada 2021 was a randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trial (n=100, 28 days) in adults with moderate eczema. It tested a regimen of a ceramide-dominant moisturizing cream used together with a ceramide hydrating body wash, both from Ego Pharmaceuticals, which funded the study. The active group "had significantly greater improvements in TEWL at all-time points" compared to placebo (P=.0342). For eczema severity, the between-group "change was not significantly different between groups" (EASI, P=.7804).[^2]

> **Bottom line.** A ceramide wash used together with a ceramide cream improved a skin-barrier measurement, but it reduced eczema no more than a plain vehicle. It tested a two-product regimen from one manufacturer, not this cleanser, and was funded by the products' maker.[^2]

Because it paired a body wash with a cream, the study cannot separate the wash from the cream, or stand in for a facial cleanser used on its own. Niacinamide's own evidence is by route: left-on niacinamide has modest, mostly small-trial support for raising keratinocyte ceramide synthesis and lowering water loss in dry skin, set out on [[niacinamide]]; its behavior in a rinse-off cleanser is not covered by those trials.

## What's In It

The ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the cleansing surfactants, texture, and preservative system.

Barrier, hydration, and active ingredients:

- [[glycerin]], a humectant and the third ingredient
- [[niacinamide]] (vitamin B3), which CeraVe features on the label
- three [[ceramides]] (ceramide NP, ceramide AP, ceramide EOP), lipids that match the skin's own barrier lipids
- hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid ([[hyaluronic-acid]]), a humectant
- [[cholesterol]] and [[phytosphingosine]], additional barrier lipids

Cleansing, texture, and preservative system: water, cocamidopropyl hydroxysultaine and sodium methyl cocoyl taurate (mild amphoteric and anionic surfactants), sodium lauroyl sarcosinate and sodium lauroyl lactylate (mild surfactants), PEG-150 pentaerythrityl tetrastearate and PEG-6 caprylic/capric glycerides (thickener and solubilizer), propylene glycol, carbomer and xanthan gum (thickeners), sodium chloride (thickener), citric acid (pH adjuster), disodium and tetrasodium EDTA (chelators), and methylparaben with propylparaben (preservatives).[^1]

Not in the formula: no added fragrance and no sulfate detergents such as sodium lauryl or laureth sulfate.[^1] Unlike the [[cerave-hydrating-facial-cleanser|CeraVe Hydrating Facial Cleanser]], this version contains niacinamide.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Cleanses & removes oil without disrupting the protective skin barrier"** and **"gently removes excess oil, dirt, and makeup while helping to balance moisture levels and strengthen your skin's protective barrier."** Label claims.[^1] No trial isolates this cleanser; the closest study, Spada 2021, tested a ceramide wash plus cream and found a barrier (TEWL) benefit but no eczema-severity advantage over vehicle.[^2]
- **"12-hour oil control"** and **"Leaves skin feeling refreshed, without a dry or tight feel."** Performance and feel claims with no product-specific trial reported.[^1]
- **"With 3 essential ceramides, niacinamide, & hyaluronic acid."** The niacinamide, three ceramides, and hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid appear in the ingredient list.[^1] Their listed presence in a rinse-off product is a composition fact, not a measured clinical outcome.
- **"Developed with dermatologists."** A brand positioning statement, not an independently tested outcome.[^1]
