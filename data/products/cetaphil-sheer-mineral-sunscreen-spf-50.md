Cetaphil Sheer Mineral Sunscreen SPF 50 is a lotion-format, 100% mineral Broad Spectrum SPF 50 sunscreen from [[cetaphil|Cetaphil]] (Galderma), FDA-labeled with titanium dioxide 9.0% and zinc oxide 7.0% as its only active ingredients.[^dailymed]

> A tube-format mineral lotion carrying the same titanium dioxide 9%/zinc oxide 7% filter load as [[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50|CeraVe's own mineral SPF 50 face sunscreen]], a higher combined percentage than Cetaphil's own stick version of this line.[^dailymed] Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case, cutting skin-cancer risk and slowing photoaging, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; no independent trial of this specific lotion was found, so its grade rests on that category evidence plus the two filters' own chemistry, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence).[^dailymed]

## Summary

Sheer Mineral Sunscreen SPF 50 is Cetaphil's tube-format lotion sunscreen, FDA-registered with Galderma Laboratories, L.P. as the labeler.[^dailymed] Its Drug Facts panel lists two active ingredients, titanium dioxide 9.0% and zinc oxide 7.0% (90 mg and 70 mg per mL of lotion), with the OTC-monograph purpose "Sunscreen" and no organic (chemical) UV filters in the formula.[^dailymed] The label's Uses section states the product "helps prevent sunburn" and, "if used as directed with other sun protection measures," "decreases the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging caused by the sun."[^dailymed] The product ships in a 3 fl oz (89 mL) tube per the label's principal display panel.[^dailymed]

Broad-spectrum UV protection is the best-evidenced way to slow photoaging and reduce sun-related skin-cancer risk, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Both actives here are physically opaque mineral filters rather than organic filters like avobenzone; each has its own evidence and chemistry page, linked in [What's In It](#whats-in-it). The filter percentages match [[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50|CeraVe's own mineral SPF 50 face sunscreen]] exactly (titanium dioxide 9%, zinc oxide 7%) and are higher than [[cetaphil-sheer-mineral-sunscreen-stick-spf-50|Cetaphil's own stick version]] of this Sheer Mineral line (titanium dioxide 6%, zinc oxide 4.7%), though all three carry the same Broad Spectrum SPF 50 rating.[^dailymed]

The brand's own product page for this lotion describes it as "lightweight, 100% mineral UVA/UVB filter sunscreen, designed for daily use on the face and body," built with "Micronized Technology to help disperse minerals evenly for easy application," and marketed as fragrance free and water resistant for up to 80 minutes.[^cetaphil]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent, product-specific study of this exact lotion was located; the product-specific facts available are its filter composition and its FDA-labeled Broad Spectrum SPF 50 rating.

The FDA drug label lists the active ingredients as titanium dioxide 9.0% and zinc oxide 7.0%, matching the ingredient declaration format used across the OTC sunscreen monograph.[^dailymed] Both are mineral (inorganic) filters rather than organic filters such as avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, or octocrylene. [[zinc-oxide]] is photostable with genuine long-wave UVA reach; [[titanium-dioxide]] is the stronger UVB absorber of the two and contributes shorter-wave UVA coverage; neither needs a photostabilizer the way avobenzone does, a comparison detailed on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

Set against [[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50|CeraVe's mineral SPF 50 face sunscreen]], whose own FDA drug label lists the identical titanium dioxide 9% and zinc oxide 7%, this lotion's filter load is not a differentiator between the two products; both list the same actives at the same percentages and the same Broad Spectrum SPF 50 rating.[^dailymed] Against Cetaphil's own stick in the same Sheer Mineral line, titanium dioxide 6% and zinc oxide 4.7%, this lotion's filter percentages are meaningfully higher despite both carrying an SPF 50 rating; differences in base and vehicle (lotion versus wax stick) and testing methodology can account for two formulas reaching a similar tested SPF at different filter loads, so this is a composition note, not a claim that one product outperforms the other in practice.

> **Bottom line.** The FDA-labeled Broad Spectrum SPF 50 rating reflects a 100% mineral filter set, titanium dioxide 9.0% and zinc oxide 7.0%, matching at least one same-brand-category competitor's filter load and exceeding Cetaphil's own stick version.[^dailymed] No trial isolates this lotion; its grade rests on category-level UV-filter evidence and the two filters' known chemistry.

## What's In It

The active filters and inactive ingredients as declared on the FDA drug label (DailyMed).[^dailymed]

**Active: titanium dioxide 9.0% (90 mg/mL), zinc oxide 7.0% (70 mg/mL).**[^dailymed] See [[titanium-dioxide]] and [[zinc-oxide]] for the graded mechanism and full evidence base.

**Purpose:** Sunscreen.[^dailymed]

**Inactive ingredients**, as declared on the label: water, isohexadecane, butyloctyl salicylate, cetyl dimethicone, styrene/acrylates copolymer, olive oil polyglyceryl-6 esters, sorbitan olivate, sodium stearoyl lactylate, VP/eicosene copolymer, cetearyl alcohol, cetearyl olivate, cetyl palmitate, sorbitan palmitate, dimethicone PEG-8 laurate, ceramide NP, ceramide AP, ceramide EOP, cholesterol, phytosphingosine, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, benzyl alcohol, bisabolol, dipotassium glycyrrhizate, propanediol, xanthan gum, aluminum hydroxide, sodium lauroyl lactylate, carbomer, tocopheryl acetate, disodium EDTA, stearic acid, triethoxycaprylylsilane, citric acid, maltodextrin, phenoxyethanol, capryloyl glycine, undecylenoyl glycine.[^dailymed]

The label's Warnings section states "For external use only" and lists "Do not use" "on damaged or broken skin," to "keep out of eyes" (rinse with water if contact occurs), and to "stop use and ask a doctor if" a rash occurs.[^dailymed] Directions call for applying liberally 15 minutes before sun exposure and reapplying after 80 minutes of swimming or sweating, immediately after towel drying, and at least every 2 hours; for children under 6 months of age, the label says "Ask a doctor."[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Micronized Technology to help disperse minerals evenly for easy application."** A formulation-positioning claim on Cetaphil's own product page; no independent test of this specific dispersion technology was found.[^cetaphil]
- **"Defends against 5 signs of skin sensitivity including dryness, irritation, roughness, tightness and a weakened skin barrier."** A brand cosmetic-benefit claim; not a claim addressed by the FDA sunscreen monograph, which covers only the sunburn-prevention and skin-cancer/aging-risk uses quoted above.[^cetaphil][^dailymed]
- **"Water resistant for up to 80 minutes."** Stated on the brand's product page; the FDA label's own Directions section separately instructs reapplication "after 80 minutes of swimming or sweating," consistent with, but not proof beyond, that instruction.[^cetaphil][^dailymed]
