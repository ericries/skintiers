Effaclar BPO is an over-the-counter acne cream whose single active is "Benzoyl peroxide 5.5%", labelled for "the treatment of acne".[^dailymed] It is a recent launch, with a marketing start date of 22 May 2026, and it is a different product from the salicylic acid cream on [[la-roche-posay-effaclar-duo]].[^dailymed]

> **Bottom line.** The simplest product La Roche-Posay makes for acne, and that simplicity is the point. One active, benzoyl peroxide at 5.5%, which is a genuine first-line acne treatment with real controlled evidence behind it (see [[benzoyl-peroxide]]). There is no salicylic acid and no lipohydroxy acid in it, so do not confuse it with the Effaclar Duo products. Two practical caveats come straight off the label: it **bleaches hair and dyed fabrics**, and the label tells you to start once daily and build up rather than going in three times a day.[^dailymed]

## Summary

This is a **monograph OTC drug**, not a cosmetic. Its FDA listing records marketing category "OTC Monograph Drug" under monograph M006, labeller L'Oreal USA Products Inc, marketed since 22 May 2026 in a 20 mL tube and a carton of fifteen 3 mL tubes.[^dailymed] That matters for reading the rest of the page: the claims it is allowed to make come from the acne monograph, not from a trial of this cream.

The Drug Facts active is "Benzoyl peroxide 5.5%" with purpose "Acne treatment", and the inactive ingredients are water, glycerin, pentylene glycol, carbomer, silica, tocopheryl acetate, sodium citrate, sodium hydroxide, sodium metabisulfite, diethylhexyl sodium sulfosuccinate, phenoxyethanol, epilobium angustifolium flower/leaf/stem extract, propanediol, an acrylates crosspolymer, disodium EDTA and potassium hydroxide.[^dailymed]

Two things are worth noticing in that list. **There is no second acne active**: no salicylic acid, no lipohydroxy acid, no niacinamide. And the base is a plain humectant-and-gelling-agent vehicle rather than a barrier-support formula, which is consistent with benzoyl peroxide's reputation for dryness.

## The Evidence

No trial of this specific cream is cited here, so the grade rests entirely on the active.

That is a comfortable position to be in, because benzoyl peroxide is one of the few acne ingredients where the evidence is genuinely strong and the guidelines agree: it is a first-line topical, it works on inflammatory lesions, and unlike topical antibiotics it does not drive resistance. The numbers and limits are on [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and are not repeated here. At 5.5% it sits inside the usual over-the-counter range,[^dailymed] and there is good reason not to assume higher is better, since the dose-response for benzoyl peroxide flattens while irritation keeps climbing, which is why [[panoxyl-acne-foaming-wash-benzoyl-peroxide-10|PanOxyl's 10% wash]] is not automatically the stronger choice.

What this product does not have is any published comparison against the brand's own alternatives. Nothing cited here establishes whether Effaclar BPO outperforms the salicylic acid [[la-roche-posay-effaclar-duo|Effaclar Duo+]] or the adapalene gel on [[la-roche-posay-effaclar-adapalene-gel-0-1]]. Those are three different actives for the same condition, and choosing between them is a clinical question rather than one this page can settle.

## What The Label Tells You

The Drug Facts carry the practical information, and two items deserve emphasis.

**It bleaches.** The label warns to "avoid contact with hair and dyed fabrics, which may be bleached by this product".[^dailymed] Pillowcases and towels are the usual casualties.

**Start low.** The directions are to "cover the entire affected area with a thin layer one to three times daily", but then explicitly: "because excessive drying of the skin mayoccur, start with one application daily, thengradually increase to two or three timesdaily if needed or as directed by a doctor", and to cut back to once daily or every other day if dryness or peeling becomes bothersome.[^dailymed] Most people who find benzoyl peroxide intolerable started at the top of that range.

Other label warnings: for external use only; do not use on very sensitive skin or if sensitive to benzoyl peroxide; irritation and dryness are more likely if another topical acne medication is used at the same time; avoid unnecessary sun exposure and use a sunscreen; avoid the eyes, lips and mouth.[^dailymed] Reported irritation is "characterized by redness, burning, itching, peeling, or possibly swelling".[^dailymed]

## Who It Is For

Someone who wants plain benzoyl peroxide in a cream rather than a wash, and who will build up slowly. If you are already using a retinoid or another acne active, the label's warning about combining topicals applies.
