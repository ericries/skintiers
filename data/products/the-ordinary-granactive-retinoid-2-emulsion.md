The Ordinary Granactive Retinoid 2% Emulsion is a 30ml leave-on facial serum from [[the-ordinary|The Ordinary]], listing both [[hydroxypinacolone-retinoate|hydroxypinacolone retinoate]] (HPR) and [[retinol]] on its ingredient list and priced at $12.10 on the manufacturer's own site.[^to]

> A low-priced emulsion that pairs two retinoid types, HPR and retinol, without disclosing either's concentration. Retinol has vehicle-controlled trial evidence for photoaging at the category level, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence), but this product's own dose of it is unknown, and no study of this exact combination was found.

## Summary

The Ordinary Granactive Retinoid 2% Emulsion is sold in a single 30ml size for $12.10 on the brand's own site, where it is listed in stock.[^to] The manufacturer describes it only as "Next-generation retinol-alternative technologies to address signs of aging."[^to]

The name and older marketing around The Ordinary's "Granactive Retinoid" line refer to a raw material sold under that trade name that is 10% [[hydroxypinacolone-retinoate|hydroxypinacolone retinoate]] (HPR) diluted in 90% dimethyl isosorbide solvent, so a "2%" label historically implied about 0.2% actual HPR ([background on that math is on the ingredient page](#the-evidence)).[^to] The current ingredient list, however, also declares plain [[retinol]] as a separate ingredient, and the product page does not state what fraction of the formula is HPR versus retinol versus the dimethyl isosorbide carrier.[^to] [[retinol|Retinol]] itself carries [vehicle-controlled trial evidence for photoaging](#the-evidence) at the category level; HPR's own evidence is [preliminary and largely in-vitro or confined to multi-ingredient human studies](#the-evidence). Because neither active's dose is disclosed here, this product's own case rests on the category evidence for the class, not on a concentration verified to match any cited trial.

Around the two retinoids, the formula carries emollients (caprylic/capric triglyceride, ethyl linoleate, cetearyl isononanoate) and the calming ingredients [[alpha-bisabolol|bisabolol]] and Tasmannia lanceolata fruit/leaf extract, an anti-irritant The Ordinary has used elsewhere in its retinoid line.[^to]

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinol improves photoaged skin is covered on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]: vehicle-controlled trials support gradual improvement in fine wrinkles and pigment. Whether HPR itself does anything in isolation is far less settled: [[hydroxypinacolone-retinoate|HPR's own page]] finds only in-vitro fibroblast data and human trials that test HPR inside combination formulas with retinol or peptides, with no controlled trial isolating HPR's own effect.

> **Bottom line.** This product blends a well-evidenced retinoid (retinol) with a thinly-evidenced one (HPR), but discloses the dose of neither, so no percentage here can be checked against any cited trial.

No published trial of this exact emulsion, or of this specific HPR-plus-retinol combination at any concentration, was located.[^to]
