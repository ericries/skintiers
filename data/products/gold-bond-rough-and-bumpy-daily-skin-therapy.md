Gold Bond® Rough & Bumpy Daily Skin Therapy Cream is a fragrance-free body cream sold by [[gold-bond|Gold Bond]] that combines an AHA, a BHA, and a PHA to exfoliate rough, bumpy skin, at an 8 oz size listed for $11.97 on both Gold Bond's own site and Walmart.[^pdp][^walmart]

> A three-acid exfoliating body cream, an AHA (ammonium lactate), a BHA (salicylic acid), and a PHA (gluconolactone), none of them listed at a stated percentage on either source checked here.[^pdp][^walmart] The acid-by-acid evidence lives on [[keratosis-pilaris]] and [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]]; Gold Bond's own bump-reduction figures for this specific cream are [manufacturer "data on file," not an independently published trial](#common-marketing-claims).[^pdp][^walmart]

## Summary

Gold Bond markets this 8 oz cream for "rough and bumpy skin," describing it as combining "AHA, BHA, and PHA to gently exfoliate rough, bumpy skin while conditioning with 7 moisturizers and 3 vitamins."[^pdp] Its ingredient panel names the three exfoliating acids as salicylic acid, gluconolactone, and an ingredient it labels "Lactic Acid" in the callout copy, though the declared ingredient list itself names that third acid ammonium lactate, the ammonium salt form of lactic acid; ammonium lactate is the same salt form graded on [[keratosis-pilaris]], where AmLactin is a widely sold over-the-counter example.[^pdp] None of the three acids carries a stated percentage on either Gold Bond's own page or Walmart's listing, a point of contrast with disclosed-concentration exfoliating products in the same category.[^pdp][^walmart]

That acid-by-acid evidence, and how the three acid types compare in depth and strength, is set out on [[keratosis-pilaris]] and [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]] rather than restated here. What matters for this specific cream is covered under [The Evidence](#the-evidence): no independent trial isolates this formula, and Gold Bond's own bump-reduction figures differ between its own site and Walmart's listing, with no published study behind either.[^pdp][^walmart]

The base of the formula is an occlusive-and-emollient body cream: petrolatum, shea butter extract, dimethicone, and cetyl alcohol, alongside a urea derivative (hydroxyethyl urea, distinct from urea itself) and aloe.[^pdp] It is fragrance-free and sold at $11.97 for 8 oz, a price confirmed on both the manufacturer's own page and Walmart (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-11).[^pdp][^walmart]

## The Evidence

Lactic acid and salicylic acid both have real controlled evidence specifically for keratosis pilaris, tested head to head against each other, and gluconolactone is a gentler polyhydroxy acid generally positioned as less irritating than either; the full case for each acid, and how they compare in depth and strength, is set out on [[keratosis-pilaris]] and [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]]. No independently published trial of this exact cream, or of this specific three-acid combination, was located. The product-specific facts available are its declared composition, salicylic acid, gluconolactone, and ammonium lactate, none at a disclosed percentage, and its own marketing claims: "Clinically shown to visibly diminish bumps in just 1 week," and "clinical results showing a 74% reduction in bumps after four weeks," on Gold Bond's own page, alongside a differing "72% reduction in bumps/the appearance of KP in 4 weeks" figure on Walmart's listing.[^pdp][^walmart] Neither source discloses a study design, sample size, or methodology behind either percentage, and the two retailers do not even agree on the same number, so these are treated as unverified manufacturer claims rather than reported evidence (see [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).

> **Bottom line.** The AHA and BHA here belong to acid classes with real, if modest, keratosis-pilaris evidence (the PHA is a gentler relative without its own trial), all covered on [[keratosis-pilaris]]; but no independent trial isolates this exact formula, none of the three acids is disclosed at a stated concentration, and Gold Bond's own bump-reduction percentages differ between its site and Walmart's, with no published methodology behind either.[^pdp][^walmart]

## What's In It

The full declared ingredient list (INCI), from Gold Bond's own product page.[^pdp]

Notable/functional ingredients:

- [[salicylic-acid|Salicylic Acid]], a BHA; Gold Bond's own callout describes it as helping to "exfoliate skin"[^pdp]
- Gluconolactone, a PHA, linked to its own evidence page as [[gluconolactone-pha]]; Gold Bond's own callout describes it as gently exfoliating "dead skin cells"[^pdp]
- Ammonium Lactate, the ammonium salt of [[lactic-acid|lactic acid]] (an AHA); Gold Bond's own marketing callout labels this ingredient "Lactic Acid" rather than its declared INCI name[^pdp]
- Hydroxyethyl Urea, a urea derivative used as a humectant, distinct from urea itself
- Aloe Barbadensis (Aloe) Leaf Juice
- Tocopheryl Acetate (vitamin E), Magnesium Ascorbyl Phosphate (a vitamin C derivative), and Panthenol (a form of vitamin B5), Gold Bond's stated "3 vitamins"[^pdp]

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, petrolatum, shea butter extract, cetyl alcohol, propanediol, potassium hydroxide (pH adjuster), dimethicone and caprylyl methicone (silicones), glyceryl stearate and steareth-21 (emulsifiers), jojoba esters, cocoa seed butter, polyacrylate crosspolymer-6 (thickener), PPG-3 benzyl ether myristate, lemon, orange, apple, sugar cane, ginger root, and green tea (Camellia sinensis) leaf extracts, bisabolol, polyglyceryl-4 laurate/succinate, stearamidopropyl PG-dimonium chloride phosphate, phenoxyethanol (preservative), propylene glycol, and hydroxyethylcellulose (thickener).[^pdp]

Not on the label: no fragrance is declared, and Gold Bond states the formula is "fragrance free."[^pdp]

## Common Marketing Claims

Claims from Gold Bond's own product page and Walmart's listing, not accompanied by a published study on either source:

- **"Clinically shown to visibly diminish bumps in just 1 week."** Gold Bond's headline claim.[^pdp] Walmart's listing states a specific figure for a related claim, "90% experienced softer, smoother skin in just 1 week," marked "*Data on file" rather than a citation to a published study.[^walmart]
- **"74% reduction in bumps after four weeks"** (Gold Bond's own page) versus **"72% reduction in bumps/the appearance of KP in 4 weeks"** (Walmart's listing).[^pdp][^walmart] The two sources give different figures for what appears to be the same underlying claim; neither discloses a sample size, study design, or methodology.
- **"Dermatologist and clinically-tested."**[^pdp] Gold Bond's FAQ separately states "this product has been tested by dermatologists," without further detail.[^pdp]
- **"Non-irritating, non-greasy formula" and "Hypoallergenic and fragrance-free."**[^pdp] Brand positioning claims not accompanied by data on either source cited here.
