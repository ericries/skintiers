Paula's Choice C15 Super Booster is a leave-on serum sold in a 20 ml bottle that pairs a disclosed 15% L-ascorbic acid (pure vitamin C) with vitamin E and ferulic acid.[^1][^2]

> Vitamin C is a well-evidenced antioxidant, and this product states its concentration and active form plainly, 15% L-ascorbic acid, rather than the vaguer "vitamin C complex" wording common on cheaper serums.[^1][^2] It pairs that vitamin C with vitamin E and ferulic acid, the same class of stabilizing combination the SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic photoprotection evidence is built on, though Paula's Choice does not disclose the vitamin E or ferulic acid percentages the way SkinCeuticals does.[^1][^3] The general antioxidant case for L-ascorbic acid is set out on [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]]; no trial has tested this exact formula.

## Summary

C15 Super Booster is a morning-use antioxidant serum built around [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]], the pure and best-studied form of vitamin C. Paula's Choice states the concentration and form directly on the product page: "This water-light liquid treatment pairs a higher 15% pure vitamin C (ascorbic acid) with antioxidants ferulic acid and vitamin E."[^1] That kind of disclosure, an exact percentage and the specific chemical form, is not universal among vitamin C serums, several of which list "vitamin C" or a stabilized derivative without stating how much is in the bottle; the vitamin C forms and how each is evidenced are mapped on [[vitamin-c]].

What the product adds to plain L-ascorbic acid is [[tocopherol|vitamin E]] and [[ferulic-acid]], the same two supporting antioxidants that stabilize and, in the studies behind [[skinceuticals-c-e-ferulic|SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic]], roughly double the measured photoprotection of a vitamin C and vitamin E solution.[^3] Paula's Choice's own description names the same two partners: "ferulic acid, which stabilizes vitamin C and helps defend against discoloration, and vitamin E."[^1] The manufacturer does not publish the vitamin E or ferulic acid percentages the way SkinCeuticals discloses its 1% and 0.5%, so the exact ratio this product uses against the studied combination is not verifiable from the product page.[^1][^2] A third-party retailer's ingredient listing also shows two added peptides, tridecapeptide-1 and palmitoyl tripeptide-5, in the current formula;[^4] general peptide evidence, which varies by peptide type and is mostly modest and cosmetic, is set out on [[peptides]].

Paula's Choice's own directions describe daily application: "Once or twice daily, apply to face and neck. May be used alone as a serum or added to your favorite moisturizer. For daytime, always finish with sunscreen."[^2] The 20 ml bottle is listed at $59.00 by one US retailer ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07), a small size relative to the 30 ml SkinCeuticals bottle.

No product-specific trial of this formula has been published; the evidence for its main active is the general vitamin C case, [linked out below](#the-evidence), and its brand marketing claims are separated out under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## The Evidence

Topical L-ascorbic acid is a well-studied antioxidant with a modest, adjunct role in UV protection alongside sunscreen and a modest role supporting collagen synthesis; the full case, by use, is set out on [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]], and the wider family of vitamin C forms on [[vitamin-c]]. Ferulic acid's best-documented role is stabilizing and boosting the measured photoprotection of a vitamin C plus vitamin E combination, an effect established in a specific patented 15% C / 1% E / 0.5% ferulic acid formula;[^3] that case is set out on [[ferulic-acid]] and the studies behind it are reported on [[skinceuticals-c-e-ferulic]].

No published study has tested C15 Super Booster itself. What is verifiable is the concentration match: Paula's Choice states the product carries "15% pure vitamin C (ascorbic acid)," the same headline concentration as the tested SkinCeuticals formula.[^1] What is not verifiable is the rest of the ratio. SkinCeuticals discloses its formula as 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% alpha-tocopherol, and 0.5% ferulic acid, the exact combination the photoprotection studies tested; Paula's Choice names vitamin E and ferulic acid as ingredients but does not publish their percentages, so whether this product matches that studied ratio cannot be confirmed from the manufacturer's own page.[^1][^2]

> **Bottom line.** The disclosed 15% vitamin C concentration matches the well-studied SkinCeuticals formula's headline strength, and the product pairs vitamin C with the same two stabilizing antioxidants, vitamin E and ferulic acid. Because the vitamin E and ferulic acid percentages are not published, this product's formula cannot be confirmed as a match to the exact ratio the photoprotection studies tested, and no trial has been run on this product itself.[^1][^2][^3]

## What's In It

The active antioxidants as described by the manufacturer and a retailer's full ingredient declaration.[^1][^2][^4]

Antioxidant and treatment actives:

- [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]] 15%, pure vitamin C, disclosed by the manufacturer[^1][^2]
- [[ferulic-acid]], a plant antioxidant the manufacturer describes as stabilizing the vitamin C and helping "defend against discoloration"[^1]
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), which the manufacturer describes as boosting the vitamin C and helping "defend against environmental stressors"[^2]
- Tridecapeptide-1 and palmitoyl tripeptide-5, two [[peptides]], per a retailer's current ingredient listing[^4]
- Ergothioneine, a mushroom-derived antioxidant, per the same listing[^4]
- Sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]), a humectant, per the same listing[^4]

Base and texture ingredients, per the same listing: water, glycerin, ethoxydiglycol (a penetration solvent), PPG-26-Buteth-26, mannitol, pentylene glycol and decylene glycol (solvents/humectants), PEG-40 hydrogenated castor oil, bisabolol, sodium gluconate, oryza sativa (rice) bran extract, polyacrylate crosspolymer-6, 1,2-hexanediol, panthenol, sodium phytate, sodium hydroxide, citric acid, and phenoxyethanol (preservative).[^4]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Higher" 15% vitamin C, and "boost glow, diminish uneven skin tone and visibly reduce the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles."** These are the manufacturer's own performance claims for the finished product; no independent trial of this formula has been published, and the general brightening and fine-line evidence for L-ascorbic acid is mapped on [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]], not a trial of this booster.[^1]
- **Compatibility with copper peptides.** The manufacturer states: "The only ingredient to keep separate is copper peptides, as they can make pure vitamin C less effective."[^5] This is manufacturer usage guidance, not an independently tested finding.
