Aveeno Calm + Restore Face Cleanser for Sensitive Skin (Aveeno) is a fragrance-free, non-foaming milky cleanser built around oat and feverfew and positioned for irritated, dry, sensitive skin.[^1]

> A gentle, fragrance-free cream cleanser whose headline ingredient is colloidal oatmeal, but it is rinsed off rather than left on, and the trials behind oat's itch-relief and barrier evidence used a leave-on cream, not a wash-off formula.[^1] No trial isolates this cleanser. The best-evidenced skincare step for eczema-prone skin is leave-on moisturizing, covered on [[moisturizing]], not cleansing.

## Summary

Aveeno Calm + Restore Face Cleanser for Sensitive Skin is a non-foaming, milky facial cleanser made for "irritated, dry, sensitive skin," designed to "lifts away dirt and impurities" while leaving skin "feeling hydrated."[^1] Its [full ingredient list](#whats-in-it) is short: water, glycerin, a humectant, avena sativa (oat) kernel flour as the fourth-listed ingredient, two mild surfactant/emulsifying agents, a corn-starch texture agent, a preservative system, and small amounts of feverfew flower/leaf/stem juice and sodium hyaluronate near the end of the list.[^1] Target's listing for the same 7.8 fl oz SKU confirms the identical ingredient list and adds that the product is "formulated free of fragrances, parabens, alcohol, dyes, and phthalates" and is "non-comedogenic."[^1]

What distinguishes it from a plainer sensitive-skin cleanser is the named oat and feverfew content rather than an added drug active. Avena sativa (oat) kernel flour is the INCI name for colloidal oatmeal, an FDA-recognized skin protectant with randomized-trial evidence for relieving itch and supporting barrier repair in mild-to-moderate eczema when used as a leave-on cream; see [[colloidal-oatmeal]]. This is a rinse-off cleanser, not a leave-on cream, so [that oat evidence does not transfer directly](#the-evidence). Sensitive, eczema-prone skin is a medical topic in its own right, covered on [[atopic-dermatitis]], where the best-evidenced skincare step is leave-on moisturizing rather than cleansing; that evidence and the role of humectants like [[glycerin]] are set out there and on [[moisturizing]].

The listing describes Aveeno as a "Dermatologist-recommended brand for over 70 years" and states the product is "tested on sensitive skin" and hypoallergenic.[^1] Those are brand-level and formulation-testing claims rather than a clinical trial of this specific cleanser; no product-specific trial is [cited below](#the-evidence). It is inexpensive: Target lists the 7.8 fl oz bottle at $9.99 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^1] It carries no drug active at a treatment concentration, and its positioning is tolerability and gentle cleansing rather than a measured clinical benefit.

## The Evidence

No trial isolates this cleanser. Colloidal oatmeal (avena sativa oat kernel flour), the product's fourth-listed ingredient, has randomized-trial evidence for relieving itch and improving barrier measures in mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis, graded on [[colloidal-oatmeal]]. That evidence comes from a leave-on cream. Because this product is a cleanser that is rinsed off rather than left on skin, whether it deposits enough oat, or holds it on skin long enough, to produce a comparable effect has not been tested for this product. The same caveat applies to glycerin and sodium hyaluronate, both humectants with solid leave-on evidence graded on [[glycerin]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]] respectively, in a formula that is washed off.
