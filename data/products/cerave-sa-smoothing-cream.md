CeraVe SA Smoothing Cream is a fragrance-free exfoliating body cream sold by [[cerave|CeraVe]] in the UK that pairs 10% urea with an undisclosed concentration of salicylic acid and three ceramides, sold as a 340g pot (listed at £23.00 at LookFantastic, mid-priced for an exfoliating body cream) and a 177ml size.[^cerave][^lf]

> A urea-plus-acids body cream in which the best-evidenced active is not the one in the name: the "SA" salicylic acid appears mid-ingredient-list at no stated percentage, while [[urea]] at a declared 10% is the ingredient with actual controlled-trial support for dry, scaling skin. CeraVe's own UK product page displays an ingredient list that contains neither urea nor salicylic acid, contradicting its own "Salicylic Acid & 10% Urea" description; the full INCI on this page therefore comes from the retailer listing.[^cerave][^lf] No independent trial of this product was located, so its grades rest on ingredient-level evidence set out on [[urea]] and [[keratosis-pilaris]].

## Summary

CeraVe describes the SA Smoothing Cream as "a rich, fragrance-free moisturiser for gentle body exfoliation. Enriched with 3 Essential Ceramides, Salicylic Acid & 10% Urea," positioned for "Dry, Rough & Bumpy Skin" and "also suitable for Keratosis Pilaris and Psoriasis-Prone Skin," for use "only on the body."[^cerave] It is the UK/EU counterpart to CeraVe's US rough-and-bumpy line, which includes [[cerave-sa-body-lotion-for-rough-and-bumpy-skin]] and the companion [[cerave-sa-smoothing-cleanser]]; the formulas differ, so evidence and composition are not interchangeable between them.

The one active declared at a concentration is urea at 10%, second on the ingredient list, a level with genuine if modest controlled-trial evidence for dryness and scaling; that case, including its limits, lives on [[urea]]. Salicylic acid sits nineteenth of thirty-two ingredients with no stated percentage, joined by a lactate/lactic acid cluster (sodium lactate, C12-13 alkyl lactate, lactic acid) and capryloyl salicylic acid, none at disclosed levels.[^lf] The acid-class evidence for bumpy, keratosis pilaris-prone skin, including the split-side trial where 10% lactic acid beat 5% salicylic acid, is set out on [[keratosis-pilaris]] and [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]] rather than restated here.

No published trial isolates this product ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)). Its marketing, including a "3 Hour Results" blemish claim unusual for a body moisturizer, is weighed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims). The 340g pot is listed at £23.00 at LookFantastic, mid-priced for the exfoliating-body-cream category (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^lf]

## The Evidence

No independent published trial of the SA Smoothing Cream, or of its exact urea/salicylic acid/lactate combination, was located. The grades above therefore rest on ingredient-level evidence:

**Urea, 10%, the declared-concentration active.** Urea's controlled-trial record, a right-versus-left trial in which 10% urea lotion cut a scaling-roughness score more than a glycerol emollient in ichthyosis vulgaris, plus Cochrane-reviewed eczema evidence at low-to-moderate certainty, is set out with its numbers and caveats on [[urea]]. That is the strongest evidence anywhere in this formula, and it supports softening and hydrating dry, scaling skin, not acne or pore claims.

**The acids, at undisclosed concentrations.** Salicylic acid and the lactic acid family both have comparator-tested evidence for keratosis pilaris, covered on [[keratosis-pilaris]]; but with no percentage disclosed for any acid in this cream, the formula cannot be placed against disclosed-concentration products in the category, such as [[amlactin-daily-moisturizing-lotion-12-lactic-acid]]'s stated 12% lactic acid, and the acid evidence transfers only loosely.[^lf]

A further product-specific accuracy problem: CeraVe's own UK product page displays a full ingredient list that contains neither urea nor salicylic acid, and does not match the retailer-published INCI; the brand's page appears to be showing a different formula's list alongside this product's description.[^cerave][^lf]

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its best-supported use is as a 10% urea emollient for dry, rough, scaling skin, per the evidence on [[urea]]; the salicylic acid in its name is an undisclosed-percentage supporting ingredient, and the keratosis pilaris positioning rests on acid-class evidence from other products, set out on [[keratosis-pilaris]].

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as published on the LookFantastic retail listing for the 340g pot; CeraVe's own UK page displays a non-matching list (see above), so the retailer's is used.[^lf]

Actives and functional ingredients:

- [[urea]], second on the list, declared at 10% in CeraVe's product description[^cerave]
- [[salicylic-acid]], nineteenth of thirty-two, no percentage disclosed
- a lactate cluster: sodium lactate, C12-13 alkyl lactate, and [[lactic-acid]], none at disclosed levels
- capryloyl salicylic acid (a lipohydroxy acid derivative of salicylic acid), near the end of the list
- [[niacinamide]], sixth, no percentage disclosed
- [[ceramides]] NP, AP, and EOP, with [[cholesterol]] and [[phytosphingosine]], CeraVe's barrier-lipid trio delivered via its MVE technology[^cerave]
- sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]])

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, cetyl and cetearyl alcohol, glyceryl stearate SE, shea butter, PEG-100 stearate, glycerin, behentrimonium methosulfate, dimethicone, triacetin, carbomer, sodium hydroxide, sodium lauroyl lactylate, phenoxyethanol, disodium EDTA, hydroxyacetophenone, citric acid, xanthan gum, and ethylhexylglycerin.[^lf]

The list declares no added fragrance, matching the brand's "fragrance-free" claim.[^cerave][^lf]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by product-specific evidence:

- **"3 Hour Results*" and "Reduce the appearance of blemishes and blackheads in 1 use*."** An asterisked manufacturer claim on the product page with no published methodology, sample size, or control; it is also an acne-adjacent claim on a body moisturizer whose acids carry no disclosed percentage.[^cerave]
- **"Developed with Dermatologists."** A brand development claim, not a study of this product.[^cerave]
- **"Locks in moisture giving continuous hydration for 24 hours" via "MVE Technology."** A manufacturer delivery-technology claim; no published trial of this product's hydration duration is cited on the page.[^cerave]
- **"Non-irritating, non comedogenic."** Manufacturer testing claims with no published methodology.[^cerave]
- **The "SA" naming itself.** The name foregrounds salicylic acid, which appears mid-list at no stated percentage; the ingredient actually declared at a concentration, and with the better trial record here, is 10% urea.[^cerave][^lf]
