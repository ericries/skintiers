Soolantra (ivermectin) Cream, 1% is a prescription-only topical drug made by Galderma, applied once daily to the face to treat the inflammatory lesions, papules and pustules, of rosacea.[^dailymed] It is not a cosmetic product: the FDA-approved indication is limited to that inflammatory-lesion pattern, and the drug's own label states plainly that the mechanism by which it works is unknown.[^dailymed]

> A branded prescription rosacea drug with its own randomized-trial base, not a compounded or generic formulation. In two 12-week trials against an inert vehicle it brought about 4 in 10 patients to clear or almost-clear skin, versus roughly 1 in 10 to 2 in 10 on vehicle, and in a 962-patient head-to-head trial it beat the older standard topical, metronidazole 0.75% cream.[^stein][^taieb] The label carries no boxed warning, and its most-reported side effects, a burning sensation and skin irritation, are each reported in 1% or less of users.[^dailymed] It has no generic equivalent; a 45 g tube lists around $100 at retail (well under the roughly $335 average retail price GoodRx cites for the drug), so [buying it usually runs through a discount card or insurance rather than full cash price](#where-to-buy).[^goodrx]

## Summary

Soolantra is the brand name for ivermectin 1% cream, a prescription topical Galderma markets exclusively for the inflammatory lesions of rosacea, the crops of red bumps (papules) and pus-topped spots (pustules) that define [[rosacea-papulopustular|papulopustular rosacea]].[^dailymed] Topical ivermectin is a well-evidenced treatment for that inflammatory pattern, with a notable effect on a solid evidence base; the drug's label states its mechanism is unknown, though it is thought to combine anti-inflammatory action with an antiparasitic effect against the *Demodex* mites implicated in rosacea, and the full cross-trial evidence base is set out on [[ivermectin-topical]].[^dailymed][^aafp]

What sets this specific product apart from a compounded pharmacy cream is regulatory: Soolantra is the actual drug the approval trials tested, not a copy of them. The FDA approved it in December 2014 on the strength of two 12-week, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials in which once-daily ivermectin 1% reached an Investigator's Global Assessment of "clear" or "almost clear" in 38.4% and 40.1% of patients, against 11.6% and 18.8% on vehicle (both P<.001), while cutting inflammatory lesion counts by about three-quarters (76.0% and 75.0%) versus roughly half (50.0%) for vehicle ([the evidence](#the-evidence)).[^stein][^galderma] A separate 962-patient trial then compared it head-to-head against twice-daily metronidazole 0.75% cream, the longstanding first-line topical, and found ivermectin significantly ahead on both lesion reduction (83.0% vs 73.7%) and clear-or-almost-clear rate (84.9% vs 75.4%), with the gap visible by week 3.[^taieb]

Side effects are limited: the most commonly reported reactions are a burning sensation and skin irritation, each occurring in 1% or less of users, and the label carries no boxed warning or systemic safety signal.[^dailymed] Dosing is once daily, a pea-sized amount for each affected area of the face (forehead, chin, nose, each cheek) spread as a thin layer and kept away from the eyes and lips, simpler than the twice-daily metronidazole comparator.[^dailymed] The FDA approval is limited to the inflammatory lesions of rosacea; the label makes no claim about the persistent background redness or visible vessels of the erythematotelangiectatic form, covered separately on [[rosacea-erythematotelangiectatic]].

Soolantra has no generic competitor.[^goodrx] A single 45 g tube lists at $101.70 retail and $99.67 at the standard GoodRx price, with coupon or membership pricing as low as $92.17 to $92.67, well under the roughly $335 average retail price GoodRx cites for the drug's most common version; most patients use insurance or a discount program rather than paying full cash price (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-05).[^goodrx]

## The Evidence

Topical ivermectin is a well-evidenced prescription treatment for the inflammatory lesions of papulopustular rosacea, with a notable effect and solid evidence built on vehicle-controlled and head-to-head trials; the full trial base and mechanism are set out on [[ivermectin-topical]].[^dailymed] The two trials below are not a separate, adjacent evidence base: Soolantra is the branded product those trials tested, and the first is the data set that supported its FDA approval.

**Vehicle-controlled approval trials (Stein Gold and colleagues, 2014).** Two identically designed, randomized, double-blind, 12-week trials in patients with moderate-to-severe papulopustular rosacea, 1,371 patients combined, compared once-daily ivermectin 1% cream against an inert vehicle cream.[^stein][^aafp] A greater share of the ivermectin group reached treatment success, an Investigator's Global Assessment of "clear" or "almost clear": 38.4% versus 11.6% in the first study and 40.1% versus 18.8% in the second (both P<.001).[^stein] Inflammatory lesion counts fell 76.0% and 75.0% with ivermectin against 50.0% for vehicle in both studies, with statistically significant superiority from week 4 through week 12.[^stein] An independent summary in American Family Physician converted those results to a number needed to treat of 4 to 5 for clearing or almost clearing lesions, and 3 for a patient-rated "good" or "excellent" improvement.[^aafp] Fewer ivermectin-treated patients reported dermatologic adverse events than on vehicle, and significantly more rated their improvement "excellent" or "good," alongside an improved quality of life.[^stein]

> Bottom line: against an inactive cream, once-daily ivermectin roughly tripled the odds of reaching clear or almost-clear skin and cut inflammatory lesion counts by about three-quarters.

**Head-to-head against metronidazole (Taieb and colleagues, 2015, the ATTRACT study).** A phase 3, investigator-blinded, randomized trial assigned 962 patients with moderate-to-severe papulopustular rosacea to once-daily ivermectin 1% (n=478) or twice-daily metronidazole 0.75% cream (n=484), the established first-line topical, over 16 weeks.[^taieb] At week 16, ivermectin produced significantly greater lesion reduction (83.0% vs 73.7%, P<0.001) and a higher clear-or-almost-clear rate (84.9% vs 75.4%, P<0.001), with the advantage visible as early as week 3; adverse-event incidence was comparable between arms and local tolerability favored ivermectin.[^taieb] More ivermectin-treated patients rated their improvement "excellent" or "good."[^taieb]

> Bottom line: head-to-head against the older standard topical, once-daily ivermectin beat twice-daily metronidazole by roughly 9 to 10 percentage points on both lesion clearance and overall improvement.

Two limits temper those results. The benefit depends on continued use: after treatment stops, rosacea symptoms return within about four months in roughly half of patients. And the cream has been studied in moderate-to-severe papulopustular rosacea, not in milder disease.[^aafp]

## What's In It

- **Active: ivermectin 1%** (10 mg per gram of cream). See [[ivermectin-topical]] for the graded mechanism and full evidence base.[^dailymed]
- **Base and preservative system:** carbomer copolymer type B, cetyl alcohol, citric acid monohydrate, dimethicone, edetate disodium, glycerin, isopropyl palmitate, methylparaben, oleyl alcohol, phenoxyethanol, polyoxyl 20 cetostearyl ether, propylene glycol, propylparaben, purified water, sodium hydroxide, sorbitan monostearate, and stearyl alcohol, as declared on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

The label lists no contraindications and states the cream is not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

Manufacturer claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:[^galderma]

- **"Well tolerated with powerful results seen as early as two weeks."** Galderma's own headline framing of its FDA approval announcement.[^galderma] The vehicle-controlled trial reports statistically significant superiority over vehicle from week 4 onward, and the head-to-head trial against metronidazole reports a significant advantage from week 3; a week-2 onset is a manufacturer characterization not tied to a specific lesion count or p-value in the sources reviewed here.[^stein][^taieb][^galderma]
- **Metronidazole 0.75% cream described as "a current gold standard."** Galderma's own characterization of the comparator drug in publicizing the head-to-head trial.[^galderma] The trial itself found ivermectin significantly ahead of metronidazole on both lesion reduction and clear-or-almost-clear rate.[^taieb]
