Aklief is the brand name for trifarotene 0.005% cream, a prescription-only topical retinoid made by Galderma and applied once daily in the evening to treat moderate acne on the face and, distinctively, the trunk (chest, shoulders, back).[^dailymed][^aklief] The FDA approved it in October 2019 for acne vulgaris in patients 9 years of age and older.[^dailymed]

> A branded prescription acne drug, not a compounded or generic formulation: the PERFECT 1 and PERFECT 2 trials tested this exact 0.005% cream, and it is the first topical retinoid whose registration trials prospectively measured truncal acne alongside facial acne.[^tan] The benefit over an inert vehicle is real but modest and class-typical, roughly a 10 to 17 percentage point gap in clear-or-almost-clear rates depending on the trial and body site, with no published trial showing it clears acne better than older retinoids like [[adapalene]] or [[tretinoin]].[^tan] Full mechanism and trial detail live on [[trifarotene]]; this page covers the marketed product itself.

## Summary

Aklief is Galderma's brand name for trifarotene, a synthetic fourth-generation retinoid formulated as a 0.005% (50 mcg/g) cream and sold only by prescription.[^dailymed] It acts directly on retinoic acid receptors, mainly the RAR-gamma subtype, rather than needing enzymatic conversion the way [[retinol]] does; the shared retinoid mechanism, receptor biology, and full cross-trial evidence base are covered on [[trifarotene]].

What sets Aklief apart from most topical retinoid launches is that its approval trials, PERFECT 1 and PERFECT 2 (2,420 patients combined), measured truncal acne (chest, shoulders, back) as a prospective endpoint alongside the face, something comparator retinoid trials generally did not do.[^tan] The benefit over vehicle is modest and sits in the same range as other topical retinoids: no published trial pits trifarotene head-to-head against [[adapalene]] or [[tretinoin]], so claims that it outperforms older retinoids are unproven ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^tan]

It is FDA-approved only for acne vulgaris, from age 9; the label does not address photoaging, and no controlled trial has tested trifarotene for wrinkles.[^dailymed] Dosing is once daily in the evening, a thin layer on clean, dry skin of the affected areas of the face and/or trunk.[^dailymed] Aklief ships in a 2 g tube and 30 g, 45 g, and 75 g pump bottles (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)).[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

Trifarotene's mechanism, receptor selectivity, and full trial base are set out on [[trifarotene]]; this section covers the two trials that tested this exact marketed product.

**PERFECT 1 and PERFECT 2 (Tan and colleagues, 2019).** Two identically designed, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled 12-week phase 3 trials of once-daily trifarotene 0.005% cream (this product) for moderate facial and truncal acne, 2,420 patients age 9 and older combined.[^tan] At week 12, facial success on the Investigator's Global Assessment (clear or almost clear with at least a 2-grade improvement) was 29.4% vs 19.5% for vehicle in PERFECT 1 and 42.3% vs 25.7% in PERFECT 2; truncal success on the Physician's Global Assessment was 35.7% vs 25.0% and 42.6% vs 29.9% (all P<.001).[^tan] The authors concluded trifarotene "appears to be safe, effective, and well tolerated in treatment of both facial and truncal acne."[^tan] The most common adverse events were local cutaneous irritation, dryness, redness, scaling, and stinging, mainly in the first weeks of treatment.[^tan][^dailymed] Three of the paper's co-authors (Alió Saenz, Ahmad, Graeber) are affiliated with Galderma R&D LLC, the manufacturer's research arm.[^tan]

> **Bottom line.** In the trials that supported its FDA approval, once-daily trifarotene 0.005% cream cleared acne on both the face and trunk at a rate roughly 10 to 17 percentage points above vehicle. That is a real but modest effect, typical of the topical retinoid class, and no head-to-head trial shows it beats [[adapalene]] or [[tretinoin]]; there is no controlled evidence for any use beyond acne.[^tan][^dailymed]

## What's In It

**Active:** trifarotene 0.005% (50 mcg per gram of cream). See [[trifarotene]] for the graded mechanism and full evidence base.[^dailymed]

**Inactive ingredients, as declared on the FDA label:** allantoin, acrylic acid/sodium acrylate copolymer, isohexadecane, cyclomethicone, alcohol (95% v/v ethanol), medium-chain triglycerides, phenoxyethanol, propylene glycol, and purified water.[^dailymed]

The label directs applying a thin layer to the affected areas of the face and/or trunk once daily in the evening, on clean, dry skin, and warns against use on eczematous or sunburned skin and against concurrent use with other potentially irritating topical products.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

Claims made for the trifarotene molecule and its regulatory novelty, not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Targets RAR-gamma, the dominant epidermal receptor, for better results."** The receptor selectivity itself is documented (trifarotene binds mainly the RAR-gamma subtype), but narrow selectivity is a mechanism, not a demonstrated clinical advantage; no head-to-head trial shows Aklief clears acne better, or irritates less, than [[adapalene]] or [[tretinoin]] (full discussion on [[trifarotene]]).[^dailymed]
- **"The first new topical retinoid in 20-plus years, effective for body acne too."** Accurate as a regulatory and trial-design fact: Aklief's registration trials were the first to prospectively measure truncal acne.[^tan] That novelty of coverage should not be read as a larger effect: the truncal benefit over vehicle is similar in size to the facial benefit, not bigger.[^tan]

## Where to Buy

Aklief is available only by prescription; no over-the-counter purchase path exists.[^dailymed] It is sold in a 2 g tube and 30 g, 45 g, and 75 g pump bottles.[^dailymed] Galderma's official product site does not publish a cash or list price, though it references a "CareConnect Patient Savings Card" for eligible patients.[^aklief]
