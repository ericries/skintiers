Epiduo Forte is a fixed-dose prescription gel combining [[adapalene]] 0.3%, a synthetic [[retinoids|retinoid]], with [[benzoyl-peroxide|benzoyl peroxide]] 2.5% in a single once-daily application, FDA-labeled as "indicated for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in adults and pediatric patients 12 years of age and older."[^dailymed]

> A higher-strength sibling of the original Epiduo gel: three times the adapalene (0.3% vs. 0.1%) at the same 2.5% benzoyl peroxide. Both actives are individually well-evidenced acne treatments, set out on [[adapalene]] and [[benzoyl-peroxide]], and this exact combination carries its own FDA indication rather than resting purely on that separate evidence. It is prescription-only ("Rx only").[^dailymed]

## Summary

Epiduo Forte is a leave-on gel applied once daily to the whole affected area, not a spot treatment. Per its FDA label (DailyMed, labeler Galderma Laboratories, L.P.), directions are to "apply a thin layer of EPIDUO FORTE to affected areas of the face and/or trunk once daily after washing," using "a pea-sized amount for each area of the face," avoiding the eyes, lips, and mucous membranes, and washing hands after application "as EPIDUO FORTE may bleach hair or colored fabrics."[^dailymed]

The formula pairs two separately proven [[acne]] actives, [[adapalene]] and [[benzoyl-peroxide|benzoyl peroxide]], at a higher adapalene concentration than the original [[epiduo-adapalene-benzoyl-peroxide-gel|Epiduo]] (0.3% here vs. 0.1%), with benzoyl peroxide held at the same 2.5%. Both ingredient pages carry the general evidence for each active; this page does not repeat those trial numbers.

Epiduo Forte's FDA prescribing information (DailyMed, labeler Galderma Laboratories, L.P.) confirms it is "Rx only" and classified as a Human Prescription Drug.[^dailymed] Unlike the original 0.1%/2.5% Epiduo, no source was found showing this 0.3% formula has cleared as an over-the-counter switch; it remains prescription-only as of this writing.

## The Evidence

Adapalene 0.3% is a well-evidenced topical retinoid for acne, level with benzoyl peroxide and tretinoin in head-to-head data; see [[adapalene]]. Benzoyl peroxide 2.5% is a well-evidenced first-line topical acne treatment whose combination with a retinoid outperforms either agent alone; see [[benzoyl-peroxide]]. The DailyMed label for this exact combination carries its own indication, "indicated for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in adults and pediatric patients 12 years of age and older," so the fixed-dose product has its own FDA sign-off rather than resting purely on the two ingredients' separate evidence.[^dailymed]

No source fetched for this page compares Epiduo Forte's 0.3% adapalene formula[^dailymed] head-to-head against the original 0.1% Epiduo for efficacy or tolerability; this page makes no claim either way on that comparison.

> **Bottom line.** Adapalene 0.3% ([[adapalene]]) is solidly evidenced and benzoyl peroxide 2.5% ([[benzoyl-peroxide]]) is a well-established but mixed/low-certainty-graded active, and this fixed-dose combination has its own FDA indication for acne vulgaris in patients 12 and older. Its adapalene strength is three times the original Epiduo's; it is prescription-only.[^dailymed]

## What's In It

Active ingredients, per the DailyMed label:

- Adapalene 0.3% ([[adapalene]]), a synthetic retinoid[^dailymed]
- Benzoyl peroxide 2.5% ([[benzoyl-peroxide]])[^dailymed]

Inactive ingredients, per the same label: "acrylamide/sodium acryloyldimethyltaurate copolymer, docusate sodium, edetate disodium, glycerin, isohexadecane, poloxamer 124, polysorbate 80, propylene glycol, purified water, and sorbitan oleate."[^dailymed]
