Medik8 Crystal Retinal 6 is a 30 ml (1 fl. oz.) leave-on night serum whose active is encapsulated retinaldehyde at 0.06%, the middle rung of a six-strength Crystal Retinal ladder that runs from 0.01% to 0.24%, priced at $87.00 direct from [[medik8]] and matched by Dermstore's US listing.[^medik8us][^dermstore] It is the next step up from [[medik8-crystal-retinal-3]] (0.03%, $66.00), the strength Medik8 recommends once a user has "comfortably used Crystal Retinal 3, or those with experience of high strength retinol products."[^dermstore]

Retinaldehyde sits one enzymatic step from the receptor-active acid, closer than retinol's two steps, covered in full on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]. No published, peer-reviewed trial tests this exact serum or isolates the 0.06% concentration. Medik8 cites its own unpublished 33-participant, 12-week study on this strength specifically for its wrinkle, dark-spot, and firmness claims, discussed under Common Marketing Claims below.[^medik8us] Like every over-the-counter retinoid it remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin]].

## Summary

Medik8 Crystal Retinal 6 is a 30 ml (1 fl. oz.) leave-on night serum from [[medik8]], a British skincare brand, priced at $87.00 direct from the manufacturer's US site and matched exactly by Dermstore's US retail listing.[^medik8us][^dermstore] Its active is retinaldehyde (retinal), declared at 0.06% in the product name and independently confirmed on both the manufacturer's and Dermstore's product pages, encapsulated in the same "double-layered stabilization system" Medik8 uses across the line, including "a crystal encapsulation that releases vitamin A gradually throughout the night."[^dermstore]

What differs from the lower rungs is strength and price, not format. Medik8's own strength-finder names all six rungs and their marketed user level: Crystal Retinal 1 (0.01%, "for sensitive skin"), Crystal Retinal 3 (0.03%, "for new vitamin A users"), this Crystal Retinal 6 (0.06%, "for regular vitamin A users"), Crystal Retinal 10 (0.10%, "for advanced vitamin A users"), Crystal Retinal 20 (0.20%, "for experienced vitamin A users"), and Crystal Retinal 24 (0.24%, "for expert vitamin A users").[^medik8us] Dermstore, selling all six from the same product page, prices Crystal Retinal 3 at $66.00 and Crystal Retinal 10 at $111.00 alongside this $87.00 listing for Crystal Retinal 6, so cost rises with each step up the ladder.[^dermstore]

Retinaldehyde's category-level evidence, [set out in full on retinaldehyde and retinoids](#the-evidence), is the strongest among over-the-counter retinoid forms, but no published trial tests this exact serum or its 0.06% strength. Like every over-the-counter retinoid it remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin]], which is retinoic acid itself.

Against comparators already covered: [[medik8-crystal-retinal-3]] is the same line's next rung down, at 0.03% and $66.00, the strength a user typically holds at before moving up to this one; [[skinmedica-retinol-complex-1-0]] is a pricier ($96) retinol serum whose own percentage is undisclosed by its manufacturer; [[trader-joes-retinol-night-serum]] (about $10) and [[anua-nano-retinol-0-3-niacin-renewing-serum]] are both retinol serums declared at 0.3%, a form that needs an extra conversion step to reach retinoic acid; [[differin-adapalene-0-1-gel]] is an FDA-cleared over-the-counter retinoid with real acne trials, about $15; and [[tretinoin-0-025-cream]] is prescription retinoic acid, the benchmark every over-the-counter retinoid, including this one, remains milder than.

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinaldehyde improves aging skin, and how it compares with retinol and prescription retinoids, is covered on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]: in short, one vehicle-controlled trial plus two independent concentration-comparison trials converge on measurable improvement in fine wrinkles, roughness, and texture, the strongest over-the-counter retinoid evidence, ahead of retinol's thinner data, though not shown to match prescription [[tretinoin]]. No published trial tests this exact serum, and no cited trial isolates the 0.06% concentration.[^medik8us][^dermstore]

Medik8's own marketing claims for this strength rest on an unpublished, company-run study: "Proven via independent clinical study on 1 tube of Crystal Retinal 6. Tested on 33 participants over 12 weeks."[^medik8us] This is manufacturer-run and unpublished, not independently peer-reviewed evidence, and no cited trial isolates the 0.06% concentration specifically.

> **Bottom line.** The case for this strength rests on the same category-level retinaldehyde evidence as every other rung of the ladder, not on a trial of this specific serum or concentration. Medik8's own 33-participant study is testimonial, company-run evidence, not independent clinical data.[^medik8us]

Among over-the-counter retinoids, retinaldehyde sits a step ahead of the retinol forms such as [[trader-joes-retinol-night-serum]] and [[anua-nano-retinol-0-3-niacin-renewing-serum]]; the FDA-cleared adapalene [[differin-adapalene-0-1-gel]] works by a different route, and it remains milder than prescription [[tretinoin-0-025-cream]] (retinoic acid itself).

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Retinal... more effective and 11x faster-working than retinol."** Dermstore attributes this to three references (Fluhr 1999, Creidi et al. 1998, Siegenthaler et al. 1990), the same trio also cited on [[retinaldehyde]], but the specific "11x" figure does not appear in those trials' own reported results. The retinaldehyde evidence review already discounts this class of claim: the peer-reviewed data support that retinaldehyde ranks closer to active retinoic acid than retinol does, not a specific "times faster" multiple.[^dermstore] Cosmetic chemists Victoria Fu and Gloria Lu (Chemist Confessions) singled out this exact Medik8 claim, noting the study behind it is "not actually testing" Medik8's own retinal or formula and is "an in vitro paper in cell culture," an early metabolism study rather than a test of the finished product.[^chemistconfessions]
- **"97% felt their skin had a more youthful appearance," a 20% reduction in wrinkles, a 20% reduction in dark spots, an 11% increase in firmness, and "100% felt Crystal Retinal was gentle."** Medik8's own page attributes these to "an independent clinical study on 1 tube of Crystal Retinal 6," tested on 33 participants over 12 weeks. It is manufacturer-run and unpublished, not an independently peer-reviewed trial, and the outlet does not disclose a control arm or statistical methodology.[^medik8us]
- **"Clinically-Proven Formula" / "Dermatologist Recommended" badges.** Manufacturer-applied claim badges on the product page, not tied to a disclosed, citable trial for this strength.[^medik8us]
