CIBINQO is Pfizer's once-daily oral abrocitinib tablet, approved under NDA 213871 on 14 January 2022 as a new molecular entity.[^drugsfda] Its label carries exactly one indication, and it is a skin one: refractory moderate-to-severe [[atopic-dermatitis]] in patients 12 years and older.[^label]

> **A single-purpose drug, which is rarer than it sounds.** The other oral JAK inhibitor approved for eczema, [[rinvoq-upadacitinib-tablets]], carries nine indications and keeps its largest tablet for bowel disease. CIBINQO has one indication and three strengths that all serve it. The more interesting consequence is at the dispensing counter: which of those three a patient gets depends partly on a gene, and nobody takes the first tablet before a blood test.

## Summary

Three film-coated strengths, 50 mg (NDC 0069-0235), 100 mg (0069-0335) and 200 mg (0069-0435), all in 30-count bottles, marketed from 24 February 2022.[^ndc] The starting dose is 100 mg once daily, with 200 mg "recommended for those patients who are not responding to 100 mg once daily".[^label]

Two things drop a patient to the 50 mg tablet, and one of them is genetic. Moderate renal impairment calls for "50 mg once daily or 100 mg once daily for those patients who are not responding to 50 mg once daily", and the label gives CYP2C19 poor metabolizers the identical reduced schedule.[^label] CYP2C19 poor metabolism is an inherited trait, so the same prescription produces a different dose in different people for reasons neither the patient nor the disease controls.

The indication is written as a last resort, in nearly the same words its competitor uses: patients "whose disease is not adequately controlled with other systemic drug products, including biologics, or when use of those therapies is inadvisable".[^label] A biologic such as [[dupilumab]] comes first.

## The Rubric

**Refractory moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (oral, prescription)**

- **Effect size: strong.** Graded on [[abrocitinib]], which holds tier good; those figures are not repeated here. Pooled real-world data place it between baricitinib and upadacitinib on clearance, and [[systemic-atopic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]] ranks the three against each other.
- **Evidence quality: established.** The indication rests on randomized controlled trials, and efficacy is not the uncertain part of this product. What a reader should weigh is the entry cost in testing and the boxed warning, both below.

## The Evidence

The efficacy evidence belongs to [[abrocitinib]]. Two product-level things are worth reading on the label itself.

The first is the workup. Before the first tablet, the label requires a tuberculosis evaluation, viral hepatitis screening, and a complete blood count, and it sets numerical floors: initiation "is not recommended in patients with a platelet count <150,000/mm 3 , an absolute lymphocyte count <500/mm 3 , an absolute neutrophil count <1,000/mm 3 , or a hemoglobin value <8 g/dL".[^label] It also asks that immunizations, "including herpes zoster vaccinations", be completed first.[^label] That is a real barrier to starting, and it is the kind of thing a reader comparing an eczema pill with an eczema cream will not expect.

The second is where the boxed warning's evidence comes from, and this label is unusually candid about it. Under Mortality it describes a postmarketing safety study "in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) patients 50 years of age and older with at least one cardiovascular risk factor comparing another JAK inhibitor to TNF blocker treatment", then adds a sentence of its own: "CIBINQO is not approved for use in RA patients."[^label] The malignancy bullet is built the same way, citing a "Higher rate of lymphomas and lung cancers with another JAK inhibitor vs. TNF blockers in RA patients."[^label] The warning is a class warning, applied deliberately; the label simply does not pretend the numbers were measured on this drug in this disease.

## What's In It

One active, abrocitinib, in a film-coated tablet at 50, 100 or 200 mg.[^ndc] Nothing topical, nothing cosmetic. The cartons instruct "Do not crush, split, or chew the tablets."[^carton]

## Common Marketing Claims

Pfizer's consumer page leads with numbers, and gives the comparator every time. On itch: "More people felt LESS ITCH (11% with CIBINQO 100 mg and 26% with CIBINQO 200 mg) after 2 weeks without topical steroids vs an inactive pill (2%)."[^brand] On skin: "More people saw 75% SKIN IMPROVEMENT (42% with CIBINQO 100 mg and 62% with CIBINQO 200 mg) after 3 months without topical steroids vs an inactive pill (11%)."[^brand] It adds, unprompted, "Not everyone will respond to CIBINQO."[^brand]

This is worth naming because the comparison is close to a controlled experiment in disclosure. AbbVie's consumer page for the rival drug in the same indication claims "75% skin clearance" without a proportion, a comparator, or a definition of what the 75% measures. Pfizer's page writes the same endpoint as "75% SKIN IMPROVEMENT", attaches the share of patients who reached it, and names the placebo rate. Same drug class, same disease, same regulator, opposite habits.

The page's own framing is promotional in the ordinary way. It calls the drug "Steroid-free" and says it "fights day and night, flaring or not", which is marketing language rather than a trial result.[^brand]
