Naturium Niacinamide Serum 12% + Zinc 2% is a mass-market leave-on serum built around 12% niacinamide and 2% zinc PCA, sold in 30 ml and 60 ml sizes through the brand's own site and Target.[^naturium][^target]

> A budget niacinamide serum at a higher concentration than most peers. Its 12% dose is above the lower concentrations at which niacinamide's health-oriented uses, barrier support, hyperpigmentation, and mild acne, have been studied, a case set out on [[niacinamide]]; no trial tests this exact 12% formula. The manufacturer's "clinically proven" claim for dark spots, pores, and fine lines rests on a manufacturer 8-week study not cited to any published journal, with no sample size, control arm, or peer review disclosed.[^naturium] At $17 for 30 ml from the manufacturer, discounted to $13.99 at Target, it is inexpensive relative to the category.[^naturium][^target]

## Summary

Niacinamide Serum 12% + Zinc 2% is Naturium's plain niacinamide serum, formulated with [[niacinamide]], a form of vitamin B3, at 12%, paired with 2% zinc PCA, a zinc salt.[^naturium] Both concentrations are declared on the manufacturer's own product page, and a Target listing for the same SKU independently confirms an identical ingredient order.[^naturium][^target] The general case for topical niacinamide, modest but real evidence for supporting the skin barrier, lightening facial hyperpigmentation, and mild inflammatory acne, drawn mostly from small or manufacturer-run trials, is set out on [[niacinamide]]; that evidence base used lower concentrations than the 12% in this product.

What distinguishes this serum from peers such as [[the-ordinary-niacinamide-10-zinc-1|The Ordinary's 10% + Zinc 1%]] is mainly the printed strength: at 12%, its niacinamide concentration is higher than most plain niacinamide serums on the market, [above the range niacinamide's health-oriented benefits have actually been tested in](#the-evidence). No published trial isolates this product or its exact 12%/2% combination.

The manufacturer's headline claim, that the serum is "clinically proven to reduce the appearance of dark spots, pores, plus fine lines and wrinkles," is attributed to "an 8-week clinical study on subjects, representation across all skin types & self-perceived sensitive skin," with no sample size, control or vehicle arm, statistical result, or peer review disclosed.[^naturium] That claim, and the rest of the label's marketing language, is weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims). It is listed at $17 for 30 ml on the manufacturer's site and $13.99 (discounted from $16.99) for the same size at Target (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^naturium][^target]

## The Evidence

Topical niacinamide is a well-tolerated active with modest evidence for supporting the skin barrier, lightening facial hyperpigmentation, and mild inflammatory acne; how each use grades out, and how much of that evidence rests on small or manufacturer-run trials, is set out in full on [[niacinamide]]. Nothing specific to this product was found: no published trial tests Naturium's 12% niacinamide plus 2% zinc PCA formula, so its grades above rest on the general category evidence for niacinamide, all of which was generated at lower concentrations than 12%.

The manufacturer's declared ingredient list runs water, niacinamide, propanediol, glycerin, then zinc PCA fifth, with niacinamide and zinc PCA the only two named actives; neither is paired with a third treatment active.[^naturium] A Target listing for the same SKU records an identical order, independently confirming the formula.[^target]

> Bottom line. Niacinamide at 12% carries the same modest, mostly small-trial case for barrier support, hyperpigmentation, and mild acne set out on [[niacinamide]], none of it tested at this concentration or in this exact formula.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Clinically proven to reduce the appearance of dark spots, pores, plus fine lines and wrinkles."** Attributed by the manufacturer to "an 8-week clinical study on subjects, representation across all skin types & self-perceived sensitive skin," with no sample size, control arm, statistical result, or peer review described.[^naturium]
- **"Helps reduce the appearance of pores."** Niacinamide's better-evidenced topical uses, set out on [[niacinamide]], are barrier support, facial hyperpigmentation, and mild acne; pore-appearance is not among the trials cited there.
- **"Reduces the look of dark spots, for a more even looking complexion."** The pigment evidence on [[niacinamide]] is specifically for facial hyperpigmentation and melasma in small trials at lower concentrations than this product's 12%.
- **The 2% zinc PCA.** Included alongside niacinamide; its independent contribution in this formula is not established by any cited trial.[^naturium]
