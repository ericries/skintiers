Anua Rice 70 Glow Milky Toner is a Korean milky toner whose aqueous base is 71% rice bran water, carrying niacinamide, a separate and much smaller dose of rice extract, three ceramide types, and hyaluronic acid, sold in a 250 ml bottle for about $23.[^anua][^stylevana]

> The brand markets the toner for hydration and a "glass skin" glow; the glow and radiance claims are cosmetic, and the pigment-brightening evidence for niacinamide, the toner's one evidenced active, is specifically for facial hyperpigmentation rather than generic dullness, covered on [[niacinamide]]. No published trial tests this toner, its 71% rice bran water base, or its separate rice extract.[^anua]

## Summary

Anua Rice 70 Glow Milky Toner is a lightweight, milky, fragrance-free toner positioned for hydration and a bright, "glass skin" finish.[^anua] Its base is unusual for a toner: rather than being mostly water, it is 71% Oryza Sativa (rice) bran water, with niacinamide appearing fifth in the ingredient list, ahead of both propanediol and plain water further down the list.[^anua] A second, much smaller ingredient, Oryza Sativa (rice) extract, is declared separately at 3,753 ppm (about 0.38%), low in the order; the "Rice 70" name refers to the bran-water base, not to this extract.[^anua]

Niacinamide is the formula's one active with an established evidence base. Topical niacinamide is well tolerated and has modest evidence for lightening facial hyperpigmentation and for supporting the skin barrier and lowering water loss, most of it from small or manufacturer-run studies; that case is set out on [[niacinamide]]. This toner does not state a niacinamide concentration. The brand's own "brighten your skin" and "radiant" language conflates that pigment-specific evidence with a general glow claim on already-even skin, which is a cosmetic rather than a health effect, and the featured 71% rice bran water and the separate rice extract have no cited clinical trial behind them.[^anua]

The rest of the formula backs the hydration and barrier claims: three ceramide types (NP, AP, EOP) alongside cholesterol and phytosphingosine, three forms of hyaluronic acid, and panthenol and betaine, all humectant or barrier-support ingredients rather than actives with their own clinical trials (see [What's In It](#whats-in-it)).[^anua] The manufacturer's own before/after "Testing Results" figures are quarantined under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims) rather than treated as independent evidence.

It is priced at $23.00 on the brand's own US site and, at the Korean-beauty retailer Stylevana, $21.00 for the 250 ml size, as of 2026-08-07.[^anua][^stylevana]

## The Evidence

No published trial tests this toner, its 71% rice bran water base, or its separate rice extract.[^anua] The product's one active with a documented clinical case is niacinamide: topical niacinamide has modest, mostly small-trial evidence for lightening facial hyperpigmentation and for supporting the skin barrier and lowering water loss in dry skin, set out in full on [[niacinamide]]. That evidence was generated at declared concentrations; this toner does not state how much niacinamide it contains, so the product's own effect size cannot be estimated from the general niacinamide case.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on the brand's own US product page.[^anua] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

Actives and notable ingredients:

- Oryza Sativa (rice) bran water, 71% of the formula and its aqueous base
- [[niacinamide]], fifth on the ingredient list with no declared concentration
- Oryza Sativa (rice) extract, declared separately at 3,753 ppm (about 0.38%), low in the list
- three ceramide types (Ceramide NP, Ceramide AP, Ceramide EOP) alongside phytosphingosine and cholesterol, the lipids typically paired with ceramides for barrier support
- three forms of hyaluronic acid (sodium hyaluronate, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, and hyaluronic acid) and hydrolyzed rice protein (3,000 ppb)
- [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|ascorbic acid]] and arbutin, both low in the list

Base, texture, and preservative system: butylene glycol, [[glycerin]], dipropylene glycol, propanediol, 1,2-hexanediol, methyl gluceth-20, panthenol, betaine, hydroxyacetophenone, diphenyl dimethicone, triethylhexanoin, hydrogenated lecithin, adenosine, ethylhexylglycerin, carbomer, tromethamine, xanthan gum, glyceryl acrylate/acrylic acid copolymer, Theobroma cacao (cocoa) seed extract, dextrin, sodium lauroyl lactylate, and allantoin.[^anua]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by independent, product-specific evidence:

- **"Provides temporary moisture immediately after only 1 use" and "Balancing oily skin after 4 weeks of use."** The brand's own "Testing Results" summary line. No sample size, design, or independent report of these results is disclosed on the product page.[^anua]
- **"After 4 weeks of use, balancing excess sebum improved by 12.9%," with "100% of participants" noticing less oil and "95%" reporting a "more radiant and glowy complexion."** A manufacturer-run figure with no disclosed sample size, comparator, or independent publication.[^anua]
- **"70% Anua Rice Water+ and Niacinamide to brighten your skin and provide an even, radiant complexion."** Niacinamide's evidenced pigment effect on [[niacinamide]] is specifically for facial hyperpigmentation; a general radiance or glow claim on already-even skin is beyond what that evidence tested, and the rice bran water base has no cited clinical trial of its own.[^anua]
- **"Ultimate Rice Toner To Achieve Glass Skin."** A cosmetic-finish positioning claim, not a measured outcome.[^anua]
