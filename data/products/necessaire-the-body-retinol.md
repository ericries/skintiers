Necessaire The Body Retinol is a $65.00, 150 ml serum-in-balm body treatment listing 0.1% retinol alongside niacinamide, glycolic acid, and mandelic acid, sold on the brand's own site as an off-face counterpart to facial retinol products.[^necessaire]

> A genuine, disclosed-concentration retinol, at a dose that sits in the range shown effective for facial photoaging, moved to the body where the trial evidence is thinner. [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]' vehicle-controlled trials back the category; this specific serum has only the brand's own small, uncontrolled 4-week self-report study behind it, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence).[^necessaire]

## Summary

The Body Retinol is a leave-on, nighttime "serum-in-balm" sold in a 150 ml / 5.1 fl oz airless package for $65.00 on Necessaire's own site.[^necessaire] The brand names the concentration directly in the product title and description: "0.1% Pure Encapsulated Retinol" paired with glycerin and "Vitamin B3" (niacinamide) to "help improve the overall texture and tone of skin."[^necessaire]

The disclosed active is [[retinol|retinol]] itself, the same well-studied over-the-counter retinoid covered in depth on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]], here formulated for use on the body rather than the face. [[niacinamide|Niacinamide]] is named in the marketing copy as a co-active; the ingredient list also shows glycolic acid and mandelic acid high in the list (ahead of niacinamide and retinol), meaning this is not a retinol-only formula but a retinoid-plus-AHA body treatment, [set out in full below](#whats-in-it).[^necessaire]

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinol improves fine wrinkles, texture, and pigment is a category question covered on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]: vehicle-controlled facial trials support gradual improvement at concentrations including 0.1%, the same dose this product discloses, though those trials tested facial skin, not body skin.[^necessaire] No published, peer-reviewed trial of this product, or of retinol on body skin generally at a matching dose, was found.

Necessaire's own product page cites a brand-run study: "Clinical Study: 30+ Panelists | 4 Weeks | Third-Party Verified Clinic | Q2 2023," reporting "85% Reported More Even Skin Texture," "75% Reported More Even Skin Tone," "85% Reported Less Crepiness," "82% Reported Less Lines," and "82% Reported Less Wrinkles."[^necessaire] These are the brand's own self-reported perception percentages from a small, short, uncontrolled panel, not a peer-reviewed vehicle-controlled trial with objective measurements; no methodology, panel demographics, or statistical analysis were published alongside the numbers.

> **Bottom line.** The retinol case rests on category-level facial evidence at a matching 0.1% dose, extrapolated to the body; the product-specific claims rest on an unpublished, uncontrolled 4-week brand survey, not an independent trial.[^necessaire]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), declared on the manufacturer's own product page.[^necessaire] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

Actives and support ingredients, in listed order:

- Aqua/Water/Eau, glycerin, humectants
- Rosa Damascena (rose) flower water, fragrance/scent ingredient (the brand states it is added "to improve the scent" given the smell of the high retinol percentage)
- Caprylic/capric triglyceride, cetearyl alcohol, coconut alkanes, emollients
- [[glycolic-acid|Glycolic acid]] and [[mandelic-acid|mandelic acid]], alpha hydroxy acids, listed ahead of niacinamide and retinol
- [[niacinamide|Niacinamide]] (Vitamin B3)
- [[retinol|Retinol]], disclosed at 0.1% in the product name and description
- Palmitoyl tripeptide-1, palmitoyl tripeptide-5, palmitoyl hexapeptide-12, Nicotiana benthamiana hexapeptide-40, sh-polypeptide-76, peptide complex
- [[tocopherol]] and tetrahexyldecyl ascorbate, vitamin E and a vitamin C ester
- Sodium hyaluronate, humectant

Base, texture, and preservative system: 1,2-hexanediol, cetearyl olivate, sorbitan olivate, propanediol, sodium hydroxide, hydroxyethyl acrylate/sodium acryloyldimethyl taurate copolymer, silica, silica silylate, magnesium stearate, cellulose acetate butyrate, C13-14 alkane, C15-23 alkane, tricaprylin, butylene glycol, pentaerythrityl tetra-di-t-butyl hydroxyhydrocinnamate, citric acid, Hippophae rhamnoides (sea buckthorn) seed oil, coco-caprylate/caprate, decyl glucoside, sodium benzoate, potassium sorbate, sodium carbonate, sodium chloride, hydroxyacetophenone, citronellol, and geraniol (the brand states the last two are residual byproducts of the rosewater, not added synthetic fragrance).[^necessaire]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"With continued use The Body Retinol helps minimize crepiness lines, wrinkles, spots, and marks."**[^necessaire] This rests on the brand's own uncontrolled, unpublished 4-week panel study described above, not an independent peer-reviewed trial of this product.
- **"Dermatologist-Tested. Hypoallergenic. Non-Comedogenic."**[^necessaire] Manufacturer testing and formulation claims; no independent study details or methodology were found published.
- **"Treat Your Body Like Your Face."**[^necessaire] A brand positioning tagline, not a clinical claim; the underlying retinol evidence for facial skin is real (graded on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]) but has not been established for body skin by an independent trial of this or a comparable body formula.
