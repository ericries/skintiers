EltaMD UV Daily Tinted Broad-Spectrum SPF 40 is a lightweight, tinted facial sunscreen lotion from EltaMD, an FDA OTC-monograph drug that pairs a chemical UVB filter, octinoxate 7.5%, with a mineral broad-spectrum filter, zinc oxide 9.0%, plus an iron oxide tint the brand markets for evening out skin tone.[^dailymed][^eltamd] It is not a mineral-only sunscreen despite the "UV Daily" naming; the FDA label confirms the hybrid filter combination.[^dailymed]

> A genuine Broad Spectrum SPF 40 sunscreen, established UVB protection from octinoxate and established UVA protection from zinc oxide, both well inside the OTC sunscreen monograph.[^dailymed] The tint adds iron oxides, which is where the case gets thinner: iron oxides plausibly help block visible light, a wavelength UV filters alone don't stop and one implicated in melasma and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, but EltaMD does not disclose an iron oxide percentage for this product, so [that part of the claim rests on the ingredient class, not a tested dose in this cream](#the-evidence).[^dailymed][^eltamd]

## Summary

UV Daily Tinted is a 1.7 oz (48 g) lotion, also sold in a 0.5 oz (14 g) mini, priced at $43.00 for the full size on EltaMD's own site.[^eltamd] The FDA Drug Facts active-ingredient section lists octinoxate 7.5% and zinc oxide 9.0%, both Sunscreen-purpose actives, making this a chemical-and-mineral hybrid rather than a pure-mineral formula.[^dailymed] The health job here is broad-spectrum UV protection, covered in full under [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; the tint is a secondary cosmetic feature built on iron oxides, listed last among the inactive ingredients on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

EltaMD's own product description calls it "a lightweight moisturizing sunscreen with a sheer tint to help even skin tone," formulated with hyaluronic acid, and states it "works best for medium light, medium or medium deep skin tones."[^eltamd] The brand positions it for normal, dry, and combination skin, and describes it as fragrance-free, paraben-free, and non-comedogenic.[^eltamd]

## The Evidence

The core, well-established health benefit is broad-spectrum photoprotection: as an OTC-monograph sunscreen tested to Broad Spectrum SPF 40, it protects against sunburn (UVB) via octinoxate 7.5% and provides broad-spectrum coverage via zinc oxide 9.0%, which also supplies the UVA protection since octinoxate is UVB-only.[^dailymed] No published trial tests this exact product; the case for the filters rests on the FDA OTC monograph and the general evidence on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

The tinted, iron-oxide component is a separate and more modest claim. Unlike UVA/UVB filters, iron oxides absorb visible light, and dermatology sources reviewed on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] describe tinted, iron-oxide-containing sunscreens as a useful tool for pigmentation-prone skin, since visible light can worsen [[melasma]] and post-inflammatory [[hyperpigmentation]] even when a non-tinted broad-spectrum sunscreen is used correctly. EltaMD's own label and product page do not disclose an iron oxide percentage for this product, so that benefit is graded modest and preliminary here, resting on the ingredient class rather than a tested dose in this specific cream.[^dailymed][^eltamd]

## What's In It

- **Active ingredients:** Octinoxate 7.5% (Sunscreen) and Zinc Oxide 9.0% (Sunscreen), as declared on the FDA Drug Facts label.[^dailymed]
- **Uses (per label):** "Helps prevent sunburn. If used as directed with other sun protection measure (See Directions), decreases the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging caused by the sun."[^dailymed]
- **Inactive ingredients (verbatim from the FDA label):** water, petrolatum, isopropyl palmitate, dimethicone, cetearyl alcohol, oleth-3 phosphate, phenoxyethanol, hydroxyethyl acrylate/sodium acryloyldimethyl taurate copolymer, polyisobutene, cetearyl glucoside, polyether-1, butylene glycol, PEG-7 trimethylolpropane coconut ether, sodium hyaluronate, iodopropynyl butylcarbamate, citric acid, tocopheryl acetate, triethoxycaprylylsilane, iron oxides.[^dailymed] The iron oxides supply the tint; the sodium hyaluronate is EltaMD's marketed [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]] ingredient.
- **Directions (per label):** "Apply liberally 15 minutes before sun exposure. Use a water-resistant sunscreen if swimming or sweating. Reapply at least every 2 hours."[^dailymed]
- **Warnings (per label):** "For external use only. Do not use on damaged or broken skin. When using this product keep out of eyes. Rinse with water to remove. Stop use and ask a physician if rash occurs. If product is swallowed get medical help or contact a Poison Control Center right away."[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"A lightweight moisturizing sunscreen with a sheer tint to help even skin tone."**[^eltamd] The SPF 40 broad-spectrum protection is FDA-substantiated; "moisturizing" and the tint's tone-evening effect are the brand's own cosmetic framing, and the iron oxide dose behind the tint claim is not disclosed.
- **"Formulated with hyaluronic acid to help reduce the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles."**[^eltamd] Hyaluronic acid's general humectant evidence is graded independently on [[hyaluronic-acid]]; this product does not disclose a hyaluronic acid concentration, so the claim is not tested at a product-specific dose.
- **"Works best for medium light, medium or medium deep skin tones."**[^eltamd] A brand shade-matching disclosure, not an efficacy claim; it signals the tint is not formulated as a universal match across the full skin-tone range.
