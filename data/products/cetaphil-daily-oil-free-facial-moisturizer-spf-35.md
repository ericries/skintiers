Cetaphil Daily Facial Moisturizer with Sunscreen SPF 35 is a lightweight, oil-free daily face lotion from [[cetaphil]] that pairs a four-filter chemical sunscreen with a moisturizer base, sold by Galderma in a 3 fl oz pump bottle.[^dailymed][^cetaphil]

> A "moisturizer with sunscreen" whose FDA drug label lists four active filters: avobenzone 2.8%, homosalate 9.5%, octisalate 4.8%, and octocrylene 7%.[^dailymed] Unlike its sibling [[cetaphil-daily-facial-moisturizer-spf-50|SPF 50 lotion]], which relies on oxybenzone and titanium dioxide for UVA reach with no avobenzone, this SPF 35 lotion carries a dedicated long-UVA absorber, avobenzone, alongside its UVB-weighted filters. Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case is well evidenced at the category level, covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; no trial isolates this specific lotion, so its grade rests on that category evidence and its filter chemistry. The "oil-free" and "matte finish" claims for oily or combination skin are the brand's own cosmetic positioning, not health claims.

## Summary

This is Cetaphil's daily oil-free facial moisturizer with built-in SPF 35, sold in a 3 fl oz pump. Its FDA drug label (via DailyMed) gives the active ingredients as avobenzone 2.8%, homosalate 9.5%, octisalate 4.8%, and octocrylene 7%, all confirmed on the same label as "Broad Spectrum" sunscreen actives, purpose "Sunscreen."[^dailymed] The product's principal display panel carries the "Oil-Free" and "All Skin Types" descriptors that give the product its name, alongside the Skin Cancer Foundation Seal of approval.[^dailymed] The brand's own product page describes it as providing "immediate oil-free hydration that lasts for 24 hours" with a "matte, satin finish," and lists it as suited to all skin types.[^cetaphil]

The health case for using any broad-spectrum sunscreen daily, slowing photoaging and reducing sun-related skin-cancer risk, has randomized-trial support and is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; that is where the evidenced value of this product lies, not in its moisturizer base or its oil-control marketing.[^dailymed] The moisturization and "oil-free" mattifying feel come from the lotion's base (glycerin, dipropylene glycol, panthenol, niacinamide, and a silicone-free emulsifier system) and are cosmetic, not health, benefits; the broader evidence for oil control in general is graded on [[oily-skin]] and for moisturizing bases on [[moisturizing]].[^dailymed]

The filter set worth flagging is its UVA coverage. It relies on [[avobenzone]] 2.8% for long-wave UVA1 absorption, backed by [[homosalate]] 9.5% and [[octisalate]] 4.8% for UVB, and [[octocrylene]] 7%, which also helps stabilize avobenzone against photodegradation.[^dailymed] That is a meaningfully different filter chemistry from the brand's own [[cetaphil-daily-facial-moisturizer-spf-50|SPF 50 sibling]], which uses octinoxate, octisalate, octocrylene, oxybenzone, and titanium dioxide with no avobenzone at all; the general evidence and controversy around individual filters (oxybenzone's systemic absorption, homosalate's endocrine questions) is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific lotion was located, so the product-specific facts available are its filter composition and its FDA-labeled Broad Spectrum SPF 35 rating.

The active filters, avobenzone 2.8%, homosalate 9.5%, octisalate 4.8%, and octocrylene 7%, come directly from the product's FDA drug label on DailyMed (labeler Galderma Laboratories, L.P.).[^dailymed] Homosalate and octisalate are UVB-weighted organic filters; avobenzone is the primary long-wave UVA1 absorber in US-approved organic filters, and octocrylene is both a UVB filter and a common photostabilizer for avobenzone, since avobenzone degrades under UV exposure unless paired with a stabilizer.[^dailymed] The set contains no titanium dioxide or zinc oxide, so its UVB protection rests entirely on the two organic UVB filters plus avobenzone and octocrylene's own modest UVB contribution.[^dailymed] The label carries an FDA Broad Spectrum claim, which requires passing a critical-wavelength test, and the product carton also displays the Skin Cancer Foundation Seal of approval.[^dailymed]

The label's own "Uses" statement is narrow: "helps prevent sunburn" and, "if used as directed with other sun protection measures," "decreases the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging caused by the sun."[^dailymed] That is the standard FDA monograph language for OTC sunscreens, not a product-specific trial result. The moisturizer base, water, glycerin, dipropylene glycol, and panthenol lead the inactive list and deliver the "daily moisturizer" and "oil-free" positioning, but that is cosmetic hydration; the evidenced benefit of the product is the UV protection, covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^dailymed]

> **Bottom line.** A retail-labeled Broad Spectrum SPF 35 daily moisturizer whose FDA label confirms a four-filter set including avobenzone, giving it a dedicated long-UVA absorber the brand's own SPF 50 sibling lacks. The sunscreen is the reason to use it; the "oil-free" and "matte finish" claims are the brand's own cosmetic marketing, not an independently tested oil-reduction result.[^dailymed][^cetaphil]
