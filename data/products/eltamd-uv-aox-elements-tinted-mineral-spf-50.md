EltaMD UV AOX Elements is a tinted 100% mineral broad-spectrum SPF 50 face sunscreen, the antioxidant-enriched successor EltaMD now sells in place of its discontinued UV Elements SPF 44, sold at about $46 for 1.7 oz.[^brand]

> A solid, dermatologist-favorite tinted mineral sunscreen. The FDA Drug Facts active ingredients are "Titanium Dioxide 4.4%" and "Zinc Oxide 13.5%," a 100% mineral SPF 50, so the core job (broad-spectrum sun protection) is genuine and well established (see [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]).[^dailymed] The iron-oxide tint is a real bonus: it adds visible-light protection useful for [[melasma]]. The antioxidant and anti-aging extras are pleasant but are backed by the brand's own small panel study, not independent trials.

## Summary

This is EltaMD's tinted mineral daily sunscreen. Note the naming: the older UV Elements SPF 44 was reformulated into UV AOX Elements SPF 50, which adds antioxidants; the tinted mineral EltaMD sold today is this SPF 50 version.[^brand] It is regulated as an OTC sunscreen: the Drug Facts list "Titanium Dioxide 4.4%" and "Zinc Oxide 13.5%" as actives, purpose "Sunscreen," used to help "prevent sunburn" and, with other measures, decrease the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging.[^dailymed]

Its value is what any good SPF 50 mineral offers, set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] and [[zinc-oxide]], plus a cosmetic tint from iron oxides that both evens skin tone and blocks some visible light. It is fragrance-free, water-resistant for 40 minutes, and marketed as gentle enough for post-procedure and sensitive skin.[^brand]

## The Evidence

The core, well-supported claim is photoprotection: this is a compliant broad-spectrum SPF 50 built on zinc oxide and titanium dioxide, and the evidence that such sunscreens prevent sunburn, skin cancer, and photoaging is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^dailymed] The iron-oxide tint adds protection against visible light, which is why tinted mineral sunscreens are preferred for [[melasma]] and post-inflammatory pigmentation.

The brand's cosmetic claims, "improve signs of aging in just 4 weeks," "reduce skin dullness by 22%," "even skin tone by 14%," and "improve skin texture by 44%, with 96% of subjects showed improvements in overall texture," are footnoted to "a 12 week study on 31 subjects," i.e. the brand's own small consumer/clinical panel, not an independent randomized trial.[^brand] They are plausible for a well-formulated tinted sunscreen with antioxidants but should be read as marketing, not proof.

> **Bottom line.** An excellent everyday tinted mineral SPF 50: reliable broad-spectrum protection from zinc oxide and titanium dioxide, with an iron-oxide tint that helps with visible light and pigment.[^dailymed] Buy it for the sun protection and the cosmetically elegant tint; treat the antioxidant anti-aging numbers as brand panel data, and see [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] for what a sunscreen can and cannot do.

## What's In It

Active ingredients, from the FDA Drug Facts: "Titanium Dioxide 4.4%" and "Zinc Oxide 13.5%."[^dailymed] Beyond the mineral filters, the brand highlights vitamin E and an ascorbyl palmitate (vitamin C ester) as antioxidants, sodium hyaluronate for hydration, and bisabolol plus ginger root extract to calm the skin; iron oxides provide the tint.[^brand] It is dye-free, fragrance-free, and non-comedogenic.[^brand]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Clinically proven" to improve dullness, tone, and texture.** Brand claims footnoted to a 12-week study on 31 subjects, the company's own panel, not an independent trial.[^brand]
- **"Blocks 56% HEV (blue light)."** A brand figure for the tinted formula; visible-light protection from iron oxides is real and relevant to [[melasma]], though the exact percentage is the brand's own measurement.[^brand]
- **"100% mineral," "gentle enough for sensitive, post-procedure skin."** Consistent with an all-mineral zinc-oxide/titanium-dioxide formula; "post-procedure" refers to testing after a chemical peel.[^brand]
