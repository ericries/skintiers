Avène RetrinAL 0.1 Intensive Cream is a 0.1%-labeled retinaldehyde anti-aging face, neck, and décolletage cream from [[avene|Avène]], the French dermocosmetics brand built on Avène thermal spring water, sold in the US at $39.99 for 0.5 fl oz ($79.98 per fluid ounce) per Target's listing.[^target]

> Retinaldehyde is the best-evidenced over-the-counter retinoid form, one enzymatic step closer to active retinoic acid than retinol, with modest but real evidence for wrinkle and texture improvement; see [the evidence](#the-evidence) and [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]. This cream states its 0.1% strength directly in the product name, a labeled concentration most competing retinaldehyde serums do not disclose, but no trial tests this specific formula, and its marketing statistics rest on the brand's own unpublished testing.[^target]

## Summary

Avène RetrinAL 0.1 Intensive Cream pairs 0.1% retinaldehyde (listed as "retinal" in the ingredient list) with niacinamide, adenosine, sodium hyaluronate, and a shea-butter and squalane emollient base, in a fragrance-free formula sold as suitable for sensitive skin.[^target] Avène also markets the RetrinAL line in Europe; this profile covers the US-market SKU stocked by Target, whose listing sources the price, ingredients, and directions cited throughout.[^target]

Whether topical retinaldehyde improves aging skin is covered on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]: it is the best-evidenced over-the-counter retinoid, closer to the active retinoic-acid molecule than [[retinol]], with modest, category-level evidence for wrinkles, roughness, and texture over the vehicle and a smaller pigmentation effect at higher concentrations. No published trial tests this exact cream, so its grade rests on that category evidence and on the strength being labeled rather than hidden. That labeling is itself a distinguishing feature: unlike [[youth-to-the-people-retinal-niacinamide-serum]], whose retailers do not disclose a retinal percentage, this product names 0.1% directly, a strength comparable to the higher rungs of the labeled ladder sold as [[medik8-crystal-retinal-3]] (0.03%).

Niacinamide also appears in the ingredient list, unranked and without a declared percentage, positioned as a support ingredient rather than a headline active; its own barrier and tone evidence is covered on [[niacinamide]]. Target's directions state the cream is meant to be applied "to the face, neck and décolleté in the evening," and that it "can be used after 7 days post-superficial aesthetic procedure or once skin has re-epithelialized."[^target] At $39.99 for 0.5 fl oz, it prices above mass-market retinol serums and in the same premium tier as [[medik8-crystal-retinal-3]].

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinaldehyde improves photoaged skin, and how it compares with retinol and prescription retinoids, is covered on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]: in short, it carries the strongest over-the-counter retinoid evidence, ahead of retinol's thinner data, though not shown to match prescription [[tretinoin]]. No published trial tests this specific cream or isolates its 0.1% strength. The label itself is the only source for that concentration; Target's listing confirms the "0.1%" figure in the product title but does not disclose where "retinal" falls by percentage weight within the ingredient list, so the strength cannot be independently checked against the trial doses covered on the retinaldehyde page.[^target]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), as declared on Target's US retailer listing, in the order given:[^target]

- Avene thermal spring water (avene aqua), the brand's signature base ingredient
- Caprylic/capric triglyceride, glycerin, squalane, emollients and a humectant ([[glycerin]])
- Butyrospermum parkii (shea) butter, carthamus tinctorius (safflower) seed oil, emollients
- Pentaerythrityl tetracaprylate/tetracaprate, cetearyl alcohol, behenyl alcohol, texture and emulsifying agents
- Niacinamide ([[niacinamide]]), no percentage disclosed

- Sodium benzoate, a preservative
- Sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]]), a humectant
- Sodium hydroxide, a pH adjuster

- Xanthan gum, a texture agent

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims from Target's listing, not established by independent, product-specific evidence above:

- **"92% saw improvement in skin firmness."** Target's listing states this figure; no underlying study, sample size, or methodology is disclosed, and no independent trial of this cream is cited here.[^target]
- **Retinaldehyde is "3X more powerful than retinol" and "10X faster than Retinol."** Target's listing states this comparison; no cited trial of this product or its retinaldehyde concentration supports the specific multiples.[^target]
- **"97% Ingredients of Natural Origin."** A formulation claim from Target's listing, not an efficacy claim.[^target]
