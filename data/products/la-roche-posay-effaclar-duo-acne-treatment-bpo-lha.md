This is the benzoyl peroxide member of the Effaclar Duo family: an acne lotion whose active is "Benzoyl peroxide 5.5%" with purpose "Acne medication", and whose inactive list includes capryloyl salicylic acid, the salicylic acid derivative La Roche-Posay markets as LHA.[^dailymed] It is a different product from both the salicylic acid cream on [[la-roche-posay-effaclar-duo]] and the single-active cream on [[la-roche-posay-effaclar-bpo]].

> **Bottom line.** Three products in one brand's acne line share the Effaclar Duo name and do not share a formula, which is the main thing to get right before buying. This one is **5.5% benzoyl peroxide plus the brand's LHA**, and the benzoyl peroxide is what carries the evidence (see [[benzoyl-peroxide]]). The LHA is declared among the **inactive** ingredients, so it is not a labelled acne active, and nothing cited here shows the pair beats benzoyl peroxide alone. **Availability is uncertain:** its FDA label is live and not end-dated, but it appears to have been superseded in the US by the newer single-active product.

## Summary

The FDA listing records this as an **OTC Monograph Drug** under monograph M006, labeller L'Oreal USA, Inc., with a marketing start date of 1 July 2012 and no marketing end date populated; the label was last updated 31 December 2023. It is listed in 40 mL and 20 mL tubes and a 3 mL packet.[^dailymed]

Drug Facts: active "Benzoyl peroxide 5.5%", purpose "Acne medication", uses "for the treatment of acne dries and clears acne blemishes and allows skin to heal".[^dailymed]

The inactive ingredients are water, isostearyl alcohol, glycerin, pentylene glycol, silica, carbomer, **capryloyl salicylic acid**, potassium hydroxide, tocopheryl acetate, an acrylates crosspolymer, disodium EDTA and epilobium angustifolium extract.[^dailymed]

That placement is the point worth understanding. Capryloyl salicylic acid is a real exfoliating ingredient, but on the label it sits with the inactives, not as a second active with its own monograph purpose. A reader comparing this against the plain [[la-roche-posay-effaclar-bpo]] is comparing the same benzoyl peroxide strength with and without that addition, and no evidence cited here settles whether the addition helps.

## The Evidence

No trial of this finished lotion is cited here, so the grade rests on the active, and that active is a strong one. Benzoyl peroxide is a first-line topical for inflammatory acne with decades of controlled evidence and no resistance problem, set out on [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and not repeated here. At 5.5% it sits in the usual over-the-counter range.[^dailymed]

The LHA is where the claim outruns the evidence. Capryloyl salicylic acid is a lipid-soluble salicylic acid derivative, and the general case for salicylic acid as an acne active is on [[salicylic-acid]]. What is missing is anything comparing this combination against benzoyl peroxide on its own. Without that, the second ingredient describes the formula rather than a demonstrated advantage, and the honest reading is that you are buying a well-evidenced active with a plausible companion ingredient.

## Is It Still Sold?

Treat availability as unresolved rather than settled. Its FDA Structured Product Label remains live, carries a 2012 marketing start date and **no marketing end date**, and was updated as recently as December 2023.[^dailymed] Against that, La Roche-Posay has since launched the single-active [[la-roche-posay-effaclar-bpo]], listed on DailyMed with a May 2026 marketing start, which looks like a successor. Search results indicate the brand's own site lists this older product as discontinued, but that page returns HTTP 403 to both a direct fetch and a rendering browser, so it could not be read and is not relied on here. If you want benzoyl peroxide from this brand today, the newer product is the one with current labelling.
