## Summary

Topicals Faded is a popular brightening serum for dark spots and discoloration that blends several evidence-backed pigmentation actives, tranexamic acid, azelaic acid, niacinamide, kojic acid, and melatonin, in a 30 ml tube at $28.[^topicals] Its honest weakness is transparency: Topicals discloses no concentration for any active, so there is no way to know whether they are dosed at effective levels, and the only product-specific evidence is a two-week consumer-perception study run by the brand. The evidence for the actives themselves lives on their ingredient pages.

## The Evidence

The case for Faded rests on its ingredient list, not on the product. The strongest actives in the blend are tranexamic acid and azelaic acid ([[tranexamic-acid]] and [[azelaic-acid]] cover their pigmentation evidence), with niacinamide and kojic acid contributing more modestly ([[niacinamide]], [[kojic-acid]]); melatonin is the weakest-evidenced. None of this establishes what THIS product does, for two reasons. First, Topicals publishes no concentrations, and pigmentation actives are dose-dependent, so a name on the label does not guarantee an effective dose. Second, there is no independent controlled trial of the finished serum. The brand's own "Faded in two weeks" data comes from "a consumer study after 2 weeks of daily use," reporting that "92.31% of users reported an improvement in overall skin appearance" and "94.87% noticed smoother skin texture."[^topicals] Those are self-reported perceptions from a brand-run study, not blinded, vehicle-controlled measurements.

## What's In It

Topicals lists five key actives, in this order and without concentrations: tranexamic acid, niacinamide, azelaic acid, kojic acid, and melatonin.[^topicals] The serum was reformulated into a "100% odorless formula," removing the glutathione that gave earlier versions a sulfur smell.[^topicals] It is sold as a 30 ml / 1 fl oz tube and directed for use once or twice a day after cleansing, with SPF in the daytime and not layered directly with AHAs, BHAs, or retinoids.[^topicals]

## Common Marketing Claims

Topicals markets Faded as "clinically proven to visibly even skin tone and texture, and reduce the look of dark spots, post-breakout marks, and scars."[^topicals] The "clinically proven" phrasing rests on the brand's two-week consumer-perception study (the 92.31% and 94.87% self-reported figures), not on an independent controlled trial, and the brand does not disclose the study's design or size beyond "a consumer study." The individual actives' real evidence is on their ingredient pages; the product's own concentrations are undisclosed.
