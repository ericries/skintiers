Geek & Gorgeous B-Bomb is a fragrance-free, water-based serum from the European brand [[geek-gorgeous]] that lists niacinamide (vitamin B3) at 10% on its own product page, paired with zinc PCA, sold in a 30 ml bottle.[^1]

The label's 10% strength is above the 2% to 5% used in those trials. The manufacturer lists the 30 ml size at EUR 8.50, among the cheaper 10% niacinamide serums.[^1]

## Summary

Geek & Gorgeous B-Bomb is a lightweight, fragrance-free serum built around [[niacinamide]], a form of vitamin B3, that the manufacturer's own product page states at 10%.[^1] Its health-oriented uses come from the niacinamide: topical niacinamide is well tolerated and has modest evidence, much of it from small or manufacturer-run studies, for supporting the skin barrier and lowering water loss in dry skin, for lightening facial hyperpigmentation, and for mild inflammatory acne. That general case, with its primary trials and their limits, is set out on [[niacinamide]]. The formula also lists zinc PCA, a zinc salt, and sarcosine, an amino-acid derivative, but the manufacturer's page does not disclose either ingredient's concentration.[^1]

The product is not distinguished by a novel active. Its 10% concentration is, like most plain niacinamide serums on the market, above the 2% to 5% tested in the barrier, pigmentation, and acne trials behind [[niacinamide]]'s evidence, so its grades rest on that category evidence rather than on any trial of this exact formula (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)). The manufacturer lists the 30 ml size at EUR 8.50, putting it among the lower-priced 10% niacinamide serums (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07); this price is sourced to the manufacturer's own listing only and was not independently confirmed on a second retailer.

The manufacturer's directions say to "apply a few drops to cleansed face (after toning/exfoliating) before emulsions or creams, in AM and/or PM," and advise against combining the serum directly in the palm with acids or vitamin C, instead layering them sequentially.[^1] The label's other marketing terms are weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## The Evidence

Topical niacinamide is a well-tolerated active with modest evidence for supporting the skin barrier, lightening facial hyperpigmentation, and mild inflammatory acne; how well each use holds up, and how much of it comes from small or manufacturer-run trials, is set out on [[niacinamide]]. No published clinical trial tests this exact B-Bomb formulation, so its grades rest on the category evidence for niacinamide at the concentrations that were actually studied, which were lower than the 10% the manufacturer lists for this product.[^1]

In the barrier work summarized on [[niacinamide]], niacinamide raised stratum-corneum lipids and lowered water loss in dry skin at levels around 2%. In the pigmentation trials it was tested at 4% to 5%, where 4% niacinamide matched hydroquinone 4% in one small independent melasma study and was better tolerated. In acne, 4% nicotinamide matched topical clindamycin in a single randomized trial. That primary evidence, with its numbers, denominators, and limits, lives on [[niacinamide]], and none of it uses a 10% product.

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer's product page.[^1] The active is linked to its own page; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

Actives:

- sarcosine, an amino-acid derivative

Base, solvent, and preservative system: water, butylene glycol, glycereth-26, propanediol, pentylene glycol, xanthan gum (a thickener), ethylhexylglycerin, and the preservative phenoxyethanol.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **The 10% concentration.** The manufacturer's stated 10% strength is above the 2% to 5% at which niacinamide was clinically tested in the barrier, pigmentation, and acne trials summarized on [[niacinamide]].[^1]
- **"Normalizes pores and reduces appearance of oiliness."** Niacinamide's better-evidenced topical uses are barrier support, facial hyperpigmentation, and mild acne, set out on [[niacinamide]]; the pore and oiliness claims are the manufacturer's own positioning and are not established for this product.[^1]
- **"Soothes acne-prone skin and reduces blemishes."** The acne evidence on [[niacinamide]] is a single small RCT of 4% nicotinamide matching topical clindamycin; it does not establish this specific 10% product's soothing or blemish-reduction claim.[^1]
- **The zinc PCA and sarcosine.** Both are listed on the manufacturer's ingredient page without a disclosed concentration, and neither ingredient's independent contribution in this formula is established by any cited trial.[^1]
