Lamisil AT is terbinafine hydrochloride 1% cream, the over-the-counter version of the allylamine that holds the only head-to-head win in the foot-infection evidence. **Bottom line:** it is a genuinely approved drug rather than a monograph product, and its label claims one thing its closest rival's label explicitly declines. The active's own evidence is on [[terbinafine]] and the class ranking is on [[otc-antifungals-by-evidence]].

> **The difference that matters is on the bottom of your foot.** Lamisil AT directs "on the bottom or sides of the foot: apply twice a day (morning and night) for 2 weeks or as directed by a doctor".[^label] [[lotrimin-ultra-athletes-foot-cream]], the other cream sold on prescription-strength framing, says instead that "Effectiveness on the bottom or sides of foot is unknown". If the scaling is on your sole rather than between your toes, that is a reason to pick this one.

## Summary

Marketing category NDA, application number NDA020980, which means FDA reviewed the evidence for this specific product rather than accepting it under an over-the-counter monograph.[^openfda] The same brand's spray sits under a separate approval, NDA021124, and the cream is currently listed by two labelers, Karo Healthcare and Voyant Beauty, in 12 g and 30 g tubes.[^openfda] The carton calls it "FULL PRESCRIPTION STRENGTH", which in this case is a defensible description of the regulatory route rather than a flourish.[^label]

Uses, verbatim: "cures most athlete's foot (tinea pedis)", "cures most jock itch (tinea cruris) and ringworm (tinea corporis)", and "relieves itching, burning, cracking and scaling which accompany these conditions".[^label] No price is quoted here; the brand site is a symptom-guide site with no storefront, and no retailer listing was consulted.

## The Rubric

**Athlete's foot, between the toes**
Graded notable on solid evidence. One week, twice daily.[^label] Terbinafine is an allylamine, and allylamines are the one class in the Cochrane review of foot infections with a head-to-head result against another class; that figure and its caveats live on [[otc-antifungals-by-evidence]] and [[athletes-foot]] and are not restated here.

**Athlete's foot, bottom or sides**
Graded notable on solid evidence, and this is the row that distinguishes the product. Two weeks rather than one, twice daily, with no disclaimer attached.[^label] The approval covers the claim, so the longer course is a dosing instruction rather than a hedge.

**Jock itch and ringworm**
Graded modest on preliminary evidence, which is lower than the foot rows on purpose. The label claims both at once daily for one week.[^label] The randomised evidence that earns terbinafine its class position was gathered in foot infection, not in the groin or on the trunk, which is the point made at [[tinea-cruris-jock-itch]] and [[tinea-corporis-ringworm]].

## The Evidence

**Three courses, three lengths, one tube.** The label gives twice daily for one week between the toes, twice daily for two weeks on the bottom or sides, and once daily for one week for jock itch and ringworm.[^label] Using the shortest of those for the wrong site is the common way to under-treat, and nothing on the carton makes the distinction prominent.

**The restrictions are specific.** It is for external use only and the label says not to use it "on nails or scalp", "in or near the mouth or eyes", or "for vaginal yeast infections", and directs adults and children 12 and over only.[^label] The nail exclusion matters, because toenail infection is the condition people most often try to treat with a foot cream, and oral terbinafine's evidence for nails is a different matter covered on [[terbinafine]].

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. Infection that does not clear, spreads, involves the toenails, or occurs in someone with diabetes or a weakened immune system is worth a doctor's evaluation rather than a second tube.

## Common Marketing Claims

**"#1 Doctor Recommended OTC Topical Anti-Fungal Brand".** The brand publishes its source, "IQVIA ProVoice Survey, 12 months ending May 2026", which is unusual and to its credit.[^brand] Read what it measures: how often doctors recommend a brand, not how well it works.

**"For over 30 years, Lamisil has led the way in prescription-strength fungal treatment".** A claim about longevity and regulatory route.[^brand] The approval under NDA020980 supports the prescription-strength part.[^openfda]

**Destroying the fungus rather than treating symptoms.** The brand draws a contrast between killing the organism and relieving symptoms.[^brand] Terbinafine's mechanism does differ from the azoles', but the label's own claim is a cure claim, "cures most athlete's foot", and the pooled evidence behind the class is about cure rates rather than about mechanism.[^label]
