Anua's Peach 70% Niacinamide Serum is a 30 ml brightening serum led by peach fruit extract, with niacinamide third on a 44-ingredient list and a long tail of other actives.[^brand] The brand describes it as "A luminous skin serum infused with Peach and Niacinamide, designed to unveil radiant, glass-like skin."[^brand]

> **Bottom line.** A legitimate niacinamide serum wearing a confusing name. Niacinamide is declared **third of 44**, which supports a real amount of a genuinely well-evidenced ingredient (see [[niacinamide]]). The catch is the number: **the 70% is the peach, not the niacinamide**, and the brand never states the niacinamide percentage anywhere on the product page.[^brand] The bottle itself is clearer than the website, reading "PEACH 70 + NIACIN".[^label] Peach extract is simultaneously the headline and the least evidenced thing in the formula.

## Summary

The declared ingredient list runs **peach fruit extract**, glycerin, **niacinamide**, butylene glycol, diethoxyethyl succinate, water, 1,2-hexanediol, lactobacillus ferment, sodium hyaluronate, sphingomonas ferment extract, **alpha-arbutin**, then neem, holy basil, safflower, chia, turmeric, coralline and centella extracts, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, hinoki and peach flower extracts, mugwort, **betaine salicylate**, panthenol, polyglutamic acid, cyanocobalamin, **3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid**, ceramide NP, lactobionic acid, the centella acids, xanthan gum and **fragrance** last of 44.[^brand]

Three placements tell you how to read it. **Peach fruit extract is first**, consistent with the stated 70%, and water does not appear until sixth, so the peach extract is largely serving as the water phase. **Niacinamide is third**, which is the formula's real argument. And the remaining actives are spread thin: alpha-arbutin at 11th is plausible but undisclosed, while 3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid at 37th, betaine salicylate at 26th and ceramide NP at 38th are present at levels that cannot carry their own evidence.

**Fragrance is last of 44**, which is the best place for it, but it is still a declared fragrance in a product sold for radiance rather than for reactive skin.[^brand]

## The Evidence

No published trial tests this serum, so its grades rest on the ingredient evidence plus what the label supports.

**The niacinamide case is the honest one.** Niacinamide has real evidence for pigment, barrier function and oil, set out on [[niacinamide]] and not repeated here, and a third-of-44 position after a water-phase extract and glycerin is consistent with a working concentration. What is missing is the number. The brand publishes "70%" for the peach and nothing at all for the niacinamide, so a buyer cannot tell whether this is the 4% to 5% used in most niacinamide trials or well under it.[^brand] That undisclosed figure is the whole reason the evidence grade is mixed rather than solid; it is not a doubt about niacinamide.

**The peach is the marketing.** No trial of topical peach fruit extract is cited here, and nothing on this page supports a skin benefit from it. Its function in the formula appears to be as the aqueous base, which is an ordinary formulation choice dressed up as the hero.

**The brightening tail does not add up to a brightening stack.** Alpha-arbutin and 3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid both have their own evidence at stated concentrations, discussed on [[arbutin]] and [[3-o-ethyl-ascorbic-acid]], and neither concentration is disclosed here. At 11th and 37th of 44 they are better read as label decoration than as a reason to choose this over a single-active serum. For what actually moves pigment, see [[hyperpigmentation]].

## Common Marketing Claims

Claims quoted from the brand's own US store page; treat each as unproven unless it also appears under The Evidence.

- **"A luminous skin serum infused with Peach and Niacinamide, designed to unveil radiant, glass-like skin."**[^brand] "Designed to" is a statement of intent, not a result. The niacinamide half is plausible at its declared position; the glass-skin outcome is an aesthetic claim no trial here measures. See [[glass-skin]].
- **The product name, "Peach 70% Niacinamide Serum".**[^brand] This is the claim to read carefully. The 70% belongs to the peach extract, and the packaging makes that explicit as "PEACH 70 + NIACIN", but the web listing's word order invites reading the percentage as the niacinamide strength.[^label] It is the same pattern as the brand's [[anua-heartleaf-pore-control-cleansing-oil]], where a headline botanical percentage sits beside an undisclosed active.

## Who It Is For

Someone who wants a gentle, pleasant niacinamide serum at a fair price and is not relying on a specific strength. If you need a known concentration, the brand's own [[anua-niacinamide-10-tranexamic-acid-4-serum]] states both of its actives, and the graded alternatives are on [[best-niacinamide-products]]. If fragrance is a problem for your skin, this is not the one.
