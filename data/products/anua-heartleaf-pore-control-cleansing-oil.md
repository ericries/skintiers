Anua's Heartleaf Pore Control Cleansing Oil is a 200 ml first-step cleansing oil built on synthetic esters rather than the plant oils its marketing foregrounds, with heartleaf (Houttuynia cordata) near the bottom of its declared ingredient list. It is the wash-off sibling of [[anua-heartleaf-77-soothing-toner]].

> **Bottom line.** A competent, cheap cleansing oil, judged as a cleansing oil. The thing to discount is the name. Heartleaf is declared **18th of 22**, and the retailer's own "10,000ppm" figure[^retailer] is 1%, so this is not the brand's 77% heartleaf formula in a different bottle. Two further flags: **fragrance is sixth**, high in a product pitched at redness-prone skin, and the instruction to massage for 3 to 4 minutes until you see "the sebaceous filaments that have left your pores"[^retailer] describes a normal structure that refills, not a problem being solved.

## Summary

The declared ingredient list, as reproduced by incidecoder from the manufacturer's label, runs **ethylhexyl palmitate**, sorbeth-30 tetraoleate, sorbitan sesquioleate, caprylic/capric triglyceride, butyl avocadate, **fragrance**, sunflower seed oil, macadamia seed oil, olive fruit oil, jojoba seed oil, grape seed oil, caprylyl glycol, ethylhexylglycerin, turmeric root extract, neem (Melia azadirachta) flower extract, tocopherol, neem leaf extract, **houttuynia cordata extract**, corallina officinalis extract, neem bark extract, moringa seed oil and holy basil leaf extract.

Three placements decide how to read this product. The first four slots are **synthetic esters and emulsifiers**, not botanicals: ethylhexyl palmitate is the bulk solvent, and sorbeth-30 tetraoleate is the surfactant that lets the oil rinse off with water. The jojoba, grapeseed, macadamia and olive oils the listing describes as a "hard-working selection" sit in positions seven to eleven, below the fragrance.[^retailer] And **heartleaf is 18th of 22** on that same incidecoder-reproduced list, which is the gap between the name on the bottle and the formula inside it.

**Fragrance at sixth** is the placement most worth knowing. This is marketed for skin "prone to acne or redness", and fragrance is a common contact allergen and irritant.[^retailer] It is a first-step product that gets rinsed, which limits exposure, but anyone reactive should treat the positioning and the formula as pulling in opposite directions.

## The Evidence

No published trial tests this cleanser, so its grades rest on what oil cleansing does generally plus what the label supports.

The core function is well established and not specific to this product: an oil-phase cleanser dissolves makeup, sunscreen and sebum, which a water-based second cleanse then removes. That is why the cleansing grade is `modest` effect on `solid` evidence; the evidence belongs to the category, set out on [[best-cleansing-balms-oil-cleansers]], not to this bottle.

The heartleaf claim is where the page parts company with the marketing. The retailer calls it the "hero ingredient 10,000pm Heartleaf", which reads as 10,000 ppm, or 1%.[^retailer] That figure is at least internally consistent with the ingredient's 18th position, which is a point in its favour: the number is plausible rather than inflated. What it does not support is a skin benefit. One percent of a botanical extract in a product designed to be rinsed off within a minute or two has little opportunity to do anything, and the general evidence for the ingredient is on [[houttuynia-cordata]]. Readers coming from the brand's 77% heartleaf toner should not assume the two deliver the same thing.

The pore-control promise is the weakest. Sebaceous filaments are normal, ubiquitous structures that refill after being emptied, as covered on [[blackheads-clogged-pores]]; seeing material on your fingertips after several minutes of massage is not evidence of a lasting change in pore size or number. Three to four minutes of friction is also a plausible route to irritation in exactly the redness-prone skin this product targets.

## Common Marketing Claims

Claims quoted from the Skin Cupid retail listing, which carries the brand-derived copy; treat each as unproven unless it also appears under The Evidence.

- **"Its formula is led by hero ingredient 10,000pm Heartleaf"**[^retailer] "Led by" is the overstatement. At 1% and 18th of 22, heartleaf is present but is not leading this formula; the first four ingredients are esters and emulsifiers.
- **"Famous for its anti-inflammatory and anti-bacterial properties, it helps to soothe irritation, reinforce the skin barrier function, and balance the complexion"**[^retailer] Not supported here for this product. These are ingredient-level claims applied to a rinse-off cleanser at 1%, with no trial of the finished product cited. See [[houttuynia-cordata]].
- **"Jojoba Seed Oil, to promote healing and fill in fine lines"**[^retailer] Discount this. "fill in fine lines" is a cosmetic appearance claim, and jojoba sits tenth, below fragrance, in a product that is rinsed off.
- **"Grapeseed Oil, to reduce inflammation and plump the skin, and Macadamia Oil to balance sebum production"**[^retailer] Same problem. No trial of this product measures inflammation, plumping or sebum, and both oils are rinse-off minor components here.
- **"Massage it into your skin for 3-4 minutes and you should be able to see the sebaceous filaments that have left your pores on your fingertips"**[^retailer] This is the claim to treat most sceptically. It reframes a normal anatomical feature as debris, and the visible result is largely oil rather than a cleared pore. See [[blackheads-clogged-pores]].
- **"Suits all skin types"**[^retailer] A positioning claim, and the one the fragrance placement argues against for reactive skin.

## Who It Is For

Someone who wears sunscreen or makeup daily and wants an inexpensive first cleanse, and who is buying it as an oil cleanser rather than as a heartleaf treatment. If the appeal is heartleaf specifically, the brand's own toner delivers far more of it and stays on the skin; see [[anua-heartleaf-77-soothing-toner]]. If skin is fragrance-reactive, a fragrance-free option from [[best-cleansing-balms-oil-cleansers]] is the safer pick.
