A retinaldehyde gel sold in two stated strengths, 0.1% at $29 and 0.2% at $35, both for 50 mL.[^brand] **Bottom line:** the strength is on the tube and the ingredient list is consistent with it, which is more than most retinoid products here can say. The case for the molecule is at [[retinaldehyde]] and is not re-argued.

> **The position supports the label.** Retinal is seventh of thirty-four declared ingredients, and the list shows no alphabetical tail: a programmatic check for ascending runs found only one four-item sequence, at positions 25 to 28, against the twenty-six-item run that made [[nuface-super-peptide-booster-serum]]'s ingredient positions meaningless.[^brand] So this list appears concentration-ordered throughout, and a named active at position seven in such a list is consistent with a declared 0.1%.

## Summary

### What is in it

Water, glycerin, propanediol, dimethicone and jojoba seed oil lead, then hydroxyapatite, then Retinal at seven, followed by ectoin, centella asiatica leaf extract and cyanocobalamin.[^brand] Thirty-four ingredients in all, preserved with phenoxyethanol and caprylyl glycol, at a stated pH of 5.5.[^tube]

The tube states "0.1% HYDROXYSOMES® RETINALDEHYDE, ECTOIN, BIOACTIVE CICA + GLYCERIN".[^tube] Cica is the centella extract; the Hydroxysomes name is addressed below.

## The Rubric

**The strength is declared, which is the whole question for this class.** Retinaldehyde products are often sold without a percentage, and the molecule's evidence is dose-dependent. Both variants here state theirs, and the ingredient ordering does not contradict it. That is a verifiable fact about the label rather than a measurement of the tube's contents, which this page did not perform.

**Price per volume is the clearest advantage.** $29.00 for 50 mL against [[avene-retrinal-0-1-intensive-cream]] at $39.99 for 0.5 fl oz, roughly 15 mL, at the same stated 0.1% strength.[^brand] Close to a fifth of the price per millilitre, assuming both labels are accurate.

**Hydroxysomes is a name, not evidence.** The brand's trademarked delivery term appears on the tube and in its copy. No independent data on it was sought or found for this page, and a proprietary delivery name tells you a formulator did something, not that it worked better. [[reading-a-brands-clinical-claim]] covers how to weigh that kind of claim.

**Irritation.** The brand's own copy positions the formula around tolerability, saying ectoin and cica "work together to refine skin texture and deliver optimal results with minimal irritation", and separately recommends a "retinal sandwich" of moisturiser before and after for anyone newer to retinoids.[^brand] Those are the brand's claims. The general irritation picture for this molecule is on [[retinaldehyde]].

## The Evidence

None specific to this product. No trial of Retinal Renew was sought or found, and the brand cites none in the copy read for this page.[^brand] The grade above is inherited from [[retinaldehyde]], which this site grades `good`, applied to a product whose declared strength falls inside the range that evidence covers.

That inheritance is the honest basis and also its limit: it assumes the tube contains what the label says, which no page here has measured.

## Common Marketing Claims

Quarantined. Prequel describes it as "An advanced retinaldehyde gel that supports skin renewal and firmness while maintaining the skin barrier", "Formulated with a highly stabilized retinaldehyde to visibly reduce fine lines and wrinkles, smooth texture, and even tone".[^brand] Highly stabilized is a formulation claim with no figure attached, and stability is the real engineering problem with this molecule, so it is the claim most worth seeing evidence for and none is offered.

## Who it is for

Someone who wants retinaldehyde at a stated strength without paying prescription-adjacent prices, and who understands that the 0.2% variant is a step up in dose rather than a different product. Anyone who has not used a retinoid before should start at 0.1%.
