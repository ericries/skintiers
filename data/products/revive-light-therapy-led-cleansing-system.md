The reVive LED Cleansing System is the device at the root of this brand's clearance chain, and reading its two filings closes a question the rest of the reVive pages left open. **Bottom line:** it is one trade name holding two clearances with opposite purposes, and both say in the same words that their conclusions rest on nonclinical tests.[^k445][^k447] Category evidence is on [[home-led-light-therapy]], the maker on [[revive-light-therapy]].

> **The whole reVive line traces back to no clinical data at any generation.** K223482 rests on K210965 and K210968; those rest on K180445 and K180447, the two filings read here; and both of these state that "The conclusions drawn from nonclinical tests demonstrate that the device is safe, as effective, and performs as well as the legally marketed devices."[^k445][^k447] That sentence appears at every level examined. Nothing in the chain is a claim about what happened to anyone's skin, and nothing in the clearance process required one.

## Summary

Two clearances, one name, in 2018. **K180447** covers an OTC device "intended for use in treatment of mild to moderate inflammatory acne" with "two types of LEDs: 630nm red and 415nm blue".[^k447] **K180445** covers an OTC device "intended for use in treating wrinkles" using "four types of LEDs: 605nm amber, 630nm red, 660nm red, and 880nm infrared", with no blue at all.[^k445] Different filings, different product codes, different wavelengths, same trade name on the box. A third clearance from 2022 carries the name as well.[^openfda]

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate acne, in the K180447 configuration**
- **Effect size: modest, and carried by the modality.** 630nm red plus 415nm blue is the blue-red pairing graded Level B with a Grade I recommendation, the best grade in the GRADE review's acne section.[^home] The grade belongs to the modality.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** The filing's conclusions are explicitly nonclinical.[^k447]

**Wrinkles, in the K180445 configuration**
- **Effect size: minimal.** LED for facial rejuvenation reached Level B, behind home fractional nonablative lasers at Level A.[^home] This configuration drops blue entirely and adds amber and infrared; no source consulted reports what those achieved on this device.[^k445]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** Same nonclinical basis.[^k445]

**Knowing which device you have**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a disclosure point.** Two devices sharing a trade name and differing in both indication and emitted wavelengths cannot be told apart by name. The acne one has blue, the wrinkle one does not, and that is the difference that decides which GRADE row applies.[^k445][^k447]
- **Evidence quality: not applicable; read directly from the two filings.**

## The Evidence

**What substantial equivalence actually rested on.** Each filing argues the device has "the same/similar intended use as the predicate device", "the similar output (mW/cm²) as predicate devices" and the "same/similar treatment duration" as the predicate.[^k445][^k447] K180445 names K171390, the dpl II Panel, among its predicates under product code OHS; K180447 sits under product code OLP with its own primary and reference predicates.[^k445][^k447] This is engineering comparison, which is what the 510(k) route asks for. It is not evidence that the device clears acne or softens wrinkles, and [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] explains why those are different questions.

**Two details worth having.** K180447 reports thermal testing in which "the temperature of the device does not exceed 41C, even after the continuous use for 30 minutes, 10 times longer than the recommended treatment time of 3 minutes", which is a real safety margin honestly reported.[^k447] It also records that a "self-selection study was not conducted, as the box labeling was comparable to that of primary predicate" , meaning no one tested whether a shopper could correctly judge the device suitable for themselves from the box.[^k447] Biocompatibility was argued by read-across: the skin-contact housing is the same ABS used in the dpl Nüve and dpl II Panel, with the brush head's TPE material tested separately.[^k445]

**Credit where it is due.** All 15 of this firm's 510(k) records are filed as Summaries rather than Statements, so every one of them can be read.[^openfda] That is the opposite of [[foreo-espada-blue-light-pen]], where 8 of 10 publish nothing. The criticism on this page is possible only because the company published the documents that support it.

**The name is gone from the shelf.** No product called the LED Cleansing System appears in the brand's current range; the sonic cleansing products sold today are the Lux Soniqué at $199.00 and the Lux Soniqué Mini at $129.00.[^shop] Whether either is this cleared device under a new name is not established by any source consulted, so this page does not assume it. That is the same retail-to-clearance naming gap recorded on [[revive-light-therapy-lux-spot]] and [[revive-light-therapy-lux-glo]].

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The cleared indication stops at mild to moderate inflammatory acne; nodular, cystic or scarring acne is a reason to see a dermatologist rather than buy a device.
