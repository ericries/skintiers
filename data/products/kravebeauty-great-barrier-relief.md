A barrier serum built on tamanu oil, from [[krave-beauty|KraveBeauty]]. **Bottom line:** the brand's "clinically proven" line rests on an independent study of twenty participants that it does not cite, and the ingredient most likely to be doing something is the niacinamide sitting fifth on the list rather than the tamanu the product is sold on. The barrier-repair case generally is at [[skin-barrier-repair]].

> **Read the clinical claim and then read the sample size.** The brand's own page says "Clinically Proven: Improves damaged skin barrier function after 2 weeks of use." and immediately adds "Based on an independent clinical study on 20 participants."[^brand] No citation, no design, no comparator and no outcome measure appears anywhere on that page. Putting the sample size next to the claim is more honest than most brands manage; it is still twenty people and an unpublished study.

## Summary

KraveBeauty sells it at **$29.00 for 45 mL and $43.00 for 100 mL**, both in stock on its own storefront.[^brand] The brand describes it as a "reparative serum" that "helps soothe and restore your skin barrier while calming redness and irritations", "Infused with 10% Tamanu Oil", and made for "All skin types but especially sensitive, eczema-prone skin."[^brand]

The brand publishes its full ingredient list, 40 entries, opening: "Water (Aqua/Eau), Propanediol, Calophyllum Inophyllum (Tamanu) Seed Oil, Dipropylene Glycol, Niacinamide".[^brand] Tamanu third at a stated 10%, niacinamide fifth.

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: unknown for the claim it is sold on.** Tamanu oil has no page on this site because there is little to put on one, and this serum's own evidence is a twenty-person study nobody outside the company can read. The brand does not overstate the sample, which is worth noting, but an uncited study is not something a reader can weigh.
- **Effect size: modest for what the formula is likely doing.** [[niacinamide]] is fifth on the brand's list and carries `tier: mid` here. Alongside [[squalane]], [[hyaluronic-acid|sodium hyaluronate]] and a set of plant oils, this is a competent emollient-and-humectant serum whatever the tamanu contributes.
- **Record finding: the ceramide is almost last.** Ceramide NP sits 39th of 40 on the brand's own declaration, with only glycine after it.[^brand] [[ceramides]] grade `tier: mid` here and work by replenishing a lipid the barrier is short of, which is a quantity argument. A position that late caps the quantity. The product does not claim a ceramide percentage, and this page does not infer one.
- **Record finding: the brand prints the sample size itself.** "Based on an independent clinical study on 20 participants" is on the product page, unprompted.[^brand] Most brands print "clinically proven" and stop. That is a point in its favour even though the study remains uncheckable.
- **A caution the brand flags and this page repeats.** The page carries its own note that the product "does contain 10% Tamanu Oil, which is derived from Tamanu nuts", which matters for anyone avoiding tree-nut-derived ingredients.[^brand]

## The Evidence

None that can be examined. The only study referenced is the unnamed twenty-participant one above, and no publication, registration number or journal appears on the page read. That is not a claim that the study does not exist; it is a statement that nothing about it can be checked from the brand's own page.

What can be said is which of its ingredients have evidence behind them, and those arguments live elsewhere: [[niacinamide]], [[ceramides]], [[squalane]] and [[skin-barrier-repair]] for the goal. [[best-ceramide-moisturizers]] ranks the products built around the lipid this one carries at the end of its list.

> **Bottom line.** A pleasant, well-formulated soothing serum at $29 whose headline ingredient is the least evidenced thing in it and whose clinical claim cannot be inspected. If you want barrier repair driven by ceramides, the order of this ingredient list argues for looking elsewhere.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own product page and **not** endorsed here.

- "Clinically Proven: Improves damaged skin barrier function after 2 weeks of use."[^brand] Uncheckable, on twenty participants, with the design unstated.
- "Infused with 10% Tamanu Oil to nurse your skin back to health".[^brand] The 10% figure is specific and the ingredient order is consistent with it. Nurse your skin back to health is not a measurable claim.
- "Repairing and strengthening your skin barrier so it can protect you from environmental aggressors and eczema flare-ups."[^brand] Eczema is a medical condition; nothing on the page read supports a claim about flare-ups, and [[atopic-dermatitis]] covers what does.
