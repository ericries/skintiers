Arazlo is a prescription topical retinoid lotion whose only active is tazarotene 0.045%, FDA-approved for acne vulgaris and sold by Ortho Dermatologics (Bausch Health).[^label]

> A real prescription acne retinoid, formulated at a lower strength than older tazarotene products for better tolerability. In its own two-trial registration program, roughly a quarter to three-tenths of patients reached clear or almost clear at 12 weeks, meaningfully more than on vehicle but well short of most patients clearing. Retinoid irritation, dryness, peeling, redness, is still a common side effect at this lower strength, and it is strictly contraindicated in pregnancy.[^label]

## Summary

Arazlo is generic-name [[tazarotene]] 0.045%, a prescription topical [[retinoids|retinoid]], formulated in a polymeric-emulsion lotion and FDA-approved "for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in patients 9 years of age and older."[^label] Tazarotene is a receptor-selective retinoid pro-drug, the same design strategy as [[adapalene]], and it has carried FDA approval in older formulations (Tazorac gel and cream) at the higher 0.1% strength for years; the lotion's selling point is a lower concentration in a different vehicle, aimed at keeping the acne benefit while lowering the irritation load that limits the older products, a formulation trade-off, not a new molecule (full class evidence and cross-formulation comparison on [[tazarotene]]).[^label]

The product-specific case rests on two of Arazlo's own multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled phase 3 trials in patients 9 years and older with moderate to severe acne, in which treatment success (a 2-grade improvement to clear or almost clear) was reached by 25.5% and 29.6% of lotion-treated patients versus 13% and 17.3% on vehicle, with larger reductions in both non-inflammatory and inflammatory lesion counts on the lotion; see [the full trial numbers](#the-evidence).[^label] That is a real, vehicle-beating effect, about 12 to 13 percentage points better than vehicle in both trials, but it means most treated patients did not reach clear or almost clear at 12 weeks.

Tolerability was the point of the lower strength, but the label still lists local skin reactions as the most common adverse effects: in the pooled trials (779 lotion patients versus 791 on vehicle), application-site pain occurred in 5% versus under 1%, dryness in 4% versus under 1%, exfoliation in 2% versus none, erythema in 2% versus none, and pruritus in 1% versus none.[^label] The drug is strictly contraindicated in pregnancy: "ARAZLO is contraindicated in pregnancy. ARAZLO may cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant patient."[^label] This is a prescription drug; see a prescriber for use and dosing, and see [[acne]] for the broader condition context.

## The Evidence

The general case for tazarotene as an acne retinoid, its receptor-selective pro-drug mechanism, and its place alongside [[tretinoin]] and [[adapalene]] are covered on [[tazarotene]] and [[retinoids]]; this section covers only what is specific to the Arazlo 0.045% lotion.[^label]

Arazlo was tested in two multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled phase 3 trials in patients 9 years of age and older with moderate to severe acne. Treatment success, the primary endpoint (at least a 2-grade improvement on the Evaluator's Global Severity Score and a rating of clear or almost clear at week 12), was reached by 25.5% of lotion patients versus 13% on vehicle in the first trial, and 29.6% versus 17.3% in the second.[^label] Non-inflammatory lesion counts fell further on the lotion than on vehicle in both trials (21.0 and 24.6 versus 16.4 and 16.6), as did inflammatory lesion counts.[^label]

> **Bottom line.** The lotion beat vehicle by roughly 12 percentage points on treatment success in both of its own registration trials, a real effect, but most patients in both arms, including active-drug patients, did not reach clear or almost clear at 12 weeks.[^label]

The formulation case is that a lower concentration (0.045% versus the older 0.1% gel and cream) in a different lotion vehicle was designed to keep meaningful efficacy while easing the irritation that limits tazarotene use; that formulation-versus-tolerability tradeoff, and how the older 0.1% strength performs by comparison, is covered on [[tazarotene]], which this page does not re-argue.[^label]

## What's In It

The active and inactive ingredients as declared on the FDA-approved prescribing information.[^label]

Active:

- [[tazarotene]] 0.045%, a prescription topical [[retinoids|retinoid]]. The label states "each gram of ARAZLO contains 0.45 mg (0.045%) tazarotene in a white to off-white topical lotion."[^label]

Inactive: carbomer copolymer type B, carbomer homopolymer type A, diethyl sebacate, edetate disodium dihydrate, light mineral oil, methylparaben, propylparaben, purified water, sodium hydroxide, sorbitan monooleate, and sorbitol solution 70%.[^label]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.[^label]

- **A lower-strength, better-tolerated tazarotene.** Directionally accurate as a formulation description, 0.045% is lower than the older 0.1% gel and cream, but the label still lists application-site pain, dryness, exfoliation, erythema, and pruritus as common reactions; it is a lower-irritation retinoid, not a non-irritating one.[^label]
- **Any implication that most patients clear.** Not supported. In both registration trials, most lotion-treated patients did not reach clear or almost clear at week 12; treatment success was 25.5% and 29.6%.[^label]
