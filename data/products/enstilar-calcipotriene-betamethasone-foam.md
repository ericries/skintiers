Enstilar Foam is a prescription-only aerosol foam made by LEO Pharma that fixed-combines calcipotriene 0.005%, a vitamin D analog, with betamethasone dipropionate 0.064%, a corticosteroid, applied once daily to treat plaque psoriasis in patients 12 and older.[^dailymed] It is not a cosmetic product: it is the FDA-approved foam formulation of the same calcipotriene/betamethasone combination that outperforms either ingredient used alone, the general case for which is set out on [[calcipotriene]].[^dailymed]

> A branded prescription combination drug, not a compounded formulation, built on the same two-ingredient rationale, vitamin D analog plus corticosteroid, that has repeatedly beaten either component alone in randomized trials, as set out on [[calcipotriene]]. Against an inert vehicle foam, it brought roughly half of patients to treatment success (clear or almost clear) in 4 weeks (53.3% vs 4.8%, P<.001); against betamethasone or calcipotriene used alone in the same base, it still won by 14 to 30 percentage points.[^dailymed] Use is capped at 4 weeks and 60 g every 4 days, and the label carries no boxed warning, but this is a corticosteroid-containing drug with real systemic-absorption risks (HPA-axis suppression, hypercalcemia) that a compounding pharmacy or an OTC vitamin D cream cannot substitute for.[^dailymed]

## Summary

Enstilar is the brand name for a fixed-combination aerosol foam of calcipotriene 0.005% and betamethasone dipropionate 0.064%, marketed by LEO Pharma exclusively for the topical treatment of plaque psoriasis in patients 12 years and older.[^dailymed] It sits entirely on the health side of the ledger: there is no cosmetic-use claim here, and the label states plainly that while the pharmacologic and clinical effects of each component are known, the exact mechanisms of their action in psoriasis are unknown.[^dailymed] The general case for combining a vitamin D analog with a corticosteroid, and the cross-formulation evidence base showing the combination beats either ingredient alone, lives on [[calcipotriene]]; this page covers what is specific to the Enstilar foam product itself.

What Enstilar adds beyond the underlying combination chemistry is the aerosol foam delivery format. Each gram contains 50 mcg calcipotriene (as calcipotriene hydrate) and 0.643 mg betamethasone dipropionate (equivalent to 0.5 mg betamethasone), suspended in a base of white petrolatum, polyoxypropylene stearyl ether, mineral oil, all-rac-alpha-tocopherol, and butylhydroxytoluene, propelled by dimethyl ether and butane into a non-expanding foam that gradually collapses after spraying.[^dailymed] FDA approval rested on two double-blind, randomized trials plus a 52-week maintenance trial, all product-specific to this foam ([the evidence](#the-evidence)).[^dailymed]

Dosing is capped: once daily for up to 4 weeks, no more than 60 g every 4 days, discontinued once control is achieved.[^dailymed] The label instructs against use on the face, groin, or axillae, or where skin atrophy is already present, and against occlusive dressings unless a clinician directs otherwise.[^dailymed] Because betamethasone dipropionate is a corticosteroid, the label carries real precautions absent from vitamin-D-only products: hypercalcemia and hypercalciuria, HPA-axis suppression (observed in 9 to 10% of subjects in two of the label's own pediatric and long-term trials), and ophthalmic risks including posterior subcapsular cataracts and glaucoma.[^dailymed] The propellants are also flammable, and the label warns patients to avoid fire, flame, and smoking during and immediately after application.[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

The general calcipotriene/betamethasone combination evidence base, including the 1,603-patient Kaufmann trial that established the combination beats either component alone, is set out on [[calcipotriene]]. The trials below are specific to this foam formulation.

**PSO-FAST, the phase 3 vehicle-controlled registration trial (Leonardi and colleagues, 2015).** A double-blind, randomized trial (NCT01866163) enrolled 426 patients with at least mild psoriasis of the trunk and/or limbs at 27 US sites, randomizing 3:1 to Cal/BD aerosol foam (n=323) or vehicle foam (n=103) once daily for 4 weeks.[^psofast] At week 4, significantly more foam-treated patients reached the primary endpoint, physician's global assessment treatment success, than on vehicle: 53.3% versus 4.8% (odds ratio 30.3, 95% CI 9.7 to 94.3, P<.001).[^psofast] Mean modified PASI score (excluding the head) was also significantly lower with the foam than vehicle (2.0 vs 5.5, adjusted difference -3.3, P<.001), and itch relief on a visual analog scale was significantly greater from day 3 onward.[^psofast] Adverse drug reactions were reported in 3.1% of foam patients versus 1.9% on vehicle, with no clinically significant change in calcium homeostasis.[^psofast]

> Bottom line: against an inert foam, once-daily Cal/BD foam brought about half of patients to clear or almost-clear skin in 4 weeks, versus under 1 in 20 on vehicle, a roughly 30-fold higher odds of treatment success.

**Three-arm trial against each active ingredient alone (label's Trial One).** A second trial cited on the FDA label randomized 302 subjects to Enstilar Foam, betamethasone dipropionate in the same foam vehicle, or calcipotriene in the same foam vehicle.[^dailymed] At week 4, treatment success (IGA clear or almost clear, with mild-baseline subjects required to reach clear) was reached by 45.0% on Enstilar Foam versus 30.7% on betamethasone dipropionate alone and 14.9% on calcipotriene alone.[^dailymed] This mirrors, in the foam formulation specifically, the same pattern the general calcipotriene/betamethasone literature on [[calcipotriene]] shows in other vehicles: the fixed combination outperforms either ingredient dosed alone.[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: in this foam-specific three-arm trial, the combination roughly tripled the treatment-success rate of calcipotriene alone and beat the steroid alone by about 14 percentage points.

**Long-term proactive maintenance, PSO-LONG (Lebwohl and colleagues, 2021).** A phase 3 trial (NCT02899962) began with a 4-week open-label run-in of once-daily Enstilar Foam; the 521 subjects who reached treatment success (IGA clear or almost clear with at least a 2-grade improvement) were then randomized to twice-weekly Enstilar Foam (proactive management) or twice-weekly vehicle foam (reactive management, with 4-week rescue courses of the active foam on relapse) for up to 52 additional weeks.[^dailymed][^lebwohl] Proactive twice-weekly maintenance with the active foam reduced the hazard of first relapse (hazard ratio 0.56, P<.001) and the overall relapse rate (rate ratio 0.52, P<.001) compared with reactive vehicle management, a benefit the authors found held irrespective of baseline disease severity and other baseline characteristics.[^lebwohl] Median time to loss of response was 56 days on twice-weekly Enstilar Foam versus 30 days on twice-weekly vehicle foam.[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: in patients who had already cleared with the foam, twice-weekly maintenance treatment roughly halved the rate of psoriasis relapsing over the following year, evidence for real relapse prevention rather than a claim the underlying disease resolves.

The label also flags that adrenal (HPA-axis) suppression occurred in 3 of 33 adolescent subjects (9%) and 2 of 21 subjects on the 52-week regimen (10%) in the trials that specifically tested for it, versus none of 35 adult subjects tested over 4 weeks; the label states the incidence of hypercalcemia and hypercalciuria beyond 56 weeks of use has not been evaluated.[^dailymed]

## What's In It

- **Actives: calcipotriene 0.005%** (50 mcg per gram, equivalent to 52.2 mcg of calcipotriene hydrate) **and betamethasone dipropionate 0.064%** (0.643 mg per gram, equivalent to 0.5 mg betamethasone). See [[calcipotriene]] for the graded mechanism and cross-formulation evidence base; betamethasone dipropionate is a synthetic corticosteroid whose page does not yet exist on this site.[^dailymed]
- **Base and propellants:** white petrolatum, polyoxypropylene stearyl ether, mineral oil, all-rac-alpha-tocopherol, and butylhydroxytoluene, propelled by dimethyl ether and butane, as declared in the FDA label's Description section.[^dailymed]

The label lists no contraindications, and states the foam is not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand marketing page for Enstilar was reviewed for this entry; the manufacturer-framed claims below come from the FDA label's own study descriptions rather than promotional copy, and are adjudicated against the product-specific trial data above.

- **"Innovative aerosol foam formulation... expected to become a valuable treatment option."** The PSO-FAST trial authors' own characterization of the foam delivery format.[^psofast] The trial data support that the foam version of the combination clears psoriasis far better than vehicle and produces rapid itch relief; whether the foam format itself outperforms the ointment or cream versions of the same combination was not tested here and is not addressed by this trial.[^psofast]
- **"Steroid-sparing" framing sometimes applied to vitamin D/corticosteroid combinations.** This product still contains a corticosteroid at a meaningful concentration (0.064%) and carries corticosteroid-specific label warnings (HPA-axis suppression, hypercalcemia, ophthalmic risk); it is not a steroid-free option, and its risk profile differs materially from calcipotriene monotherapy (see [[calcipotriene]]).[^dailymed]
