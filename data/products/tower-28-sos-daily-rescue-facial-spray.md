Tower 28's SOS Daily Rescue Facial Spray (also labeled "SOS Rescue Spray" or "SOS (Save. Our. Skin.)") is a three-ingredient hypochlorous acid (HOCl) toner spray, sold in a 120 mL / 4 fl oz Standard size for $28.00, alongside Jumbo and Travel sizes.[^1]

> An extremely short formula: water, sodium chloride, and hypochlorous acid, the same three-ingredient shape as competing HOCl sprays like [[prequel-universal-skin-solution|Prequel's Universal Skin Solution]].[^1] The general case for topical hypochlorous acid, an antimicrobial the immune system itself produces, is set out on [[hypochlorous-acid]]; it is preliminary for eczema, weak for wound care, and unresolved for acne, resting mostly on small trials, an animal model, and expert-panel consensus rather than a controlled trial of a leave-on spray like this one. Tower 28's own page carries no hypochlorous acid percentage, and its "Helps with blemishes, flare-ups, eczema, redness, irritation" claim is explicitly footnoted to consumer testimonials, not a clinical trial.[^1] The product's clearest independent credential is a packaging seal: the National Eczema Association's "Accepted" mark, shown alongside "Accepted" from the National Rosacea Society and "Recognized" from the National Psoriasis Foundation.[^1]

## Summary

SOS Daily Rescue Facial Spray is Tower 28's hypochlorous acid mist, positioned as a daily "toner treatment" for calming irritation, reducing redness, and managing flare-ups on sensitive, reactive, acne-prone, and eczema-prone skin.[^1] The brand's declared ingredient list has only three entries: water, sodium chloride, and hypochlorous acid, put in that order.[^1] HOCl sprays like this are generally produced by electrolyzing saline; the mechanism and general evidence for the ingredient are set out on [[hypochlorous-acid]].

Tower 28 does not disclose a hypochlorous acid percentage anywhere on the product page. Its FAQ says only that "our chemists worked on stabilizing hypochlorous acid for 8 years before we launched SOS Daily Rescue Facial Spray, not to mention testing concentration and pH until we felt it was optimized for facial skin," without naming a figure.[^1]

Both this spray and [[prequel-universal-skin-solution|Prequel's Universal Skin Solution]] share the same three-ingredient hypochlorous acid formula; the visible difference is in third-party seals, where Tower 28's packaging shows three, "Accepted" from the National Eczema Association and the National Rosacea Society and "Recognized" from the National Psoriasis Foundation, to Prequel's one, the National Rosacea Society.[^1] These are third-party recognitions the brand displays on its packaging, not a substitute for a clinical trial of the spray itself; [general topical hypochlorous acid evidence is graded on the ingredient page](#the-evidence). The brand's own "Helps with blemishes, flare-ups, eczema, redness, irritation" claim is footnoted directly on the page as "Based on 3rd-party consumer studies and consumer testimonials," which the brand does not name or link, so it is [quarantined as a marketing claim](#common-marketing-claims) rather than graded as evidence.[^1]

It is priced at $28.00 for the 120 mL / 4 fl oz Standard size, with Jumbo and Travel sizes also sold (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-15).[^1]

## The Evidence

No independent, product-specific trial of SOS Daily Rescue Facial Spray was found. Its grades above rest on the general evidence for topical hypochlorous acid, set out in full on [[hypochlorous-acid]]: preliminary for eczema and atopic dermatitis (a small, antibiotic-confounded pediatric bleach-bath RCT and a steroid-comparable but mouse-only gel study), weak for wound care and post-procedure skin (an expert-panel consensus review rather than a reported trial), and anecdotal/unresolved for acne (named as a use in a broad dermatology review with no dedicated acne trial).

The product's closest thing to independent recognition is its packaging: the bottle image on Tower 28's own page shows "Accepted" from the National Eczema Association, "Accepted" from the National Rosacea Society, and "Recognized" from the National Psoriasis Foundation.[^1] These are third-party program logos shown on the brand's own packaging photography, not evidence of a clinical trial; the graded evidence for the ingredient is on [[hypochlorous-acid]].

Tower 28's FAQ attributes its "Helps with blemishes, flare-ups, eczema, redness, irritation" language to "3rd-party consumer studies and consumer testimonials," a disclosure that appears directly beneath the claim on the brand's own page.[^1] No study name, sample size, or publication is given, so this is treated as a marketing claim rather than graded evidence; see [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its grades rest on hypochlorous acid's general evidence, preliminary at best and use-dependent, set out on [[hypochlorous-acid]]; its own "helps with" claim is self-described as resting on consumer testimonials, not a clinical trial, and its concentration is undisclosed.
