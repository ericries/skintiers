A silicone gel that dries to a flexible film, sold for use straight after procedures and on open or compromised skin. **Bottom line:** it has something most cosmetic-adjacent products here do not, a randomized trial against an active comparator, and that trial found its advantage lasted one day.

> **Not on the US device list its sibling is on.** A query of the FDA device registration and listing database on 2026-11-17 returns four device listings held by STRATPHARMA AG: Stratacel, Stratamark, Colsigen and a vaginal applicator.[^fda] Stratamed is not among them, and separate searches of the 510(k) database for the device name Stratamed and for Stratpharma as applicant return no records.[^fda] That is a statement about those three queries on that date, not proof the product is unavailable in the US. The regulatory background for this class of dressing is on [[stratacel-film-forming-dressing]] and is not repeated here.

## Summary

### The trial

A randomized, single-blinded, split-scalp comparison in people having follicular unit extraction hair transplants, which the authors chose because the wound area is large and a patient can serve as their own control.[^fue] Twenty enrolled, nineteen completed, average 1,778 follicles harvested. Stratamed on one side of the scalp, bacitracin on the other.

The result, in the authors' words: "Healing response and outcome preference were significantly higher at day 1 in the SM group and by day 7, both groups were similar."[^fue] Everything else was a tie. "There were no significant differences between groups for edema, erythema, or crusting", and none "for subject-reported outcomes of pain, pruritis, or FACE-Q scores".[^fue] Asked which they preferred, "44% of subjects preferred using SM versus 22% who preferred Bac".[^fue]

## The Rubric

**What the evidence supports: a faster start, not a better finish.** The authors conclude it "may speed the healing response in the early phase of wound healing", and that hedge is theirs.[^fue] A one-day advantage on a clinician's assessment is a real finding from a design that controls for the patient, and it is also the narrowest possible version of the claim.

**What it did not do.** No difference in the things a patient actually experiences: swelling, redness, crusting, pain, itch. If you are buying it expecting a visibly calmer week, the trial does not support that.

**Scale and comparator both matter.** Nineteen completers is small. Bacitracin is an antibiotic ointment rather than a neutral control, and it is a well-known contact allergen, so beating it on day 1 and matching it thereafter is a different statement from beating petrolatum or nothing. No trial against either was read for this page.

**No price recorded**, and no photograph; the brand page in the local cache lists the product without either.

## The Evidence

Six papers mention Stratamed in PubMed. One is the randomized trial above; the rest are case series and case reports, including preterm infant skin injuries, non-healing burn wounds and nonhealing scalp wounds.[^pubmed] This page cites only the trial, because a case series cannot tell you whether the dressing or the time did the work. PubMed carries no conflict-of-interest statement for the trial.

## Common Marketing Claims

Quarantined, not endorsed. Stratpharma calls Stratamed "a breakthrough in wound healing" and describes it as "an advanced flexible, film-forming wound dressing for use immediately following medical and aesthetic procedures, on acute or chronic wounds and compromised skin surfaces", developed "with a special consistency to ensure it remains in full contact with the wound bed or damaged surface", which "allows prevention of abnormal scarring to begin earlier than ever before".[^brand] The scarring claim is the one to hold lightly: the trial read for this page ran to day 7 and measured no scar outcome at all.

## Who it is for

Someone who has just had a procedure, wants a dressing with an actual randomized comparison behind it, and understands that the measured advantage was on day one. [[silicone-gel-and-sheeting-for-scars]] covers the separate and better-evidenced question of silicone for established scars.
