Kiehl's Ultra Facial Cream with Squalane is a fragrance-free daily face moisturizer built around 4.5% [[squalane]], a proprietary "Glacial Glycoprotein," and what Kiehl's calls "Pro-Ceramides," sold in five sizes from 0.95 fl oz to an 8.0 fl oz jar.[^1]

> Kiehl's longstanding "#1 facial cream," recently reformulated and renamed to headline its squalane content. The core idea, an occlusive lipid plus barrier-supporting ceramides plus a humectant, is a well-evidenced way to hydrate and support a dry or compromised skin barrier (see [[moisturizing]]), and squalane and ceramides both have supportive class-level evidence.[^2][^3] The specific numbers Kiehl's markets, "hydrates skin 15 layers deep," "barrier repair in just 1 hour," "72-hour hydration," come only from the brand's own unpublished instrumental testing, not a peer-reviewed or third-party trial.[^1]

## Summary

Kiehl's Ultra Facial Cream is the brand's best-selling face moisturizer, reformulated and renamed "Ultra Facial Cream with Squalane" for a version built around 4.5% [[squalane]], the ingredient the brand now leads with on the label and jar.[^1] The rest of the named formula is a proprietary "Glacial Glycoprotein," which Kiehl's says is "originally sourced from Antarctic sea glaciers" and helps "deeply hydrate and maintain skin's moisture balance," and a group the brand calls "Pro-Ceramides."[^1] Kiehl's markets it as fragrance-free, non-comedogenic, dermatologist-tested, and "sensitive skin tested and suitable for all skin types."[^1]

The underlying idea, combining an occlusive emollient with barrier lipids and a humectant, is one of the better-evidenced approaches to daily moisturizing and support of a dry or barrier-impaired skin surface; the general case for that combination, including [[squalane]]'s role as a skin-identical occlusive and [[ceramides]]' role as barrier lipids, is set out on [[moisturizing]], [[squalane]], and [[ceramides]]. What is specific to this product is narrower: Kiehl's headline numbers ("hydrates skin 15 layers deep," "barrier repair in just 1 hour," "72-hour hydration") are footnoted on the brand's own product page to "an instrumental study," in most cases run against untreated rather than competitor-treated skin, not a published or third-party clinical trial (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1]

Kiehl's states on its product page that Ultra Facial Cream "has earned the National Eczema Association's Seal of Acceptance," and the jar packaging shown on the page carries the Seal's mark; this is reported as the brand's own claim, since an independent listing on the National Eczema Association's own product directory could not be located as of 2026-08-23.[^1] As of 2026-08-23, Kiehl's own site listed it at $26 for 0.95 fl oz, $39 for 1.7 fl oz, $72 for 4.2 fl oz, and $115 (marked "best value") for 8.0 fl oz, plus a 5.07 fl oz refill at $72 (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)).[^1]

## The Evidence

The formulation logic here, pairing an occlusive lipid with ceramide-type barrier lipids and a humectant, is the same general approach covered on [[moisturizing]]: keeping skin's surface moisturized this way is one of the more consistently supported routines for dry, sensitive, or barrier-impaired skin. [[Squalane]]'s page describes it as "a well-tolerated, skin-identical emollient with a favorable safety review," though "no controlled trial isolates squalane's effect on human skin" on its own.[^2] [[Ceramides]] have a stronger, "solid" category-level evidence base as barrier lipids, per that ingredient's own rubric.[^3] Kiehl's "Glacial Glycoprotein" and "Pro-Ceramides" are brand-specific ingredient names; no independent, non-Kiehl's trial of either as used in this formula, or of the finished product, was found.

Kiehl's product page attributes its headline hydration claims to internal instrumental testing rather than a published trial: "hydrates skin 15 layers deep" is sourced "in the stratum corneum, based on an instrumental study vs untreated skin"; "barrier repair in just 1 hour" is "based on an instrumental study on tape-stripped skin"; and "72-hour hydration" is "based on an instrumental study," with no comparator or methodology detail given.[^1] These are measurements of the brand's own device-based readouts against untreated skin in most cases, not independent clinical endpoints and not benchmarked against a competing moisturizer.

> **Bottom line.** The general moisturizing approach behind this cream, occlusive plus barrier lipids plus a humectant, has solid class-level support. The product's own headline hydration numbers rest entirely on unpublished testing run by Kiehl's, not an independent or peer-reviewed source.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"72-hour hydration."** Sourced on Kiehl's own product page to "an instrumental study," with no published methodology or comparator disclosed.[^1]
- **"Hydrates skin 15 layers deep."** Footnoted as "based on an instrumental study vs untreated skin," measured in the stratum corneum.[^1]
- **"Barrier repair in just 1 hour."** Footnoted as "based on an instrumental study on tape-stripped skin," a lab model of a damaged barrier rather than a clinical condition.[^1]
- **"1 Ultra Facial Cream sold every 30 seconds in the US."** A sales-volume claim Kiehl's attributes to its own distribution data across all Ultra Facial Cream sizes and formats from August 2023 through August 2024; it is a popularity statistic, not an efficacy claim.[^1]
- **"National Eczema Association's Seal of Acceptance."** Stated by Kiehl's and shown on the jar in product photography; an independent listing on the NEA's own site could not be located as of 2026-08-23, so this is reported as the brand's claim.[^1]
