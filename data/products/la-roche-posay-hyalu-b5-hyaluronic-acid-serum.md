La Roche-Posay Hyalu B5 Pure Hyaluronic Acid Serum is a 30 mL hydrating face serum built around two forms of hyaluronic acid, panthenol (vitamin B5), and madecassoside.[^lrpme]

> A hydrating serum whose case rests on the well-evidenced humectant roles of hyaluronic acid and glycerin, see [[hyaluronic-acid]] and [[moisturizing]]; glycerin sits second in the declared ingredient list, immediately after water and ahead of both hyaluronate forms. [No independent study of this specific product was located](#the-evidence); La Roche-Posay's own claims that it reduces wrinkles and repairs skin "within only one hour" are the brand's own marketing, not independently tested, and are quarantined below.

## Summary

La Roche-Posay markets Hyalu B5 on its Middle East product page as an anti-wrinkle serum, listing benefits it calls "REPAIR," a "repair serum that is effective within only one hour," "REPLUMP," an "instant replumping efficacy," and "REDUCE," which it says "reduces wrinkles, re-plumps the skin, and increases skin volume and elasticity."[^lrpme] The two forms of hyaluronic acid the product's name implies are borne out by the declared ingredient list, which contains both sodium hyaluronate and hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid.[^lrpme]

Its declared ingredient list opens water, glycerin, alcohol denat., propylene glycol, panthenol, pentylene glycol, dimethicone, PEG-6 caprylic/capric glycerides, PPG-6-decyltetradeceth-30, and glyceryl isostearate, then madecassoside, before reaching either hyaluronic-acid form: sodium hyaluronate (twelfth of 22 ingredients) and hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid (fifteenth).[^lrpme] [[glycerin|Glycerin]], a longer-established and higher-tier humectant on [[moisturizing]]'s own evidence ranking, sits second in the list, ahead of every other named active, so it is likely doing at least as much of the hydrating work as the two hyaluronate forms the name emphasizes. Of those two forms, sodium hyaluronate is the standard, typically higher-molecular-weight form, and hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid is a more processed, lower-molecular-weight form; the label does not state either molecular weight in kilodaltons, so it is not possible to confirm whether the hydrolyzed form falls in the range the general evidence on [[hyaluronic-acid]] shows actually crosses the stratum corneum.

No published trial has tested this exact serum. Its case instead rests on the general evidence for hyaluronic acid and glycerin as humectants ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence), and [[hyaluronic-acid]], [[glycerin]], [[moisturizing]]).

## The Evidence

Hyaluronic acid and glycerin, applied topically as humectants, reliably raise a measured skin-hydration reading in controlled testing; the case for hyaluronic acid, including the molecular-weight distinction that determines whether it penetrates past the skin's outer layer, is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]], and glycerin's case is set out on [[glycerin]] and [[moisturizing]], where glycerin sits in a higher evidence tier than hyaluronic acid. This serum's declared ingredient list places glycerin second overall, ahead of both hyaluronate forms, so glycerin's well-evidenced humectant effect is at least as relevant to this formula as the headline hyaluronic-acid claim.[^lrpme]

No independent study of this specific product was located; the product-specific facts available are its composition and label claims. What is specific to this formulation is its combination of a standard hyaluronate form (sodium hyaluronate) with a more processed, lower-molecular-weight form (hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid), the two hyaluronate forms on its ingredient list.[^lrpme] That composition is directionally consistent with the general finding on [[hyaluronic-acid]] that only lower-molecular-weight forms cross the stratum corneum, but no independent study isolates this specific two-form blend's own effect, and the label does not disclose the molecular weight of either form.

> **Bottom line.** No independent study has isolated this serum's own effect. Its case rests on the general evidence for hyaluronic acid and glycerin as humectants, applied to a formula that leads with glycerin ahead of either named hyaluronate form.
