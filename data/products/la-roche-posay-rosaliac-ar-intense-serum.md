La Roche-Posay Rosaliac AR Intense Serum is a fragrance-free daily serum marketed to visibly reduce facial redness, built on a glycerin-and-silicone soothing base plus two of the brand's proprietary anti-redness actives, ambophenol and neurosensine.[^inkee]

> The brand calls it a "visible redness reducing serum" enriched with "ambophenol, a powerful natural extract, soothing neurosensine, and light reflectors."[^inkee] Stripped of the trade names, that is a botanical extract (Tambourissa Trichophylla) and a soothing peptide (Acetyl Dipeptide-1 Cetyl Ester), neither of which has independent, peer-reviewed evidence for reducing rosacea or facial redness, in an otherwise gentle glycerin-and-silicone base. Judge it as a pleasant, fragrance-free soothing moisturizer for reactive skin, not a proven redness treatment; the topicals that actually reduce rosacea redness are on [[rosacea]].

## Summary

Rosaliac AR Intense is positioned within La Roche-Posay's Rosaliac line for redness-prone and sensitive skin. The brand describes it as an "intensive daily formula [that] helps to visibly reduce facial redness," enriched with ambophenol, neurosensine, and "light reflectors," and it is formulated alcohol-free and fragrance-free.[^inkee] Two independent ingredient databases list the same short INCI, so the formula is well characterized.[^inkee][^hse]

The two names doing the marketing work decode to ordinary INCI entries. "Ambophenol" is Tambourissa Trichophylla Leaf Extract, a plant extract that sits seventh on the list; "neurosensine" is Acetyl Dipeptide-1 Cetyl Ester (Calmosensine), a soothing dipeptide listed last.[^inkee] Neither has published, independent clinical evidence for reducing rosacea or facial erythema. Notably, the formula contains no niacinamide, the ingredient most often reached for in redness products, so this is not a niacinamide serum.[^inkee]

## The Evidence

No independent, controlled trial of this serum was located; its case rests on its two proprietary actives and its soothing base.

**Calming and moisturizing (the defensible part).** The base is a gentle, fragrance-free, alcohol-free glycerin-and-silicone system that hydrates and comforts reactive skin. Of the named actives, only Acetyl Dipeptide-1 Cetyl Ester (Calmosensine) has any cited soothing data: in a single manufacturer-referenced test it reduced capsaicin-induced stinging, though a comparator soothing molecule (4-t-butylcyclohexanol) performed somewhat better.[^inkee] That is a comfort effect, not a demonstrated reduction in clinical redness.

**Visibly reducing facial redness (the marketed claim).** This is the weak part. Ambophenol (Tambourissa Trichophylla Leaf Extract) has no independent description or trial data in the ingredient databases, and neurosensine's evidence is the capsaicin-sting model above, not rosacea.[^inkee] The brand's "light reflectors" language implies an optical blurring of redness, but the declared INCI contains no pigments or micas to provide it.[^inkee][^hse] For redness that is actually rosacea, the treatments with controlled evidence, azelaic acid, brimonidine, ivermectin, and metronidazole, are covered on [[rosacea]] and are not what this serum contains.

> **Bottom line.** A gentle, fragrance-free soothing serum that will comfort and hydrate reactive, redness-prone skin, sold on two proprietary "anti-redness" actives that lack independent evidence for reducing facial erythema.[^inkee] Reasonable as a sensitive-skin moisturizing step; not a substitute for the evidence-based rosacea topicals on [[rosacea]].

## What's In It

Full declared ingredient list, in order, corroborated by two independent ingredient databases: Aqua/Water, Glycerin, Isostearyl Neopentanoate, Butylene Glycol, Pentylene Glycol, Polysorbate 20, Tambourissa Trichophylla Leaf Extract (ambophenol), Glyceryl Acrylate/Acrylic Acid Copolymer, Dimethicone, Dimethiconol, Ammonium Polyacryldimethyltauramide/Ammonium Polyacryloyldimethyl Taurate, Disodium EDTA, Acetyl Dipeptide-1 Cetyl Ester (neurosensine/Calmosensine).[^inkee][^hse] It is alcohol-free and fragrance-free.[^inkee]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Visibly reduce facial redness," enriched with "ambophenol... soothing neurosensine, and light reflectors."** The brand's own positioning; the two named actives lack independent redness-reduction evidence, and the declared INCI shows no pigment "light reflectors."[^inkee][^hse]
