An oral prescription tablet for psoriasis, included here because this site covers [[psoriasis]] and almost everything else it covers for that disease goes on the skin. **Bottom line:** it is genuinely FDA-approved, checked rather than assumed, with randomised trials behind it, and the trade-off is that it is a systemic drug with systemic side effects. The trial numbers live on the two study pages and are not restated here.

> **Read the route before the evidence.** This is a swallowed tablet. Nothing about it transfers to a cream, a serum or anything else applied to skin, and nothing on this page supports a topical apremilast product. The label's own warnings are diarrhoea, nausea and vomiting, depression, weight decrease, drug interactions and hypersensitivity.[^spl]

## Summary

Apremilast is marketed by Amgen as **OTEZLA** and **OTEZLA XR** under two applications, NDA205437 and NDA210745, and openFDA records the marketing category for both as **NDA**, meaning a genuinely approved new drug application rather than one of the older unapproved-drug categories this site has been caught by before.[^ndc][^spl] Route is oral and the product type is Human Prescription Drug.[^spl]

The approved indications, quoted from the label: "Adult patients with: Active psoriatic arthritis", "Plaque psoriasis who are candidates for phototherapy or systemic therapy" and "Oral ulcers associated with Behçet's Disease", plus "Pediatric patients 6 years of age and older" for psoriatic arthritis and for "Moderate to severe plaque psoriasis who are candidates for phototherapy or systemic therapy".[^spl] Paediatric use is weight-gated: "OTEZLA is indicated for patients weighing at least 20 kg, and OTEZLA XR is indicated for patients weighing at least 50 kg."[^spl]

It comes as 10 mg, 20 mg and 30 mg film-coated tablets, with OTEZLA XR as a 75 mg extended-release tablet.[^spl] No price is given here; it is a prescription drug and what anyone pays depends on insurance.

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: modest, and that is this site's own reading of its own trials.** The grade is not taken from the approval. It comes from the figures already set out on [[paller-2025-apremilast-paediatric-psoriasis-sprout-pmid-40737559]], where the one timepoint with a comparator showed about a twenty percentage point gap over placebo. [[merola-2026-apremilast-genital-psoriasis-discreet-pmid-41369155]] covers genital psoriasis, where the later response rates have no concurrent placebo arm at all.
- **Evidence quality: solid, by the standards of this site.** Randomised trials, an FDA review, a published label with full warnings. Compared with the home devices and cosmetic serums covered elsewhere here, that is a different tier of evidence entirely, and the honest comparison is not with a moisturizer.
- **Record finding: the label does not claim to know how it works.** "Apremilast is an oral small molecule inhibitor of phosphodiesterase 4 (PDE4) specific for cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP). PDE4 inhibition results in increased intracellular cAMP levels. The specific mechanism(s) by which apremilast exerts its therapeutic action is not well defined."[^spl] That last sentence is worth holding next to any skincare product that explains its mechanism with complete confidence and has no trial at all.
- **Record finding: no boxed warning, but a real warning list.** The label carries no boxed warning. It does carry hypersensitivity, noting that "Cases of angioedema and anaphylaxis have been reported during post marketing surveillance.", advice to "Consider OTEZLA/OTEZLA XR dosage reduction or suspension if patients develop severe diarrhea, nausea, or vomiting", and a depression warning telling patients, caregivers and families "to be alert for the emergence or worsening of depression".[^spl] The commonest adverse reactions in plaque psoriasis are given as "diarrhea, nausea, upper respiratory tract infection, and headache, including tension headache".[^spl]
- **The only contraindication is the drug itself.** "OTEZLA/OTEZLA XR is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity to apremilast or to any of the excipients in the formulation."[^spl]

## The Evidence

Not restated here. Two trials are already written up on this site and the numbers belong to them: [[paller-2025-apremilast-paediatric-psoriasis-sprout-pmid-40737559]] for paediatric plaque psoriasis and [[merola-2026-apremilast-genital-psoriasis-discreet-pmid-41369155]] for genital psoriasis. Read both before forming a view, because the second one's headline figures come from a timepoint with no placebo arm.

The disease itself is at [[psoriasis]]. Whether a systemic drug is the right choice is a decision made with a prescriber and nothing on this page is advice.

> **Bottom line.** The best-evidenced thing this site covers for psoriasis, and the one with the most to weigh against it. It is approved, it is randomised-trial backed, the measured gap over placebo is about twenty points at the one comparator timepoint this site has read, and it is a tablet with systemic side effects whose mechanism its own manufacturer calls not well defined.

## Common Marketing Claims

> This section exists on every product page here to quarantine the maker's own claims from the evidence. On this page it works differently, and the difference is the point.

- **The claims are the approved indications, and those were reviewed.** Elsewhere on this site a quarantined claim is a sentence the company wrote that nobody checked, as on [[nuface-plus]], where a firming claim sits inside a 510(k) whose only testing was electrical safety. Here the indication text quoted above went through an FDA review of efficacy data. Treating the two as equivalent would flatter the device pages and short-change this one.
- **The hedge is in the same document as the claim.** The label states the indications and then says "The specific mechanism(s) by which apremilast exerts its therapeutic action is not well defined."[^spl] A claim that works and a mechanism nobody can state are not in conflict, and it is unusual to see both printed by the same party.
- **No promotional copy was read for this page.** Amgen's own marketing site was not fetched, so nothing above comes from advertising; every quotation is from the FDA label or the openFDA product records.
