CeraVe Skin Renewing Night Cream is a fragrance-free face moisturizer for nighttime use, built around three ceramides, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, and a pair of named peptides, sold in a 1.7 fl oz size.[^2]

> A ceramide-and-humectant night cream whose barrier case rests on the category-level evidence for occlusive-plus-humectant moisturizers with barrier lipids set out on [[moisturizing]] and [[ceramides]]. Its niacinamide and peptide additions are declared on the ingredient list but not disclosed at a stated percentage,[^1][^2] and CeraVe markets the cream as "Developed With Dermatologists"[^1] and able to "help restore your skin's protective barrier overnight."[^1] CeraVe's own site lists it at $21.99; Target lists the 1.7 fl oz size at $15.49.[^1][^2]

## Summary

CeraVe Skin Renewing Night Cream is a night moisturizer for the face marketed to "soften and renew the look of tired skin" and, per CeraVe, to "lock in skin's moisture and help restore your skin's protective barrier overnight."[^1] Its main job is a health one shared with any well-formulated barrier moisturizer: slowing water loss and holding water at the skin surface for dry or barrier-impaired skin. It does that with an occlusive (dimethicone, plus a dimethicone/vinyl dimethicone crosspolymer and cyclopentasiloxane), humectants (glycerin and hyaluronic acid), and three ceramides declared on the ingredient list, ceramide NP, ceramide AP, and ceramide EOP, matching CeraVe's marketed "3 essential ceramides."[^1] That barrier-lipid case is category-level; the general evidence for ceramide moisturizers is set out on [[ceramides]], and for moisturizing generally on [[moisturizing]].

What distinguishes this cream from a plainer CeraVe moisturizer is the addition of niacinamide and two named peptides, tripeptide-1 and caprooyl tetrapeptide-3, marketed together under CeraVe's "biomimetic peptides" name.[^1][^2] Niacinamide is declared on the ingredient list without a stated percentage;[^1][^2] the general evidence for niacinamide, including its barrier-lipid effect, is set out on [[niacinamide]]. The peptides are marketed to "help stimulate collagen production"[^1] and, on Target's listing, to "help[] to reduce the look of fine lines and wrinkles";[^2] the general evidence for signal peptides on that cosmetic endpoint is set out on [[peptides]], though the peptide behind the best-known trial there (palmitoyl pentapeptide-4) is a different named molecule than the ones in this formula.

No published trial of this exact cream is cited here (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)). It is priced in the middle of the drugstore ceramide-moisturizer range: CeraVe's own site lists it at $21.99, and Target lists the 1.7 fl oz size at $15.49 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).

## The Evidence

Ceramide moisturizers are a physiologically reasonable way to replace a lipid that eczema-prone and dry skin measurably lack, and in a randomized trial a ceramide-dominant regimen improved a barrier measurement (transepidermal water loss) without beating a matched base on eczema severity; that evidence and its limits are set out on [[ceramides]]. Niacinamide, in mechanistic and manufacturer research, raised stratum-corneum ceramide and free-fatty-acid levels and lowered transepidermal water loss in dry skin, a barrier-lipid effect that overlaps what the formula's own ceramides are meant to do; that evidence is set out on [[niacinamide]]. Peptides as a class are well tolerated and show a small, mostly manufacturer-run cosmetic effect on the look of fine lines; that evidence, including its limits, is set out on [[peptides]].

No published trial has tested this exact cream, its combination of ceramides NP/AP/EOP, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, and the two named peptides, or CeraVe's MVE delivery technology, which the manufacturer describes as providing "controlled release for all day hydration"[^1] and which Target's listing describes as "continually releas[ing] moisturizing ingredients."[^2]

> **Bottom line.** No study has isolated this exact cream's own effect. Its case rests on the category-level evidence for ceramide-and-humectant barrier moisturizers, and on the mechanistic and preliminary evidence for niacinamide and signal peptides, covered on [[ceramides]], [[niacinamide]], [[peptides]], and [[moisturizing]].

## What's In It

The ingredient list as declared by the manufacturer and matched on Target's listing.[^1][^2]

Barrier and hydration ingredients:

- [[dimethicone]], an occlusive, plus a dimethicone/vinyl dimethicone crosspolymer and cyclopentasiloxane
- glycerin and sodium hyaluronate (hyaluronic acid), humectants
- ceramide NP, ceramide AP, and ceramide EOP (see [[ceramides]]), plus cholesterol and phytosphingosine, additional barrier lipids
- [[niacinamide]], declared without a stated percentage
- tripeptide-1 and caprooyl tetrapeptide-3 (see [[peptides]]), CeraVe's "biomimetic peptides"[^1]

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, caprylic/capric triglyceride, shea butter (butyrospermum parkii butter), glyceryl stearate SE, cetyl alcohol, saccharide isomerate, stearic acid, palmitic acid, soybean sterols (glycine soja sterols), allantoin, carbomer, cetearyl alcohol, behentrimonium methosulfate, sodium hydroxide, myristic acid, sodium lauroyl lactylate, sodium benzoate, sodium citrate, phenoxyethanol and ethylhexylglycerin (preservatives), tocopherol, laureth-9, citric acid, biosaccharide gum-1, xanthan gum, dextran, and butylene glycol.[^1][^2]
