A hyaluronic acid serum whose most prominent humectant is not hyaluronic acid. **Bottom line:** glycerin sits second in the ingredient list and sodium hyaluronate sixth, which is not a criticism of the formula so much as a correction to the name on the bottle.

> **Read the order.** The declared list runs Aqua, Glycerin, Cetearyl Ethylhexanoate, Dimethicone, Ammonium Polyacryloyldimethyl Taurate, Sodium Hyaluronate, Panthenol, then the three ceramides. That order comes from INCIDecoder, an aggregator of manufacturer-declared lists, because CeraVe's own product page carried no ingredient list when it was fetched on 2026-11-09; it is reported here as a reading anyone can repeat rather than as evidence.[^brand] Glycerin is the better-evidenced humectant of the two and it is the one present in larger quantity; what each does is on [[glycerin]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]].

## Summary

A leave-on serum from CeraVe, 24 declared ingredients. The brand describes it as a serum "with vitamin B5 and three essential ceramides" that "helps bind moisture to your skin's surface, smoothing it and providing up to 24 hours of hydration while helping restore your skin's protective barrier".[^brand]

**Both of those component claims check out against the list.** Vitamin B5 is panthenol, seventh of 24, and the three ceramides are NP, AP and EOP, eighth through tenth, alongside cholesterol and phytosphingosine. That lipid set is the CeraVe signature and it is genuinely present rather than a trace.

**No price.** CeraVe's product page carries no price in its markup and no retailer was fetched.

## The Rubric

**Hydrating the skin surface (cosmetic)**
Graded modest on solid evidence, and the grade belongs to the ingredients rather than to this bottle, because no product-specific trial was found. Humectants work and two well-evidenced ones are here in quantity. Modest rather than notable because surface hydration is a real but shallow and reversible effect.

**Supporting the skin barrier (cosmetic)**
Graded modest on mixed evidence. The ceramide, cholesterol and phytosphingosine combination is the set barrier research is built around, and [[ceramides]] grades that evidence mid on its own page. No concentration is declared for any of them here.

**Improving the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles (cosmetic)**
Graded minimal on preliminary evidence. Hydrated skin shows fine lines less, which is why the claim is phrased as appearance. It reverses when the hydration does. Anything that changes a wrinkle rather than its appearance is on [[retinoids]] and [[skin-firmness-elasticity]].

## The Evidence

**What the ingredient order tells you and what it does not.** Positions six through ten are where this formula does its work, and INCI order is only a ranking, not a set of concentrations; anything below one percent may be listed in any order. Sodium hyaluronate is effective at fractions of a percent, so sixth place is not a complaint. The point is narrower: a product named for one ingredient is led by another.

**No trial of this serum was found.** Cosmetic moisturisers are not required to carry one, so the absence is structural rather than a lapse, and the honest route is to grade the ingredients and say which bottle contains them.

**Where the 24 hour figure comes from is not stated.** The brand's claim of "up to 24 hours of hydration" is quantified and uncited on the page read.[^brand] Corneometry studies of humectant formulations exist as a category, but nothing links this product to one here.

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven unless it also appears in The Evidence above.

- **"providing up to 24 hours of hydration".** A specific number with no study attached on the page it appears on.[^brand] Up to is also doing work in that sentence.
- **"improve the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles".** True in the narrow optical sense and routinely read as something stronger.[^brand]
- **"helping restore your skin's protective barrier".** The lipid set is present and the ingredient-level evidence is graded mid rather than strong; restore is a stronger verb than that evidence carries.[^brand]
- **The name itself.** Hyaluronic acid is in it, sixth, under a larger quantity of glycerin.
