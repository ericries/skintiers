The dpl Nuve is the oldest device in this family that is still worth reading, cleared in 2010, and it is the one that breaks two patterns the later filings established. **Bottom line:** its predicates are other companies' devices rather than the firm's own, and its summary claims clinical testing while its own test-data section lists none. Modality evidence is on [[home-led-light-therapy]]; the family tree is at [[dpl]].

> **This is the only filing in the family that says the word clinical, and it does not back it up.** The equivalence sentence reads "Conclusions drawn from the nonclinical and clinical tests demonstrate that the" device "is as safe and effective as the legally marketed predicate devices", a page break falling between those two fragments in the original.[^k101382] Then the Test Data section lists IEC 60601-1, EN 60601-1-2 and EN 60601-2-22 for electrical safety, and "Patient contact materials are demonstrated safe under ISO 10993 testing", and stops.[^k101382] No clinical study, population or result appears anywhere in the document. Compare [[dpl-spotlite]] and [[dpl-faceware]], whose 2019 summaries say plainly that their conclusions come from nonclinical tests.

## Summary

Cleared 2010-11-09 as K101382 under two product codes at once, listed in the file as "OHS: light-based over-the-counter wrinkle reduction" and "GEX: powered laser surgical instrument (for acne treatment)", regulation 878.4810, with the classification name given as "Infrared lamp".[^k101382] The device is described as "lightweight, handheld" with "interchangeable heads which emit light energy": a blue head near 415nm for acne, and a 625nm red head plus an 830nm infrared head used in combination for periorbital wrinkles.[^k101382] The handle holds the electronics plus "a fan for cooling and an automatic shut-off safety feature".[^k101382] No price or photograph is given here; the brand's domains were unreachable and this is a 2010 clearance.

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate acne**
- **Effect size: modest.** The indication reads "Indicated as an over-the-counter phototherapy device for the treatment of mild to moderate acne", delivered by a blue head near 415nm.[^k101382] Blue and blue-red LED carries the best grade any home acne device received in the 2026 review.[^grade]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** That grade belongs to the modality. This file asserts clinical testing without describing any, which is weaker than a clean statement of nonclinical testing because it cannot be checked.

**Periorbital wrinkles**
- **Effect size: minimal.** The wrinkle indication is narrower than the later devices in this family: periorbital, meaning the skin around the eyes. LED sits at Level B for facial rejuvenation, behind home fractional nonablative lasers at Level A.[^grade]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** No wrinkle outcome is reported in the filing.

**Dose, which this file does not give at all**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a gap in the record.** Checked by string search of the full extracted text, this document contains no irradiance figure, no energy-level row and no treatment-area measurement.[^k101382] That is worth knowing because the firm's 2018 perioral filing does print those numbers, as recorded on [[revive-light-therapy-lux-lip-care]]. The disclosure appears later in the company's history, not at the start.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable.**

## The Evidence

**Its predicates belong to other companies.** The file states the device is "substantially equivalent in function and technology" to "the Omnilux New U for periorbital wrinkle treatment and the" Tanda skin care product "for treatment of acne".[^k101382] Neither is an LED Technologies product. Every later filing this site has read names only the firm's own earlier devices as predicates, so the self-predication pattern documented at [[dpl-faceware]] is something the company grew into rather than started with. This 2010 clearance is the outside anchor the rest of the chain eventually traces back to.

**The acne code changed later.** Acne here sits under GEX, described in the file as a powered laser surgical instrument, while the firm's 2019 acne devices sit under OLP.[^k101382][^openfda] Same company, same kind of blue light, different regulatory bucket nine years apart, which is a reminder that a product code records an administrative decision rather than a property of the device.

**The corporate entity was different too.** The submitter is given as LED Technologies, LLC at an address in Elizabeth, Colorado, with an outside regulatory consultant as the contact, where the 2017 to 2019 filings are from LED Technologies, Inc. in Greenwood Village.[^k101382] Reported as what the documents show, with nothing inferred about the company's history from it.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The cleared indications are mild to moderate acne and periorbital wrinkles; moderate to severe or scarring acne warrants a dermatologist rather than a light, and the ladder is on [[acne]]. A device emitting infrared near the eyes makes the manufacturer's eye-protection instructions matter more here than on a cheek.
