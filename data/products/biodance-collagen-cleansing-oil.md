## Summary

A 200 ml first-step oil cleanser, 24,000 KRW on the brand's Korean store and 20,400 KRW after a standing discount.[^brand] **Bottom line:** as a cleansing oil it is built correctly, with four esters and polyglyceryl emulsifiers doing the work. The collagen in the name is declared at **100 ppb**, which is one part in ten million, and it is washed off.

> **Read the number the brand published.** The full ingredient list on Biodance's own Korean product page gives "콜라겐추출물(100ppb)", collagen extract at 100 parts per billion, twenty-first of thirty-two ingredients.[^brand] Most brands do not publish a figure at all, so this is more transparency than usual. It is also a figure that answers the question: 0.00001% of a rinse-off product.

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: modest, for cleansing.** The first six ingredients are Ethylhexyl Palmitate, Cetyl Ethylhexanoate, Polyglyceryl-10 Diisostearate, Polyglyceryl-6 Dicaprate, Sorbitan Sesquioleate and Polyglyceryl-2 Laurate.[^brand] Two esters to dissolve makeup and sunscreen, then the polyglyceryl emulsifiers that let it rinse. That is the right arrangement, and the brand's own directions confirm the format: apply to a dry face with dry hands, massage, add water to emulsify, rinse with lukewarm water.[^brand] Whether the oil step is worth doing at all is argued on [[double-cleansing]].
- **Effect size: minimal, for the collagen.** 100 ppb is the declared amount, and the product is removed within a minute. No page here needs to litigate whether topical collagen does anything, because at this concentration in this format the question does not arise.
- **The peptides are real, numerous, and last.** Eleven of them close the list, in order: Acetyl Hexapeptide-8, Oligopeptide-1, Copper Tripeptide-1, Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4, Palmitoyl Tripeptide-5, Tripeptide-1, Hexapeptide-9, Palmitoyl Tripeptide-38, Pentapeptide-4 and Hexapeptide-12, with Tocopherol among them.[^brand] Three have pages here and all three are graded **weak**: [[peptides]], [[copper-peptides]] and [[argireline-acetyl-hexapeptide-8]]. A long peptide list is a formulation signature, not a dose.
- **Record finding: the brand has not registered an efficacy claim.** The Korean product record includes a line for functional cosmetic status, and the answer given is "무", no.[^brand] Korea's 기능성화장품 category is the route that requires submitted efficacy data for claims such as wrinkle improvement or brightening. A cleanser would not normally be registered there, which is the point: the collagen and peptide framing is marketing language sitting outside any claim the company has had to support.
- **Who makes it.** The record names the manufacturer as Nowcos and the responsible distributor as Beauty Selection, manufactured in Korea.[^brand] Biodance is a brand, not a factory, which is ordinary in Korean cosmetics and worth knowing when comparing formulas across brands.

## What the two published ingredient lists disagree about

For the second Korean product covered here this week, the aggregator's list does not match the brand's. Biodance publishes thirty-two ingredients with collagen extract twenty-first and the ppb figure attached. The entry on incidecoder, an ingredient aggregator rather than a primary source and named here only as that, lists thirty-four, places Collagen Extract twenty-third with no concentration, and adds three botanicals the brand's own list does not carry: Eclipta prostrata extract, Melia azadirachta leaf extract and Moringa oleifera seed oil.

This page follows the brand. The lesson generalises: on a Korean product, the 전성분 block on the company's own page is the list to read, and it is the only one of the two that carried the number that matters here.

## The Evidence

None specific to this product. The category evidence, including the one controlled test of an oil cleanser against a foaming cleanser, belongs to [[double-cleansing]] and is not repeated here.

> **Bottom line.** A competent, fairly priced oil cleanser wearing a collagen costume. Buy it to take off sunscreen, not to put collagen on your face; at 100 ppb in a rinse-off, the name is the active ingredient.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted and **not** endorsed here.

- **"Pore Perfecting, Deep Cleansing"**, read directly off the bottle in the product photograph carried above, which also reads "COLLAGEN CLEANSING OIL" and "NET 6.76 fl.oz. (200 ml)", matching the brand page's 200ml size option. Deep cleansing is a texture-and-feel claim with no measurement behind it, and pore perfecting is not something a rinse-off oil has been shown to do.
- **The product name.** Calling it a Collagen Cleansing Oil is the strongest claim on the package, and the brand's own ingredient list quantifies it at 100 ppb.[^brand]
- **"클렌징부터 시작하는 스킨케어"**, the brand's tagline, skincare that starts from cleansing.[^brand] Positioning rather than a claim, and the most defensible thing on the page.
