Anua Azelaic Acid 10 Hyaluron Redness Soothing Serum is a lightweight, green-tinted Korean serum (30ml) that lists azelaic acid as its second ingredient at a labeled 10%, in a base of centella (cica), panthenol, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, and ceramide NP.[^1][^2]

> Azelaic acid has good controlled evidence for acne, melasma, and rosacea, but that evidence comes from the prescription 15% gel and 20% cream, not a 10% cosmetic (see [[azelaic-acid]]). This serum's labeled 10% is half the 20% in the prescription cream AZELEX and in Cos De BAHA AZ20, below the 15% in the rosacea gel FINACEA, and the same 10% carried by The Ordinary's and Paula's Choice's azelaic products.[^1][^3][^4]

## The Evidence

Whether azelaic acid works, and for what, is covered on [[azelaic-acid]]. In short, it is FDA-approved as a prescription drug for inflammatory acne as a 20% cream (AZELEX) and for papulopustular rosacea as a 15% gel (FINACEA); its melasma evidence is covered on [[azelaic-acid]].[^3][^4] The product-specific question is how this 10% cosmetic serum sits against other azelaic acid options.

Most azelaic acid sold without a prescription is formulated at about 10%, and Anua states this serum is "formulated with 10% Azelaic Acid."[^1] That is half the concentration of the 20% prescription cream AZELEX and of the 20% cosmetic Cos De BAHA AZ20, and below the 15% of the prescription rosacea gel FINACEA.[^1][^3][^4] It is the same labeled strength as The Ordinary's and Paula's Choice's 10% azelaic products. The foundational trials that established azelaic acid for acne and melasma used 20% cream, and its rosacea approval rests on the 15% gel.[^3][^4] The 10% figure is a cosmetic label claim, not an independently assayed or FDA-reviewed concentration.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** At a labeled 10%, this serum carries azelaic acid at the lower end of the range sold to consumers and below the 15% gel and 20% cream strengths behind azelaic acid's controlled evidence. Its expected azelaic benefit is inferred from the ingredient at that strength (see [[azelaic-acid]]).[^1][^3][^4]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer and the Olive Young listing.[^1][^2] The ingredients with a functional role are linked or named; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

Active and supporting ingredients:

- [[azelaic-acid|azelaic acid]], labeled 10% and listed second on the INCI (after water)
- betaine salicylate, a [[salicylic-acid|salicylic acid]] derivative used as a mild leave-on exfoliant
- [[centella-asiatica|centella asiatica]] extract with madecassoside, asiaticoside, asiatic acid, and madecassic acid, the cica soothing fraction
- [[niacinamide]], listed low in the order (nineteenth)
- sodium hyaluronate and biosaccharide gum-1 ([[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]] and a botanical humectant)
- one of the [[ceramides]] (ceramide NP), a barrier lipid
- squalane, an emollient, and zinc PCA
- panthenol (provitamin B5), allantoin, dipotassium glycyrrhizate (licorice), aloe, beta-glucan, and green tea (Camellia sinensis leaf water), soothing and antioxidant agents

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, propylene glycol, dipropylene glycol, butylene glycol, and pentylene glycol (solvents and humectants), sodium hydroxide (neutralizes the acid and sets pH), hydroxyethylcellulose (thickener), 1,2-hexanediol and ethylhexylglycerin (preservative and humectant), caprylyl/capryl glucoside (a mild surfactant), and hydrogenated lecithin (emulsifier).

The declared ingredient list carries no synthetic colorant (no CI dye) and no added fragrance; the green tint comes from the botanical extracts (neem, Melia azadirachta, and green tea).[^1][^2]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and listing claims not established by a trial of this product:

- **"10% Azelaic Acid."** A concentration stated on a cosmetic label, not an independently assayed figure. Azelaic acid's controlled trials used the 15% gel and 20% cream, and this is a cosmetic rather than an FDA-reviewed drug (see [[azelaic-acid]]).[^1][^3][^4]
- **"Instantly soothe irritated skin and redness."** Anua attributes an immediate redness effect to a "naturally derived green tint."[^1] A green tint offsets red optically, a cosmetic effect distinct from azelaic acid's measured pharmacological action.
- **"Gentle Calming Complex including Cica and Blue Tansy Oil."** The cica (centella) fraction is on the ingredient list, but blue tansy (Tanacetum) oil does not appear on the declared INCI.[^1][^2]
- **Hyaluronic-acid hydration marketing.** Sodium hyaluronate and biosaccharide gum-1 are present as humectants;[^1] the product's demonstrated case rests on its azelaic acid, evidenced only at the ingredient level (see [[azelaic-acid]]).
- **"Non-comedogenic, Low-Irritation."** Manufacturer testing-result labels, not independently published outcomes.[^1]
