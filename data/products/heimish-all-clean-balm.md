Heimish All Clean Balm is a Korean sherbet-textured cleansing balm, used to dissolve makeup and sunscreen as the first step of a [[double-cleansing|double cleanse]]. Heimish's US store lists the 120 mL jar at $13.99, marked down from a $19.99 regular price.[^heimish]

> A cleansing balm earns its keep by removing what a water-based cleanser leaves behind, and this one does that job with the same emollient-ester-and-PEG-emulsifier mechanism as any oil balm, with the removal evidence set out on [[double-cleansing]]. Where it stands apart is a long list of citrus peel oils and essential oils (orange, grapefruit, and bergamot peel oils; lavender, eucalyptus, tea tree, geranium, and frankincense oils) plus a run of flower extracts. There is no single "Fragrance" or "Parfum" entry on the label, but that panel of essential oils is itself the fragrance, and citrus and essential oils are common sensitizers and irritants. This is a "natural"/botanical marketing angle, not a treatment benefit, and reactive or fragrance-sensitive skin may do better with a fragrance-free balm such as [[clinique-take-the-day-off-cleansing-balm]].

## Summary

Heimish calls this a "revolutionary formula" that "easily and quickly removes stubborn waterproof makeup, dirt, and impurities," transforming from balm to oil to a hydrating milk, and markets it as vegan, hypoallergenic, and causing "no eye irritation."[^heimish] The formula opens with the same two emollient esters used across the category (ethylhexyl palmitate and cetyl ethylhexanoate), followed by PEG emulsifiers and polyethylene for texture, then shea butter and (on the incidecoder decode) donkey milk, then a long run of botanical extracts and essential oils, ending with tocopheryl acetate (vitamin E) and a preservative. It is listed at $13.99 for the 120 mL jar at Heimish's US store ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-27).[^heimish]

## The Evidence

The product-specific claim worth checking is removal, and it is directionally sound for the format: as set out on [[double-cleansing]], an oil-based cleanser removes water-resistant sunscreen and long-wear makeup that a foaming water-based cleanser leaves largely behind. That controlled evidence is a single small study and is a property of the oil-balm format generally, not of this brand; no independently published trial of this exact product is cited here.

The formula's real point of difference from other balms on the site is its fragrance load. Instead of a single "Fragrance/Parfum" entry, it carries a wide panel of citrus peel oils (orange, grapefruit, bergamot) and essential oils (lavender, eucalyptus, tea tree, geranium, amyris, juniper, frankincense), plus extracts of jasmine, rose, lily, iris, freesia, narcissus, and lotus flower. Those oils are the fragrance, functionally, even though no ingredient is literally named "Fragrance." Citrus peel oils and essential oils are recognized skin sensitizers and irritants, which matters most as leave-behind residue and cumulative fragrance load rather than as a photosensitivity risk, since the citrus oils rinse off with the balm. Heimish frames this as "a mild, refreshing herbal scent" and a "relaxing spa experience"[^heimish]; that is a marketing description of the same ingredient panel, not evidence the blend is gentler than a synthetic fragrance would be. Shea butter (and, on the incidecoder decode, donkey milk) sits mid-list as an emollient, marketing-forward addition that carries no product-specific efficacy claim here.

> **Bottom line.** A workable oil-to-milk cleansing balm whose removal job is the same evidence-backed mechanism as any balm in the category. Its distinguishing feature is a large botanical and essential-oil panel marketed as natural and spa-like, which functions as fragrance and is a plausible irritant for reactive skin, an argument for a fragrance-free balm instead if tolerance is a concern.
