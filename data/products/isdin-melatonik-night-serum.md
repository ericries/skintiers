Isdin Melatonik Night Serum (sold by the brand as Isdinceutics Melatonik) is a prestige, oil-based "serum-in-oil" sold in a single 30 mL bottle, built around melatonin, bakuchiol and three forms of vitamin C, and marketed as an overnight recovery treatment.[^1][^2]

> A high-priced night oil whose headline claims rest on the brand's own unpublished 12-week study rather than independent trials. Its two ingredients with outside evidence, bakuchiol and vitamin C, are set out on [[bakuchiol]] and [[vitamin-c]]; both sit at undisclosed, likely modest concentrations here, with plain ascorbic acid listed near the very end of the ingredient list. Melatonin, the product's namesake active, has no independent published trial behind this product. It is listed at $175 for 30 mL on Isdin's own site.[^1]

## Summary

Melatonik Night Serum is Isdin's prestige "Isdinceutics" night oil, positioned as a restorative treatment that "works while you sleep."[^1][^2] It is sold in one 30 mL size for $175, applied as 4 to 5 drops massaged into the face and neck.[^1][^2] The formula is oil-continuous (a "serum-in-oil"): its INCI list opens with two emollient oils, then its highest-listed active (ascorbyl tetraisopalmitate, a vitamin C ester), before water appears 12th of 16 ingredients, and its three headline actives, melatonin, bakuchiol, and vitamin C, are not assigned a percentage anywhere in the sourced material.[^2] The full ingredient order is sourced to Dermstore's listing alone; Isdin's own product page does not publish a complete INCI list.[^1]

Of the three, bakuchiol and vitamin C have outside evidence covered on this site. Bakuchiol's case rests mainly on a single independent 12-week trial (n=44) that found 0.5% bakuchiol matched 0.5% [[retinol]] on wrinkle area and pigmentation with better tolerability; the wider bakuchiol literature is mostly open-label or uncontrolled (full case on [[bakuchiol]]). Vitamin C's antioxidant and brightening case, strongest for L-ascorbic acid at established concentrations, is set out on [[vitamin-c]]. This product's version of both is diluted across an undisclosed dose: bakuchiol sits 7th of 16 ingredients, and its three vitamin C forms are split between ascorbyl tetraisopalmitate (3rd), ascorbyl palmitate (14th), and plain ascorbic acid (15th, second to last).[^2]

Melatonin, the product's namesake ingredient and the 10th of 16 listed per Dermstore's INCI declaration,[^2] has no independent published trial specific to this formula. The brand's own clinical claims, 97% of a 12-week study group reporting increased hydration, 93% reporting a "rejuvenated" look and a repairing effect, and 100% reporting softer skin, are unpublished, company-run figures without a stated control arm; they are weighed against the record under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1][^2]

## The Evidence

No independent trial isolates this product, its exact melatonin/bakuchiol/vitamin C combination, or the concentration of any of the three. What outside evidence exists lives on the ingredient pages for its two actives with independent human data.

Bakuchiol's strongest support is one independent, double-blind, 12-week trial (n=44) finding 0.5% bakuchiol matched 0.5% [[retinol]] on wrinkle surface area and hyperpigmentation, with fewer reports of scaling and stinging; the rest of the bakuchiol literature is largely open-label, uncontrolled, or manufacturer-run (full rubric on [[bakuchiol]]). This product does not disclose a bakuchiol percentage, and its position (7th of 16 ingredients, after two emollient oils, a vitamin C ester, an emollient, a solvent, and a preservative booster) is consistent with a lower dose than the tested 0.5%, though INCI order alone cannot confirm an exact figure.[^2]

Vitamin C's antioxidant and pigment-brightening case, covered on [[vitamin-c]], is strongest for L-ascorbic acid formulated at low pH and effective concentration. This product splits its vitamin C between three forms, none at a disclosed percentage; ascorbyl tetraisopalmitate (a fat-soluble ester) is 3rd of 16 ingredients, while ascorbyl palmitate and plain ascorbic acid are 14th and 15th, after water, positions consistent with trace-level rather than treatment-level amounts of those two forms.

No published, independent trial of topical melatonin on human skin exists for this product or its formula. The brand's 12-week clinical figures, 97% reporting increased hydration, 93% reporting a rejuvenated look, 93% reporting a repairing effect, and 100% reporting softer skin, are presented on Isdin's own product page without a sample size, control group, or published methodology.[^1][^2] Absent an independent trial or a published protocol, those figures cannot be treated as established evidence for melatonin's effect and are quarantined under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its two actives with outside evidence, bakuchiol and vitamin C, carry modest, mostly preliminary support at concentrations this product does not disclose and its ingredient order suggests are low. Melatonin, its namesake and marketed centerpiece, rests on the brand's own unpublished study alone.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), in the manufacturer's declared order.[^2] Ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest is the oil, solvent, and preservative system.

Actives:

- ascorbyl tetraisopalmitate, a fat-soluble form of [[vitamin-c]], 3rd on the list
- bakuchiol, 7th on the list, no disclosed percentage
- melatonin, 10th on the list, no disclosed percentage, the product's namesake active
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant, 13th on the list
- ascorbyl palmitate, a fat-soluble form of [[vitamin-c]], 14th on the list
- ascorbic acid, the pure water-soluble form of [[vitamin-c]], 15th of 16, after water

Base, oil, and preservative system: caprylic/capric triglyceride and dicaprylyl carbonate (emollient oils, 1st and 2nd on the list), [[squalane]] (an emollient), alcohol denat. (a solvent), 1,2-hexanediol and caprylyl glycol (preservative boosters), fragrance, PEG-8 (a solubilizer), water (12th of 16), and citric acid (pH).[^2]

The full declared ingredient list runs to 16 ingredients and includes added fragrance.[^2]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"97% noted increased hydration," "93% reported skin looks rejuvenated," "93% noted repairing effect," "100% found skin softer to the touch" (12-week study).** Isdin's own testing, described on the brand's product page and repeated by a retailer, without a published trial, sample size, control arm, or peer review.[^1][^2] No independent data corroborates these figures (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)).
- **"Restorative nighttime serum-in-oil which works while you sleep to help your skin recover from daily stresses."** A positioning claim resting on melatonin's marketed antioxidant role; the brand describes melatonin as "an effective indirect antioxidant" that "stimulates the skin's natural defenses,"[^1] and the retailer describes it as melatonin that "stimulates antioxidant enzymes."[^2] No independent trial supports this mechanism for the product itself (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)).
- **Hydration "for up to 8 hours after application."** A duration claim on the brand's product page without a stated method or published trial.[^1]
- **"Non-comedogenic," "paraben-free," "sulfate-free," "mineral oil-free."** Formulation and testing claims for which no methodology or published result is given on the product page.[^1]
