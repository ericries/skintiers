Head & Shoulders Clinical Strength Dandruff Defense Sensitive Scalp is an over-the-counter medicated shampoo built around selenium sulfide 1%, the same active as [[head-shoulders-clinical-strength-dandruff-defense-selenium-sulfide|the brand's Intensive Itch Relief formula]] and [[selsun-blue-selenium-sulfide-shampoo|Selsun Blue]], positioned as the gentler member of the line rather than a different antifungal.[^dailymed]

> An OTC drug, not a cosmetic: the FDA Drug Facts label lists the active as "Selenium Sulfide 1%" with the purpose "Anti-dandruff, anti-seborrheic dermatitis."[^dailymed] Selenium sulfide is one of the best-evidenced antidandruff antifungals, ranked strong on [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]]. What makes this variant "sensitive scalp" is its base, not its dose: the formula drops the menthol and peppermint oils used in the brand's cooling "itch relief" version and adds aloe barbadensis leaf juice, but it still runs on a standard sodium lauryl sulfate surfactant and still lists fragrance, so "sensitive scalp" is a formulation choice, not a fragrance-free or sulfate-free claim.[^dailymed]

## Summary

This is the selenium sulfide member of the Head & Shoulders line aimed at people who find the brand's mintier formulas irritating. It is regulated as an OTC drug: the Drug Facts label states the active as "Selenium Sulfide 1%," the purpose as "Anti-dandruff, anti-seborrheic dermatitis," and the use as helping "prevent recurrence of flaking, itching, irritation, scaling and redness associated with dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis."[^dailymed]

Selenium sulfide's evidence sits at the ingredient-class level, set out on [[seborrheic-dermatitis]] and ranked on [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]], where it is a first-line topical antifungal alongside [[ketoconazole]]. No trial of this exact shampoo was located, so the dandruff grade rests on that active plus its FDA monograph status, not on a product-specific study. Nothing in the formula difference (no menthol, added aloe) changes the antifungal grade; those are comfort and skin-feel choices layered on the same 1% active.

## The Evidence

Two health uses, graded above:

**Dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis (the labeled use).** This is what the product is approved and labeled for. Selenium sulfide is an FDA OTC-monograph anti-dandruff and anti-seborrheic-dermatitis active,[^dailymed] and it is one of the best-evidenced options in its class: a 2026 randomized trial found a 0.6% selenium disulfide shampoo statistically matched 2% ketoconazole on efficacy, the detail carried on [[farris-2026-beyond-efficacy-0-6-selenium-disulfide-shampoo-matches-2-k]]. The caveat specific to this product is that no trial tested this exact 1% formulation, sensitive-scalp base included.

**Fungal acne (off-label, mechanistic).** Malassezia folliculitis responds to antifungals, and selenium sulfide is one of the better off-label washes for it, but that use is not on this product's label and the general [[fungal-acne]] evidence is what supports it, not a study of this shampoo.

> **Bottom line.** A well-formulated selenium sulfide 1% antidandruff shampoo whose labeled dandruff and seborrheic-dermatitis use is strongly supported at the ingredient level.[^dailymed] The "sensitive scalp" positioning removes the cooling menthol and mint oils and adds aloe, but the base is still an SLS surfactant system with added fragrance, so people who react to those specific ingredients should still check the label rather than assume this formula is hypoallergenic.

## What's In It

As an OTC drug the label separates the active from everything else. Active ingredient: "Selenium Sulfide 1%."[^dailymed]

Inactive ingredients, verbatim from the Drug Facts label: "Water, sodium lauryl sulfate, glycol distearate, sodium chloride, cocamidopropyl betaine, sodium citrate, sodium xylenesulfonate, dimethicone, fragrance, citric acid, sodium benzoate, tetrasodium EDTA, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, aloe barbadensis leaf juice."[^dailymed]

Compared with the brand's Intensive Itch Relief selenium sulfide shampoo, this formula drops menthol, mentha piperita (peppermint) oil, and mentha arvensis leaf oil, and adds aloe barbadensis leaf juice; both still use sodium lauryl sulfate as the primary surfactant and both still list fragrance.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Sensitive Scalp."** The label supports this as a formula difference, no menthol or mint oils, plus added aloe, versus the brand's other selenium sulfide variants.[^dailymed] It does not mean fragrance-free or sulfate-free: the label still lists fragrance and sodium lauryl sulfate, so it is a relative, not absolute, gentleness claim.
- **"Clinical Strength."** The active is selenium sulfide 1%, a standard OTC-monograph concentration, not an elevated or prescription-only dose.[^dailymed]
- **Off-label "fungal acne" use.** Plausible on mechanism (selenium sulfide is antifungal) but not on the product's OTC label and documented on [[fungal-acne]] at the ingredient level, not for this shampoo.
