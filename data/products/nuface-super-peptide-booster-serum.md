A $65 peptide serum sold as a companion to NuFACE's microcurrent devices, $2.17 a millilitre. **Bottom line:** the label cannot tell you how much of the peptides it is named for you are getting, the best-evidenced thing in the bottle is the niacinamide at position five, and the two regulatory-sounding badges on the page do not mean what a shopper would reasonably take them to mean.

> **"CLINICALLY PROVEN" turns out to mean a survey.** The brand's three headline figures are "85% saw improvement of forehead lines & wrinkles", "80% saw lifted brow & awakened eye" and "83% saw improvement of fine lines & crows feet around eyes". Its own asterisk explains them: "results from a post-usage survey of 42 female subjects age 38-65 after 12 weeks of use".[^clinpanel] Saw, in a survey, with no control group and no measurement. The brand does print the sample size, the age range and the word survey, which is more disclosure than most of this shelf offers.

## Summary

NuFACE sells it at **$65.00 for 1.0 fl oz / 30 mL**, in stock on its own storefront, described as "This firming, line-smoothing serum is powered by a triple-peptide blend and IonPlex® to visibly reduce fine lines, boost hydration and enhance your microcurrent treatment."[^brand] The bottle reads "SUPER PEPTIDE BOOSTER" under the line "FIRMING + RADIANT", above "FORMULATED WITH IONPLEX".[^photo]

The declared list runs 36 ingredients and begins "Water/Aqua/Eau, Glycerin, Propanediol, Diglycerin, Niacinamide, Sea Silt Extract".[^brand]

**Only the first six positions mean anything.** From position seven, "Ammonium Acryloyldimethyltaurate/VP Copolymer", through position 32, "Zinc Gluconate", the list runs in strict alphabetical order, and the last four entries are a second alphabetical run of preservatives.[^brand] Alphabetising is permitted for ingredients at or below one percent, so the ordering below position six carries no concentration information at all. Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7 at 24 and Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1 at 25 are where the letter P puts them, not where their dose does.

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: unknown, from the peptides.** [[palmitoyl-tripeptide-1]] and [[palmitoyl-tetrapeptide-7]] are the pair marketed as Matrixyl 3000 elsewhere, and their evidence is on their own pages rather than restated here. What this label cannot tell you is the dose: they sit in the alphabetical tail, so their position is a fact about spelling. Anyone reading position 24 as meaning barely present would be guessing, and so would anyone reading it the other way.
- **Effect size: modest, and not from the ingredient in the name.** [[niacinamide]] is fifth, inside the concentration-ordered head, so it genuinely is present in more quantity than anything in the alphabetical tail. It is the best-evidenced active declared, and that evidence belongs to [[niacinamide]], not to this bottle.
- **The "triple-peptide blend" counts a dipeptide.** The list contains exactly two palmitoyl peptides. Carnosine, at position 14, is a dipeptide, so reading it as the third is defensible rather than wrong.[^brand] It is worth knowing that the third member is carnosine and not another signal peptide; [[carnosine]] grades it separately.
- **Record finding: the FDA CLEARED badge belongs to the devices.** openFDA lists at least ten 510(k) clearances to this company, and every one is a device: microcurrent stimulators under product code NFO, one wrinkle-reduction device under OHS, and the Gel Primer conductive medium under GYB.[^fda] None is a serum. FDA does not clear cosmetics at all, so on a serum page that badge can only be referring to something else in the catalogue.
- **IonPlex is a brand term, not an ingredient.** It appears on the bottle and in the copy but not in the ingredient list, so there is nothing to check it against.[^brand][^photo]

## The Evidence

None independent. The 42-person survey above is the whole of it, and it measures opinion rather than skin. [[peptides]] carries the class evidence and [[niacinamide]] the strongest single case for anything in this formula; neither is repeated here.

> **Bottom line.** A competent, unremarkable serum at a premium price, wearing two badges that borrow authority from elsewhere. If the peptides are why you are buying it, note where they sit on the list.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own page and **not** endorsed here.

- "powered by a triple-peptide blend and IonPlex® to visibly reduce fine lines, boost hydration and enhance your microcurrent treatment"[^brand] Three claims. The microcurrent one is the only one specific to this brand and no test of it is offered.
- "85% saw improvement of forehead lines & wrinkles"[^clinpanel] Self-report from 42 people, as the brand's own asterisk says.
- "FDA CLEARED" and "CLINICALLY PROVEN", as banners above the product.[^brand] Neither describes this serum.
