The Ordinary AHA 30% + BHA 2% Peeling Solution is a rinse-off exfoliating mask from [[the-ordinary|The Ordinary]] combining five acids (glycolic, lactic, tartaric, and citric AHAs plus salicylic acid, a BHA) at a brand-stated total concentration of 32%, formulated at pH 3.50-3.70.[^1][^2]

> A high-strength, rinse-off acid mask: glycolic acid leads a blend of AHAs plus a BHA, at a brand-stated total of 32% acid and a pH of 3.50-3.70.[^1][^2] The brand's own directions limit it to once or twice weekly, "ideally in the evening," left on "no more than 10 minutes" and then rinsed off, and restrict it to "experienced users of acid exfoliation," warning against use on "sensitive, peeling, or compromised skin" and flagging increased sun sensitivity.[^1] The general evidence for [glycolic acid](#whats-in-it) and [salicylic acid](#whats-in-it) is [modest and use-specific](#the-evidence); no trial isolates this five-acid combination.

## Summary

The Ordinary AHA 30% + BHA 2% Peeling Solution is a leave-on-but-rinsed-off treatment mask, not a daily leave-on serum: the brand's directions call for applying it to clean, dry skin and rinsing it off within 10 minutes, used once or twice a week.[^1] Its [full ingredient list](#whats-in-it) is led by glycolic acid, followed by four other acids further down the list: lactic, tartaric, and citric acid (all AHAs) and salicylic acid (a BHA), which the brand states combine to "5 exfoliating acids, at a total of 32%."[^1] Neither the brand nor Target discloses the individual percentage of each acid, so the product name's "30%" and "2%" figures describe the AHA-blend total and the BHA figure rather than a single-ingredient concentration; the formula's pH is stated as 3.50-3.70 by both the brand and Target.[^1][^2]

This is a markedly higher total acid load than a leave-on toner or serum: the general evidence for glycolic acid covers controlled trials of 8% to 10% leave-on creams and reserves 20% to 30% concentrations for in-office peels, and salicylic acid's OTC monograph range for acne tops out at 2%, matching this formula's stated BHA level; see [[glycolic-acid]] and [[salicylic-acid]]. The brand frames the product accordingly: its own directions restrict use to "experienced users of acid exfoliation," warn against use on "sensitive, peeling, or compromised skin," and carry a "Sunburn Alert" that the product "may increase your skin's sensitivity to the sun."[^1] That sun-sensitivity warning matches the clearest documented health effect of glycolic acid generally, that AHAs increase UV sensitivity, an effect graded on [[glycolic-acid]].

It is inexpensive: the brand lists the 30ml bottle at $9.50, and Target separately lists the same 1 fl oz (30ml) size at $9.50 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^1][^2] It carries no product-specific clinical trial; its case rests on a high total acid concentration and a short use window (rinsed off within 10 minutes) rather than a measured outcome, and the general evidence for its lead actives is [modest and use-specific](#the-evidence).

## The Evidence

No trial isolates this five-acid combination or this product. Glycolic acid's clinical evidence, covering photoaging, acne peels, and melasma, is graded in full on [[glycolic-acid]]; its clearest health finding is that 10% glycolic acid increases skin's sensitivity to UV light, an effect that reverses within a week of stopping, at a concentration well below this product's stated total acid load. Salicylic acid's clinical evidence, strongest for wart removal and modest but guideline-recognized for acne at OTC strengths up to 2%, is graded in full on [[salicylic-acid]]; this formula's BHA component sits at the top of that OTC range. Neither ingredient page's evidence isolates a 32%-total, five-acid, rinse-off combination, and no source fetched here reports a trial of this specific formula.[^1][^2]
