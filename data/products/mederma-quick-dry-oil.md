Mederma Quick Dry Oil is a fast-absorbing, fragrance-free body oil from [[mederma|Mederma]], a 32-ingredient blend of plant oils and botanical extracts, including a red onion (Allium cepa) bulb extract, sold in 2.02oz (60ml), 3.4oz (100ml), and 5.1oz (150ml) sizes and marketed for stretch marks, scars, uneven skin tone, and dry skin.[^mederma]

> **Bottom line.** This is an emollient multi-oil blend, not a treatment that remodels stretch marks or scars. Its named active botanicals, onion extract chief among them, have weak or no independent trial support for improving established striae or scars, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence); at best it moisturizes and may make skin feel and look temporarily smoother.

## Summary

The declared ingredient list on [incidecoder](https://incidecoder.com/products/mederma-quick-dry-oil) runs 32 ingredients long, opening with caprylic/capric triglyceride and sunflower seed oil and continuing through a long run of plant oils (sweet almond, rose hip, coconut, emu, soybean) and botanical extracts (green tea, apricot, red onion, cinnamon, cantaloupe, clove, apple, rose, ylang ylang, orchid, ginger), a base of water, and standard solvents, humectants, and preservatives. Mederma's own product page confirms the fast-absorbing oil format and calls out sweet almond oil, rose hip oil, sunflower oil, and coconut oil by name, describing sweet almond as a nourishing and moisturizing agent, rose hip as "a natural skin brightener" rich in vitamin C, sunflower oil as helping "maintain skin's natural moisture level," and coconut oil as sealing in moisture.[^mederma]

This is a cosmetic body oil, not an FDA-monograph drug: it carries no listed active ingredient and makes only cosmetic-appearance claims. The onion extract, the one botanical shared with its sibling product [[mederma-advanced-scar-gel]] and the ingredient carrying Mederma's Cepalin branding, is the only one of the 32 ingredients with independent trial data behind it, [reviewed on [[allium-cepa-onion-extract]]](#the-evidence); the rest of the blend functions as an emollient, occlusive, and fragrance-carrying oil base.

## The Evidence

No published trial of Mederma Quick Dry Oil itself was located. The product's link to the evidence base runs through two ingredient-level pages this site already reviews in full: [[allium-cepa-onion-extract|onion (Allium cepa) extract]], the botanical Mederma brands as Cepalin, and the broader question of whether any topical meaningfully treats established stretch marks, covered on [[stretch-marks]].

In short, a 2021 meta-analysis of 13 randomized trials found onion extract gel beats doing nothing but performs no better than other topical scar treatments while causing more irritation, and the independent split-scar trials against plain petrolatum found no advantage over a cheap emollient. Even the silicone-gel formulations that show a texture edge cannot separate onion extract's contribution from the silicone base, which itself carries only weak Cochrane-graded evidence. The full numbers are on [[allium-cepa-onion-extract]] and [[mederma-advanced-scar-gel]]. None of that trial evidence tested onion extract in an oil vehicle, on stretch marks specifically, or at this product's concentration, which is undisclosed.

> **Bottom line.** The single ingredient in this oil with any trial support, onion extract, has weak and mixed evidence even in its best-studied form (a gel on surgical scars); no trial supports this oil blend, or onion extract generally, for stretch marks.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"BOTANICAL EXTRACTS of natural origins, including Cepalin to improve appearance of stretch marks, scars, uneven skin tone and dry skin."**[^mederma] The onion-extract-driven claim; the underlying ingredient's independent trial evidence is weak and mixed even in its best-studied gel form, [set out above](#the-evidence), with no trial of it in this oil vehicle or for stretch marks specifically.
- **"Rose Hip oil, rich in Vitamin C, is a natural skin brightener."**[^mederma] A brand ingredient claim; no independent trial of this product's brightening effect was found.
- **"#1 RECOMMENDED BRAND: Mederma is the #1 recommended brand of scar treatments" (of U.S. physicians and pharmacists surveyed by an independent market research firm).**[^mederma] A brand-level recommendation survey, not a product-specific efficacy result, and it is a scar-treatment recommendation cited on a stretch-mark product page.
