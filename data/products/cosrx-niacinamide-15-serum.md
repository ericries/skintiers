COSRX The Niacinamide 15 Serum is a short, water-based leave-on serum built around 15% niacinamide paired with zinc PCA, sold in a single 20 mL bottle and marketed for oil control and post-acne marks.[^1]

> A short, cheap niacinamide serum at an unusually high printed strength. Topical niacinamide has modest evidence, much of it from small or manufacturer-run trials, for supporting the skin barrier, lightening hyperpigmentation, and mild inflammatory acne; that case is set out on [[niacinamide]]. Every one of those trials used far less than 15%: cosmetic chemists put niacinamide's clinically tested range at 2% to 5% and say higher doses "will always increase the risk of skin irritation" without a proven benefit in return.[^2] It is listed at $25 for 20 mL, with its niacinamide printed at roughly triple the top of the 2% to 5% range used in the trials behind niacinamide's evidence.[^1][^2]

## Summary

The Niacinamide 15 Serum is COSRX's plain niacinamide serum: a short ingredient list built around [[niacinamide]] at a manufacturer-declared 15%, paired with zinc PCA, in a single 20 mL bottle.[^1] The general case for topical niacinamide, modest but real evidence for barrier support, facial hyperpigmentation, and mild inflammatory acne, drawn mostly from small or manufacturer-run trials, is set out on [[niacinamide]]. That evidence base was generated at 2% to 5% niacinamide, well below this product's 15%.[^2]

What distinguishes this serum from category peers such as [[the-ordinary-niacinamide-10-zinc-1|The Ordinary's 10% + Zinc 1%]] and [[naturium-niacinamide-serum-12-zinc-2|Naturium's 12% + Zinc 2%]] is mainly the printed strength: at 15%, it is higher than either, and higher than the range niacinamide's health-oriented benefits have actually been tested in ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)). No published trial isolates this product or its exact 15%/zinc PCA combination. Cosmetic chemists Victoria Fu and Gloria Lu of Chemist Confessions put niacinamide's clinically tested range at 2% to 5%, with no established efficacy gain and a higher irritation risk above it.[^2]

The manufacturer's own claim, that the serum is "clinically proven to reduce sebum by up to 50% in 4 weeks," is a company-run test not tied to any published trial; it and the rest of the label's language are weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims). It is listed at $25 for the single 20 mL size on COSRX's own site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-15).[^1]

## The Evidence

Topical niacinamide is a well-tolerated active with modest evidence for supporting the skin barrier, lightening facial hyperpigmentation, and mild inflammatory acne; how each use holds up, and how much of it rests on small or manufacturer-run trials, is set out in full on [[niacinamide]]. Nothing specific to this product was found: no published trial tests COSRX's 15% niacinamide plus zinc PCA formula, so its grades above rest on the general category evidence for niacinamide, all of it generated at lower concentrations than 15%.[^1]

The barrier, pigmentation, and acne evidence summarized with its numbers and limits on [[niacinamide]] was generated at roughly 2% to 5% niacinamide, not 15%. On the concentration question directly, Chemist Confessions state that "niacinamide is actually clinically tested at 2% to 5%" and that "higher doses will always increase the risk of skin irritation even for the most seemingly vanilla ingredient."[^2] At 15%, this serum sits well outside that tested range, on both sides of the trade-off: no cited evidence that more niacinamide clears more sebum, more pigment, or more acne, and a plausibly higher chance of irritation than a 2% to 5% product.

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its grades rest on niacinamide's general, modest, mostly small-trial case; its 15% strength is well above the 2% to 5% that evidence was built on, with no shown extra benefit and a higher irritation risk flagged by cosmetic chemists for any high-dose niacinamide product.[^2]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] The actives with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

Actives:

- [[niacinamide]] 15%
- acetyl glucosamine (N-acetylglucosamine, NAG), an amino sugar
- zinc PCA, a zinc salt the brand pairs with niacinamide for oil control
- [[allantoin]], a soothing agent
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant, last on the list

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, pentylene glycol and butylene glycol (solvents and humectants), 1,2-hexanediol and ethylhexylglycerin (preservative boosters), trehalose (a humectant sugar), xanthan gum and pullulan (thickener and film former), sodium phytate (a chelator), and citric acid (pH).[^1]

The ingredient list is short, 15 ingredients total, and declares no added fragrance.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Clinically proven to reduce sebum by up to 50% in 4 weeks."** COSRX's own testing, described on the product page without a linked published trial, sample size, control arm, or peer review.[^1] The independent published evidence for topical niacinamide is for barrier support, pigmentation, and acne, not a quantified sebum-reduction percentage.[^2]
- **"Deeply refine the appearance of pores" and "regulate excess sebum."** Positioning claims; niacinamide's better-evidenced topical uses, set out on [[niacinamide]], are barrier support, facial hyperpigmentation, and mild acne, not pore size or oil volume.
- **"Non-comedogenic tested," "dermatologist tested," and "hypoallergenic tested."** Manufacturer testing claims for which no methodology, sample, or result is published on the product page.[^1]
- **The 15% concentration itself, as a selling point.** Niacinamide's clinical evidence tops out around 4% to 5%; cosmetic chemists say higher doses bring no established added benefit and more irritation risk, not less.[^2]
