Aestura Atobarrier 365 Hydro Cera-HA Ampoule, sold by the brand internationally as the ATOBARRIER365 Hydro Cera-HA Serum, is a 30 ml Korean hydrating serum built on ceramide, hyaluronic acid, and niacinamide, the lightest-format entry in the Atobarrier 365 line alongside the [[aestura-atobarrier-365-cream|Cream]] and [[aestura-atobarrier-365-lotion|Lotion]].[^aestura]

> A thinner, serum-format entry in the same ceramide-barrier line as the Atobarrier 365 Cream and Lotion, adding hyaluronic acid and a prominent dose of niacinamide. Ceramides reliably move barrier measurements like water loss but have not beaten a plain base on eczema severity in the closest controlled trial, and hyaluronic acid reliably raises a skin-hydration reading without a proven edge on wrinkles at this molecular form, the general cases set out on [[ceramides]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]]. No independent trial tests this exact ampoule; the brand's own hydration and glow figures come from a single uncontrolled, brand-commissioned test.[^aestura]

## Summary

Aestura Atobarrier 365 Hydro Cera-HA Ampoule is a fluid serum from the South Korean brand [[aestura|Aestura]], marketed under the name ATOBARRIER365 Hydro Cera-HA Serum on the brand's international site, and positioned as a lightweight, layerable step for normal-to-dry, oily/combination, and dehydrated sensitive skin.[^aestura] Its health-relevant case rests on two ingredients covered on their own pages: [[ceramides]], the barrier lipids that eczema-prone skin is measurably short of, and [[hyaluronic-acid]], a humectant that reliably raises a measured skin-hydration reading. Ceramide moisturizers move barrier measurements such as water loss, but the one randomized trial of a ceramide cream on that page found no advantage over a matched base for eczema severity itself; hyaluronic acid's water-binding is real, but only its lower-molecular-weight forms cross the outer skin layer, and this product's ingredient list does not specify a molecular weight for its sodium hyaluronate. That general evidence, with its numbers and limits, is set out on [[ceramides]] and [[hyaluronic-acid]] and applies to the moisturizing category more broadly on [[moisturizing]].

The declared ingredient list puts [[niacinamide]] fourth, just behind water, butylene glycol, and glycerin, a markedly higher position than in the cream or lotion, alongside [[hyaluronic-acid|sodium hyaluronate]] and a 3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid (a vitamin C derivative), then further down the same barrier-lipid set as the cream and lotion: [[ceramides|ceramide NP]], cholesterol, phytosphingosine, sphingolipids, and the free fatty acids stearic, palmitic, arachidic, and oleic acid.[^aestura] The brand markets the combination as "Cera-Hyaluronic Acid" or "Cera-HA" technology, a formulation name rather than an independently tested claim.[^aestura] No published, independent trial tests this exact ampoule; the brand cites its own uncontrolled, single-arm test of 31 women, which is not the kind of evidence that supports a grade and is reported, quarantined, under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims) rather than folded into the case above.

The brand states the ampoule contains less than 1% synthetic fragrance, the same tolerability profile as the cream and lotion, a consideration for fragrance-avoidant [[atopic-dermatitis|eczema-prone]] skin ([see Tolerability](#tolerability)).[^aestura]

## The Evidence

Ceramides are a genuine barrier lipid that atopic and sensitive skin is measurably depleted in, and ceramide moisturizers move barrier measurements such as transepidermal water loss; the gap is between that measured barrier effect and a proven edge on eczema severity itself, where the one randomized trial cited on [[ceramides]] found a ceramide cream beat an inactive base on water loss but not on eczema severity. Hyaluronic acid separately and reliably raises a measured skin-hydration reading in placebo-controlled testing, with the caveat that only lower-molecular-weight forms cross the outer skin layer, set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. Moisturizing as a category has stronger evidence for reducing eczema flares, set out on [[moisturizing]], but that category benefit is not specific to either ingredient.

No published, independently reviewed trial of Aestura Atobarrier 365 Hydro Cera-HA Ampoule itself is cited here, so its grades rest on that category-level ceramide and hyaluronic-acid evidence rather than on a product-specific result. The brand's own product page reports a single brand-commissioned test on 31 adult women, ages 20 to 39, with inner dry sensitive skin, run from April 15 to May 14, 2024, covering inner hydration, moisture retention, transepidermal water loss, skin glow, and satisfaction measures.[^aestura] The test had no stated control group, and its results are reported by the brand rather than in an independently published or peer-reviewed source, so it does not support a grade here; its specific claimed figures are reported and weighed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on the manufacturer's product page.[^aestura] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

Hydrating and barrier ingredients:

- [[niacinamide|Niacinamide]], fourth of 42 declared ingredients, ahead of the humectants and barrier lipids below
- [[hyaluronic-acid|Sodium hyaluronate]], a hydrating humectant; the brand does not state its molecular weight
- 3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid, a vitamin C derivative

- panthenol and adenosine, additional conditioning agents
- [[ceramides|Ceramide NP]], cholesterol, and sphingolipids, physiologic barrier lipids

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, butylene glycol, 1,2-hexanediol, carbomer, ethylhexylglycerin, cellulose gum, tromethamine, sodium trimetaphosphate, dipotassium glycyrrhizate, propanediol, hydroxypropyl bispalmitamide MEA, sodium metaphosphate, glyceryl caprylate, potassium hydroxide, sodium lactate, biosaccharide gum-1, caprylic/capric triglyceride, pentylene glycol, citric acid, hydroxyethyl urea, polyglyceryl-10 stearate, propylene glycol, potassium cetyl phosphate, tocopherol (vitamin E), mannitol, silica, acrylates/ammonium methacrylate copolymer, and hydrogenated lecithin.[^aestura]

The formula contains no parabens, formaldehyde or formaldehyde-releasing agents, phthalates, mineral oil, retinyl palmitate, oxybenzone, coal tar, hydroquinone, SLS/SLES sulfates, triclocarban, or triclosan, and the brand states it contains less than 1% synthetic fragrance.[^aestura]

## Tolerability

The brand describes the ampoule as dermatologist-tested, non-comedogenic tested, and sensitive-skin panel tested, without stating participant counts or durations for those programs on the product page.[^aestura] These are brand-reported test programs rather than independently published outcomes. The formula is not fragrance-free: the brand states it contains less than 1% synthetic fragrance, a consideration for fragrance-avoidant or reactive skin.[^aestura]

The brand's own usage directions state the ampoule is meant to be applied "onto clean skin AM & PM, after toner and before moisturizer," with gentle massage for absorption.[^aestura]
