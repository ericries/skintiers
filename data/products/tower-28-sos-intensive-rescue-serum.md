Tower 28's SOS Intensive Rescue Serum is a lightweight, fragrance-free treatment serum built around [[hypochlorous-acid]], from [[tower-28]], sold at about $34 for 59.2 mL (2 fl oz).[^brand] It is the thicker, more concentrated counterpart to the brand's [[tower-28-sos-daily-rescue-facial-spray|SOS Daily Rescue Facial Spray]], and it uses the same single active.[^brand]

> Essentially a stabilized, thickened hypochlorous acid solution: the full ingredient list is water, a silicate thickener, two salts, and hypochlorous acid.[^brand] Hypochlorous acid is a gentle antimicrobial the skin makes itself, well tolerated on reactive skin, but its topical evidence for calming eczema and irritation is preliminary, and it is set out on [[hypochlorous-acid]] rather than repeated here. The three acceptance seals it carries speak to how gently it is tolerated, not to how well it works.

## Summary

This is Tower 28's serum-format hypochlorous acid product, marketed to "soothe chronically sensitive, inflamed skin" and described by the brand as a "concentrated dose of hypochlorous acid," pH balanced to 5.5 and alcohol-free.[^brand] The brand positions it as more intensive than the [[tower-28-sos-daily-rescue-facial-spray|SOS spray]] and notes it is not a moisturizer and should be paired with one.[^brand]

The health case is entirely the hypochlorous acid case, which is covered on [[hypochlorous-acid]]: it is a genuinely gentle antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory agent that reactive, eczema-, and rosacea-prone skin tends to tolerate well, but the controlled evidence that topical hypochlorous acid visibly clears acne or redness is limited.[^brand] The product carries the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance, the National Rosacea Society Seal of Acceptance, and a National Psoriasis Foundation seal; these are tolerability and suitability seals for sensitive-skin conditions, not proof that the serum treats them.[^brand]

The efficacy claims are the reason for caution. The brand's own "helps with blemishes, flare-ups, eczema, redness" line is footnoted to "3rd-party consumer studies and consumer testimonials," and the enthusiastic user reviews (including one crediting it for clearing fungal acne) are anecdotes, not trial evidence.[^brand] No independent, product-specific trial was located.

## The Evidence

The active and its evidence are shared with the spray and belong to the ingredient, so they are set out once on [[hypochlorous-acid]]: hypochlorous acid is antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory, is produced naturally by immune cells, and is well tolerated, but the human topical evidence for skin-condition endpoints is preliminary.[^brand] Being a "concentrated dose" in a serum rather than a spray changes the format and contact time, not the underlying evidence base.

The product-specific facts available are its composition, its price, and its three sensitive-skin acceptance seals. Those seals are awarded for gentleness and suitability on eczema-, rosacea-, and psoriasis-prone skin, which is a real and useful signal for reactive skin, but they are not efficacy trials.[^brand] The "clears skin" and "reduces redness" claims rest on the brand's consumer studies and testimonials, which cannot separate the active from regression to the mean, a gentler overall routine, or placebo.[^brand]

> **Bottom line.** A gentle, well-tolerated hypochlorous acid serum for sensitive and reactive skin, backed by acceptance seals for tolerability rather than trials for efficacy.[^brand] If a simple hypochlorous acid product suits your skin, this is a fragrance-free option; just treat the "clears skin" claims as marketing, and see [[hypochlorous-acid]] for what the active can and cannot do.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list, from the brand: water, sodium magnesium fluorosilicate, sodium phosphate, sodium chloride, and hypochlorous acid.[^brand] In plain terms, that is water plus a silicate thickener (which gives the serum its thicker texture versus the watery spray), two pH- and tonicity-adjusting salts, and the active. There is no added fragrance, no alcohol, and no moisturizing or occlusive base, which is why the brand says to layer a moisturizer over it.[^brand]
