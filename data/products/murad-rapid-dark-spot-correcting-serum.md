Murad Rapid Dark Spot Correcting Serum is a leave-on facial serum built around tranexamic acid, glycolic acid, and 4-ethylresorcinol, sold in a 1.0 fl oz bottle (also offered in a 0.33 fl oz travel size) and marketed for dark spots, uneven tone, and fine wrinkles.[^1]

> A pigment-correcting serum that stacks three actives with distinct mechanisms, tranexamic acid, glycolic acid, and 4-ethylresorcinol, none at a disclosed percentage, and none tested together as this formula. [[tranexamic-acid|Tranexamic acid]]'s best evidence is for oral melasma treatment; its topical evidence, summarized on that page, is thinner and more mixed. [[glycolic-acid|Glycolic acid]]'s one direct pigment trial found it added nothing to hydroquinone in a split-face study, though it has separate, modest evidence for photodamage appearance. The brand's own "reduces dark spots within 7 days" and 91%/94% satisfaction claims are self-reported and not tied to a published trial.[^1] It is listed at $88.00 for the 1.0 fl oz size on Murad's own site.[^1]

## Summary

Rapid Dark Spot Correcting Serum is Murad's flagship pigment-correcting treatment: an alcohol-based leave-on serum whose top ingredients after water are alcohol and [[glycolic-acid]], followed by [[tranexamic-acid]] and 4-ethylresorcinol, a synthetic resorcinol derivative with no dedicated evidence page on this site.[^1] None of the three headline actives are declared at a percentage on the product page or label.[^1]

The general case for each headline active is set out on its own page. [[tranexamic-acid|Tranexamic acid]] has solid evidence when taken orally for melasma but a thinner, more mixed record topically, per a systematic review that still gave it a strong recommendation ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)). [[glycolic-acid|Glycolic acid]]'s one direct pigment trial found no added benefit over hydroquinone alone, though it has separate, modest evidence for photodamage appearance. No published trial isolates this product's specific three-active combination or its undisclosed concentrations.

The manufacturer's own claims, "reduces dark spots within 7 days," 91% reporting brighter skin and 94% noting smoother skin at 14 days, and improved wrinkles and texture by day 28, are self-reported brand testing without a linked published trial, sample size, or control arm; they and the rest of the label's language are weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1] It is listed at $88.00 for the 1.0 fl oz size on Murad's own site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^1]

## The Evidence

Topical tranexamic acid and topical glycolic acid each have their own evidence records, summarized in full on [[tranexamic-acid]] and [[glycolic-acid]]. Nothing specific to this product was found: no published trial tests Murad's combination of tranexamic acid, glycolic acid, and 4-ethylresorcinol at the concentrations used in this serum, so the grades above rest on the general category evidence for each active.[^1]

Tranexamic acid has solid oral-route evidence for melasma but weaker, mixed topical evidence, per a systematic review that still recommends it; see [[tranexamic-acid]] for the underlying trials.

Glycolic acid's one direct pigment trial found peels added no benefit over hydroquinone, though it has separate modest evidence for photodamage appearance; see [[glycolic-acid]] for the underlying trials.

4-ethylresorcinol, the third headline active, has no dedicated evidence page on this site and is not graded here; leaving it out rather than guessing at its evidence follows this site's practice of declining to grade actives it has not yet researched.

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its tranexamic acid grade rests on a topical record that is thinner and more mixed than the oral melasma evidence; its glycolic acid component has direct pigment-trial evidence that found no added benefit over hydroquinone, with glycolic acid's better-supported role being modest photodamage-appearance improvement, not pigment correction specifically.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Reduces dark spots within 7 days."** Brand-run testing described on the product page without a linked published trial, sample size, control arm, or peer review.[^1] The independent published evidence for tranexamic acid and glycolic acid on pigmentation, summarized above, does not support a 7-day timeline.
- **"91% reported brighter skin" and "94% noted smoother skin" in 14 days.** Self-reported consumer-perception results from brand testing, not a controlled clinical trial with an objective pigmentation measure.[^1]
- **"Visibly reduces wrinkles and improves texture by day 28."** A brand testing claim; no independent trial of this specific formula was found.[^1]
- **"Intensive brightening treatment" for "all skin tones without irritation."** A positioning claim; the formula contains alcohol second on the ingredient list and a fragrance blend with several named allergens, both plausible irritants not addressed by the "without irritation" framing.[^1]
