The Philips Lumea IPL 9000 Series (model BRI984/03, with the brand's SenseIQ sensor) is an at-home intense-pulsed-light (IPL) hair-removal device, the market-leading competitor to the [[braun-silk-expert-pro-5-ipl|Braun Silk-expert Pro 5]]. Like all home IPL, it works only on the narrow light-skin-with-dark-hair combination and its evidence is weak; the general light- and laser-hair-removal evidence is set out on [[laser-hair-removal]].[^philips]

> At-home IPL sits at the weak end of an already-limited evidence base, the same verdict as the Braun sibling. A Cochrane review found little evidence for IPL specifically and no long-term hair removal documented for any light or laser device, and home units run at lower energy than the in-office machines in that review (see [[laser-hair-removal]]). No independent trial of this specific device was found. Philips markets it as "clinically proven to achieve up to 5 years of smooth-feeling skin," an asterisked brand clinical claim, not independent evidence, and "smooth-feeling" is a sensory descriptor rather than measured permanent hair removal.[^philips]

## Summary

The Lumea IPL 9000 Series (BRI984/03) is Philips' flagship at-home IPL device, described by the brand as its Lumea "with SenseIQ" and offering "4 dedicated attachments for face, body, armpits and bikini."[^philips] It is the market-leading competitor to the [[braun-silk-expert-pro-5-ipl|Braun Silk-expert Pro 5]]. Like all home IPL, it is built for hair reduction, not permanent removal, and depends on pigment contrast (light skin, dark hair); the general laser/light evidence is on [[laser-hair-removal]]. Philips does not display a price on its accessed US product page (the device is sold through Philips and major retailers at varying prices), and the brand's headline efficacy claim is quarantined below.[^philips]

## The Evidence

There is no independent published trial of this device. Its mechanism, intense pulsed light targeting pigment in the hair follicle, is the home-IPL category whose evidence is set out on [[laser-hair-removal]]: a Cochrane review found "a short-term effect of approximately 50% hair reduction with alexandrite and diode lasers up to six months," "little evidence" of an effect for intense pulsed light specifically, and that "long-term hair removal was not documented with any treatment," and home units run at lower energy than the in-office machines that review studied.[^cochrane] It cannot exceed what that category has shown. Like all IPL, it is designed for light skin with dark hair, where the light can target follicle pigment without the surrounding skin absorbing too much, and it is a repeated-use cosmetic device, not a one-time permanent treatment.

Philips states the device "is clinically proven to achieve up to 5 years of smooth-feeling skin," carried with an asterisk whose qualifier is not shown on the accessed page.[^philips] This is the brand's own clinical testing, not an independent trial, and the outcome described is "smooth-feeling skin," a sensory measure, not verified permanent hair removal; it is quarantined here rather than taken as established.

> **Bottom line.** An at-home IPL device for light skin with dark hair, with the same weak evidence as every home IPL unit: no independent trial of this product, and the category shows no documented long-term hair removal (see [[laser-hair-removal]]). Philips' "up to 5 years of smooth-feeling skin" is an asterisked brand claim, not independent proof.[^philips]

## What It Is

Philips describes the Lumea 9000 Series as its IPL device "with SenseIQ," offering "4 dedicated attachments for face, body, armpits and bikini," and markets the SenseIQ sensor as adapting the light intensity to the skin.[^philips] As with the Braun sibling, the skin-tone sensor and multiple attachments are convenience and safety features; they do not change the underlying IPL evidence. Philips does not display a price on its accessed US product page; the device is sold through Philips and major retailers at prices that vary.
