CeraVe SA Lotion for Rough & Bumpy Skin is a fragrance-free body lotion sold by [[cerave|CeraVe]] that pairs salicylic acid (a BHA) with ammonium lactate (an AHA, the salt form of lactic acid), hyaluronic acid, and three ceramides, listed at $19.99 on CeraVe's own site (8 oz, per a corroborating Amazon listing).[^pdp]

> A two-acid exfoliating lotion, salicylic acid and ammonium lactate, neither listed at a stated percentage; salicylic acid appears only on the ordinary declared-ingredient list, not in a Drug Facts Active Ingredient panel, on the manufacturer's page.[^pdp] The acid-by-acid evidence lives on [[keratosis-pilaris]] and [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]]; CeraVe's own marketing describes the lotion as "developed with dermatologists," a brand claim rather than a study of this product (see [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^pdp]

## Summary

CeraVe markets this lotion for "Rough & Bumpy Skin," describing a "lightweight, non-greasy formula containing salicylic acid, lactic acid and hyaluronic acid" that is "designed to improve your skin's texture by exfoliating and hydrating it, while using 3 essential ceramides that work together to lock in skins moisture and help restore your skin's protective barrier."[^pdp] Its declared ingredient list names the acids Salicylic Acid and Ammonium Lactate, the ammonium salt form of lactic acid; ammonium lactate is the same salt form graded on [[keratosis-pilaris]], where AmLactin is a widely sold over-the-counter example. Neither acid carries a stated percentage on CeraVe's own page, and salicylic acid is listed only among the ordinary declared ingredients rather than in a separate Drug Facts Active Ingredient panel, a point of contrast with an OTC acne product where salicylic acid is regulated as a monograph active.[^pdp]

That acid-by-acid evidence, and how AHAs and BHAs compare in depth and strength, is set out on [[keratosis-pilaris]] and [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]] rather than restated here. What matters for this specific lotion is covered under [The Evidence](#the-evidence): no independent trial isolates this formula, and CeraVe's own product description is marketing copy rather than a published study.[^pdp]

The base of the formula is a lightweight lotion built on mineral oil, glyceryl stearate, and cetearyl/cetyl alcohol, with three ceramides (NP, AP, EOP) delivered through CeraVe's patented MVE technology, plus hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid and cholecalciferol (vitamin D).[^pdp] It is fragrance-free and priced at $19.99 on CeraVe's own site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-12).[^pdp]

## The Evidence

Ammonium lactate (lactic acid) and salicylic acid both have real controlled evidence specifically for keratosis pilaris, tested head to head against each other in a split-side trial where 10% lactic acid outperformed 5% salicylic acid; the full case for each acid is set out on [[keratosis-pilaris]] and [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]]. No independently published trial of this exact lotion, or of this specific acid combination, was located. The product-specific facts available are its declared composition, salicylic acid and ammonium lactate, neither at a disclosed percentage, alongside hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid and ceramides NP, AP, and EOP, and its own marketing claim to be "developed with dermatologists" (see [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)).[^pdp] CeraVe's page discloses no Drug Facts Active Ingredient panel for salicylic acid, meaning it is formulated as a cosmetic ingredient rather than as a regulated OTC monograph active at a stated concentration.[^pdp]

> **Bottom line.** The AHA and BHA here belong to acid classes with real, if modest, keratosis-pilaris evidence, tested head to head in the trial covered on [[keratosis-pilaris]]; but no independent trial isolates this exact lotion, neither acid is disclosed at a stated concentration, and salicylic acid is not declared in a Drug Facts panel on the manufacturer's page.[^pdp]

## What's In It

The full declared ingredient list (INCI), matching between CeraVe's own product page and a US retail listing checked for corroboration.[^pdp]

Notable/functional ingredients:

- [[salicylic-acid|Salicylic Acid]], a BHA; CeraVe's own callout describes it as "promotes the skin's natural exfoliation process, which helps skin feel softer and smoother"[^pdp]
- Ammonium Lactate, the ammonium salt of [[lactic-acid|lactic acid]] (an AHA); CeraVe's own callout, labeled "Lactic Acid," describes it as "a gentle alpha-hydroxy acid that helps promote the skin's natural exfoliation process"[^pdp]
- [[ceramides|Ceramide NP, Ceramide AP, and Ceramide EOP]], CeraVe's "3 essential ceramides"; the brand's own callout describes them as helping "restore your skin's natural barrier, helping to keep moisture in and harmful elements out," delivered via CeraVe's patented MVE (MultiVesicular Emulsion) technology[^pdp]
- Hydrolyzed [[hyaluronic-acid|Hyaluronic Acid]]; CeraVe's own callout describes it as "essential for maintaining skin's moisture balance," helping "give skin a smooth, plump texture"[^pdp]
- Cholecalciferol (vitamin D)

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, glycerin, paraffinum liquidum (mineral oil), triethanolamine (pH adjuster), glyceryl stearate and PEG-100 stearate (emulsifiers), cetearyl alcohol and cetyl alcohol (thickeners/emollients), zea mays (corn) oil, carbomer (thickener), behentrimonium methosulfate, dimethicone, methylparaben and propylparaben (preservatives), sodium lauroyl lactylate, cholesterol, disodium EDTA (chelator), phytosphingosine, and xanthan gum (thickener).[^pdp]

Not on the label: no fragrance is declared, and the declared ingredient list carries no niacinamide, even though a US retail listing's marketing copy separately names niacinamide as a key ingredient.
