The Ordinary Alpha Arbutin 2% + HA is a water-based leave-on serum that lists alpha-arbutin second on the ingredient declaration at a labeled 2%, paired with hyaluronic acid (as hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate), sold in 30 ml and 60 ml sizes.[^1]

> Alpha-arbutin's case for treating pigmentation is thin: one independent, evaluator-blinded trial tested it at 5% (with 2% kojic acid) against a hydroquinone-based triple combination cream and came out statistically indistinguishable on objective melasma measures, not better; a separate case series tested 7% but combined it with laser, making arbutin's own contribution impossible to isolate. That full case is set out on [[arbutin]]. This product is labeled 2%, below both of those tested strengths, so its grades here rest on category evidence generated at a higher concentration, not a trial of this product.[^1]

## Summary

Alpha Arbutin 2% + HA is The Ordinary's plain arbutin serum: water, then [[arbutin|alpha-arbutin]] at a manufacturer-declared 2%, then a set of stabilizers and thickeners and a small, undisclosed amount of hyaluronic acid.[^1] Arbutin's pigment-fading case, real but modest and resting on a single small independent trial plus a laser-combination case series, both run at 5% to 7%, is set out in full on [[arbutin]]. Nothing in the published record tests arbutin at 2%, the strength printed on this bottle.[^1]

That gap matters most for melasma and disordered facial pigmentation, where the trial evidence exists but sits above this product's labeled strength ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)). The hyaluronic acid is present but its exact level is not disclosed; it sits fifth on the ingredient list, well below arbutin, propanediol, and a thickening polymer, so its hydration contribution cannot be sized from the formula alone. The manufacturer's "high-strength" and "minimize the appearance of dark spots" language is weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims). The manufacturer lists it at $11.50 USD;[^1] retailer Cult Beauty lists the 30 ml size at 12.08 EUR (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^2]

## The Evidence

Alpha-arbutin's evidence, its one independent trial against an active hydroquinone-based comparator and a laser-combination case series where its own contribution cannot be isolated, is set out in full on [[arbutin]]. Nothing specific to this product was found: no published trial tests The Ordinary's 2% arbutin plus hyaluronic acid formula, so the grades above rest on the general ingredient case, all of it generated at 5% to 7% arbutin.[^1]

The melasma trial behind arbutin's case combined 5% alpha-arbutin with 2% kojic acid and matched, without beating, a hydroquinone-based triple combination cream on objective pigment measures over 12 weeks; a separate case series used 7% alpha-arbutin alongside ten sessions of Q-switched Nd:YAG laser, so the laser and not the arbutin may account for most of its reported clearance (full numbers on [[arbutin]]). At a labeled 2%, this serum sits below both tested concentrations, with no published data on whether 2% delivers a smaller, equal, or negligible effect relative to 5% to 7%.[^1]

The hyaluronic acid here is hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate, fifth on the ingredient declaration with no percentage disclosed.[^1] Topical hyaluronic acid is a well-evidenced humectant, raising hydration and elasticity readings above placebo across the molecular weights tested; see [[hyaluronic-acid]]. How much of that applies to this specific, undisclosed concentration cannot be determined from the label.

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its pigment grade rests on arbutin's modest, single-trial case at 5% to 7%; this serum is labeled 2%, below that tested range, with no data on the effect at this strength. Its hyaluronic acid content is undisclosed.[^1]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer, confirmed against an independent retailer listing.[^1][^2] The actives with a functional role are linked; the rest is the solvent, stabilizer, and preservative system.

Actives:

- [[arbutin|alpha-arbutin]], labeled 2%, second on the INCI after water
- hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate, a form of [[hyaluronic-acid]], fifth on the list with no declared percentage
- ergothioneine, an antioxidant amino acid, listed after the hyaluronic acid

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, propanediol (solvent), polyacrylate crosspolymer-6 (thickener), polysorbate 20 (surfactant), citric acid and sodium citrate (pH buffer), sodium hydroxide (pH adjuster), sodium metabisulfite (antioxidant/preservative), trisodium ethylenediamine disuccinate (chelator), ethoxydiglycol (solvent), and phenoxyethanol and chlorphenesin (preservatives).[^1]

The declared list is 15 ingredients total and carries no added fragrance.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"A high-strength serum to minimize the appearance of dark spots and brighten."** "High-strength" is a manufacturer characterization of the 2% label claim; the independent trial evidence behind arbutin's pigment case used 5% to 7%, not 2% (on [[arbutin]]).[^1]
- **"Designed to target uneven skin tone and dryness."** A cosmetic positioning claim. Arbutin's better-evidenced use is melasma and disordered pigmentation specifically, not generic unevenness or dryness; the formula's hydration ingredient (hyaluronic acid) carries no disclosed concentration.[^1]
- **"...suitable for all skin types."** A manufacturer label claim, not an independently published outcome.[^1]
