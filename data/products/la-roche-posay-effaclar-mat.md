La Roche-Posay Effaclar Mat is an oil-free daily face moisturizer for oily skin, built on glycerin and an oil-absorbing base of silica and perlite, with salicylic acid and its derivative capryloyl salicylic acid (LHA) for a matte finish and the look of refined pores.[^1][^2]

> A light, oil-free moisturizer for oily and shine-prone skin. Its two health-relevant parts are ordinary ones: it hydrates like other light moisturizers ([[moisturizing]] covers why that helps), and it carries salicylic acid, a legitimate but second-tier acne active. Its selling point, the matte finish, is a real cosmetic effect from absorbent silica and perlite powders soaking up surface oil. The brand's stronger claim, that it regulates sebum "superior to Zinc," rests on the manufacturer's own consumer tests rather than a controlled trial, so it is [held apart below](#common-marketing-claims). It is premium-priced for a 40 ml oily-skin moisturizer.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Effaclar Mat is a lightweight, oil-free moisturizer made for oily and shine-prone skin. Two of its jobs are health-oriented and ordinary. The first is basic hydration: it delivers water and glycerin in a base with no facial oils, and keeping skin moisturized is a well-evidenced routine, set out on [[moisturizing]]. The second is a mild treatment role from [[salicylic-acid|salicylic acid]] and its gentler relative capryloyl salicylic acid (LHA), an oil-soluble acid that penetrates the pore; salicylic acid is a genuine, guideline-recognized acne active, though a [second-tier one that the American Academy of Dermatology ranks below benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids](#the-evidence). Both acids sit low on the ingredient list at unstated, likely low levels, so the treatment effect here is minor next to a dedicated acne product.

The distinctive part, the reason to reach for this over a plainer oil-free lotion, is cosmetic: the matte finish. That comes from [[silica]] and [[perlite]], absorbent mineral powders that soak up oil on the skin surface, plus a light polymer film. La Roche-Posay markets it as an "oil-control moisturizer" that combats shine and reduces pore size,[^2] and reports a four-week consumer test in which "96% say skin is immediately mattified."[^1] That is a real, immediate anti-shine effect, but it is oil absorption at the surface and a consumer-perception measurement, not proof the skin makes less oil.

On the question of whether it does more than mattify, the brand's international pages go further and market a "Sebulyse" technology whose "sebo-regulating efficacy is clinically proven to be superior to Zinc."[^2] No published trial of this product supports a reduction in oil-gland output, so that claim is [quarantined below](#common-marketing-claims). For outside opinion, ELLE included it in a 2024 roundup of the best non-comedogenic moisturizers for acne-prone skin, describing it as helping "replenish moisture without leaving the skin looking shiny."[^3] Unlike La Roche-Posay's fragrance-free [[la-roche-posay-toleriane|Toleriane]] cream, this formula contains fragrance and denatured alcohol, a [composition point](#whats-in-it) that matters for sensitive or reactive skin. It is expensive for its category: the 40 ml tube retails at about $40 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-07-28), several times the per-ounce price of a basic drugstore moisturizer.

## The Evidence

Two general bodies of evidence sit behind this product, and both live on other pages. Keeping skin moisturized is one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines, strongest for dry and barrier-impaired skin; that case is on [[moisturizing]]. And [[salicylic-acid|salicylic acid]] is an oil-soluble acid that penetrates the sebum-filled pore and is a legitimate, if second-tier, acne active: the 2024 American Academy of Dermatology guideline gives it a conditional recommendation and ranks [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and topical [[retinoids]] above it, and the one head-to-head trial Cochrane could pool put it level with [[tretinoin]]. Capryloyl salicylic acid (LHA) is a lipophilic salicylic acid derivative in the same family. The full graded verdicts, with the trial statistics, are on those pages.

What is specific to this product is narrower. La Roche-Posay reports a four-week consumer test of Effaclar Mat: "96% say skin is immediately mattified," "96% say skin is less oily," and "94% say skin texture is refined."[^1] These are self-reported perceptions from a consumer panel rather than instrument measurements of sebum or a controlled comparison against another moisturizer.

> **Bottom line.** The product-specific data is a four-week consumer-perception panel, not a controlled trial. It shows people felt their skin looked more matte and less oily; it does not measure how much oil the skin produced or compare the product against a plainer moisturizer.

The mattifying itself has a plain mechanical basis rather than a biological one. [[silica]] and [[perlite]] are inert, porous mineral powders whose surfaces absorb oil and reduce shine on contact, which is why they appear in primers, setting powders, and blotting products. That absorbs oil already on the skin. It is distinct from a claim that the skin's oil glands make less sebum, which would require a controlled measurement this product does not have.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer and corroborated against the US retail listing.[^2][^1] The ingredients with a functional role are linked or named; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

Functional ingredients:

- [[glycerin]], a humectant that draws in water
- [[silica]] and perlite, absorbent mineral powders that soak up surface oil for a matte finish
- dimethicone and dimethicone/vinyl dimethicone crosspolymer, silicones that give slip and a soft-focus finish
- methyl methacrylate crosspolymer, a light polymer powder that adds to the matte, blurred look
- capryloyl salicylic acid (LHA) and [[salicylic-acid|salicylic acid]], oil-soluble exfoliating acids

Base, texture, and preservative system: water (La Roche-Posay thermal spring water), isocetyl stearate and isohexadecane (emollients), alcohol denat. (a solvent and quick-dry agent), acrylamide/sodium acryloyldimethyltaurate copolymer and acrylates/C10-30 alkyl acrylate crosspolymer (texturizers), butylene glycol and pentylene glycol (humectant solvents), PEG-100 stearate, cocamide MEA, sarcosine, glyceryl stearate and polysorbate 80 (emulsifiers and surfactants), triethanolamine (pH adjuster), tetrasodium EDTA (chelator), and parfum/fragrance.[^2]

Two composition facts matter for oily and sensitive skin. Unlike La Roche-Posay's fragrance-free [[la-roche-posay-toleriane|Toleriane]] and [[cerave-moisturizing-cream|CeraVe Moisturizing Cream]], this formula contains added fragrance (parfum) and denatured alcohol. The base has no petrolatum or facial oils, consistent with its oil-free, non-comedogenic positioning.[^2][^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Sebulyse" and "sebo-regulating efficacy... clinically proven to be superior to Zinc."** La Roche-Posay's international pages present Sebulyse as an active that "helps to mattify skin and tighten pores" and whose sebo-regulating efficacy is "superior to Zinc, the standard active ingredient used to address oily skin concerns."[^2] No published trial of this product demonstrates a reduction in the skin's oil-gland output; the measured effect that is documented is surface-oil absorption and consumer-reported mattifying.[^1]
- **"96% say skin is less oily" and other four-week test figures.** These come from a manufacturer consumer-perception panel, self-reported and without a control group.[^1]
- **"Dermatologist tested," "non-comedogenic," "allergy tested."** Manufacturer label and safety claims.[^1]
