La Roche-Posay's Effaclar Mat is a daily mattifying moisturizer for oily skin, built on absorbent particles plus a small, low-list amount of a salicylic-acid-family exfoliant, priced at about $31 for 40 ml as listed at Escentual.[^escentual]

> Effaclar Mat's mattifying job is cosmetic: silica and perlite absorb surface oil the way any oil-absorbing powder does, a mechanism covered generally on [[silica]]. La Roche-Posay brands this action "Sebulyse" and calls it "clinically proven to mattify skin and tighten pores," but no independently published, controlled trial of this exact product is available, so that claim rests on the brand's own unpublished testing.[^escentual] The formula also carries capryloyl salicylic acid and salicylic acid near the bottom of the ingredient list, too small a dose and too minor a position to expect the acne-treating effect covered on [[salicylic-acid]].

## Summary

La Roche-Posay describes Effaclar Mat as a moisturizer meant to "regulate sebum production right at the source," an "anti-shine formula for oily skin types" that provides "long-lasting hydration with added Sebulyse, a sebo-regulating active ingredient that is clinically proven to mattify skin and tighten pores."[^escentual] It calls the formula "oil-free" and says it "acts as a blotter to mop up excess oil and keep skin clear and soft," with daily use meant to leave "enlarged pores are tightened" and "sebum production slows down."[^escentual] Read literally, "Sebulyse" is not a single named ingredient on the INCI list but La Roche-Posay's marketing name for the formula's combined mattifying action; the ingredients that physically do the mattifying are the absorbent minerals silica and perlite, the same category of ingredient covered on [[silica]], which absorb oil already on the skin's surface rather than changing how much the skin glands produce ([[oily-skin]] covers that distinction generally).

The formula leads with water, glycerin, and dimethicone (a silicone that gives the light, blurring finish typical of mattifying moisturizers), followed by isocetyl stearate and alcohol denat, then the absorbent silica. Perlite, a second absorbent mineral, sits further down the list, alongside a minor amount of capryloyl salicylic acid and salicylic acid, a BHA pairing too small and too low on the list to be read as an acne treatment rather than a minor exfoliating addition (see [[salicylic-acid]] for what a treatment-strength dose looks like). The rest of the list is texture and preservative chemistry, and the formula carries added fragrance ([full list below](#whats-in-it)).

It is listed at $31.00 for 40 ml ($77.50 per 100 ml) at the retailer Escentual ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-21).[^escentual]

## The Evidence

No independent controlled trial of Effaclar Mat is publicly available; the "clinically proven" language in its marketing refers to testing the brand has not published for outside review.[^escentual] Its mattifying mechanism rests on absorbent mineral particles, silica and perlite, which soak up surface oil and scatter light for a matte look, a cosmetic effect whose general evidence and limits are set out on [[silica]]. That mechanism absorbs oil already on the skin rather than reducing how much the sebaceous glands produce, a distinction covered for oily skin generally on [[oily-skin]]. The capryloyl salicylic acid and salicylic acid appear near the end of the list, after preservatives and chelating agents, which signals a low concentration; treat this as a minor exfoliating addition rather than an acne treatment, the standard for which is covered on [[salicylic-acid]].
