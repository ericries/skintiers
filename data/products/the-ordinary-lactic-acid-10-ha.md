The Ordinary Lactic Acid 10% + HA is a water-based, leave-on exfoliating serum built around a labeled 10% lactic acid, with a manufacturer-stated pH of 3.60 to 3.80, sold for about $9.20 for a 30 ml bottle.[^1][^2]

> Lactic acid is an alpha-hydroxy acid (AHA) tested alongside glycolic acid in the controlled trial behind [[glycolic-acid|glycolic acid's]] modest, mostly cosmetic evidence for smoothing texture and softening photodamage, and it is generally regarded as milder and more hydrating than glycolic acid because its larger molecule penetrates skin more slowly. This serum's labeled 10% sits above the 8% cream used in that trial, at a manufacturer-stated pH (3.60-3.80) low enough to be exfoliation-active, in a widely available bottle priced at about $9.20 for 30 ml. The formula also carries hyaluronic acid (as sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer) for hydration, and the brand's own directions say it should not be used "on sensitive, peeling, or compromised skin."[^1]

## Summary

The Ordinary Lactic Acid 10% + HA is a daily leave-on exfoliating serum built around lactic acid, an alpha-hydroxy acid (AHA), at a labeled 10%.[^1] Lactic acid has modest, mostly appearance-level evidence for smoothing skin texture and softening the signs of photodamage, evidence it shares with glycolic acid: the controlled trial behind that case tested both an 8% glycolic acid cream and an 8% lactic acid cream side by side, and judged both "modestly useful" against photodamage; that trial and the fuller AHA case, by use, are set out on [[glycolic-acid]]. Lactic acid is also the larger of the two molecules, and is generally described as milder and more hydrating than glycolic acid because it penetrates skin more slowly.

What distinguishes this serum from a plainer, cheaper acid product is less the concentration itself and more the combination: a 10% acid held at a manufacturer-stated pH of 3.60 to 3.80, low enough to stay exfoliation-active, paired with [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]] in the form of sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer for hydration, plus a Tasmanian pepperberry extract that The Ordinary describes as "a known anti-irritant."[^1] It is sold cheaply relative to other 10% acid products: The Ordinary's own page lists it at $9.20 USD for 30 ml, a price corroborated on Target's Ulta Beauty storefront listing ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^1][^2]

The other side of that acid strength is the documented downside shared by AHAs: increased skin sensitivity to sunlight while in use (see [[glycolic-acid]]). The Ordinary's own directions carry a "Sunburn Alert," say the product should not be used "on sensitive, peeling, or compromised skin," and advise a patch test before use.[^1] Its marketing describes the product as suited to dry skin and targeting textural irregularities, dullness, and uneven tone; those claims are separated out under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## The Evidence

Lactic acid is an AHA exfoliant with modest, largely cosmetic (appearance-level) evidence for smoothing texture and softening the signs of photodamage; the full case for AHAs as a class, including the higher-strength peel evidence and the documented UV-sensitivity downside, is set out on [[glycolic-acid]], which grades lactic acid alongside glycolic acid because the vehicle-controlled trial behind that grade tested both. No study of this specific serum formula has been published; the product-specific question is how its labeled concentration and pH sit against that ingredient-level evidence. That trial used an 8% lactic acid cream. This serum is labeled 10% lactic acid,[^1] above that studied strength, which puts the formula at least as concentrated as the trial condition that was judged "modestly useful" for photodamage. The Ordinary states the finished product's pH as 3.60 to 3.80 on its specifications,[^1] a range Target's product listing corroborates, describing the pH as "approximately 3.8."[^2] A pH in that range keeps the acid largely in its active, un-ionized (free-acid) form, the state that drives exfoliation (the mechanism is covered on [[glycolic-acid]]); neither The Ordinary nor the Target listing publishes a separate free-acid-value figure.[^1][^2]

> **Bottom line.** This serum's labeled 10% and stated pH of 3.60-3.80 place it at or above the concentration used in the controlled trial behind the AHA texture/photodamage evidence (an 8% lactic acid cream, tested alongside 8% glycolic acid), though no trial has tested this specific formula. The evidence for what lactic and glycolic acid do, and do not do, is set out on [[glycolic-acid]].[^1][^2]

## What's In It

The full declared ingredient list (INCI), matching between the manufacturer's own page and an authorized retailer's listing:[^1][^2]

Active and supporting ingredients:

- Lactic Acid, listed second on the INCI (after water), labeled 10%
- Sodium Hyaluronate Crosspolymer, a form of [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]], which The Ordinary describes as a hydrating component[^1]
- Tasmannia Lanceolata Fruit/Leaf Extract (Tasmanian pepperberry), which The Ordinary describes as "a known anti-irritant"[^1]

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, glycerin and propanediol (humectants), pentylene glycol, sodium hydroxide (pH adjuster), acacia senegal gum and xanthan gum (thickeners), isoceteth-20 (a surfactant), trisodium ethylenediamine disuccinate (a chelator), and ethylhexylglycerin, 1,2-hexanediol, and caprylyl glycol (preservatives).[^1][^2]
