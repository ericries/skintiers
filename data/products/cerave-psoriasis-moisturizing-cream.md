CeraVe Psoriasis Moisturizing Cream is an over-the-counter salicylic acid 2% cream in CeraVe's ceramide moisturizer base, sold to relieve the scaling, flaking, and itch of psoriasis.[^dailymed]

> This is two useful things bundled together, and neither is a treatment for psoriasis itself. The salicylic acid is a keratolytic that lifts the thick scale off plaques, and the base is a solid ceramide-and-urea moisturizer that calms the dryness and itch. Both are genuine supportive care, and both are worth having, but psoriasis is an immune-driven disease and the drugs that actually clear plaques (topical steroids, vitamin D analogues, and newer non-steroidals like [[zoryve-roflumilast-cream|roflumilast]]) are ranked on [[psoriasis]]. Judge this as a scale-softening moisturizer to use alongside real treatment, not instead of it.

## Summary

This is regulated as an over-the-counter drug. The label lists the active ingredient as salicylic acid 2% with the purpose "Psoriasis," and the uses as "for the relief of skin itching, scaling, redness, flaking and irritation associated with psoriasis" and to "help prevent the recurrence of the symptoms of psoriasis."[^dailymed] Salicylic acid is a keratolytic: it works on the surface scale, softening and shedding the built-up dead skin of a plaque; its evidence and mechanism are on [[salicylic-acid]].

The rest is a CeraVe moisturizer. Notable inactive ingredients from the label include urea and glycerin (humectants), shea butter and dimethicone (emollients), niacinamide, and the brand's ceramide blend (ceramides 1, 3, and 6-II with phytosphingosine and cholesterol).[^dailymed] The queue name for this product called out "lactic acid," but the label lists C12-13 alkyl lactate, an emollient ester, and urea rather than free lactic acid, so the humectant and mild keratolytic support here comes from urea and salicylic acid, not an AHA.

## The Evidence

Two jobs, graded above; the psoriasis disease evidence is on [[psoriasis]] and is not repeated here.

**Removing scale (the labeled keratolytic use).** Salicylic acid is an established over-the-counter treatment for the scaling of psoriasis: it loosens the corneocyte bonds in the plaque so scale sheds, which improves appearance and comfort and can help other topicals penetrate. This is real but limited to the scale; it does not switch off the underlying inflammation that keeps making plaques, which is why the label frames it as relief of "itching, scaling, redness, flaking" rather than clearance of the disease.[^dailymed]

**Supporting the barrier (the base).** Regular emollient use is standard adjunctive care in psoriasis because the skin is dry and barrier-impaired, and a ceramide, urea, glycerin, and niacinamide moisturizer is a reasonable vehicle for that. It reduces dryness and itch and makes the skin more comfortable, but like the salicylic acid it is supportive rather than disease-modifying.

> **Bottom line.** A sensible, inexpensive scale-softening moisturizer for psoriasis-prone skin, combining a keratolytic (salicylic acid 2%) with a good barrier base.[^dailymed] Use it to manage scale and dryness, and see [[psoriasis]] for the treatments that actually reduce the plaques.

## What's In It

As a drug the label separates the active from the rest. Active ingredient: salicylic acid 2% (purpose: psoriasis).[^dailymed] Inactive ingredients, verbatim from the start of the label list: "water, urea, glyceryl stearate SE, cetearyl alcohol, butyrospermum parkii (shea) butter, glycerin, cetyl alcohol, niacinamide, laureth-9, limnanthes alba (meadowfoam) seed oil, PEG-100 stearate, gossypium herbaceum (cotton) seed oil, behentrimonium methosulfate, 1,2-hexanediol, C12-13 alkyl lactate, dimethicone, tasmannia lanceolata fruit/leaf extract," followed by the brand's ceramide blend (ceramides 1, 3, and 6-II), phytosphingosine, and cholesterol.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Relieves the itching, scaling, and flaking of psoriasis."** Accurate and label-permitted for a 2% salicylic acid product; it relieves the scale and dryness, which is what the OTC monograph supports.[^dailymed]
- **"Helps prevent the recurrence of symptoms."** This is standard OTC salicylic-acid psoriasis wording on the label, referring to ongoing scale control with continued use, not to preventing the disease or its flares.[^dailymed]
