Cetaphil Sheer Mineral Sunscreen Stick SPF 50 is a stick-format, 100% mineral Broad Spectrum SPF 50 sunscreen from [[cetaphil]], sold in a 0.5 fl oz travel size and marketed for sensitive skin.[^1][^2]

> A stick sunscreen built on titanium dioxide 6% and zinc oxide 4.7%, an all-mineral filter set at a lower combined filter load than Cetaphil's own market peers such as [[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50|CeraVe's mineral SPF 50 face sunscreen]] (titanium dioxide 9%/zinc oxide 7%).[^1][^3] Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; no independent trial of this specific stick was found, so its grades rest on that category evidence plus the filter chemistry covered on [[zinc-oxide]] and [[titanium-dioxide]].[^1]

## Summary

Sheer Mineral Sunscreen Stick SPF 50 is Cetaphil's stick-format facial sunscreen, listed on the brand's own site with active ingredients titanium dioxide 6% and zinc oxide 4.7%, a 100% mineral filter combination.[^1] Cetaphil markets it as "designed for sensitive skin," water resistant for up to 80 minutes, and holding the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance.[^1]

Broad-spectrum UV protection is the best-evidenced way to slow photoaging and reduce sun-related skin-cancer risk, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Both actives here are physically opaque mineral filters rather than organic (chemical) filters like avobenzone; each has its own evidence and comparator page, linked in [What's In It](#whats-in-it). Compared with [[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50|CeraVe's own mineral SPF 50 face sunscreen]], which lists titanium dioxide 9% and zinc oxide 7% on its FDA label, this stick's filter percentages are lower despite carrying the same SPF 50 rating; both are still FDA-rated Broad Spectrum SPF 50 products, so the printed filter percentages are not directly comparable to a single "how much protection" number.[^3]

A Target listing for the same product independently confirms titanium dioxide 6% among its active ingredients and lists a $6.99 price for the 0.5 fl oz travel size (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^2] No independent, product-specific study of this exact stick was located; the grades above rest on the filter composition and category-level UV-filter evidence, not a trial of this SKU.

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific product was located; the product-specific facts available are its filter composition and its brand-listed Broad Spectrum SPF 50 rating.

Cetaphil's own product page lists the active ingredients as titanium dioxide 6% and zinc oxide 4.7%.[^1] Both are mineral (inorganic) filters rather than organic filters such as avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, or octocrylene. [[zinc-oxide]] is photostable with genuine long-wave UVA reach; [[titanium-dioxide]] is the stronger UVB absorber of the two and contributes shorter-wave UVA coverage; neither needs a photostabilizer the way avobenzone does, a comparison detailed on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. A Target retailer listing for the same item independently lists titanium dioxide 6% as an active, corroborating the brand's own figure, though the Target listing does not itself repeat the zinc oxide percentage.[^2]

Set against [[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50|CeraVe's mineral SPF 50 face sunscreen]], whose FDA drug label lists titanium dioxide 9% and zinc oxide 7%, this stick's brand-listed filter percentages are meaningfully lower while carrying the same SPF 50 rating.[^3] Differences in base and vehicle (stick versus lotion) and testing methodology can account for two formulas reaching the same tested SPF at different filter loads, so this is a composition note, not a claim that one product outperforms the other in practice.

> **Bottom line.** The brand-listed Broad Spectrum SPF 50 rating reflects a 100% mineral filter set, titanium dioxide 6% and zinc oxide 4.7%, lower filter percentages than at least one same-SPF mineral competitor.[^1][^3] No trial isolates this stick; its grades rest on category-level UV-filter evidence and the two filters' known chemistry.
