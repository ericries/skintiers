Aveeno Daily Moisturizing Lotion is Kenvue's fragrance-free everyday lotion for normal to dry skin, a ten-ingredient formula built around Avena Sativa (Oat) Kernel Flour, the oat ingredient the brand markets as colloidal oatmeal, alongside glycerin and dimethicone.[^1][^2]

> A cheap, fragrance-free everyday lotion for normal to dry skin, built on Aveeno's signature oat ingredient rather than the ceramides or hyaluronic acid in a peer lotion like [[cerave-daily-moisturizing-lotion|CeraVe's]]. Colloidal oatmeal carries a real FDA skin-protectant monograph and randomized-trial support for itch relief and barrier support in dry, eczema-prone skin, and glycerin is a well-evidenced humectant, on top of the general moisturizing case set out on [[moisturizing]]. No trial has tested this exact lotion, and its dimethicone is declared as a plain cosmetic ingredient here, with no Drug Facts panel or disclosed percentage.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Aveeno Daily Moisturizing Lotion is a fragrance-free lotion marketed for daily use on normal to dry skin, for both face and body. Its formula splits into two functional groups on the manufacturer's own ingredient breakdown: a "moisturize" group (water, glycerin, petrolatum, isopropyl palmitate, Avena Sativa (Oat) Kernel Flour, and dimethicone, described as "~92% of the formula") and a "protect the product" group (distearyldimonium chloride, cetyl alcohol, benzyl alcohol, and sodium chloride, "~8% of the formula").[^1] The oat ingredient, Avena Sativa (Oat) Kernel Flour, is the oat kernel flour Aveeno markets as colloidal oatmeal, which carries an FDA skin-protectant monograph and randomized-trial evidence for itch relief and barrier support, set out on [[colloidal-oatmeal]]. Glycerin is a well-evidenced humectant with its own vehicle-controlled hydration data, set out on [[glycerin]]. Dimethicone also appears in the list, but only as a plain cosmetic emollient: neither the manufacturer's page nor a retailer listing shows a Drug Facts panel, an "Active Ingredient" section, or a stated percentage for it, unlike colloidal oatmeal and glycerin, which the FDA monograph recognizes as active only at disclosed concentrations.[^1][^2]

Aveeno's product page markets the lotion as "Clinically proven to moisturize skin for 48 hours," attributed on the page to "Assessment of moisturization after 48 hours in a clinical study" (Kenvue, 2024), and describes it as "allergy tested, non-comedogenic & free of fragrances, parabens & dyes."[^1] Those are the manufacturer's own claims, discussed further in [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

No published trial tests this exact lotion. The oat and glycerin case rests on the general evidence for those ingredients, detailed in [The Evidence](#the-evidence) and on [[colloidal-oatmeal]] and [[glycerin]]. A confirmed size is listed in [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy); neither the manufacturer's page nor the retailer listing fetched for this profile displayed a price.

## The Evidence

Keeping dry or barrier-impaired skin moisturized reduces flares and supports the skin barrier, one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines; that general case, including the roles of occlusives and humectants, is set out on [[moisturizing]]. No published trial tests this exact lotion. Its case rests on the general evidence for its two notable actives, applied to this formulation.

For the oat ingredient, colloidal oatmeal (the oat kernel flour declared here as Avena Sativa (Oat) Kernel Flour) is one of the few cosmetic ingredients with a formal FDA skin-protectant monograph, and randomized trials of a colloidal oatmeal cream found it non-inferior to a prescription barrier cream and ahead of a standard moisturizer on eczema severity and barrier measures; those trials, their manufacturer funding, and their limits are set out on [[colloidal-oatmeal]]. This product does not disclose what proportion of colloidal oatmeal it contains, and carries no Drug Facts panel naming it, or any other ingredient, as an active.

For the glycerin, a placebo-controlled trial found a glycerol cream significantly raised stratum corneum hydration and restored epidermal barrier function over its glycerol-free vehicle, though the same trial found no significant difference from vehicle on clinical eczema-severity scores; that trial and a systematic review rating glycerin's evidence base are set out on [[glycerin]]. This product's ingredient list does not specify glycerin's concentration.

> **Bottom line.** No study has isolated this lotion's own effect. Its case rests on general evidence for colloidal oatmeal and glycerin individually, in a formula that does not disclose either ingredient's concentration and carries no Drug Facts panel.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), as declared on a retailer listing and cross-matching the manufacturer's own ingredient-story text.[^1][^2] There is no Drug Facts or "Active Ingredient" panel on either page, and no ingredient percentage is disclosed anywhere in the fetched pages.

Notable functional ingredients:

- Avena Sativa (Oat) Kernel Flour, the oat kernel flour Aveeno markets as colloidal oatmeal, called a "Hero Ingredient" on the manufacturer's page; see [[colloidal-oatmeal]]
- [[glycerin]], a humectant
- [[dimethicone]], an occlusive emollient; declared without a Drug Facts panel or stated percentage, a cosmetic ingredient here rather than an OTC skin-protectant active
- [[petrolatum]], an occlusive
- isopropyl palmitate, an emollient

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, distearyldimonium chloride (conditioning/emulsifying agent), cetyl alcohol (emollient/emulsifier), benzyl alcohol (preservative), and sodium chloride (thickener).[^2]

The formula contains no [[ceramides]] and no [[hyaluronic-acid]] (unlike [[cerave-daily-moisturizing-lotion|CeraVe's Daily Moisturizing Lotion]]), and no added fragrance.[^1][^2]
