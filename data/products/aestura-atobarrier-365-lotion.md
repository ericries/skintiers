Aestura Atobarrier 365 Lotion is a lighter, pump-dispensed counterpart to [[aestura-atobarrier-365-cream|Aestura Atobarrier 365 Cream]], a Korean ceramide moisturizer sold in 90 ml and 150 ml sizes for dry and sensitive skin.[^aestura]

> A lighter-textured version of the same ceramide-barrier idea as the Atobarrier 365 Cream: ceramide moisturizers reliably move barrier measurements like water loss, but the closest controlled trial of the category found no advantage over a plain base on eczema severity itself, the general case set out on [[ceramides]]. No independent trial tests this exact lotion; the brand's own barrier, absorption, and texture figures come from two small, uncontrolled, brand-commissioned tests.[^aestura]

## Summary

Aestura Atobarrier 365 Lotion is a fluid, pump-bottle moisturizer from the South Korean brand [[aestura|Aestura]], positioned as the lightweight option in the Atobarrier 365 line for dry, sensitive, and barrier-compromised skin, next to the richer [[aestura-atobarrier-365-cream|Atobarrier 365 Cream]].[^aestura] Its health-relevant case rests on the same ground as the cream: [[ceramides]], the barrier lipids that eczema-prone skin is measurably short of. Ceramide moisturizers move barrier measurements such as water loss, but the one randomized trial of a ceramide cream on that page found no advantage over a matched base for eczema severity itself. That general evidence, with its numbers and limits, is set out on [[ceramides]] and applies to the moisturizing category more broadly on [[moisturizing]].

The declared ingredient list confirms the same core barrier-lipid set as the cream, [[ceramides|ceramide NP]], cholesterol, phytosphingosine, sphingolipids, and the free fatty acids stearic, palmitic, arachidic, and oleic acid, plus [[niacinamide]], formulated into a thinner, faster-absorbing base built on dimethicone, squalane, and glycerin rather than the cream's heavier occlusive load.[^aestura] The brand markets the ceramide as delivered in "melted ceramide capsules," a description of the formulation rather than an independently tested claim.[^aestura] No published, independent trial tests this exact lotion; the brand cites two of its own uncontrolled, single-arm tests of 31 and 23 women, which are not the kind of evidence that supports a grade and are reported, quarantined, under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims) rather than folded into the case above.

The brand states the lotion contains less than 1% synthetic fragrance, the same tolerability profile as the cream, a consideration for fragrance-avoidant [[atopic-dermatitis|eczema-prone]] skin ([see Tolerability](#tolerability)).[^aestura]

## The Evidence

Ceramides are a genuine barrier lipid that atopic and sensitive skin is measurably depleted in, and ceramide moisturizers move barrier measurements such as transepidermal water loss; the gap is between that measured barrier effect and a proven edge on eczema severity itself, where the one randomized trial cited on [[ceramides]] found a ceramide cream beat an inactive base on water loss but not on eczema severity. Moisturizing as a category has stronger evidence for reducing eczema flares, set out on [[moisturizing]], but that category benefit is not specific to ceramides.

No published, independently reviewed trial of Aestura Atobarrier 365 Lotion itself is cited here, so its grades rest on that category-level ceramide evidence rather than on a product-specific result. The brand's own product page reports two separate brand-commissioned tests on 31 and 23 women over roughly a month each in 2023, covering instant and 4-week barrier, absorption speed, texture, and satisfaction measures.[^aestura] Neither test had a control group, and both results are reported by the brand rather than in an independently published or peer-reviewed source, so they do not support a grade here; their specific claimed figures are reported and weighed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on the manufacturer's product page.[^aestura] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

Barrier and hydration ingredients:

- [[ceramides|Ceramide NP]], the formula's named barrier lipid
- cholesterol, phytosphingosine, and sphingolipids, additional physiologic barrier lipids
- stearic acid, palmitic acid, arachidic acid, and oleic acid, free fatty acids that are the third component of the skin's own barrier-lipid mix
- [[glycerin]] and betaine, humectants
- [[niacinamide]], 19th of the 38 declared ingredients, ahead of ceramide NP, phytosphingosine, and sphingolipids in INCI order
- squalane and dimethicone, emollients

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, butylene glycol, pentaerythrityl tetraisostearate, polyglyceryl-3 methylglucose distearate, butylene glycol dicaprylate/dicaprate, caprylic/capric triglyceride, 1,2-hexanediol, hydroxyethyl acrylate/sodium acryloyldimethyl taurate copolymer, glyceryl stearate, hydroxypropyl bispalmitamide MEA, glyceryl stearate citrate, dimethiconol, hydroxypropyl bislauramide MEA, behenyl alcohol, glyceryl caprylate, carbomer, disodium EDTA, ethylhexylglycerin, tromethamine, sorbitan isostearate, mannitol, tocopherol (vitamin E), hydrogenated lecithin, acrylates/ammonium methacrylate copolymer, and silica.[^aestura]

The formula contains no parabens, formaldehyde or formaldehyde-releasing agents, phthalates, mineral oil, retinyl palmitate, oxybenzone, coal tar, hydroquinone, SLS/SLES sulfates, triclocarban, or triclosan, and the brand states it contains less than 1% synthetic fragrance.[^aestura]

## Tolerability

The brand states the lotion underwent a 48-hour dermatologist-supervised patch test, a non-comedogenic assessment on 20 acne-prone or sensitive-skin participants over 4 weeks, and a sensitive-skin panel test over 4 weeks.[^aestura] These are brand-reported test programs rather than independently published outcomes. The formula is not fragrance-free: the brand states it contains less than 1% synthetic fragrance, a consideration for fragrance-avoidant or reactive skin.
