Murad Resurgence Retinal ReSculpt Overnight Treatment is a 1.0 fl oz nightly leave-on cream from Murad, priced at $109.00 on the brand's own site, built around encapsulated retinaldehyde (called "retinal" in the marketing copy) as its named active.[^murad] Retinaldehyde is a cosmetic retinoid the skin converts to retinoic acid in a single enzymatic step, one fewer than retinol requires; the category-level case for it is set out on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]].[^murad]

> A premium retinaldehyde night cream that will not say how much retinaldehyde is in it. Per its FAQ, Murad states: "as part of our Corporate policy, we do not disclose percentages in our high performance formulations as this information is considered proprietary."[^murad] The brand's lift and sagging claims are its own marketing copy ("clinically proven to dramatically lift + sculpt with retinal"), with no published trial, sample size, method, or vehicle control shown on the product page, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence).[^murad]

## Summary

This is a 1.0 fl oz (per-jar size stated on the brand's own site) nightly cream sold for $109.00 direct from Murad, part of the brand's Resurgence line.[^murad] The ingredient list names the active simply as "Retinal," Murad's shorthand for retinaldehyde, with no percentage disclosed anywhere on the product page.[^murad]

The health-relevant story here is entirely cosmetic: this is not a drug and carries no FDA-reviewed indication. Retinaldehyde as an ingredient class has real, if more limited, published evidence for improving fine lines and skin texture, [set out in full on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]](#the-evidence). Because Murad won't disclose the concentration in this specific formula, that category-level evidence cannot be tied to a known dose here, which caps the grade at modest and preliminary rather than higher.

Murad frames its retinaldehyde as encapsulated for "enhanced stability and targeted delivery," claiming "2.5x more effectiveness (vs. free retinal)," a comparison the brand shows with no study citation, method, or comparator formula on the page.[^murad] The supporting cast includes kangaroo paw flower (Anigozanthos flavidus) extract and olive leaf extract, which the brand says "help improve saggy appearance, smooth wrinkles and support resilience," plus "oat lipids and alpha glucan" it ties to replenishing ceramides and moisture.[^murad]

## The Evidence

Whether topical retinaldehyde improves photoaged skin at the ingredient level is covered on [[retinaldehyde]] and [[retinoids]]: category evidence supports gradual improvement in fine lines and texture, generally at concentrations and follow-up periods shorter than what's established for prescription tretinoin. No independent, peer-reviewed, vehicle-controlled trial of this specific Murad formula was found.

The only product-specific results Murad shows come from its own marketing copy: the product is "clinically proven to dramatically lift + sculpt with retinal," and it "visibly lifts neck + jowl sagging in 2 weeks" and "visibly smooths deep-set wrinkles + crepey skin in 2 weeks." The page shows no sample size, comparison arm, follow-up beyond these timed claims, or published citation for any of them, and the "2.5x more effectiveness (vs. free retinal)" figure appears with no linked study or named comparator.[^murad]

> **Bottom line.** The category-level case for retinaldehyde is real and is graded independently on [[retinaldehyde]], but this cream's own results rest entirely on unsupported brand marketing claims, with no sample size, control arm, method, or independent publication shown; the undisclosed concentration also means the ingredient-level dose evidence cannot be matched to this specific product.
