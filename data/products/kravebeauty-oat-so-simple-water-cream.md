A nine-ingredient lightweight moisturiser, $28 for 80 mL. **Bottom line:** the short list is the honest part of the pitch and the formula is sensible, but the oat it is named after is the last of the nine, and it is not the oat with the regulatory backing. The hydration is coming from the squalane and the emollients above it.

> **The headline ingredient is ninth of nine.** KraveBeauty publishes the full list: "Water (Aqua/Eau), Butylene Glycol, Caprylic/Capric Triglyceride, Squalane, 1,2-Hexanediol, Trehalose, Behenyl Alcohol, Ammonium Acryloyldimethyltaurate/VP Copolymer, Avena Sativa (Oat) Meal Extract".[^brand] That is exactly nine, so the brand's "Made with only 9 ingredients" is true and checkable. It also means the oat extract sits last, below the gelling polymer, on a product whose name, marketing and soothing claim all rest on oat.

## Summary

KraveBeauty sells it in two formats: an 80 mL jar at **$28.00** and a 200 mL refill pouch at **$51.00**, both in stock on its own storefront.[^brand] The brand describes it as "A calming lightweight moisturizer with a burst of hydration" whose "light-as-water texture cools sensitive areas and Oat Extract protects against irritants to provide comforting relief to oversensitized areas."[^brand] It is made for "All skin types, especially sensitive skin" and is said to help with "Sealing in moisture and preventing further water loss from the skin."[^brand]

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: modest, and credited to the wrong ingredient.** [[squalane]] is fourth and caprylic/capric triglyceride third; between them, with butylene glycol and trehalose as humectants, this is a competent light moisturiser. None of that is unusual or hard to find, and none of it depends on the oat.
- **Evidence quality: an efficacy claim this time, with the sample size printed and the study withheld.** "Clinically Proven: After one use, the moisturizer was shown to improve skin's hydration for up to 48 hours. Based on an independent clinical study of 21 subjects."[^brand] Unlike the safety claim on [[kravebeauty-kale-lalu-yaha]], this is a claim about whether it works. No journal, registration number or method is given, so the 48 hours cannot be checked.
- **Record finding: the fourth KraveBeauty product here to print its own n.** 20 participants on [[kravebeauty-great-barrier-relief]], six tests on [[kravebeauty-beet-the-sun-spf-40]], 30 on [[kravebeauty-kale-lalu-yaha]], 21 here. Most brands give a percentage and no denominator. This one consistently gives the denominator, which is worth saying plainly even though the studies themselves stay unpublished.
- **Record finding: this is not the oat with the monograph.** The declared ingredient is "Avena Sativa (Oat) Meal Extract", an extract.[^brand] [[colloidal-oatmeal]] is finely milled whole oat and is the form named in the FDA over-the-counter skin protectant monograph; that page carries the detail and the regulatory citations. An oat extract at the bottom of a nine-item list is not that ingredient and does not inherit its evidence.
- **Record finding: the refill saves less than the brand says.** The refill pouch listing claims it will "save more than $0.10/mL versus repurchasing a new one."[^brand] From the two prices on the same feed, the jar is 35.0 cents per mL and the refill 25.5, a difference of 9.5 cents.[^brand] The refill is the better buy and the packaging argument stands; the number is just slightly generous.

## The Evidence

None specific to this product beyond the uncited 21-subject study. The ingredient arguments are at [[squalane]] and, for the oat the product does not contain, [[colloidal-oatmeal]]; neither is restated here.

> **Bottom line.** A good short formula with a misleading centre of gravity. If you want oat for irritated skin, the evidence sits with colloidal oatmeal, not with an oat extract listed ninth.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own product pages and **not** endorsed here.

- "Made with only 9 ingredients to hydrate parched, easily sensitized skin."[^brand] The count is accurate, which is more than most short-ingredient claims manage.
- "Oat Extract protects against irritants to provide comforting relief to oversensitized areas."[^brand] No evidence for this formula is offered, and the ingredient is last of nine.
- "Clinically Proven: After one use, the moisturizer was shown to improve skin's hydration for up to 48 hours."[^brand] An efficacy claim resting on 21 subjects, with the study uncited.
- "you're also using 86% less packaging material versus buying a new jar"[^brand] A packaging claim, not a skin claim, and no method for the 86% is given.
