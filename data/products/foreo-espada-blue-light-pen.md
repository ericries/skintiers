The FOREO ESPADA is a handheld blue-light device for acne, cleared by the FDA in November 2016 under 510(k) number K162450.[^fda] Its cleared indication, quoted from the FDA's Indications for Use form, is that it "is intended for use as an at-home device for the treatment of mild to moderate inflammatory acne".[^fda] The device category is on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

> **The light is the right colour. The paperwork tells you less than any other device on this site.** FOREO filed this clearance as a 510(k) **Statement** rather than a Summary, which openFDA records for K162450.[^openfda] A Statement is a certification to supply safety and effectiveness information on request; a Summary publishes it. The practical consequence is that the public file contains the clearance letter and the indication, and **no description of the predicate device, the wavelengths, or what was tested**. For the other two home LED devices profiled here you can at least read that the testing was bench or nonclinical. Here you cannot read anything at all.

## Summary

The cleared device is the original "ESPADA Acne-Clearing Blue Light Pen", a Class II device under 21 CFR 878.4810, product code OLP, cleared Traditional 510(k) on 30 November 2016.[^fda][^openfda] What FOREO currently sells is **ESPADA 2**, listed at $169.00 on the brand's own site, which states it uses "415 nm blue LED".[^brand] 415nm is squarely in the blue range that the 2026 GRADE review of home devices graded Level B with a Grade I recommendation, the best grade in its acne section.[^home] That wavelength figure comes from the brand, not from the FDA file, because the FDA file does not contain one.

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate inflammatory acne**
- **Effect size: modest, and carried entirely by the category.** Blue LED for acne is Level B in the GRADE review, and the brand's stated 415nm sits in that range.[^home][^brand] Nothing in the clearance tests this device, and unlike the other devices here the clearance does not even say what testing was done.[^fda]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary for the device, mixed at the category level.** Note the asymmetry this creates: the better-evidenced configuration is paired with the least informative public record.

**Knowing what you are buying**
- **Effect size: not applicable; this is a disclosure problem rather than an efficacy question.** The clearance names the "ESPADA Acne-Clearing Blue Light Pen".[^fda] A search of openFDA returned 10 FOREO clearances, and the only ESPADA entry among them is this 2016 one; no separate clearance naming ESPADA 2 was found.[^openfda] A manufacturer may modify a cleared device without a new submission where the change does not significantly affect safety or effectiveness, so this is not by itself an irregularity. It does mean the FDA record describes the predecessor, not the product on sale.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable; these are database facts.**

## The Evidence

**What a 510(k) Statement changes for a reader.** Under the Statement route the submitter certifies it will provide safety and effectiveness information to anyone who asks, instead of publishing a summary. That is lawful and common. It is also why this page can tell you the indication and almost nothing else. **8 of the 10 FOREO clearances returned by openFDA are Statements**, so this is the company's usual practice rather than a one-off choice for this device.[^openfda]

**The brand's own claim goes further than its clearance can.** FOREO's product data describes ESPADA 2 as "an FDA-cleared medical device clinically proven to treat and heal acne-prone skin".[^brand] Those are two separate claims joined by a comma. FDA clearance means substantial equivalence to a predicate, which the agency says in its own letters is not a determination that a device works. "Clinically proven" is a claim about trial evidence, and no trial evidence is visible in this clearance, because the Statement route does not publish any. The claim may rest on studies held by the company; the public record does not show them.

**Where it sits against the others.** The comparison across the three home LED devices profiled here is about what each discloses, not about which works better, since none has published device-level efficacy data. [[revive-light-therapy-wrinkle-and-acne-led-device]] publishes a summary showing software validation. [[revive-light-therapy-essentials]] publishes summaries saying the conclusions come from nonclinical tests. This one publishes no summary. The modality ladder is on [[home-use-skincare-devices-by-evidence]].

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"FDA-cleared"**. Accurate for K162450.[^openfda] It means substantial equivalence to a predicate the public file does not name.
- **"clinically proven to treat and heal acne-prone skin"**. The brand's wording.[^brand] Not evidenced by the clearance, which under the Statement route publishes no testing at all.
- **"415 nm blue LED"**. The brand's specification, not an FDA-recorded figure.[^brand] It is in the better-evidenced blue range.[^home]
- **"Up to 3000 mW of blue LED"**. A power figure from the brand.[^brand] No source consulted here relates a power figure to a clinical outcome.
