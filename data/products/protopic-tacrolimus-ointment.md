Protopic is the original branded formulation of tacrolimus ointment, a prescription-only topical calcineurin inhibitor available in 0.03% and 0.1% strengths and approved by the FDA as second-line therapy for the short-term and non-continuous chronic treatment of moderate to severe atopic dermatitis in non-immunocompromised adults and children 2 years and older who have failed to respond adequately to other topical prescription treatments, or when those treatments are not advisable.[^dailymed] It carries the FDA's boxed warning over a rare, unconfirmed malignancy signal shared by the whole topical-calcineurin-inhibitor class, the same warning that sits on its milder sister drug [[elidel-pimecrolimus-cream]].[^dailymed]

> A steroid-sparing option with a genuinely solid trial base behind it: against an inert vehicle, roughly a third to over a third of patients reached 90%+ improvement on Protopic versus about 1 in 14 (7%) on vehicle, and in a large pooled head-to-head it also beat pimecrolimus cream, the other topical calcineurin inhibitor, on both speed and effect size.[^dailymed][^paller2005] The label's boxed warning over rare malignancy reports remains unresolved by follow-up data, restricting continuous long-term use, barring use under age 2 outright, and reserving the 0.1% strength for adults only.[^dailymed]

## Summary

Protopic is tacrolimus ointment, applied twice daily to affected skin in a base of mineral oil, paraffin, propylene carbonate, white petrolatum, and white wax.[^dailymed] It is a health product, not a cosmetic one: its only FDA-cleared use is as a steroid-sparing second-line treatment for an inflammatory skin disease, [[atopic-dermatitis]], prescribed when a topical corticosteroid has failed or is inadvisable.[^dailymed] Both strengths are approved for adults; only the 0.03% strength is indicated for children 2 to 15, and the drug is not indicated at all under age 2.[^dailymed] The graded mechanism, the boxed-warning malignancy question, and the trial comparing tacrolimus directly against a mid-potency steroid are set out in full on [[tacrolimus-topical]]; this page covers what is specific to the branded Protopic product itself.

## The Evidence

Protopic is the actual product tested in its own approval trials, not a copy of them. Three randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, multicenter phase 3 studies evaluated it in moderate to severe atopic dermatitis: one pediatric study in 351 patients ages 2 to 15, and two identically designed adult studies pooled to 632 patients ages 15 to 79.[^dailymed] At baseline, 58% of patients had severe disease and the mean body surface area affected was 46%, with over 80% of patients having disease affecting the face and/or neck.[^dailymed] Patients applied 0.03% Protopic, 0.1% Protopic, or vehicle ointment twice daily for up to 12 weeks.[^dailymed]

On the primary endpoint, at least 90% improvement by physician's global evaluation of clinical response, the pediatric study found a significantly greater percentage of patients responding on 0.03% Protopic than vehicle (36% vs 7%, P<.001), with no evidence 0.1% added further benefit in children.[^dailymed] In the pooled adult studies, both strengths beat vehicle at that same 90%+ bar (28% on 0.03%, 37% on 0.1%, versus 7% on vehicle, all P<.001), with evidence that 0.1% outperformed 0.03% in adults, particularly in those with severe baseline disease, extensive body surface area involvement, and in black adults.[^dailymed] A significant difference over vehicle in adults appeared by week 1 for the 0.1% strength and by week 3 for 0.03%; in children it appeared by week 2.[^dailymed] Among adult patients who reached 90%+ improvement, 35% (0.03%) and 41% (0.1%) relapsed within 2 weeks of stopping treatment; in the pediatric group, 54% relapsed within that same 2-week window.[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: against an inert vehicle, Protopic brought roughly a quarter to a third of adults, and over a third of children on the pediatric-labeled strength, to 90%+ improvement, versus about 1 in 14 (7%) on vehicle, with the higher 0.1% strength working faster and somewhat harder in adults.[^dailymed]

**Head-to-head against pimecrolimus (Paller and colleagues, 2005).** A pooled analysis of three randomized, comparative 6-week trials in 1,065 patients found tacrolimus ointment significantly more effective than pimecrolimus cream, the other topical calcineurin inhibitor, at study end on an Eczema Area Severity Index (EASI) measure of effectiveness: 54.1% vs 34.9% in adults (P<.0001) and 67.2% vs 56.4% in children with moderate to severe disease (P=.04), with a faster onset of action and a similar overall safety profile.[^paller2005] The full comparison and its evidentiary limits are set out on [[tacrolimus-topical]] and [[elidel-pimecrolimus-cream]].

> Bottom line: head-to-head against its milder sister drug, tacrolimus won on both speed and effect size, at similar safety.

## What's In It

- **Active: tacrolimus 0.03% or 0.1%** (each gram of ointment contains, w/w, either 0.03% or 0.1% tacrolimus, per the FDA label). See [[tacrolimus-topical]] for the graded mechanism, the trial against a mid-potency steroid, and the full boxed-warning evidence base.[^dailymed]
- **Base:** mineral oil, paraffin, propylene carbonate, white petrolatum, and white wax, as declared on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

The boxed warning states: "Although a causal relationship has not been established, rare cases of malignancy (e.g., skin and lymphoma) have been reported in patients treated with topical calcineurin inhibitors, including PROTOPIC Ointment," and on that basis instructs that continuous long-term use in any age group be avoided, application be limited to areas of involvement with atopic dermatitis, that Protopic not be indicated for children under 2, and that only the 0.03% strength be indicated for ages 2 to 15.[^dailymed] The label states the safety of Protopic under occlusion, which may promote systemic exposure, has not been evaluated, and that it should not be used with occlusive dressings.[^dailymed] The only listed contraindication is a history of hypersensitivity to tacrolimus or any other component of the ointment.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"A steroid-free alternative with no downside."** Protopic does avoid the corticosteroid skin-thinning risk, and the trial comparing 0.1% Protopic directly against a mid-potency steroid found comparable efficacy, but that same trial found more application-site burning and itching with tacrolimus than the steroid, and the label still carries a boxed warning restricting continuous long-term use; full detail on [[tacrolimus-topical]].[^dailymed]
- **"Works fast."** The label itself supports a fast onset relative to vehicle, a statistically significant difference appeared by week 1 for 0.1% and week 3 for 0.03% in adults, and by week 2 for 0.03% in children, but this is a controlled-trial timeline against an inert comparator, not a guarantee of fast individual results.[^dailymed]

## Where to Buy

Protopic is available only by prescription; no over-the-counter purchase path exists, and generic tacrolimus ointment versions in both strengths are also FDA-approved. This review found no verifiable, primary-sourced retail price for the branded product at the time of writing, so no price is quoted here (see the DailyMed label linked below for the current manufacturer of record).
