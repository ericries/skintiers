CeraVe AM Facial Moisturizing Lotion SPF 30 is the morning half of CeraVe's AM/PM pair: a daily facial moisturizer with broad-spectrum [[sunscreen-uv-filters|SPF 30]] built on the brand's ceramide-and-cholesterol barrier base. It is the drugstore counterpart to [[la-roche-posay-toleriane-double-repair-uv-spf-30|La Roche-Posay's Toleriane Double Repair UV]]: the same idea of a barrier moisturizer that doubles as your morning sunscreen step. It is a low-cost drugstore product, listed below the price of the Toleriane UV comparator.[^target]

> This does two evidence-backed jobs in one step. Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen is the single best-supported way to prevent photoaging (see [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]), and the [[ceramides|ceramide]], [[glycerin]], and [[niacinamide]] base is a genuinely well-built barrier moisturizer. The honest limits are the sun protection: it is SPF 30, not 50, and it uses an older US filter set (octinoxate and meradimate with zinc oxide and octocrylene) whose UVA breadth and cosmetic finish lag the newer filters available abroad, and the zinc oxide can leave a slight white cast on deeper skin tones.

## Summary

CeraVe markets this as an "oil-free daily facial moisturizing lotion" with broad-spectrum SPF 30.[^target] It is used in the morning, doubling the moisturizer and sunscreen steps.

The sun protection comes from a mixed chemical-and-mineral filter system, listed in the Drug Facts as homosalate 10%, menthyl anthranilate (meradimate) 5%, octinoxate 5%, octocrylene 2%, and zinc oxide 6.3%. The moisturizer base carries CeraVe's signature three ceramides (AP, NP, EOP) with cholesterol and phytosphingosine, plus niacinamide, glycerin, and hyaluronic acid; because this is an over-the-counter drug, the inactive ingredients are listed alphabetically rather than by amount, so ingredient order does not indicate concentration. It is a low-cost drugstore product; retailer listings put it below the price of the Toleriane UV comparator ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)).[^target]

## The Evidence

There are two product-specific things worth grading, and both are well-supported, so each gets one clause rather than a re-argument. Broad-spectrum sunscreen is the best-evidenced intervention against photoaging and the reason to use a morning SPF at all (the filter evidence and how to choose an SPF are on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]). The barrier base is CeraVe's well-known combination: [[ceramides]] with cholesterol to support the skin barrier, plus [[glycerin]] and [[niacinamide]]. So as a single morning product it delivers two things that independently have good evidence.

The caveats are about the sun protection. It is SPF 30, which blocks slightly less UVB than an SPF 50. Its filter set is the older US line-up: [[octinoxate]] and meradimate for UVB and short UVA, with [[zinc-oxide]] and [[octocrylene]] doing the broad-spectrum work; this combination is less cosmetically elegant and narrower on UVA than the newer filters (such as Tinosorb and Mexoryl) standard in the EU, Korea, and Australia but not approved for US sunscreens, as set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. The zinc oxide can also leave a faint white cast, most noticeable on deeper skin tones. As with any facial sunscreen, the labeled protection assumes generous application and reapplication on high-exposure days.

> **Bottom line.** A well-built morning two-in-one: a ceramide and niacinamide barrier moisturizer that is also a broad-spectrum SPF 30, and a direct drugstore alternative to [[la-roche-posay-toleriane-double-repair-uv-spf-30|Toleriane Double Repair UV]]. If sun protection is the priority, an SPF 50 gives more margin and a newer-filter or all-mineral sunscreen may finish better, but for daily barrier support plus baseline UV cover in one step it covers both jobs in one product.
