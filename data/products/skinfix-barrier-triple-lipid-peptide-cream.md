Triple Lipid-Peptide Cream is a face moisturizer from [[skinfix|Skinfix]] built around what the brand calls a "3% Triple Lipid Complex" of ceramides, phytosterols, and fatty acids, plus a "3% Peptide Solution" of hydrolyzed rice protein, sold in 1.7 oz, 0.5 oz, and 3.3 oz sizes.[^skinfix]

> A physiologic-lipid barrier cream from [[skinfix|Skinfix]] that names all three structural skin lipids, ceramides, cholesterol, and fatty acids, on its label. Ceramide moisturizers reliably move barrier measurements such as water loss in a controlled trial, though that has not translated into a proven edge over a plain base on eczema severity itself, covered on [[ceramides]]. The brand's own declared ingredient order confirms three named ceramides and a fatty acid fairly high on the list, but shows cholesterol and phytosterols much lower, [detailed below](#whats-in-it), a composition detail that qualifies how close the formula comes to the roughly equal three-lipid ratio the barrier-repair research describes.[^skinfix]

## Summary

Triple Lipid-Peptide Cream is a moisturizer from [[skinfix|Skinfix]] marketed for dry skin, sold at $54 for the regular 1.7 oz size (also available in a 0.5 oz mini and a 3.3 oz "XL" size) directly from the brand.[^skinfix] Its positioning rests on three named physiologic barrier lipids on the label, ceramides, cholesterol, and fatty acids, the same trio that barrier-repair research describes skin needing in roughly equal proportion, plus a hydrolyzed-rice-protein peptide ingredient and a humectant blend of sodium hyaluronate, glycerin, and Chondrus Crispus extract.[^skinfix]

Ceramides are structural lipids of the skin's outer barrier, and topical ceramide moisturizers have been shown in a controlled trial to lower transepidermal water loss and raise hydration measurements compared with a matched base, without a measurable edge on eczema severity itself; that general case, with its numbers and limits, is set out on [[ceramides]]. Barrier-repair experiments on stripped skin found that ceramides, cholesterol, and free fatty acids "are required for permeability barrier homeostasis" only when combined in roughly equal amounts, and that applying just one or two of the three delayed recovery, covered on [[free-fatty-acids]] and [[cholesterol]]. The brand's own declared ingredient list for this cream names three ceramide types (Ceramide NP, AP, EOP) 22nd to 24th of 47 ingredients and palmitic acid, a free fatty acid, 12th; the sterol picture is mixed, since a sterol ester, Phytosteryl Sunflowerseedate, ranks 19th, above the ceramides, while free cholesterol (41st) and free phytosterols (36th) rank well after several ingredients typically present at low, preservative-adjacent levels; [the full order is enumerated below](#whats-in-it).[^skinfix] That order does not establish the finished concentration of any single ingredient, but it shows the two free sterol forms trailing the ceramides and palmitic acid even as an esterified sterol form outranks them, a gap the [[ceramides]] research flags as mattering: an unbalanced or incomplete lipid mixture is not shown to repair the barrier as well as a complete, roughly equal-parts one.

The brand also cites its own self-reported testing, including hydration and barrier-function percentages after use, [reported below as manufacturer claims not independently verified](#common-marketing-claims), and states the cream carries the 2025 Allure Best of Beauty award for Best Clean Skincare.[^skinfix]

## The Evidence

No independently published trial of Triple Lipid-Peptide Cream itself is cited here. Its case for barrier support rests on the general, controlled-trial evidence that ceramide moisturizers lower measured water loss and raise hydration readings versus a matched inactive base, without a demonstrated edge on eczema severity, set out with its study design and numbers on [[ceramides]], combined with experimental evidence that ceramides, cholesterol, and free fatty acids need to be present together in roughly equal proportion for normal barrier recovery, covered on [[free-fatty-acids]]. The brand's own declared ingredient list confirms three named ceramide types and the fatty acid palmitic acid appear well up the 47-item order (12th to 24th), and a sterol ester, Phytosteryl Sunflowerseedate, appears even higher (19th), while the free forms, cholesterol (41st) and phytosterols (36th), appear much further down; the sterol picture is therefore mixed rather than uniformly low on the list, a composition detail rather than a concentration figure, since INCI order alone does not disclose exact percentages below the top few ingredients.[^skinfix] The brand's separately stated "3% Triple Lipid Complex" figure covers ceramides, phytosterols, and fatty acids together as one combined percentage, not broken out per ingredient.[^skinfix]

> **Bottom line.** No study isolates this exact cream's own effect. Its case rests on the general, controlled-trial evidence for ceramide moisturizers on barrier measurements, covered on [[ceramides]], applied to a formula that names all three structural barrier lipids on its label but, by declared ingredient order, places cholesterol and phytosterols well behind the ceramides and the fatty acid palmitic acid.[^skinfix]
