Naturium Alpha Arbutin Serum 2% is a water-based leave-on serum built around a manufacturer-declared 2% alpha-arbutin paired with undosed niacinamide, sold in a single 30 mL bottle and marketed for brightening and dark-spot appearance.[^1]

> A cheap, widely available brightening serum with a printed alpha-arbutin percentage well below the range tested in the clinical literature (see Summary), paired with an undosed niacinamide whose own general evidence is summarized on [[niacinamide]]. It is listed at $20 for 30 mL on Naturium's own site.[^1]

## Summary

Alpha Arbutin Serum 2% is Naturium's plain brightening serum: a short ingredient list built around [[arbutin|alpha-arbutin]] at a manufacturer-declared 2%, with [[niacinamide]] as a second, undosed active, in a single 30 mL bottle.[^1] The general case for topical arbutin, a modest, preliminary case for hyperpigmentation and melasma resting on one small split-face trial and one uncontrolled laser-combination case series, both run at 5% to 7% alpha-arbutin, is set out on [[arbutin]]. This product's declared 2% sits below the concentration used in either study.[^1][^2]

Niacinamide's own modest, mostly small-trial evidence for barrier support, hyperpigmentation, and mild acne is set out on [[niacinamide]]. Here niacinamide is not dosed on the label at all, so it cannot be checked against the roughly 2% to 5% range that evidence was built at ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)).

What distinguishes this serum from category peers such as [[beauty-of-joseon-glow-deep-serum-rice-alpha-arbutin|Beauty of Joseon's Glow Deep Serum]] is mainly ingredient order: alpha-arbutin sits third on Naturium's list, right after water and butylene glycol, ahead of niacinamide in fourth position, versus Beauty of Joseon's formula where alpha-arbutin sits seventh.[^1] The manufacturer's marketing describes the serum as carrying a "highly efficacious level of alpha arbutin," a claim weighed against the concentration evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims). It is listed at $20 for the single 30 mL size on Naturium's own site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^1]

## The Evidence

Topical alpha-arbutin has a modest, preliminary case for hyperpigmentation and melasma; how that case holds up, including its one active-comparator trial and one uncontrolled laser case series, is set out in full on [[arbutin]]. Nothing specific to this product was found: no published trial tests Naturium's 2% alpha-arbutin plus undosed niacinamide formula, so its grades above rest on the general category evidence, all of it generated at higher alpha-arbutin concentrations than 2%.[^1]

Arbutin's melasma case rests on trials run at 5% to 7% alpha-arbutin, above this product's declared 2%; niacinamide's general evidence is built mostly at 2% to 5% niacinamide, a concentration this label does not disclose. See [[arbutin]] and [[niacinamide]] for the full trial designs, numbers, and limits.[^2]

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Both grades rest on the general ingredient evidence (see Summary above) rather than on a test of this formula or its concentrations.[^1][^2]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Helps brighten appearance of skin tone" and "to target and fade the look of dark spots."** Positioning claims resting on arbutin's tyrosinase-inhibition rationale, not on a trial of this product or its concentration.[^1]
