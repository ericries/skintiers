This is the 2012 original of the light head NuFACE still sells for its older handle, and its file is more interesting than the device. **Bottom line:** the indication is a real wrinkle indication, the testing behind it is three electrical and optical safety standards, and in 2022 FDA quietly replaced the published summary because the device characteristics on record had been wrong since 2012. The current version is [[nuface-trinity-plus-wrinkle-reducer]]; modality evidence is at [[home-led-light-therapy]].

> **The attachment model was documented from the start.** The filing says this device "is a new device design based on portions of the NuFACE® Trinity Facial Toning Device, which was cleared under K103472", whose "main body ... is used in conjunction with a new Treatment Head, that emits energy in the Red and IR region of the spectrum for reducing fine lines and wrinkles".[^k120560] So the thing FDA cleared is the handle you already own plus a new head, which is exactly what $160 buys. The 2022 filing for the newer head describes a complete device instead, which is why that page flags the mismatch and this one does not.

## Summary

Cleared 2012-11-06 as K120560, a Traditional 510(k) published as a Summary, applicant Carol Cole Company, product code OHS under 21 CFR 878.4810, Class II, General and Plastic Surgery panel.[^openfda] The indication, in full: "The Trinity Wrinkle Remover is an over-the-counter hand-held device intended for the use in the treatment of full-face wrinkles."[^k120560]

The head "comprises (36) Light Emitting Diodes (LED's) which emit light at the 605nm, 630nm, 660nm and 855nm wavelengths", the device "continually pulses the LEDs output, and provides a fixed output intensity level", and "The device does not allow the output level to be changed."[^k120560] A beep every three minutes tells you to move it to a new part of the face.[^k120560]

NuFACE sells it as the Trinity Classic LED Red Light Wrinkle Reducer Attachment at **$160.00**, the same price as the Trinity+ version, described as "Attachment only - device sold separately. Compatible with Trinity Classic Only."[^feed]

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: minimal, and the file offers nothing to raise it.** LED is Level B for facial rejuvenation in the 2026 GRADE scoping review, a grade that belongs to the modality and is set out at [[home-led-light-therapy]].[^home] Searched in full, this document contains no irradiance or power figure, no energy dose, no subject count, no endpoint and no result.[^k120560]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary, and here the absence is total.** The compliance section reports conformity to IEC 62471:2006 for photobiological safety of lamps, IEC 60601-1-2 for electromagnetic compatibility and IEC 60601-1 for electrical safety.[^k120560] That is the whole of the testing. The word clinical does not occur anywhere in the document, not even inside the word nonclinical, so unlike its 2022 successor this filing never had to decline a clinical study.[^k120560]
- **Record finding: FDA corrected the public record nine years late, without re-reviewing anything.** The letter at the front of the file is dated 19 January 2022 and says FDA "is sending this letter to notify you of an administrative change related to your previous substantial equivalence (SE) determination letter dated November 6th, 2012", that it "is updating this SE Letter with a corrected 510(k) Summary, which includes the correct device characteristics, as an administrative correction", and, in its own italics, that "the 510(k) submission was not re-reviewed".[^k120560] The summary now posted is stamped "Prepared: December 15, 2021".[^k120560] Everything quoted on this page therefore comes from a document written nine years after the clearance it describes. What the original summary got wrong is not stated and this page does not guess.
- **Record finding: its predicates are LightStim, not the reVive devices this site has been reading.** The filing claims equivalence to K101190 and K120775, both from LED Intellectual Properties, LLC, under OHS.[^k120560] The first is quoted as "intended for the use in the treatment of periorbital wrinkles", the second as covering "full-face wrinkles", which is how the scope widened.[^k120560] The document names both devices "Light for Wrinkles" in their proprietary-name lines, then quotes the second one's indication as beginning "The LightStim for Wrinkles"; that inconsistency is the source's own. The same LightStim device turns up as a predicate in LED Technologies' K141181, recorded at [[dpl-nuve-for-wrinkles]].
- **Record finding: the handle's controls go dead.** "The '+' and '-' keys on the handle (main body) are not active when the Trinity Wrinkle Remover treatment Attachment is attached to the handle."[^k120560] Those keys set microcurrent intensity on [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]]. You are running one mode or the other, never both.
- **Record finding: a second filing confirms the Trinity naming.** This document calls K103472 the "NuFACE® Trinity Facial Toning Device", while K103472's own summary calls that device the NuFACE Plus throughout.[^k120560] [[nuface-plus]] already reports that equivalence from the 2014 Mini filing; this is an independent second source for it. The two documents also disagree about the company address, Carlsbad in the summary against Poway in the letter, the same split that page notes.[^k120560]

## The Evidence

None specific to this device. Three safety standards and a substantial-equivalence argument that rests on emitting "Red and IR energy at the same wavelengths and similar intensities as the predicate device".[^k120560]

What the pair of NuFACE light filings is good for is watching a cleared scope move. In 2012 the indication was the treatment of full-face wrinkles. In 2022 it became the reduction of full-face fine lines and wrinkles plus an increase in local circulation in the perioral region, on four wavelengths going to five and 36 diodes going to 34.[^k120560][^k212947] Neither change was supported by a trial of the device. [[home-device-wrinkle-claims-by-cleared-scope]] ranks the home devices here by exactly this, and [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] covers what clearance does and does not establish.

> **Bottom line.** Worth owning only if you own the older handle, in which case it is the same money as the current version for one fewer wavelength and two more diodes. Worth reading for the correction letter, which is a reminder that the device characteristics in a 510(k) summary are the company's own description and can sit wrong on the public record for most of a decade.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own product listing and **not** endorsed here.

- "Our professional-grade LED Red Light Wrinkle Reducer Attachment harnesses 3 targeted light therapies—red, amber, & infrared—to help smooth fine lines, boost radiance, calm skin, and even skin tone."[^feed] The brand uses one description for both attachments. The FDA file for this one lists **four** wavelengths and describes them as red and IR; radiance, calming and even tone appear nowhere in the cleared indication, which is wrinkles and nothing else.[^k120560]
- "professional-grade".[^feed] Not a regulatory category. The clearance is an over-the-counter one, in those words.[^k120560]
