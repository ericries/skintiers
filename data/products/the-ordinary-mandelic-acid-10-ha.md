The Ordinary Mandelic Acid 10% + HA is a water-based, leave-on exfoliating serum built around a labeled 10% mandelic acid, with a manufacturer-stated pH of 3.5 to 4.5, sold for about $7.80 for a 30 ml bottle.[^1][^2]

> Mandelic acid is an alpha-hydroxy acid (AHA) with a larger molecule than glycolic acid, placing it on the slower-penetrating, gentler side of the exfoliating-acid family alongside [[lactic-acid|lactic acid]] (see [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]]), but its clinical evidence is thinner and more mixed than glycolic or lactic acid's, and the one trial that tested mandelic acid alone against lactic acid found lactic acid worked better, detailed on [[mandelic-acid]]. This serum's labeled 10% is far below the 30-45% peel concentrations used in that evidence, at a manufacturer-stated pH (3.5-4.5) that includes both exfoliation-active and higher, less-active values. The formula also carries hyaluronic acid (as sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer) for hydration, and the brand's own directions say it should not be used "on sensitive, peeling, or compromised skin."[^1]

## Summary

The Ordinary Mandelic Acid 10% + HA is a daily leave-on exfoliating serum built around mandelic acid, an alpha-hydroxy acid (AHA), at a labeled 10%.[^1] Mandelic acid's molecule is larger than glycolic acid's, which places it on the slower-penetrating, gentler side of the AHA family alongside lactic acid; how it lines up against the rest of the exfoliating acids is set out on [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]]. Its clinical evidence is thinner and more mixed than glycolic or lactic acid's: the trials behind it are almost all small in-office peel studies at 30% to 45%, testing acne or hyperpigmentation rather than daily leave-on use, and in the one trial that pitted mandelic acid alone against a comparably gentle AHA, lactic acid, for hyperpigmentation, lactic acid produced better pigment reduction, higher satisfaction, and less irritation; that full case is set out on [[mandelic-acid]].

What distinguishes this serum from a plainer, cheaper acid product is less the concentration itself and more the combination: a disclosed 10% acid, unlike many undisclosed-strength body exfoliants, paired with [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]] in the form of sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer for hydration, plus a Tasmanian pepperberry extract. It is sold cheaply relative to other 10% acid products: The Ordinary's own page lists it at $7.80 USD for 30 ml, a price corroborated on Cult Beauty's listing ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-13).[^1][^2]

The other side of that acid family is the documented downside shared by AHAs: increased skin sensitivity to sunlight while in use (see [[glycolic-acid]]). The Ordinary's own directions carry a "Sunburn Alert," say the product should not be used "on sensitive, peeling, or compromised skin," and advise a patch test before use.[^1] Its marketing describes the product as a gentle exfoliator suited to all skin types and targeting textural irregularities, dullness, and uneven tone; those claims are separated out under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## The Evidence

Mandelic acid is an AHA exfoliant with thinner, more mixed evidence than glycolic or lactic acid: its controlled trials are almost entirely small in-office peel studies at 30% to 45%, mostly from Indian dermatology departments, testing acne or hyperpigmentation rather than daily leave-on use, and the one trial testing mandelic acid alone against another gentle AHA, lactic acid, found lactic acid produced better results; the full case, by use, is set out on [[mandelic-acid]]. No study of this specific serum formula has been published; the product-specific facts are its composition and label claims. This serum is labeled 10% mandelic acid,[^1] far below the 30% to 45% concentrations used in the peel trials behind that evidence. The Ordinary states the finished product's pH as 3.5 to 4.5 on its specifications,[^1] a range Cult Beauty's listing does not independently state; that range spans both exfoliation-active and higher, less-active pH values, and neither source publishes a separate free-acid-value figure.

> **Bottom line.** This serum's labeled 10% mandelic acid sits well below the 30-45% peel concentrations used in mandelic acid's own evidence, which is itself thinner and more mixed than glycolic or lactic acid's. No trial has tested this specific leave-on formula. The evidence for what mandelic acid does, and does not do, is set out on [[mandelic-acid]].[^1][^2]

## What's In It

The full declared ingredient list (INCI), matching between the manufacturer's own page and an independent retailer's listing:[^1][^2]

Active and supporting ingredients:

- Mandelic Acid, listed third on the INCI (after propanediol and water), labeled 10%

- Tasmannia Lanceolata Fruit/Leaf Extract (Tasmanian pepperberry)

Base, texture, and preservative system: propanediol and glycerin (humectants), dimethyl isosorbide (a solvent), pentylene glycol, sodium hydroxide (pH adjuster), polysorbate 20 (a surfactant), and ethylhexylglycerin, 1,2-hexanediol, and caprylyl glycol (preservatives).[^1][^2]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by a trial of this product:

- **"A gentle exfoliator, in serum form."** The manufacturer's own product description.[^1] No trial of this leave-on formula measures gentleness against a comparator; the one trial testing mandelic acid alone against lactic acid found irritation more frequent with mandelic acid, not less (see [[mandelic-acid]]).
- **"Helps improve the look of skin clarity by balancing uneven skin tone, and correcting the look and feel of skin texture in a gentle, yet effective manner."** The manufacturer's own product description.[^1] The trials behind mandelic acid's evidence measured acne and hyperpigmentation outcomes from in-office peels at far higher concentrations, not this leave-on serum's texture or clarity (see [[mandelic-acid]]).
- **Targets "Textural Irregularities, Dullness, Uneven Skin Tone" and suited for "All Skin Types."** The manufacturer's own marketing descriptors.[^1]
