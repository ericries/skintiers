PanOxyl Acne Creamy Wash (PanOxyl, made by Crown Laboratories) is an over-the-counter creamy rinse-off wash with benzoyl peroxide at 4%, positioned on its label as "Daily Control" and "formulated to be gentle on skin," sold in 85 g and 6 oz (170 g) tubes.[^1]

> The lower-strength sibling of PanOxyl's 10% foaming wash: 4% benzoyl peroxide in a rinse-off creamy wash. Benzoyl peroxide's own evidence shows lower concentrations match higher ones in efficacy with less irritation, which makes 4% the more defensible strength, but that trial base was built on leave-on gels rather than a wash rinsed off after a minute or two; see [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and [[acne]].

## Summary

PanOxyl Acne Creamy Wash Benzoyl Peroxide 4% is a single-active rinse-off cleanser built around [[benzoyl-peroxide]] at 4%, the "Daily Control" tier of PanOxyl's wash line below the 10% [[panoxyl-acne-foaming-wash-benzoyl-peroxide-10|Acne Foaming Wash]], which its own label positions as "Maximum Strength."[^1][^2] The FDA drug label directs users to wet the area, massage the wash in for 1-2 minutes, rinse and pat dry, starting once daily and increasing to 2-3 times daily as needed, reducing frequency if dryness occurs.[^1]

Benzoyl peroxide is a well-evidenced, resistance-proof first-line acne treatment, but its effect size is modest, and trials find that concentration does not track efficacy: lower strengths perform about as well as higher ones with less irritation, a finding that favors this 4% wash over its 10% sibling; the grade and trial detail are laid out on [[benzoyl-peroxide]]. As with all acne washes, the format is the caveat: this is a rinse-off product, while most of benzoyl peroxide's acne evidence was generated with leave-on gels and creams that stay in contact with the skin ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)).

The [full ingredient list](#whats-in-it) is otherwise a standard cleansing base, near-identical to the 10% foaming wash's: water, mild glucoside surfactants, thickeners, glycerin, and pH adjusters, with no other treatment actives.[^1]

## The Evidence

No trial isolates this product or its wash format. The general evidence for benzoyl peroxide as an acne treatment, including its head-to-head standing against adapalene and topical clindamycin and the finding that higher concentrations are not meaningfully more effective, only more irritating, is graded on [[benzoyl-peroxide]]. That evidence base was generated with leave-on gels and creams; acne washes as a category are noted separately as giving an active less contact time than the leave-on products their labels borrow from, since a wash is rinsed off within minutes rather than left on the skin (see [[acne]]).

Within that caveat, this product's 4% strength is the better-supported end of the trade-off: the concentration-comparison trials summarized on [[benzoyl-peroxide]] found lower strengths matching higher ones on lesion counts with less dryness and irritation, and this label's own "formulated to be gentle on skin" framing tracks that direction, though no comparative irritation trial of this specific wash is cited on its label.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its grade rests on benzoyl peroxide's general first-line acne evidence, discounted for the rinse-off wash format; choosing 4% over 10% is consistent with that evidence rather than a compromise.[^1][^2]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Clears existing acne and helps prevent new breakouts."** Principal display panel (carton) marketing copy, not the label's Drug Facts Use statement, which reads "for the treatment of acne"; the graded clinical evidence for benzoyl peroxide is a modest effect on lesion counts, generated mostly with leave-on formats rather than rinse-off washes.[^1]
- **"Daily Control."** A line-positioning name distinguishing this 4% wash from the brand's "Maximum Strength" 10% wash; no trial comparing the two products is cited on either label.[^1][^2]
- **"Formulated to be gentle on skin."** Directionally consistent with lower benzoyl peroxide concentrations being less irritating, but no comparative irritation study of this product is cited on its label.[^1]
