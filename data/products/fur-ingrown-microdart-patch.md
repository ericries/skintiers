The Fur Ingrown Microdart Patch is a dissolving-microneedle spot patch from [[fur]] for individual ingrown-hair bumps. Each patch has tiny microneedles that dissolve into the skin to deliver its actives, led by [[salicylic-acid]]. Fur lists it at $17.00 for a 6 pack and $32.00 for a 12 pack.[^fur]

> **Bottom line.** A microneedle spot patch that delivers [[salicylic-acid]], a genuine keratolytic for the follicular problem behind ingrown hairs, straight into a single bump, which makes it the best-matched mechanism of Fur's three ingrown products (ahead of the [[fur-ingrown-eliminator-serum|serum]] and the [[fur-ingrown-concentrate|oil]]).[^fur] But the salicylic acid strength is undisclosed, there is no trial of this specific product, its "deeper than any topical" claim is marketing rather than demonstrated, and it treats bumps that have already formed rather than preventing them.

## Summary

Fur markets these as "ingrown hair patches" that "soothe bumps and reduces redness, made for sensitive areas," using "microneedle patch technology to penetrate the skin deeper than any topical formula."[^fur] By the brand's own ingredient list the dissolving matrix is sodium hyaluronate, algin, glycerin, trehalose and propanediol, carrying dipotassium glycyrrhizate (a licorice-derived soother), centella (CICA), and [[salicylic-acid]] as the keratolytic active. Salicylic acid is a beta hydroxy acid that sheds the skin plugging the follicle, which is the mechanism the ingrown-hair evidence supports on [[ingrown-hairs]]. It costs $17.00 for a 6 pack or $32.00 for a 12 pack ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-10-04).[^fur]

## The Evidence

There is no published trial of this specific product, so it is judged on its active and delivery against the ingrown-hair evidence, which favors changing hair removal plus topical keratolytics that shed the follicular plug (set out on [[ingrown-hairs]] and [[pseudofolliculitis-barbae]]). The patch delivers a real keratolytic, [[salicylic-acid]], and its dissolving-microneedle format is a plausible way to get that active into a single raised bump more directly than a cream wiped over the area. The hyaluronic-acid matrix hydrates and the centella and licorice are mild soothers.

The limits are concentration, scope, and the delivery claim. Fur does not disclose the percentage of salicylic acid, and there is no trial showing this patch clears ingrown hairs. The brand's claim that the microneedles "penetrate the skin deeper than any topical formula" is a marketing statement, not a measured result for this product. And a patch is a spot treatment for a bump that has already formed, so it sits alongside, not in place of, the preventive basics (changing the shave or wax and routine exfoliation) that do the most work. Treat it as a reasonable targeted salicylic-acid spot option, better matched to the evidence than the brand's [[fur-ingrown-concentrate|oil]], not a proven or preventive treatment.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms attributed to the brand:

- **"Ingrown hair patches" that "soothe bumps and reduces redness."**[^fur] Plausible for a patch pairing salicylic acid with soothing centella and licorice on an individual bump, but framed as symptom relief on existing bumps, not a measured reduction in ingrown lesions.
- **"Microneedle patch technology to penetrate the skin deeper than any topical formula."**[^fur] A delivery claim; dissolving microneedles can carry actives past the surface, but "deeper than any topical formula" is a comparative marketing statement with no product data behind it here.
- **"Made for sensitive areas."**[^fur] A positioning claim for the bikini line and similar sites; reasonable given the soothing ingredients, but it is the brand's framing rather than a tested low-irritation result.
