The Ordinary Argireline Solution 10% is an inexpensive, water-based single-peptide serum built on Acetyl Hexapeptide-8 (Argireline), marketed as a needle-free way to soften forehead lines, crow's feet, and frown ("eleven") lines, sold at $9.70 for 30 ml.[^to]

> Argireline is the peptide sold as "topical Botox," and the honest evidence is thin: an independent toxicology study found almost none of it gets through the skin's outer layer, and the small trials that exist show only a modest reduction in surface wrinkle depth, not a Botox-like muscle relaxation (see [[argireline-acetyl-hexapeptide-8]]).[^to] The Ordinary's claim of visible improvement "in just 2 weeks" comes from a test the brand ran itself, with no disclosed method or control. Judge this as a cheap, well-tolerated way to try the peptide, with realistic expectations of a subtle effect at most.

## Summary

This is The Ordinary's dedicated Argireline serum: a lightweight, water-based formula whose one active is Acetyl Hexapeptide-8, third on the ingredient list, in a simple propanediol-and-water base.[^to] The brand describes it as "an advanced formulation targeting forehead, 'eleven eye', and smile lines," incorporating "a high concentration of Argireline" to leave skin "smoother, more relaxed-looking."[^to] "Argireline" is a trademarked peptide of Lipotec S.A.U.; the "10%" refers to the concentration of the supplied Argireline solution, not 10% pure peptide.[^to]

The general evidence for the active, including its marketed botulinum-like mechanism and the skin-penetration problem that undercuts it, is set out on [[argireline-acetyl-hexapeptide-8]] and is not repeated here. What that page establishes is the ceiling for this product: a peptide with weak independent evidence and a modest, surface-level wrinkle effect at best.

## The Evidence

No independent, controlled trial of this specific serum was located; its case rests on the Argireline active plus the brand's own testing.

**Dynamic expression lines and wrinkles.** Acetyl Hexapeptide-8 is marketed as a needle-free alternative to injectables, but the independent evidence is weak: a US government toxicology study found almost none of the peptide penetrates past the stratum corneum, leaving the claimed neuromuscular mechanism largely unconfirmed, and small trials report only a modest reduction in surface wrinkle depth (see [[argireline-acetyl-hexapeptide-8]]).[^to] At $9.70 it is one of the cheapest ways to try the ingredient, and the water-based, fragrance- and oil-free formula is well tolerated, but the realistic expectation is subtle smoothing, not the "relaxed" muscles the "topical Botox" framing implies.

> **Bottom line.** A cheap, gentle, single-peptide serum for anyone who wants to try Argireline; the active's own evidence is weak and its skin penetration is the sticking point, so temper expectations accordingly.[^to] The peptide's full evidence picture is on [[argireline-acetyl-hexapeptide-8]].

## What's In It

Full declared ingredient list, verbatim from the brand: "Aqua (Water), Propanediol, Acetyl Hexapeptide-8, Trisodium Ethylenediamine Disuccinate, Gellan Gum, Sodium Chloride, Isoceteth-20, Dimethyl Isosorbide, Potassium Sorbate, Phenoxyethanol, Chlorphenesin."[^to] It is water-based (pH 6.0-7.0), alcohol-free, oil-free, silicone-free, and vegan.[^to] The brand advises against using it in the same routine as direct acids or direct vitamin C, which it says can damage peptide integrity.[^to]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Improvement in just 2 weeks" in crow's feet, forehead, glabellar, and nasolabial-fold lines.** Attributed by the brand to "recent tests of our Argireline Solution 10%," with no disclosed sample size, control arm, or independent publication; an uncontrolled claim the brand made about its own product.[^to]
- **"High concentration of Argireline," "needle-free," "more relaxed-looking skin."** Positioning that leans on the injectable comparison; the independent evidence for a muscle-relaxing effect through intact skin is weak (see [[argireline-acetyl-hexapeptide-8]]).[^to]
