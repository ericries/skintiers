The Neutrogena Light Therapy Acne Mask+ is a wearable full-face LED mask from Johnson & Johnson Consumer, Inc., cleared by the FDA in June 2018 under 510(k) number K180847.[^fda] Its cleared indication, quoted from the FDA's Indications for Use form, is that it "is intended to emit energy in the red and blue region of the spectrum, specifically indicated to treat mild to moderate acne on the face".[^fda] The device category and its evidence are on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

> **Right configuration, no clinical data, and it appears to be off the market.** The mask runs blue at 425-450nm and red at 620-640nm, which is the blue-red pairing that a 2026 GRADE review placed at Level B with a Grade I recommendation, the best grade any home acne device received.[^home][^fda] But the clearance rests on bench testing: the summary states that "The bench performance tests demonstrated that the Light Therapy Acne Mask + is as safe, and as effective as the predicate device", and there is no clinical efficacy study in the file.[^fda] **Separately, this product looks discontinued**, and the recall that is widely reported for it could not be confirmed in any FDA database. What was and was not checked is set out below, because the difference matters.

## Summary

A rechargeable full-face mask worn for a fixed session, "10 minutes/day", with "a continuous working time of 10 minutes" and automatic shut-off, for home use.[^fda] It emits blue 425-450nm and red 620-640nm.[^fda] Its FDA-cleared indication is mild to moderate facial acne only, with no wrinkle or rejuvenation claim, which distinguishes it from the dual-mode [[revive-light-therapy-wrinkle-and-acne-led-device]]. The clearance contains no clinical efficacy study; the evidence that applies to it is category-level evidence about blue and red LED, set out on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate facial acne**
- **Effect size: modest, and the grade belongs to the light rather than to this mask.** Blue 425-450nm plus red 620-640nm is the combination the 2026 GRADE review of home-use devices graded Level B, "moderate quality evidence", with "favorable safety profiles and Grade I recommendations".[^home][^fda] That judgement is about a modality tested across many devices, and nothing in this clearance tests this one.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary for the device, mixed at the category level.** The clearance reports bench performance testing only.[^fda] The countervailing Cochrane picture is on [[home-led-light-therapy]] rather than repeated here.

**Covering the whole face in one session**
- **Effect size: not applicable; this is a form-factor advantage, not an efficacy claim.** A worn mask treats the whole face in a single 10-minute session.[^fda] That is the practical contrast with a contact spot device like the reVive unit, whose cleared treatment window is 7 cm2 per placement. Neither arrangement has device-specific efficacy data here, so this is about convenience and adherence, not results.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable; manufacturer specification from the 510(k) summary.**

## The Evidence

**What the clearance actually establishes.** This is a Traditional 510(k), and its predicate is another company's device: "Pulsaderm Acne Mask 28 (K170111)".[^fda] That is a slightly stronger form of comparison than a manufacturer citing its own earlier model, but it is still a comparison between devices. The summary concludes the mask "does not have any differences in the safety or efficacy as the predicate device (K170111), and therefore the subject and predicate devices are substantially equivalent".[^fda] Equivalence to a predicate is not a demonstration that either works.

**The performance data are bench tests.** The summary's Performance Data section reports consensus-standard testing for basic safety and essential performance, including a collateral standard for the home healthcare environment and one for non-laser light sources, and concludes from bench tests that the device is as safe and as effective as the predicate.[^fda] No human acne trial appears in the file.

**Availability, checked rather than assumed.** Three primary checks were run on 2026-10-16. The openFDA device recall and device enforcement endpoints return no match for this product, searched both by product description and by recalling firm. The openFDA device registration and listing endpoint returns no record tied to K180847. Neutrogena's own site returns 404 at the product URLs tried. Together these are consistent with a product no longer being marketed.

**What could NOT be confirmed, stated plainly.** An eye-safety withdrawal of this mask is widely reported. **No FDA recall or enforcement record for it was found**, so that account is not confirmed here and this page does not assert it. Note what that absence does and does not mean: a voluntary market withdrawal by a company is not necessarily an FDA-classified recall, so a product can leave the market without appearing in those databases. Absence of a record is therefore not evidence that nothing happened. If you own this mask and have a safety question, the manufacturer and the FDA are the right sources, not this page.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"FDA cleared"**. For this device it means the FDA "determined the device is substantially equivalent" to a predicate.[^fda] Clearance is a market-entry decision and the file behind it contains bench tests, not an acne trial.
- **"treat mild to moderate acne on the face"**. The cleared indication verbatim, so legitimate labelling.[^fda] It describes what the device may be sold for, and the qualifier "mild to moderate" is doing real work.
- **Wavelength claims**. Accurate as specifications, and in this case they fall in the better-evidenced blue-red range.[^fda][^home] A wavelength is an engineering fact rather than a clinical outcome.
