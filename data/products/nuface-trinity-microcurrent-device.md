The NuFACE Trinity is a handheld microcurrent device from Carol Cole Company, cleared by the FDA in October 2018 under 510(k) number K181008.[^fda] Its summary describes it plainly: "It produces microcurrent discharged through the two spherical probes".[^fda] The current retail version is the TRINITY+ Starter Kit at $395.00.[^brand] The home device landscape is on [[home-use-skincare-devices-by-evidence]].

> **Read the cleared indication, because it is narrower than anything the marketing says.** K181008 clears the device as "intended for facial and neck stimulation and is indicated for over-the-counter cosmetic use".[^fda] Stimulation, for cosmetic use. Not lifting, not toning, not treating wrinkles or laxity. Compare the LED devices on this site, which are cleared for "the treatment of wrinkles" or "mild to moderate inflammatory acne": those are therapeutic indications, and this is not one. **And microcurrent does not appear anywhere in the 2026 GRADE review that this site's device ladder is built from.**[^home]

## Summary

A microcurrent device applied with two spherical probes, cleared over the counter for facial and neck stimulation as a cosmetic device.[^fda] The clearance rests on the device being "identical to the predicate", and its testing section is headed NON-CLINICAL.[^fda] NuFACE markets it for facial lifting and sculpting, and the site carries both an "FDA CLEARED" and a "CLINICALLY PROVEN" badge.[^brand] No source consulted here grades microcurrent for any skin outcome.

## The Rubric

**Facial and neck stimulation (the FDA-cleared indication)**
- **Effect size: not an efficacy question.** The cleared indication describes what the device does to tissue, not a benefit it produces. A clearance for "stimulation" with "cosmetic use" is a permission to market, and the FDA is explicit in its own letters that clearance is not a determination that a device works.[^fda]
- **Evidence quality: not applicable; this is the regulatory status rather than a finding.**

**Facial lifting, toning or sculpting (the marketed benefit)**
- **Effect size: minimal, and read that as an absence of graded evidence rather than a measured small effect.** The 2026 GRADE scoping review of home-use devices covers seven modalities, naming fractional nonablative lasers, light-emitting diodes, radiofrequency, microneedling, intense pulsed light, heat-based and light-heat energy devices.[^home] **Microcurrent is not among them**, so this site's evidence ladder has no rung for it and this page cannot place the device on that ladder.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal in the sources consulted here.** The clearance offers "Literature provided and usability studies conducted", which is more than the LED devices offered, but literature submitted to support equivalence is not a trial of this device, and a usability study measures whether people can operate a product.[^fda]

## The Evidence

**What the clearance actually rests on.** The summary states that "The NuFACE Trinity device is identical to the predicate which was tested to the applicable standards for electrical safety, EMC, and biocompatibility", and that "Literature provided and usability studies conducted demonstrate that the device is substantially equivalent to the predicate given the new indications".[^fda] The section carrying that is headed NON-CLINICAL TESTING.[^fda] The conclusion is that it is "substantially equivalent to the NuFACE Plus with regards to safety and effectiveness".[^fda] Equivalence to a predecessor, once again, rather than a measurement on skin.

**Where this one does better than its peers.** Carol Cole Company publishes 510(k) **Summaries**. A search of openFDA returned clearances including K072260, K133823, K181008, K201680 and K201906, and every one carries a Summary rather than a Statement.[^openfda] That is the opposite of the pattern on [[foreo-espada-blue-light-pen]], where 8 of 10 clearances publish nothing. You can actually read what NuFACE submitted, which is why this page can tell you it was non-clinical.

**The gap between the badge and the indication.** NuFACE's site displays "FDA CLEARED" and "CLINICALLY PROVEN" together, and files the device under categories including "15-minute Facial-Lift" and "Results - Jawline".[^brand] The clearance supports the first badge and says nothing about the second. A lift and a jawline result are claims about appearance over time; the cleared indication is stimulation. The company may hold studies that speak to this, and the clearance is not where they would appear.

**Successor products.** The device documented here is the Trinity cleared in 2018. What is sold is TRINITY+.[^brand] A 2021 clearance exists under K201906 for "Trinity ELE Plus and Trinity ELE Plus Pro", which may or may not be the same retail product; this page does not assume it is.[^openfda]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"FDA CLEARED"**. Accurate.[^openfda] It means substantial equivalence to an earlier NuFACE device, established without clinical testing.[^fda]
- **"CLINICALLY PROVEN"**. The brand's badge.[^brand] Not evidenced by this clearance, whose own testing section is headed NON-CLINICAL.[^fda]
- **"Facial-Lift", "Facial Sculpting", "Results - Jawline"**. Marketing categories on the brand's site.[^brand] All describe outcomes beyond the cleared indication of stimulation for cosmetic use.[^fda]

## Where to Buy

NuFACE lists the **TRINITY+ Starter Kit at $395.00** on its own site, captured 2026-10-17.[^brand] That is the successor to the device cleared under K181008.
