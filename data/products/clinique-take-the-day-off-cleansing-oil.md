The Clinique Take The Day Off Face Cleansing Oil is the liquid oil-cleanser sibling of the [[clinique-take-the-day-off-cleansing-balm|Take The Day Off balm]]: a fragrance-free ester-oil makeup remover used as a first cleanse. Clinique lists the 6.7 fl oz bottle at $37.00.[^clinique]

> This is a first-step oil cleanser, the same job as the balm sibling in a pourable liquid: emollient esters dissolve long-wearing makeup and water-resistant sunscreen, and PEG- and PPG-type emulsifiers let it rinse off instead of leaving an oily film. The general evidence that an oil-based first cleanse removes what a water wash leaves behind is a single small study, set out on [[double-cleansing]], and no published trial covers this exact product. Its genuine formulation plus is that it is fragrance-free, uncommon in this category.

## Summary

Clinique markets this as an "oil based cleanser that dissolves all traces of makeup and impurities," that "easily glides on and rinses off cleanly leaving no residue" and is "ophthalmologist tested."[^clinique] It is positioned to dissolve long-wearing face, eye, and lip makeup and SPF. The general case for oil cleansing as a first step, and its limits, lives on [[double-cleansing]] and [[best-cleansing-balms-oil-cleansers]]; this page covers what is specific to this product. It ships as a 6.7 fl oz bottle for $37.00 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-30).[^clinique]

## The Evidence

There is no published clinical trial of this specific product. Its job, dissolving oil-based makeup and water-resistant sunscreen as a first cleanse, is the oil-cleansing mechanism whose controlled evidence (a single small study) is set out on [[double-cleansing]]; it cannot exceed what that format has shown. It is a cosmetic cleansing step, not a treatment for any skin condition, and it is designed to be followed by a water-based cleanser or the rest of a routine.

Two things are specific and verifiable here rather than inferred. First, the formula is fragrance-free: no fragrance or parfum appears in the ingredient list, and Clinique's own "Free From" list names fragrance (along with parabens and phthalates) among the excluded ingredients, which is uncommon among cleansing oils and balms and a reasonable point in its favor for reactive or eye-area use.[^clinique] Second, it is an ester-oil cleanser (not a plant-oil one): the base is emollient esters with PEG and PPG emulsifiers that make it rinse off, the same rinse-clean design as the [[clinique-take-the-day-off-cleansing-balm|balm]].[^clinique]

> **Bottom line.** A fragrance-free ester-oil first cleanse (6.7 fl oz, $37.00).[^clinique] It dissolves long-wear makeup and sunscreen like other oil cleansers, on evidence that is a single small study for the format and none for this product; the fragrance-free base is its real distinguishing feature. The general oil-cleansing case is on [[double-cleansing]].

## What's In It

The full ingredient list, from Clinique's own product page: Cetyl Ethylhexanoate, Triethylhexanoin, Polybutene, PEG-20 Glyceryl Triisostearate, PEG-8 Diisostearate, PPG-15 Stearyl Ether, PEG-12 Diisostearate, Water, Tocopheryl Acetate, Glycerin, Caprylyl Glycol, Glyceryl Laurate, BHT.[^clinique]

Notable: the first two entries (cetyl ethylhexanoate and triethylhexanoin) are lightweight emollient esters that do the makeup-dissolving work; the PEG and PPG entries are the emulsifiers that let the oil rinse away with water rather than leaving a film. Tocopheryl acetate (a vitamin E ester) and BHT are antioxidants, glycerin a humectant, and caprylyl glycol a conditioning and preservative-boosting agent. There is no added fragrance.[^clinique] (Incidecoder's archived page for this product, at incidecoder.com/products/clinique-take-the-day-off-cleansing-oil, lists it as discontinued and shows an older formulation that also contained chamomile extract; the list above is the current Clinique formula.)

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms attributed to the brand:

- **"Dissolves all traces of makeup and impurities" / dissolves long-wear makeup and SPF.**[^clinique] This is the oil-cleansing mechanism covered on [[double-cleansing]]; the format's controlled evidence is a single small study, and "all traces" is a marketing absolute rather than a measured result.
- **"Rinses off cleanly leaving no residue."**[^clinique] Consistent with the PEG and PPG emulsifiers in the formula, which are what let an oil cleanser rinse away; residue and feel still vary by user and water.
- **"Ophthalmologist tested."**[^clinique] A testing claim about eye-area tolerability, not an efficacy claim; it is not a published trial reported here.
