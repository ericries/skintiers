The Fur Ingrown Eliminator Serum is a leave-on water-based serum from [[fur]] marketed for ingrown hairs after hair removal. Unlike the brand's oil-based [[fur-ingrown-concentrate]], this one contains actual exfoliating acids, so it is closer to the approach the ingrown-hair evidence supports. Fur lists the 50 ml standard size at $37.00.[^fur]

> **Bottom line.** A $37.00 leave-on serum whose exfoliating acids, lactic acid and willow bark, are the keratolytic mechanism that genuinely helps ingrown hairs, which makes it a better match to the evidence than the brand's [[fur-ingrown-concentrate|oil concentrate]].[^fur] But the acid strengths are undisclosed, there is no trial of this specific product, and its lavender and clary sage oils are fragrance sensitizers on freshly shaved or waxed skin. The keratolytic comparison on this site is [[tend-skin-the-skin-care-solution]].

## Summary

Fur markets this as an "ingrown hair treatment" and a "serum for ingrown hairs" that targets ingrowns "Leaving your skin clear and smooth," positioned as "Ideal for post-hair removal for face, body, and sensitive areas."[^fur] By Incidecoder's decoded list the formula is water-based, with witch hazel extract, willow bark extract, and aloe high in the list, then [[lactic-acid]] and phytic acid as the exfoliating acids, in a glycerin and polyglyceryl base, finished with lavender and clary sage oils. Lactic acid is an AHA and willow bark is a natural source of salicylates (the family that includes [[salicylic-acid]]), so unlike the brand's oil this serum does carry a keratolytic mechanism. It costs $37.00 for the 50 ml size ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-10-03).[^fur]

## The Evidence

There is no published trial of this specific product, so it is judged on its actives and how they map to the ingrown-hair evidence. That evidence favors two things: changing how you remove the hair, and topical keratolytics (exfoliating acids or retinoids) that shed the skin around the follicle, both set out on [[ingrown-hairs]] and [[pseudofolliculitis-barbae]]. This serum delivers that mechanism where the brand's [[fur-ingrown-concentrate]] did not: it contains [[lactic-acid]], an alpha hydroxy acid, plus willow bark extract, a natural salicylate related to [[salicylic-acid]], and phytic acid, a milder acid.

The limits are concentration and format. Fur does not disclose the percentage of lactic acid or the salicin content of the willow bark, and lactic acid sits fifth on the ingredient list, after water, witch hazel, willow bark, and aloe, so it is present at a modest level rather than the higher strengths studied for exfoliation. Witch hazel is an astringent, not a treatment active. The serum also contains lavender oil, clary sage oil, and the fragrance markers linalool, limonene, and geraniol, which are common contact sensitizers and sit on skin that hair removal has already compromised. Treat it as a reasonable keratolytic-containing option for ingrowns, better matched to the evidence than an oil, but not a proven or high-strength treatment.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms attributed to the brand:

- **"Ingrown hair treatment" / "serum for ingrown hairs."**[^fur] The exfoliating acids give this a plausible mechanism for ingrowns, unlike a plain oil, but with undisclosed strengths and no product trial, "treatment" overstates what is demonstrated; the realistic benefit is gentle exfoliation around the follicle (see [[ingrown-hairs]]).
- **"Leaving your skin clear and smooth."**[^fur] A cosmetic smoothness and clarity claim, consistent with a mild leave-on exfoliating acid, not a measured reduction in ingrown lesions.
- **"Ideal for post-hair removal for face, body, and sensitive areas."**[^fur] A positioning claim; the lavender and clary sage essential oils are plausible sensitizers, so "ideal for sensitive areas" is the brand's framing rather than a low-irritation guarantee.
