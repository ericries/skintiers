Revitalift Derm Intensives Night Serum, 0.3% Pure Retinol is a mass-market leave-on retinol serum from L'Oreal Paris, sold through the brand's own site and at Walmart.[^loreal][^walmart]

> Retinol has vehicle-controlled trial evidence for fine wrinkles and pigment in aging skin at concentrations of 0.1% to 0.4% (see [[retinol]]), and this serum states 0.3% on its label and product name, near the top of that range, a disclosure many mass-market OTC retinol serums skip.[^loreal] The manufacturer's own supporting claim, that "100% of women" in a 12-week study showed reduced wrinkles, is not cited to any published journal, sample size, or control arm, detailed in [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^loreal] At $39.99 for 1 fl oz from the manufacturer, discounted to $29.88 at Walmart for the same size, it is mid-priced for a single-form OTC retinol serum.[^loreal][^walmart]

## Summary

Revitalift Derm Intensives Night Serum, 0.3% Pure Retinol is a 1 fl oz night serum sold at $39.99 direct from L'Oreal Paris's US site, and for $29.88 (discounted from $39.99) at Walmart for the same SKU.[^loreal][^walmart] The declared ingredient list names the active simply as "Retinol," and both the product name and the manufacturer's marketing state its concentration directly: "0.3% Pure Retinol," described as "our most potent retinol serum" and holding "our...highest concentration of Pure Retinol."[^loreal] Retinol sits 14th of 23 ingredients on the full declaration.[^loreal]

Topical [[retinol]], a vitamin A precursor, has vehicle-controlled trial evidence for improving fine wrinkles and pigment in photoaged and naturally aged skin, graded modest in effect size with solid evidence for an over-the-counter retinoid on its own page. The trials behind that grade tested concentrations from 0.1% to 0.4%; 0.3% sits near the top of that range and is stated on the label, a distinguishing point against mass-market competitors that name "Retinol" as an active without disclosing a percentage, such as [[naturium-retinol-complex-serum|Naturium's Retinol Complex Serum]].

The formula also contains glycerin and sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]), which the manufacturer says help "balance the potential drying effects of retinol," while tocopherol ([[tocopherol|vitamin E]]) provides antioxidant support.[^loreal] The brand's own directions describe introducing the serum gradually: "Use two times a week at first and slowly move to nightly use," applied "after cleansing" and followed by moisturizer, with a morning broad-spectrum SPF because "retinol increases skin's photosensitivity."[^loreal]

The brand's "Derm Intensives" marketing quotes a named board-certified dermatologist, Dr. Melissa K. Levin, MD: "I recommend Pure Retinol to help reduce wrinkles and exfoliate, improving skin's texture and tone. Start with 0.3% and let your skin acclimate."[^loreal] The manufacturer's own headline efficacy claim, that "in a clinical study, 100% of women showed a reduction in wrinkles, even deep ones," rests on an unpublished study, detailed in [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^loreal]

## The Evidence

Topical [[retinol]] is a well-studied vitamin A precursor with vehicle-controlled trial evidence for improving fine wrinkles and pigment in aging skin, at tested concentrations of 0.1% to 0.4%; that evidence is graded in full on its own page. What is specific to this product is that the manufacturer discloses a 0.3% concentration directly on the label and product name, near the top of that studied range, unlike many mass-market competitors that list "Retinol" without a percentage.[^loreal] A Walmart listing for the same SKU independently confirms the product name and its stated 0.3% concentration, along with the $39.99 list price before Walmart's own discount.[^walmart]

No independent, peer-reviewed trial of this specific serum was found. The manufacturer attributes its own supporting claim only to "a 12-week study of women aged 35-75 when used as directed," reporting that "100% of women showed reduction in wrinkles, even deep ones," with no sample size, control or vehicle arm, statistical result, or peer review disclosed.[^loreal] The brand separately states that "a panel of dermatologists validated that Derm Intensives serums uphold strict formulation standards, independent clinical results and protocols," without naming the panel or publishing its findings.[^loreal]

> Bottom line. Retinol has real category-level evidence for fine wrinkles and pigment (see [[retinol]]), and this serum's disclosed 0.3% concentration falls within the range those trials tested, unlike undisclosed-concentration competitors. The manufacturer's own product-specific claim, however, rests on an unpublished 12-week study with no sample size, control arm, or peer review, so it is not an independently verifiable trial.[^loreal]
