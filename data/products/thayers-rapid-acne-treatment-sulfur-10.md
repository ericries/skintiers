## Summary

An over-the-counter acne treatment whose active is **sulfur 10%**, which makes it the odd one out in its own brand.[^label] **Bottom line:** the sulfur is at the top of the strength range filed for acne, and the evidence for sulfur is on [[sulfur]], graded mid. The more interesting thing is further down the label, where two ingredients this site grades as actives are printed under the heading "Inactive ingredients".

> **Every other Thayers acne product is salicylic acid; this one is sulfur.** A search of current US drug labels under the Thayers brand returns seven records. Six declare salicylic acid at 1.5% or 2%. This is the only one with "Active ingredient Sulfur 10%".[^label] Its own inactive ingredient list contains no witch hazel either, in a range where four of the seven records carry "Witch Hazel Aloe Vera Formula" in the product name.[^label]

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: modest, inherited.** The Purpose line is "Acne treatment" and the Uses line is "Use for the treatment of acne".[^label] What sulfur does for acne is argued on [[sulfur]] and is not repeated here.
- **10% is the top of the filed range.** Across fifty current sulfur acne labels returned by the FDA label index, the declared strengths run from 3% to 10%, with nothing higher and 10% the most commonly filed.[^survey] So this is a full-strength sulfur product rather than a token inclusion.
- **Record finding: the label's "inactive" list contains two graded actives.** Niacinamide is the second inactive ingredient and glycolic acid the fourth, ahead of most of the formula.[^label] Both have pages here and both are graded **mid**: [[niacinamide]] and [[glycolic-acid]]. A Drug Facts panel can only name the monograph ingredient as active, so everything else is filed as inactive whatever it does. Reading that word as "does nothing" is the mistake the format invites.
- **The directions are unusually careful, and worth following.** The label says to "start with one application daily, then gradually increase to two or three times daily if needed", and that "if bothersome dryness or peeling occurs, reduce application to once a day or every other day".[^label] Sulfur at 10% is drying, and the titration instruction is the manufacturer saying so.
- **Who makes it.** The labeller of record is L'Oreal USA Products Inc.[^label] The brand line reads "Natural Remedies Since 1847"; the corporate owner is current.

## The Evidence

None specific to this product. Sulfur's trials, including the rosacea comparison against metronidazole and the acne evidence review, are set out on [[sulfur]]. The other graded ingredients in the formula have their own pages at [[niacinamide]] and [[glycolic-acid]], and the minor botanicals at [[licorice-root-extract]] and [[allantoin]], both graded weak.

What this page adds is the label reading. A shopper comparing Thayers acne products would reasonably assume they share an active, and they do not.

> **Bottom line.** A full-strength sulfur treatment wearing a brand built on witch hazel, with two well-graded actives hidden in the inactive list by the rules of the format. Buy it for the sulfur, start once a day, and do not read "inactive" as "inert".
