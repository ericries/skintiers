EltaMD UV Physical Broad-Spectrum SPF 41 is a 100% mineral, lightly tinted facial sunscreen cream, combining zinc oxide 9.0% and titanium dioxide 7.0% with an iron-oxide tint and an antioxidant blend, sold by EltaMD as an OTC drug under the FDA's sunscreen monograph.[^dailymed][^brand] It is EltaMD's mineral-only tinted option, positioned against its own chemical-mineral hybrid tinted sunscreens such as [[eltamd-uv-daily-tinted-broad-spectrum-spf-40|UV Daily Tinted SPF 40]] and its higher-SPF sibling [[eltamd-uv-elements-tinted-mineral-sunscreen-spf-44|UV Elements SPF 44]] (now [[eltamd-uv-aox-elements-tinted-mineral-spf-50|UV AOX Elements SPF 50]]).[^dailymed-daily]

> A genuinely mineral-only Broad Spectrum SPF 41, no octinoxate or other chemical filter, built on zinc oxide and titanium dioxide, both well-established UVA/UVB filters (see [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]).[^dailymed] The iron-oxide tint plausibly adds some visible-light protection relevant to [[melasma]] and hyperpigmentation, but neither the FDA label nor the brand discloses an iron oxide percentage, so that part of the claim rests on the ingredient class, not a tested dose in this specific product.[^dailymed][^brand]

## Summary

UV Physical is an 85 g (3 oz) tinted cream that EltaMD lists at $48.00 on its own site, described there as "an all mineral sunscreen formulated for sensitive & oily to normal skin," "lightly tinted to even skin tone," that "can be used alone or under makeup" and is "fragrance-free, paraben-free, hypoallergenic, and non-comedogenic."[^brand] It is one of two tint options in EltaMD's Tinted Sunscreen line built on this same mineral base, alongside its companion UV Physical Broad Spectrum SPF 40 - Rosy Tint.[^brand]

The FDA Drug Facts label confirms the mineral-only formulation, active ingredients zinc oxide 9.0% and titanium dioxide 7.0%, tested to Broad Spectrum SPF 41.[^dailymed] That distinguishes it from EltaMD's chemical-mineral hybrid tinted products, such as [[eltamd-uv-daily-tinted-broad-spectrum-spf-40|UV Daily Tinted SPF 40]], which pairs zinc oxide with the chemical filter octinoxate.[^dailymed-daily] The current FDA-listed NDC packaging for UV Physical, an 85 g tube and a 2 g packet, has a marketing start date of August 5, 2024.[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

The core, well-established health benefit is broad-spectrum photoprotection: as a 100% mineral sunscreen with zinc oxide 9.0% and titanium dioxide 7.0% as its declared active ingredients, tested to Broad Spectrum SPF 41, it protects against sunburn and, per the FDA-required label language, "if used as directed with other sun protection measures, decreases the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging caused by the sun."[^dailymed] No published trial tests this exact product; the case for the filters rests on the general mineral-filter evidence on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] and [[zinc-oxide]].

The tinted, iron-oxide component is a separate and more modest claim. Iron oxides, listed last in the FDA label's inactive-ingredient list, absorb visible light in a way UV-only filters do not, and that property is relevant to pigmentation-prone skin because visible light can worsen [[melasma]] and post-inflammatory [[hyperpigmentation]] even when a non-tinted broad-spectrum sunscreen is used correctly ([[sunscreen-uv-filters]]). Neither the FDA label nor EltaMD's own product page discloses an iron oxide percentage, so that benefit is graded modest and preliminary here, resting on the ingredient class rather than a tested dose in this specific product.[^dailymed][^brand]

## What's In It

- **Active ingredients (FDA Drug Facts label):** "Zinc Oxide 9.0% Sunscreen" and "Titanium Dioxide 7.0% Sunscreen."[^dailymed]
- **Purpose:** "Sunscreen."[^dailymed]
- **Uses:** "Helps prevent sunburn. If used as directed with other sun protection measure (See Directions), decreases the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging caused by the sun."[^dailymed]
- **Directions:** "Apply liberally 15 minutes before sun exposure. Reapply after 40 minutes of swimming or sweating, immediately after towel drying and at least every 2 hours."[^dailymed]
- **Inactive ingredients (verbatim from the FDA label):** water, isopropyl palmitate, ethylhexyl stearate, octyldodecyl neopentanoate, alumina, polyacrylate-13, phenoxyethanol, hydrogen dimethicone, polyisobutene, hydrogenated polyisobutene, triethoxycaprylylsilane, oleth-3 phosphate, tocopheryl acetate, bisabolol, tocopherol, carnosine, ethylhexylglycerin, sorbitan isostearate, polysorbate 20, zingiber officinale (ginger) root extract, iron oxides.[^dailymed] The [[iron-oxides|iron oxides]] supply the tint; tocopheryl acetate and [[tocopherol]] are the antioxidant pairing the brand markets, and ginger root extract and bisabolol are marketed as skin-calming additions.[^brand]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"An all mineral sunscreen formulated for sensitive & oily to normal skin... lightly tinted to even skin tone."**[^brand] The mineral-only, chemical-free filter combination is confirmed by the FDA-listed actives; the "sensitive & oily" skin-type positioning and "even skin tone" tint effect are the brand's own framing, not tied to a specific irritation or colorimetry trial in the sources reviewed here.
- **"Contains antioxidants to help protect your skin from environmental stressors and improve the visible signs of aging."**[^brand] The product contains tocopheryl acetate and tocopherol, general antioxidants whose broader evidence is graded independently on [[tocopherol]]; this product did not disclose a concentration for either, so the claim is not tested at a product-specific dose.
- **Iron oxides "provide protection against blue light, and can help prevent the appearance of dark spots."**[^brand] Iron oxides' general visible-light absorption is a real property of the ingredient class ([[sunscreen-uv-filters]]), but the brand does not disclose an iron oxide percentage for this product, so the strength of that protection here is unverified.
