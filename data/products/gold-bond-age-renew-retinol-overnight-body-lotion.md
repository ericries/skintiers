Gold Bond Age Renew Retinol Overnight Body & Face Lotion is a drugstore night lotion built around an undisclosed level of stabilized [[retinol]] and a peptide complex, sold in a 7 oz tube (also a 13 oz Walmart-exclusive bottle) and marketed to firm and smooth aging body and face skin overnight.[^1][^2]

> A body-and-face retinol lotion that names its headline actives but not their strength: "stabilized Retinol" and a "Retinol and Peptide Complex" appear with no printed percentage on either the brand's own page or a retailer listing.[^1][^2] The general case for topical retinol, real but modest, vehicle-controlled evidence for fine lines, texture, and pigment in aging skin, is set out on [[retinol]]; part of that evidence base was generated on body (inner-arm) skin, which fits this product's body-lotion format better than most facial retinol serums do. No trial isolates this product, and its exact retinol level cannot be checked against the concentrations that evidence used.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Age Renew Retinol Overnight Body & Face Lotion is Gold Bond's retinol-and-peptide night lotion: a long, 40-plus-ingredient formula led by water, humectants, and shea butter, with [[retinol]] appearing roughly two-thirds of the way down the list and palmitoyl hexapeptide-12 shortly after it.[^1][^2] The general case for topical retinol, modest but real vehicle-controlled evidence for fine lines, texture, and pigment in aging skin, drawn largely from small trials, is set out on [[retinol]]. Part of that evidence was built on sun-protected inner-arm (body) skin, a closer match to this product's body-lotion format than the facial serums most retinol evidence comes from.[^1]

Neither Gold Bond's own product page nor a Target retailer listing prints a percentage for the retinol or the peptide complex, so this product cannot be placed against the specific concentrations (0.1% to 0.4%) that the cited retinol trials tested ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1][^2] No published, independent trial of this exact formula was found.

The brand's own claims, "clinically shown to reveal smoother looking skin overnight" and "clinically shown to firm the look of skin in one week," are company-described tests without a linked published study, methodology, or sample size; they and the rest of the label's language are weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1][^2] It is listed at $11.99 for the 7 oz tube on Target's site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-15).[^2]

## The Evidence

Topical retinol has genuine, if modest, vehicle-controlled trial evidence for improving fine wrinkles, texture, and pigment in aging skin; the full rubric, its numbers, and its limits are set out on [[retinol]]. Part of that evidence comes from a 0.4% retinol trial on sun-protected inner-arm (body) skin, one of two vehicle-controlled trials the ingredient page draws on, which is a closer match to this body lotion's format than the facial-skin trial most retinol evidence uses.[^1][^2]

Neither Gold Bond's brand page nor the retailer listing states what percentage of retinol or palmitoyl hexapeptide-12 the lotion contains; both describe it only as "stabilized Retinol" and a "Retinol and Peptide Complex."[^1][^2] Retinol sits 18th in a roughly 45-ingredient list, after the base, humectants, and several emollients but before other minor-percentage ingredients like propylene glycol and citric acid; INCI order past the first several ingredients is not a reliable percentage guide, so no concentration estimate is offered here.[^1][^2]

The peptide ingredient, palmitoyl hexapeptide-12, is a peptide of the general signal-peptide type covered on [[peptides]], where the most-cited controlled trial (for a related peptide, pal-KTTKS) was industry-run, and an independent systematic review of the wider peptide literature found it sparse, with only six of fifteen studies placebo-controlled.[^3]

> **Bottom line.** The retinol grade rests on retinol's general, modest, vehicle-controlled case, part of it from body-skin trials, at concentrations this product does not disclose; the peptide grade rests on a wider, sparser, mostly industry-run peptide literature and an unnamed concentration of palmitoyl hexapeptide-12.[^1][^2][^3]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as it appears on Gold Bond's own product page and independently on a Target retailer listing, which match.[^1][^2] The actives with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

Actives and notable functional ingredients, in INCI order:

- [[niacinamide]], 7th on the list
- selaginella lepidophylla (resurrection plant) extract, an antioxidant/humectant marketing ingredient
- squalene, an emollient
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant
- [[retinol]], unpercentaged, "stabilized" per the brand
- sodium ascorbate, a vitamin C salt
- palmitoyl hexapeptide-12, an unpercentaged signal peptide (on [[peptides]])
- watermelon, apple, and lentil fruit extracts, minor botanical additions
- [[panthenol]] (provitamin B5), a humectant/soothing agent
- ceramide NG, a barrier lipid, near the end of the list

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, hydroxyethyl urea and glycerin (humectants), shea butter extract and dimethicone (emollients), stearyl/cetyl/cetearyl alcohol and glyceryl stearate (emulsifiers/thickeners), steareth-21, jojoba esters, jojoba oil/macadamia seed oil esters, phytosteryl macadamiate, phytosterols, caprylic/capric triglyceride, cocoa seed butter, and C12-15 alkyl benzoate (emollients), polysorbate 60, tribehenin, and PEG-10 phytosterol (emulsifiers), sodium lactate and sodium PCA (humectants), propylene glycol and butylene glycol (solvents), hydroxyacetophenone and ethylhexylglycerin (preservative boosters), behentrimonium methosulfate, stearamidopropyl PG-dimonium chloride phosphate, and guar hydroxypropyltrimonium chloride (conditioning agents), polyacrylate crosspolymer-6 (thickener), disodium EDTA (chelator), citric acid (pH), and caramel (colorant).[^1][^2]

The product is fragrance-free per both sources, and the brand describes it as free of parabens, phthalates, and dyes.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Clinically shown to reveal smoother looking skin overnight" / "clinically shown to firm the look of skin in one week."** Company-described results on the brand and retailer pages, with no linked published trial, sample size, control arm, or peer review.[^1][^2]
- **"The most clinically tested anti-aging ingredient" (of retinol).** Retinol does have real trial evidence, set out on [[retinol]], but that evidence was generated at specific disclosed concentrations (0.1% to 0.4%); this product's own undisclosed level cannot be checked against it.[^1][^2]
- **"Non-drying, non-irritating retinol formula."** A brand description, not a published tolerability trial result for this product.[^1]
- **"Dermatologist-tested," "hypoallergenic," "non-comedogenic."** Manufacturer testing claims for which no methodology, sample, or result is published on either source.[^1]
