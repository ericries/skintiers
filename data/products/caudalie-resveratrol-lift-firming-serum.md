Caudalie Resveratrol-Lift Instant Firming Serum is the French brand's peptide-forward "retinol alternative" firming serum, built around grape-derived extracts and a multi-peptide blend in a squalane and hyaluronic-acid base, sold in a single 30 mL (1 fl oz) bottle for $84.[^1]

> Despite the "Resveratrol-Lift" line name, no ingredient named resveratrol appears in the declared INCI list; the grape-derived actives are Palmitoyl Grapevine Shoot Extract and Vitis Vinifera (Grape) Seed Extract, both proprietary to Caudalie and without independent published trials of their own.[^1] The serum's individually evidenced ingredients, peptides, adenosine, and hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid, sit in categories whose general evidence is modest and mostly preliminary for the first two and solid for hyaluronic acid's hydration effect; nothing published tests this specific 36-ingredient formula, and the brand's headline "3x more firming than retinol" claim rests on a company-run study that is not linked, sized, or peer reviewed on the product page.[^1]

## Summary

Resveratrol-Lift Instant Firming Serum is marketed as a "retinol alternative," but its declared ingredient list contains no retinol, retinoid, or other retinol-alternative active with independent evidence, such as [[bakuchiol]]; the comparison rests on the brand's own unpublished testing.[^1] What the formula does contain, by INCI order, is a base of water, glycerin, squalane, and tocopheryl acetate, then a proprietary Palmitoyl Grapevine Shoot Extract, then, after the fragrance/sub-1% marker, hydrolyzed [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]], Vitis Vinifera (Grape) Seed Extract, [[adenosine]], and acetyl dipeptide-1 cetyl ester, with fragrance and several EU-declared fragrance allergens (linalool, limonene, coumarin, citrus peel oil) also present, and tripeptide-1 and hexapeptide-9 named dead last of the 36 declared ingredients.[^1]

Of the ingredients with independent, product-agnostic evidence, [[peptides]] and [[adenosine]] carry a modest, mostly preliminary, mostly small-trial case for firming and wrinkle appearance, well below prescription retinoids; hydrolyzed [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]], the kind used here, has a solidly evidenced but purely cosmetic-to-barrier hydration benefit and a smaller wrinkle-depth effect in small trials ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)). No published trial isolates this exact 36-ingredient formula or its named peptides, tripeptide-1 and hexapeptide-9, which do not match the specific peptide classes (signal, neurotransmitter-inhibiting, or copper-carrier) that have their own dedicated human trials.

The brand's own claims, "3x more firming than retinol," over "500 pro-collagen grape multi-peptides," and "100% saw firmer, younger, bouncier-looking skin after 1 bottle," are company-run findings not tied to any published, peer-reviewed trial; they are weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1] It is listed at $84 for the single 30 mL size on Caudalie's own US site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-15).[^1]

## The Evidence

No published trial was found that tests this product, its exact 36-ingredient formula, or its proprietary Palmitoyl Grapevine Shoot Extract; the grades above rest on the general, category-level evidence for the ingredients here that have their own independent evidence base.[^1]

Cosmetic peptides are graded by class on [[peptides]], because evidence differs sharply between them: signal peptides (Matrixyl-type) have a small, mostly manufacturer-run controlled trial behind them; neurotransmitter-inhibiting peptides (Argireline-type) have thin independent evidence undercut by poor skin penetration; copper-carrier peptides rest mainly on lab and wound-healing work, not cosmetic trials. This product's peptides, tripeptide-1, hexapeptide-9, and acetyl dipeptide-1 cetyl ester, are not the specific molecules behind any of those dedicated trials, so no class-specific finding transfers directly; the general framing on [[peptides]], a modest and mostly preliminary cosmetic effect overall, is the closest applicable evidence. Adenosine's case is set out on [[adenosine]]: a handful of small trials, several run or funded by the companies selling the delivery system tested, reporting statistically significant but modest wrinkle-appearance improvement.

Hyaluronic acid's evidence, on [[hyaluronic-acid]], depends on molecular weight: only lower-molecular-weight forms cross the outermost skin layer and have shown a wrinkle-depth reduction in small trials, while all molecular weights raise a skin-hydration reading against placebo. This product declares Hydrolyzed Hyaluronic Acid and Hydrolyzed Sodium Hyaluronate, both processed to a lower molecular weight, which is the form with the better-evidenced case, though the product does not disclose a specific molecular weight or concentration.[^1]

Against other peptide-forward firming serums: [[skinmedica-tns-advanced-serum|SkinMedica TNS Advanced+ Serum]] is likewise graded on category evidence rather than a product-specific trial, since its central growth-factor active has no independent trial of the finished serum either, so neither product clears the bar of published, product-specific evidence. [[the-ordinary-multi-peptide-copper-peptides-serum|The Ordinary Multi-Peptide + Copper Peptides Serum]] differs in one respect: its own product name discloses a headline concentration figure and names copper tripeptide-1 (GHK-Cu) as its specific differentiating active, where this Caudalie serum discloses no concentration for any of its three peptides; that naming does not make The Ordinary's copper-peptide claim evidenced, since the one randomized trial of topical GHK-Cu found no objective benefit over control, but it puts more of what a buyer is paying for on the label.

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its peptides and adenosine carry modest, mostly preliminary, mostly small-trial evidence for firming and wrinkle appearance; its hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid carries solid evidence for hydration and a smaller wrinkle-depth effect. The "retinol alternative" and "3x more firming than retinol" positioning is not supported by any retinoid-comparable ingredient in the declared list, and rests on an unpublished company-run study.[^1]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on the manufacturer's product page, in order.[^1] The actives with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest is the base, texture, preservative, and fragrance system.

Actives (grouped by role, not INCI position; see per-item notes below for each ingredient's actual list position):

- Palmitoyl Grapevine Shoot Extract, a proprietary grape-derived extract tied to the "Resveratrol-Lift" branding; no dedicated independent evidence page exists for it here
- [[squalane]], an emollient, also listed near the top of the ingredient list
- tocopheryl acetate, an ester form of vitamin E ([[tocopherol]]), used as an antioxidant and emollient rather than free tocopherol
- [[glycerin]], a humectant, listed third overall
- Vitis Vinifera (Grape) Seed Extract, a grape-derived antioxidant extract, listed after the fragrance (Parfum), the point at which EU convention allows sub-1% ingredients to appear in any order
- hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid and hydrolyzed sodium hyaluronate, lower-molecular-weight forms of [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]]
- [[adenosine]], listed after the fragrance/sub-1% marker
- acetyl dipeptide-1 cetyl ester, tripeptide-1, and hexapeptide-9, three named [[peptides]], all listed after the fragrance/sub-1% marker (hexapeptide-9 is last of 36 ingredients)

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, butylene glycol, hexyldecanol, hexyldecyl laurate, C20-22 alkyl phosphate, C20-22 alcohols, polyacrylate crosspolymer-6, potassium cetyl phosphate, sunflower seed oil, caprylyl glycol, pentylene glycol, sodium dehydroacetate, maltodextrin, cellulose gum, levulinic acid, sodium phytate, and sodium hydroxide.[^1]

Fragrance: Parfum (Fragrance), plus seven separately declared fragrance components, linalyl acetate, tetramethyl acetyloctahydronaphthalenes (a synthetic musk), linalool, lavender oil/extract, limonene, coumarin, and citrus aurantium peel oil, several of which are on the EU's declared-allergen list.[^1]

The ingredient list runs to 36 ingredients total, longer than a typical single-active serum, and includes added fragrance and several fragrance allergens.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"3x more firming than retinol & non-irritating."** The product page attributes this to Caudalie's own testing, without a linked published trial, sample size, control arm, or peer review, and the ingredient list contains no retinol or other retinoid to compare against directly.[^1]
- **"Over 500 pro-collagen grape multi-peptides."** This describes the proprietary composition of the Palmitoyl Grapevine Shoot Extract complex, not 500 separately declared INCI ingredients; only three individual peptides (acetyl dipeptide-1 cetyl ester, tripeptide-1, hexapeptide-9) are named on the label.[^1]
- **"Targets 20 types of collagen essential to maintaining skin structure."** A mechanistic positioning claim with no linked study, sample, or methodology on the product page.[^1]
- **"100% saw firmer, younger, bouncier-looking skin after 1 bottle."** A self-reported consumer-perception result described on the product page without sample size, duration, or controls disclosed.[^1]
- **"Retinol Alternative" (product name).** No ingredient with independent evidence as a retinol substitute, such as [[bakuchiol]], appears in the declared list; the comparison rests on the brand's own unpublished study above.[^1]
