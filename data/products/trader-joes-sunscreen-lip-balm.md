A four-filter organic sunscreen in a lip balm, and the one thing a sunscreen page normally leads with is missing. **Bottom line:** the actives and their percentages are on the public record, the SPF value is not, and there is a second Trader Joe's lip balm that *is* labelled SPF 30. Lip-specific advice is on [[lip-care]].

> **The label never says what the SPF is.** Checked across the whole SPL, the only SPF text is the generic Sun Protection Measures boilerplate telling you to use "a sunscreen with a Broad Spectrum SPF value of 15 or higher", which is advice about sunscreens in general rather than a statement about this one.[^label] Its own SPF appears nowhere, and openFDA lists it simply as "Trader Joes Sunscreen Lip Balm" with no number in the name.[^openfda]

## Summary

Active ingredients, verbatim: "Avobenzone 3% Homosalate 7% Octisalate 4.5% Octocrylene 9%", purpose "Sunscreen".[^label] Listed as NDC 87967-001, and worth noting the labeler of record is Weckerle Cosmetics rather than Trader Joe's Company, which is who most of the chain's other sunscreens are filed under.[^openfda]

**There are two Trader Joe's SPF lip products and only one of them states a number.** Alongside this one, the FDA record carries "TRADER JOES LIP BALM SPF 30", a lipstick-form product filed under Trader Joe's Company with the same four actives.[^openfda] They are not the same filing, so an SPF 30 figure seen online for one of them should not be assumed to apply to the other.

**The formula.** Inactive ingredients in label order: beeswax, octyldodecanol, butyloctyl salicylate, jojoba seed oil, octyldodecyl neopentanoate, pomegranate sterols, caprylic/capric triglyceride, flavor (aroma), sodium hyaluronate, raspberry seed oil, tocopherol, limonene, tocopheryl acetate, hydrogenated castor oil and castor seed oil.[^label] Butyloctyl salicylate is in there as a solubiliser, mainly to keep avobenzone dissolved, and despite the salicylate name it is not one of the four UV filters.

**No price is quoted here.** Trader Joe's does not run an online storefront, no retailer was consulted and no paid scraping service was used.

## The Rubric

**Sunburn prevention on the lips**
Graded modest on solid evidence. The label carries the monograph wording in full, that it "Helps prevent sunburn" and, used as directed with other measures, "decreases the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging caused by the sun".[^label] Four organic filters at those percentages is a serious filter load, and [[avobenzone]], [[homosalate]], [[octisalate]] and [[octocrylene]] each have their own case. The grade stops at modest because the SPF is unpublished: the claim is label-backed, the magnitude is not checkable.

**Staying on through swimming, sweating or drinking**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence, and this is the row worth reading twice. The label never uses the word resistant at all.[^label] The Directions still say to reapply "after 40 minutes of swimming or sweating", which is the monograph's standard reapplication instruction rather than a durability claim.[^label] On a lip product that distinction matters more than elsewhere, because eating and drinking remove it.

**Lip conditioning and hydration**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. Beeswax, jojoba, castor and raspberry seed oils and sodium hyaluronate are a conventional balm base, and the Drug Facts panel makes no conditioning or hydration claim and publishes no concentration.[^label]

## The Evidence

**It is flagged flammable, which is unusual on a lip balm.** The Warnings read "For external use only. Flammable. Keep away from heat or flame."[^label] That follows from a wax-and-oil stick rather than from anything exotic, and it is on the label, so it belongs on the page.

**It contains fragrance material, on a product you will swallow a little of.** The printed inactive list carries both "FLAVOR (AROMA)" and "LIMONENE", and the SPL's structured ingredient data goes further than the printed panel does, naming two limonene stereoisomers where the panel names limonene once.[^label] Limonene is a recognised contact allergen, and fragrance is the most common allergen in skincare, a point made on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]] and across the site's sensitive-skin coverage. This is a tolerance consideration rather than a safety alarm, and it is a real reason someone with perioral or lip dermatitis might pick something else; see [[lip-care]].

**What the label does not claim.** Checked by reading the Drug Facts panel in full: no SPF value, no water resistance, no broad spectrum claim about itself, no antioxidant claim despite tocopherol being present, and no hydration claim despite the hyaluronate.[^label] For a product whose entire category is built on a number, the absence of that number is the most informative thing on the label.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. The lips burn easily and are a site where skin cancer does occur, so reapplication matters more than the exact SPF of any one balm. A sore or scaly patch on the lip that does not heal is worth a doctor's look rather than more balm, which is the case [[actinic-cheilitis]] makes.

## Common Marketing Claims

**No brand marketing copy is quoted here.** Trader Joe's publishes no online product page for this item, so the only claims available are the regulated Drug Facts panel ones assessed above, and nothing is placed in quotation marks and attributed to the chain.

**"Sunscreen Lip Balm", as the product name.** Accurate as far as it goes: it is a sunscreen, and it is a lip balm. The name is also where a shopper would expect the SPF to be, and it is not there.[^openfda]
