Anthelios Mineral Tinted Sunscreen for Face is a 100% mineral face sunscreen from [[la-roche-posay]], sold in four shades (Tinted, Medium, Medium-Deep, Deep).[^1] The base "Tinted" shade carries titanium dioxide 11% as its sole active and is rated Broad Spectrum SPF 50, per a Target retailer listing and its FDA Drug Facts label on DailyMed.[^2][^4]

> A single-filter mineral sunscreen: titanium dioxide 11%, no zinc oxide, in the Tinted shade, tinted with iron oxides for a "healthy glow without white cast."[^1][^2] Broad-spectrum UV protection's health case, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Because it skips zinc oxide, the filter this brand's own comparator page credits with the broader UVA-UVB curve of the two mineral filters, its UVA coverage is plausibly narrower than a titanium-dioxide-plus-zinc-oxide mineral sunscreen, a comparative point drawn from [[titanium-dioxide]] rather than a test of this product ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1][^3]

## Summary

Anthelios Mineral Tinted Sunscreen for Face is La Roche-Posay's tinted, 100% mineral face sunscreen, sold in four shades (Tinted, Medium, Medium-Deep, and Deep) that together span Broad Spectrum SPF 40 to 50 on the brand's own product page.[^1] The base "Tinted" shade, the one named in this page's title, lists titanium dioxide 11% as its only active ingredient and no zinc oxide; its FDA Drug Facts label on DailyMed names the product "ANTHELIOS 50 TINTED MINERAL FACE BROAD SPECTRUM SPF 50 SUNSCREEN" and rates it Broad Spectrum SPF 50.[^1][^4] Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

What distinguishes this shade from many mineral sunscreens, including [[cerave-hydrating-mineral-sunscreen-spf-50|CeraVe's Hydrating Mineral Sunscreen SPF 50]], is that it relies on titanium dioxide alone rather than pairing it with zinc oxide. [[titanium-dioxide]]'s own evidence page notes a comparative review crediting zinc oxide, not titanium dioxide, with "a broad UVA-UVB absorption curve," meaning a titanium-dioxide-only formula likely covers less of the UVA band than a mineral sunscreen that combines both filters ([detail below](#the-evidence)). The formula's other three shades (Medium, Medium-Deep, Deep) do carry titanium dioxide 17% plus zinc oxide 8%, per the brand's own ingredient listing, but those are separate SKUs from the Tinted/SPF 50 shade this page covers.[^1]

Iron oxides, mineral pigments, sit in the inactive-ingredient list and supply the tint; the brand cites a named dermatologist's view that iron oxides "can help reduce signs of skin damage caused by visible light," a general claim about the ingredient class rather than a published trial of this product, [weighed under Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1] A retailer listing independently matches the brand's active-ingredient percentage and full inactive-ingredient list verbatim, and lists it at $39.99 for a 1.7 fl oz bottle, matching the price shown in the brand's own page metadata.[^1][^2]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific product was located; the product-specific facts available are its filter composition and the manufacturer's own label rating.

La Roche-Posay's own product page lists the Tinted shade's active ingredient as titanium dioxide 11%, with no zinc oxide; the page describes the four-shade line generally as a "100% mineral sunscreen" with a "healthy glow without white cast," and its stated SPF range, "SPF 40-50," covers all four shades rather than naming a shade-specific number.[^1] A Target listing for the same shade independently confirms the 11% titanium dioxide active, the full inactive-ingredient list matching the brand's page ingredient for ingredient, and states the Tinted shade specifically as "100% mineral sunscreen with broad spectrum UVA/UVB protection SPF 50."[^1][^2] The Tinted shade's own FDA Drug Facts label on DailyMed independently corroborates both figures, listing "Titanium dioxide 11%" as the sole active and naming the product "ANTHELIOS 50 TINTED MINERAL FACE BROAD SPECTRUM SPF 50 SUNSCREEN."[^4]

On the UVA question specifically: [[titanium-dioxide]]'s evidence page cites a comparative review (Schneider and Lim) stating titanium dioxide "provides better UVB protection" than zinc oxide, while zinc oxide has "a broad UVA-UVB absorption curve" that titanium dioxide's narrower UVA coverage does not match.[^3] Because this shade's only active is titanium dioxide, that comparative gap applies directly: its UVB protection is likely solid, consistent with its FDA-registered Broad Spectrum SPF 50 rating, but its UVA (photoaging) coverage is plausibly the weaker side of what a two-filter mineral sunscreen would offer, a general property of the filter rather than a measurement of this exact formula.[^3][^4]

> **Bottom line.** The Tinted shade is a straightforward Broad Spectrum SPF 50 mineral sunscreen built on titanium dioxide alone. Its SPF 50 rating is retailer- and FDA-label-corroborated; its relative UVA coverage is inferred from titanium dioxide's known narrower UVA curve versus zinc oxide, not from an independent test of this product.[^2][^3][^4]
