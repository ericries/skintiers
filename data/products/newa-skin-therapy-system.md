The NEWA is a handheld radiofrequency device for facial wrinkles, and its regulatory history is unlike anything else on this site. **Bottom line:** FDA did not clear it by comparison to an existing device. It granted a **De Novo** request that created a brand new device classification, reviewed on NEWA's own clinical evidence, and that evidence is real but uncontrolled.[^denovo] Category evidence is on [[home-led-light-therapy]]; the device ranking is on [[home-use-skincare-devices-by-evidence]].

> **This device created the category every home RF device now sits in.** The De Novo established a new generic type, "Electrosurgical device for over-the-counter aesthetic use", defined as "a device using radiofrequency energy to produce localized heating within tissues for over-the-counter non-invasive aesthetic use", under a new regulation numbered 21 CFR 878.4420, Class II, product code PAY.[^denovo] Twelve devices now sit under that code, including the Silk'n Titan Allways, the Pollogen STOP U, the CurrentBody Skin RF, and, notably, two FOREO FAQ devices.[^pay]

## Summary

Granted December 2015 as DEN150005. The indication is narrower than most readers would guess: "an over-the-counter home use device intended for non-invasive treatment of mild to moderate facial wrinkles for adult women who have Fitzpatrick Skin Types I-IV".[^denovo] Adult **women** only, and phototypes **I to IV** only, which leaves out deeper skin tones entirely. Suggested use is "up to 5 times a week, up to 6 treatment areas per session, 4 minutes per area".[^denovo] No price is quoted here; no brand page was fetched and no metered lookup was spent.

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate facial wrinkles, in the granted population**
- **Effect size: modest.** In the study FDA reviewed, success meant at least two of three independent dermatologists agreeing on "at least one degree of wrinkle score reduction" on the Fitzpatrick Wrinkle Severity Scale. 95.16% of completers (59 of 62) met that at four weeks and 100% (62 of 62) at three months, against a pre-specified bar of 75%.[^denovo] One grade on a five-point wrinkle scale is a modest change, and the summary itself says further improvement is "unlikely once treatment is discontinued".[^denovo]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary, despite being the best on this site.** The study was "prospective, single center", 69 enrolled and 62 completing, all female, ages 37 to 72.[^denovo] The decision summary contains no mention of a sham arm, randomization, or split-face design. Without a control, a one-grade photographic improvement cannot be separated from regression to the mean, lighting, or expectation, and a 100% success rate is what uncontrolled before-and-after scoring reliably produces.

**Compared with the LED devices on this site**
- **Effect size: not applicable; an evidence comparison.** Radiofrequency is graded Level B for facial rejuvenation in the 2026 GRADE review, the same tier as LED and behind home fractional nonablative lasers at Level A.[^home] The difference is the paperwork: every LED device covered here was cleared on nonclinical testing, while this one has an FDA-reviewed clinical study attached.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable.** Read it as a genuine step up in documentation that still falls short of controlled evidence.

**Safety, which is where the label is most specific**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a harms finding.** No burns, skin breakdown or scarring were reported; two users had moderate redness that resolved within 30 minutes and 59 had minimal redness resolving within 15 minutes.[^denovo] But FDA added a labeling warning after finding no data on facial hair: "Use in subjects without total removal of facial hair may result in follicular burn in these areas".[^denovo] It was also not evaluated for wrinkles under the chin.[^denovo]
- **Evidence quality: solid for the harms, within a 62-person study.**

## The Evidence

**Why the De Novo pathway matters here.** A 510(k) argues a device is substantially equivalent to something already on the market, which is why so many pages on this site end up reporting that the file describes nonclinical testing only. A De Novo is used when no predicate exists: FDA reviews the device on its own evidence and, if it grants the request, writes a new classification with **special controls**. The special controls FDA attached to this category require that "Non-clinical performance data must demonstrate that the device meets all design specifications and performance requirements", alongside clinical performance testing and a label comprehension and self-selection study.[^denovo] That last one is unusual and worth knowing about: the submission had to show ordinary shoppers could correctly judge whether the device suited them. Of 247 subjects, all 52 who said they could not use it were right, and 180 of 195 (92.3%) who said they could were right, with a Kappa of 0.81.[^denovo] Compare [[foreo-faq-led-masks]], where a self-selection study was explicitly not conducted. None of this is FDA **approval**, which remains a higher bar; see [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]].

**What the 100% figure should do to your reading.** Three blinded dermatologists scored photographs, which is a real attempt at rigour. But the threshold was one grade, and by three months every single completer passed it on majority agreement. A treatment that genuinely moved wrinkles in 95% of people at four weeks and 100% at three months would be remarkable; the simpler reading is that an uncontrolled one-grade threshold is easy to meet.[^denovo] The summary's own observation that scores did not improve further between four weeks and three months, while still differing from baseline, is consistent with either a durable real effect or a stable measurement shift.

**A prescription version came first.** The decision summary references the "Newa prescription home use device (K130793)" as the source of the biocompatibility data, so the OTC grant followed an earlier prescription clearance of the same hardware.[^denovo]

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The granted indication covers adult women with Fitzpatrick types I to IV and mild to moderate facial wrinkles; nothing here supports use outside that population, and the facial-hair warning is on the label for a reason.
