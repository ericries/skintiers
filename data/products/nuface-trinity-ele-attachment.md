The second $160 head for the NuFACE Trinity+ handle, and the one where the gap between the marketing sentence and the cleared sentence is widest. **Bottom line:** the brand sells it to lift brows and smooth under-eyes; the clearance says facial stimulation for over-the-counter cosmetic use, and the filing states in its own words that no clinical testing was performed. The light head for the same handle is [[nuface-trinity-plus-wrinkle-reducer]]; the handle itself is [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]].

> **Read the two sentences next to each other.** The brand: "Lift brows, smooth under-eyes, and target marionette lines with artistic precision using the FDA-Cleared Effective Lip & Eye Attachment."[^feed] The clearance: "The Trinity ELE Plus is intended for facial stimulation and is indicated for over-the-counter cosmetic use."[^k201906] The words FDA-Cleared sit inside the first sentence. Nothing in the second one covers lifting, smoothing or marionette lines, and section 11 of the same filing reads "no clinical testing was performed".[^k201906]

## Summary

Cleared 2021-02-24 as K201906, trade name "Trinity ELE Plus and Trinity ELE Plus Pro", applicant Carol Cole Company dba NuFACE, product code NFO under 21 CFR 882.5890, Class II.[^k201906][^openfda] Its predicate is the company's own K131251 "Trinity ELE" from 2013, whose indication is the same sentence with the model name swapped, plus "The anatomical site for application of the Trinity ELE is the face."[^k131251] The earlier NuFACE Plus, K103472, is listed as a reference device.[^k201906]

Physically it is a pair of "dual chrome-plated precise wands" on an attachment head, delivering "microcurrent output at selected frequencies from 0.3 to 50 Hz".[^k201906] It ships in two models: Standard at a maximum output current without Boost of 170 microamps, Pro at 200 microamps, and "All other aspects of the Trinity ELE Plus except those related to maximum output current, are the same between the Standard model and Pro models."[^k201906]

NuFACE sells it as the TRINITY+ Lifting & Smoothing Lip & Eye Microcurrent Attachment at **$160.00**, "Only compatible with the TRINITY+ Device", with a Trinity Classic twin at the same price.[^feed]

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: unknown, because the cleared indication contains no effect.** "The Trinity ELE Plus is intended for facial stimulation and is indicated for over-the-counter cosmetic use."[^k201906] That is the whole of it. The same shape as every other NuFACE microcurrent clearance, set out on [[nuface-plus]] and [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]], and the reason [[home-device-wrinkle-claims-by-cleared-scope]] exists.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal, and the filing is unusually direct about it.** Section 10: "The substantial equivalence for the subject device will not be supported by animal testing. Therefore, no animal testing was conducted." Section 11: "The substantial equivalence for the subject device will not be demonstrated by results of clinical testing. Therefore, no clinical testing was performed."[^k201906] What was done is listed in 9.1: a biocompatibility evaluation, "Safety, Performance and Bench Testing including EMC", and "Software Verification and Validation Testing".[^k201906] Microcurrent is also absent from the 2026 GRADE review this site's device ladder is built from, which is explained at [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]] and not repeated here.
- **Record finding: it is classified as a nerve stimulator for pain relief.** The filing gives the classification name as "Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulator for pain relief", regulation 21 CFR 882.5890, "FDA Panel: 84 - Neurology".[^k201906] Note that this differs from the product-code record for NFO itself, which openFDA names "Stimulator, Transcutaneous Electrical, Aesthetic Purposes" under the same regulation; the two sources disagree and this page reports both rather than resolving it.[^nfo] Either way, a brow-and-under-eye head is regulated under the neurology part, and its indication mentions neither pain nor any cosmetic outcome.
- **Record finding: what changed since 2013 is the battery, the charging and the app.** The comparison table marks Type of Energy Output, Energy Delivery, Energy Flow and Energy Output level all "Same" as the predicate, and marks Different only the temporary user-controllable Microcurrent Boost, a lithium-ion cell in place of nickel-metal hydride, and wireless in place of contact charging.[^k201906] The substantial-equivalence section states the device "has the same intended use and indications for use as the predicate device".[^k201906] Eight years of product cycles, and the energy delivered to your face is recorded as unchanged.
- **Record finding: the parts are named differently in the two documents.** The brand calls them "Dual microspheres"; the filing calls them dual chrome-plated precise wands and specifies that they use chromium.[^feed][^k201906] The photograph on this page shows two of them.
- **A safety detail worth having.** "Microcurrent output is cut-off while the device is sitting on the charging cradle either charging the battery or when the battery is fully charged."[^k201906]

## The Evidence

None specific to this device, and section 11 says so outright rather than leaving it to be inferred. That makes this the cleanest example on the site of a 510(k) that establishes a route to market and nothing about whether the thing works. What clearance does and does not mean is at [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]]; where the home-device categories rank is at [[home-use-skincare-devices-by-evidence]].

The useful comparison is with its sibling. Both heads cost $160, both fit the same handle, and one of them is cleared under OHS with an indication for the reduction of full-face fine lines and wrinkles while this one is cleared under NFO for stimulation.[^k212947] If you are buying a NuFACE attachment because of what the box says about wrinkles, that claim lives in the other head.

> **Bottom line.** A $160 accessory whose regulatory file explicitly contains no clinical testing, sold with the strongest cosmetic claims in the NuFACE range. The claims are not thereby false. They are simply not what the clearance covers, and nothing published here tests them.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own product listing and **not** endorsed here.

- "Lift brows, smooth under-eyes, and target marionette lines with artistic precision using the FDA-Cleared Effective Lip & Eye Attachment."[^feed] Three cosmetic outcomes and a regulatory badge in one sentence. The clearance names none of the three, and the filing reports no clinical testing.[^k201906]
- "Dual microspheres allow for precise treatment of harder to reach areas and delicate contours around the eyes and lips."[^feed] The shape claim is fair and matches the filing's description of the wands; precise treatment of what is the part the file does not establish.
- "FINAL SALE - No returns or exchanges."[^feed] Worth knowing before a $160 accessory purchase, and it applies to the wrinkle head too.
