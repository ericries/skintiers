The Heimish Watermelon Moisture Soothing Gel Cream is a lightweight Korean (K-beauty) hydrating gel-cream from [[heimish]], named for the watermelon extract high in its formula. Heimish lists the 110 ml jar at $23.80.[^heimish]

> This is a well-built lightweight moisturizer: humectants (glycerin, butylene glycol, sorbitol, sodium hyaluronate, trehalose) draw in water while light silicones and the barrier lipid ceramide 3 help hold it, the standard moisturizing mechanism whose evidence is on [[moisturizing]]. The watermelon extract it is marketed around is the second ingredient but a thin-evidence botanical, and the "cooling" and "soothing" language is sensory marketing, not a demonstrated treatment effect. There is no published trial of this specific product.

Heimish markets this as a "Moisturizing & Cooling & Soothing" gel cream built around "watermelon extract, which helps to saturate the skin fully with the necessary moisture and restore the hydrolipidic balance."[^heimish] The general evidence for why humectant-and-occlusive moisturizers work lives on [[moisturizing]]; this page covers what is specific to this product. ## The Evidence

There is no independently published trial of this specific product. Its moisturizing job is the humectant-and-occlusive mechanism set out on [[moisturizing]]: the formula pairs humectants ([[glycerin]] third on the list, plus butylene glycol, sorbitol, sodium hyaluronate, and trehalose) with light silicone occlusives ([[dimethicone]] and phenyl trimethicone) and the barrier lipid [[ceramides|ceramide 3]], a sound build for lightweight hydration. That supports the "moisturizing" claim; the "cooling" and "soothing" language is sensory and not a measured outcome.[^heimish]

The product is named for watermelon (Citrullus lanatus) fruit extract, which sits second on the ingredient list.[^heimish] Watermelon extract is a plant antioxidant with thin standalone human evidence for a skin benefit; it is the brand's marketing hero rather than the reason the product moisturizes, which is the humectant-and-occlusive base above. The long tail of botanical extracts (witch hazel, aloe, hydrangea, neem, turmeric, holy basil) are low-level additions typical of K-beauty formulas and are not individually evidenced here.

> **Bottom line.** A lightweight, well-formulated hydrating gel-cream (110 ml, $23.80).[^heimish] It moisturizes on the standard humectant-and-occlusive mechanism covered on [[moisturizing]], with a barrier-lipid ceramide included; the watermelon extract and "cooling/soothing" claims are marketing rather than a demonstrated treatment effect, and no trial covers this exact product.
