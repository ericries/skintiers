The Pollogen STOP U, sold under the TriPollar name, is the home radiofrequency device that much of this category's regulatory paperwork points back to: it is the named predicate for [[currentbody-skin-rf]]. **Bottom line:** reading its own file shows the chain has no clinical anchor in any over-the-counter device. The category evidence overall is on [[home-radiofrequency-skin-tightening]].

> **Follow the evidence back and it leaves the OTC product entirely.** The 2023 clearance's Clinical performance data section offers a self-selection study and human factors validation conducted on a previously cleared, different model, then adds that "a clinical study was conducted demonstrating the effect of the TriPollar technology for mild to moderate facial wrinkles and rhytides while using the FDA-cleared STOP U device for prescription use (K140255)".[^k220322] The efficacy study was run on a prescription device. The file then reasons that it "substantiates the safety and effectiveness of the TriPollar Technology for wrinkle reduction incorporated into the subject STOP U Model UXV and thus substantiates the safety and effectiveness of the subject STOP U Model UXV."[^k220322]

## Summary

Pollogen holds 18 FDA clearances, every one of them filed with a published 510(k) Summary rather than a Statement, which is why any of this is readable.[^openfda] Five sit under product code PAY, the over-the-counter radiofrequency code: K182774 (2019), K203665 (2021), K220322 (2023), and two Geneo X Elite clearances in 2024 and 2025.[^openfda] The three STOP U filings are successive versions of the same device, each naming the previous one as its predicate; K220322's predicate is "Pollogen Ltd., STOP U Model UXV (K203665)".[^k220322]

Cleared indication, unchanged across them: "The STOP U Model UXV device is intended for use in the non-invasive treatment of mild to moderate facial wrinkles for adult users who have Fitzpatrick Skin Types I-IV."[^k220322] Published specifications: RF "at a frequency of 1 MHz (+/- 10%) and a maximum output RMS power of 5.7 watts (+/- 10%)", with a temperature sensor in the electrode area that "constantly monitors the skin temperature and disables/reduces RF transmission once the maximal skin temperature is obtained".[^k220322] A 7-year shelf life is claimed, matching the predicate.[^k220322]

No price appears here. Pollogen sells through clinics and distributors rather than direct, and the former consumer domain tripollarbeauty.com no longer resolves to the brand at all, which is worth knowing before following an old link.[^brand]

## The Rubric

**Reducing mild to moderate facial wrinkles, the cleared use**
Graded minimal on anecdote, and the grade is about what the file contains rather than about radiofrequency as a technology. Three things sit in the clearance's Clinical performance data section, and none of them is an efficacy study of this device. The self-selection study and the human factors validation, in which "61 subjects which had successfully identified themselves as potential device users participated in the Human Factors validation with a 100% success rate", test whether buyers can correctly judge their own eligibility and operate the device, not whether it works, and they were run on "the previously cleared STOP U (K182774)".[^k220322] The efficacy study was conducted on the prescription device K140255.[^k220322] openFDA records K140255 under product code GEI, a different classification from the over-the-counter PAY code this device sits in.[^openfda]

## The Evidence

**This is where the CurrentBody chain terminates.** [[currentbody-skin-rf]] states that no clinical testing was performed with its device because it is substantially equivalent to its predicate, and its predicate is this one. This device's own file then points at a prescription device for efficacy. So two successive over-the-counter clearances rest on a study of a product nobody can buy over the counter, which is a legitimate use of the 510(k) pathway and also the reason a buyer should not read either clearance as evidence the thing in the box was tested.

**A 100% success rate is a usability result, not a skin result.** It is easy to read "100%" in a clearance summary as a performance figure. In context it means every one of 61 people who thought they were eligible were able to use the device safely, which is what a human factors validation is for.[^k220322]

**The one device in this code that did run a study is a different company's.** [[silkn-titan-allways]] published a 32-woman study with blinded evaluators, uncontrolled but real. Pollogen's three STOP U filings, between them, add none.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The cleared indication covers adult users with Fitzpatrick skin phototypes I to IV, so the two deepest phototypes are outside what this device was assessed for, and the device works by heating the dermis to a sensor-limited ceiling.
