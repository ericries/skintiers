Neutrogena Hydro Boost Water Gel (full name: Hydro Boost Hyaluronic Acid Water Gel with Signature Fragrance) is a fragranced, oil-free gel moisturizer built around a single notable active, hyaluronic acid as sodium hyaluronate, marketed for normal, oily, and combination skin.[^1][^2]

> A lightweight, fragrance-containing humectant gel for normal-to-oily and combination skin. Its hydrating case rests on the well-evidenced general role of hyaluronic acid and glycerin as humectants (see [[hyaluronic-acid]] and [[moisturizing]]); this exact water gel has not been tested in a published trial. Its only occlusive is a thin silicone (dimethicone) rather than petrolatum or ceramides, so it is built for lighter, non-dry skin rather than a dry or barrier-impaired case. Unlike CeraVe's and La Roche-Posay's fragrance-free options in the same category, it contains added fragrance, worth noting for sensitive skin.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Neutrogena Hydro Boost Water Gel is a thin, oil-free gel moisturizer from Neutrogena's Hydro Boost line, sold with "Signature Fragrance" and marketed for normal, oily, and combination skin, including as "suitable for acne-prone skin."[^1] Its main active is [[hyaluronic-acid]] (listed as sodium hyaluronate), a humectant that binds water at the skin's surface, backed by [[glycerin]], a second humectant, and dimethicone, a light silicone that slows water loss without the heaviness of a cream.[^1] Keeping skin moisturized this way, pairing a humectant with an occlusive, is a well-evidenced approach in dermatology; that general case is set out on [[moisturizing]]. The distinctive part of this formula is what it leaves out: it has no petrolatum and no ceramides, the two stronger-evidenced barrier ingredients that dermatology-focused creams such as [[cerave-moisturizing-cream|CeraVe Moisturizing Cream]] build around, which is why the line is positioned for oily and combination skin rather than dry or barrier-impaired skin.[^1][^2]

Two things separate this exact product from Neutrogena's other Hydro Boost moisturizers, and from the plainer fragrance-free options common in this category. First, it contains added fragrance: "Fragrance" appears in the declared ingredient list, and Target's listing describes it as "Formulated with signature fragrance and hyaluronic acid."[^1] CeraVe's and La Roche-Posay's comparable moisturizers in this category are fragrance-free, a real consideration for anyone with sensitive or fragrance-reactive skin. Second, within the Hydro Boost range itself, this "Water Gel" is the lightest of three related textures: Neutrogena also sells a fragrance-free "Gel Cream" for normal-to-dry skin and a fragrance-free "Water Cream" for normal-to-extra-dry skin, so within its own line this is the version built for oilier, non-dry skin, not a general-purpose formula.[^2]

No published trial has tested this exact water gel; its case rests on the general evidence for hyaluronic acid and glycerin as humectants ([The Evidence](#the-evidence), and [[hyaluronic-acid]] and [[moisturizing]]). It is inexpensive: Target lists the 1.7 fl oz jar at $19.99 ($11.76 per ounce), and a 0.5 fl oz travel size is also sold ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-06).[^1][^2]

## The Evidence

Hyaluronic acid, applied topically as a humectant, reliably raises a measured skin-hydration reading in placebo-controlled testing, and pairing a humectant with an occlusive to slow water loss is a well-evidenced way to keep skin moisturized; both cases, including the caveat that only lower-molecular-weight hyaluronic acid crosses the outermost skin layer, are set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]] and [[moisturizing]]. This product's ingredient list does not specify which molecular weight of hyaluronic acid it uses.

No published trial has tested this exact water gel. What is specific to this formulation is its occlusive: dimethicone, a silicone that sits lower on [[moisturizing]]'s own evidence-ranked tier of moisturizing ingredients than petrolatum or ceramides, and the formula contains neither of those stronger options.[^1] That composition fact, not a study result, is why the barrier-support grade above is lower than the hydration grade: the case for this exact water gel on dry or barrier-impaired skin is thinner than the case for it on normal, oily, or combination skin, where a lighter occlusive plus a humectant is a more typical fit.

> **Bottom line.** No study has isolated this water gel's own effect. Its case rests on the general evidence for hyaluronic acid and glycerin as humectants, applied to a formula whose only occlusive (dimethicone) is lighter than the petrolatum- or ceramide-based options built for drier skin.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on the product label.[^1] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

Hydration ingredients:

- sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]) and [[glycerin]], humectants
- [[dimethicone]], dimethiconol, and dimethicone/vinyl dimethicone crosspolymer, silicones that give the gel its texture and slow water loss

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, dimethicone crosspolymer, phenoxyethanol, polyacrylamide, cetearyl olivate and sorbitan olivate (emulsifiers), C13-14 isoparaffin, chlorphenesin, carbomer, laureth-7, ethylhexylglycerin, C12-14 pareth-12, sodium hydroxide (pH adjuster), fragrance, and Blue 1 (colorant).[^1]

The formula contains no petrolatum and no ceramides, unlike [[cerave-moisturizing-cream|CeraVe's]] and other dermatology-focused creams in this category, and it does contain added fragrance and a blue dye (Blue 1), unlike CeraVe's and La Roche-Posay's fragrance-free options.[^1]
