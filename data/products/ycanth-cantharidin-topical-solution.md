YCANTH is cantharidin 0.7% topical solution, FDA-approved under NDA 212905 and sold by Verrica Pharmaceuticals for molluscum contagiosum in patients 2 years and older.[^openfda][^spl] **Bottom line:** it works, the trials behind it are genuinely good, and it is not a product you can buy or apply. The carton says "FOR ADMINISTRATION BY A HEALTHCARE PROFESSIONAL ONLY", the label says a blister is the point, and the manufacturer added a bittering agent because swallowing it can kill.[^carton][^spl] How it compares with the other molluscum treatments is on [[molluscum-contagiosum]] and not repeated here.

> **Nobody knows why it works.** The label states plainly that "The mechanism of action of cantharidin in the treatment of molluscum contagiosum is unknown" and that "Cantharidin is a vesicant", a substance that raises blisters.[^spl] An approved drug with two positive phase 3 trials and no known mechanism is unusual, and it is the honest state of the evidence rather than a gap in this page.

## Summary

Each single-use applicator holds a glass ampule containing "approximately 0.45 mL of 0.7% cantharidin solution", broken open with a co-packaged break tool.[^spl] A clinician applies one application to each lesion, uses no more than two applicators in a session, and the patient washes it off with soap and water 24 hours later; the cycle repeats every three weeks for up to four treatments.[^spl] The solution is "a light violet to dark purple, slightly viscous liquid" whose colour comes from gentian violet, and its inactive ingredients include ethanol at 32% and denatonium benzoate, which the label describes as "an oral deterrent to help mitigate the risk of accidental ingestion".[^spl] No price is given here; it is a prescription product administered in a clinic rather than one with a shelf price.

## The Rubric

**Complete clearance by Day 84, the approved endpoint**
Graded notable on solid evidence. Two double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trials enrolled 528 subjects aged 2 to 60, median age 6, randomized by household, with a healthcare professional blinded to treatment counting the lesions.[^spl] Complete clearance of all treated lesions by Day 84 was 46% against 18% on vehicle in Trial 1, a difference of 29% (95% CI 19% to 38%), and 54% against 13% in Trial 2, a difference of 40% (95% CI 30% to 51%).[^spl] That is a real drug effect replicated across two trials. It is also worth reading the vehicle column: 13% to 18% of children cleared completely with no active drug over the same 12 weeks, which is what a self-limiting viral infection looks like.

**Clearing lesions quickly**
Graded minimal on solid evidence. At Day 21, after a single application, clearance was 11% against 4% in Trial 1, a difference of 8% (95% CI 2% to 14%), and 5% against 2% in Trial 2, a difference of 3% (95% CI -1% to 8%).[^spl] The second interval crosses zero, so that trial did not show a detectable benefit after one treatment. Most of the separation from vehicle arrives between Day 63 and Day 84.

## The Evidence

**The dose-by-dose numbers.** The label publishes clearance at all four timepoints, which is more informative than the headline. Trial 1 ran 11%, 21%, 32%, 46% at Days 21, 42, 63 and 84 against 4%, 9%, 17%, 18% on vehicle; Trial 2 ran 5%, 13%, 28%, 54% against 2%, 4%, 5%, 13%.[^spl] Two things follow. The benefit accumulates with repeat applications rather than arriving at once, and the two trials disagree about how much the vehicle arm improves, 18% against 13% at Day 84, which is a reminder that spontaneous clearance rates in molluscum are variable.

**Local skin reactions are not a side effect, they are the mechanism showing up.** Cantharidin raises blisters, and "Local skin reactions at the application site were observed in 97% of subjects treated with YCANTH during both trials."[^spl] Application site vesicles were reported by 60% mild, 32% moderate and 4% severe; pain by 41%, 20% and 2%; pruritus by 47%, 8% and 1%; scabbing by 39%, 9% and 0%.[^spl] Vehicle produced reactions too, but no severe reaction in any of the listed categories and far fewer moderate ones. Anyone weighing this for a young child should read those numbers as the expected experience rather than the unlucky one.

**More than a third of treated patients washed it off early.** The label reports that "YCANTH Solution or vehicle were removed prior to the 24-hour timepoint in 109/311 (35%) subjects treated with YCANTH Solution and 46/216 (21%) subjects treated with vehicle due to treatment-emergent adverse events."[^spl] That is an intolerance signal sitting inside the efficacy result: the reported clearance rates were achieved by a population in which a third of the treated subjects cut an exposure short.

**The toxicity warnings are unusually blunt.** "Life threatening or fatal toxicities can occur if YCANTH is administered orally", with reported reactions to oral cantharidin including renal failure and severe damage to tissue.[^spl] The label also warns that "YCANTH is flammable, even after drying" and directs avoiding fire, flame or smoking near treated lesions until it is removed.[^spl] The formulation carries a bittering agent for the same reason. Together these explain why the product is restricted to professional administration rather than sold as a wart-style home paint.

**It is not for everywhere on the body.** The label is explicit that YCANTH is for topical use only and "not for oral, mucosal, or ophthalmic use", that it should not be applied near the eyes, that contact with healthy skin should be wiped off immediately, and that treated lesions should not be covered with bandages.[^spl]

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. This is a prescription drug applied by a trained professional, it can cause severe blistering and pain, and it can be fatal if swallowed. Molluscum in or near the eye, and any treatment decision for a young child, belongs with a dermatologist.

## Common Marketing Claims

**"YCANTH is the first FDA-approved treatment option for molluscum."** Accurate, and approval under NDA 212905 is a real one rather than a marketed-unapproved status.[^brand][^openfda] First approved is a statement about regulatory history, not about ranking: how cantharidin compares with the other treatments studied in children is on [[molluscum-contagiosum]].

**"In just a few treatments, many people were completely free of their bumps."** The brand site's own wording, and the label puts numbers on both halves of it.[^brand] A few treatments means up to four applications spread across nine weeks, with the result read at Day 84, and many means 46% in one trial and 54% in the other.[^spl]

**"YCANTH can help start the healing process to clear the bumps in a matter of weeks."** Weeks is right, but at the slow end.[^brand] After the first application, at Day 21, 11% and 5% of treated patients were clear in the two trials, against 4% and 2% on vehicle, and the second of those differences was not distinguishable from zero.[^spl]

## Where to Buy

Not applicable as a shelf purchase. YCANTH is a prescription product whose carton is marked "FOR ADMINISTRATION BY A HEALTHCARE PROFESSIONAL ONLY" and which is applied in a clinic, so there is no consumer purchase route and no retail price to quote here.[^carton] The brand site runs a copay programme inviting patients to "see if you are eligible to pay just $0 per treatment", which is a manufacturer offer with its own conditions rather than a statement of what the drug costs.[^brand]
