Dove Sensitive Skin Beauty Bar is a fragrance-free bar cleanser (marketed as hypoallergenic) for the face, body, and hands whose lead ingredient is sodium lauroyl isethionate, a mild synthetic detergent (syndet), though the formula also carries true soap salts, making it a syndet-soap hybrid rather than a pure syndet bar.[^1] An 8-pack of 3.75 oz bars lists for $12.99 at Target, about $1.62 per bar.[^2]

> A hybrid bar that leans mild. Its primary detergent belongs to the same lower-pH, less barrier-stripping surfactant class that separates gentle syndet cleansers from classic soap, but the bar also lists sodium oleate, sodium stearate, and sodium laurate, the sodium salts of fatty acids that are true soap by definition, and cocamidopropyl betaine, a foam-boosting co-surfactant and a recognized cleanser allergen in a sensitized minority.[^1] No trial isolates this exact bar; the case rests on its surfactant chemistry and the category evidence for gentle cleansers generally.

## Summary

Dove Sensitive Skin Beauty Bar is a solid, bar-soap-format cleanser marketed for dry and sensitive skin on the face, body, and hands. Dove positions it as fragrance-free, hypoallergenic, pH balanced, and free of sulfate cleansers and parabens, and says it "doesn't dry skin like soap can" because roughly a quarter of the bar is a moisturizing cream blended into the cleansing base.[^1] The lead ingredient, sodium lauroyl isethionate, is a synthetic detergent (syndet) with [a documented mildness edge over true soap at the category level](#the-evidence). The formula is not a pure syndet, though: it also lists sodium oleate, sodium stearate, and sodium laurate, which are sodium salts of fatty acids and true soap by definition, so this bar sits between a syndet and a soap rather than being mild-in-marketing-only.[^1]

No trial isolates this exact bar. Its mildness case rests on the surfactant-class evidence for gentle, low-surfactant washes set out on [[best-gentle-cleansers-sensitive-skin]], and on a rinse-off product the better-evidenced skin-barrier work happens afterward, in a leave-on moisturizer, covered on [[moisturizing]]. One ingredient worth a second look on a bar sold specifically for sensitive skin: cocamidopropyl betaine, a foam-boosting co-surfactant and one of the more consistently identified allergens in cleanser-related contact dermatitis in large patch-test registries, covered on [[cocamidopropyl-betaine]] (see [Tolerability](#tolerability)).

It is inexpensive. Target lists an 8-pack of 3.75 oz bars at $12.99 ($0.43 per ounce), about $1.62 per bar, and Walmart lists the same 8-pack at $12.97.[^2][^3] It is not a treatment: it delivers no active drug, and claims like "pH balanced" and "#1 dermatologist recommended" are [label claims](#common-marketing-claims) that the product-specific evidence does not establish.

## The Evidence

Low-surfactant, non-alkaline washes are gentler on the skin barrier than high-pH true soap bars at the category level; that evidence, and the comparison across the site's other gentle cleansers, is set out on [[best-gentle-cleansers-sensitive-skin]]. No published trial isolates Dove Sensitive Skin Beauty Bar itself, so its grade rests on its surfactant chemistry. Sodium lauroyl isethionate is a lower-pH anionic syndet; sodium oleate, sodium stearate, and sodium laurate are sodium salts of fatty acids, the textbook definition of soap.[^1] That mix places the bar closer to the syndet end of the spectrum than a classic tallow-and-lye bar, but it is not a pure syndet the way some liquid cleansers are.

## What's In It

The ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] Functional ingredients with their own evidence page are linked; the rest are the base, texture, and chelator system.

Cleansing system:

- sodium lauroyl isethionate, the lead syndet, and sodium isethionate, a related salt from the same manufacturing step
- [[stearic-acid|stearic acid]] and lauric acid, fatty acids that also act as foam boosters and opacifiers
- sodium oleate, sodium stearate, and sodium laurate, sodium salts of fatty acids, true soap
- [[cocamidopropyl-betaine]], a mild foam-boosting co-surfactant (see [Tolerability](#tolerability))

Base and functional ingredients: water, [[glycerin]] (humectant), sodium chloride (a bar-hardening salt), tetrasodium etidronate and tetrasodium EDTA (chelators that keep the bar from discoloring), and titanium dioxide, CI 77891 (a white opacifying pigment).[^1]

## Tolerability

Dove markets this bar as hypoallergenic.[^1] Cocamidopropyl betaine, the formula's foam-boosting co-surfactant, is genuinely milder on irritation testing than straight sulfate detergents, but it is also one of the more consistently identified allergens in cleanser-related contact dermatitis in large patch-test registries, a tension worth noting on a bar marketed specifically for sensitive skin; most exposed people do not react, and the sensitization data are covered on [[cocamidopropyl-betaine]].

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"#1 Dermatologist and Pediatrician recommended bar brand."** A brand-wide claim covering the whole Dove Beauty Bar line rather than a tested claim about this SKU, and Dove does not cite the underlying recommendation data on the product page.[^1]
- **"Doesn't dry skin like soap can."** Consistent with the syndet share of the formula, but the bar also contains true soap salts (sodium oleate, sodium stearate, sodium laurate), so the contrast with "ordinary soap" is a matter of degree rather than a syndet-versus-soap split.[^1]
- **"Hypoallergenic" and "pH balanced."** Label claims.[^1] Cocamidopropyl betaine, one of the formula's surfactants, is a recognized contact allergen in a sensitized minority; see [[cocamidopropyl-betaine]].
- **"Plant based cleanser."** A composition claim about the surfactant feedstock, not an independently tested outcome.[^1]
