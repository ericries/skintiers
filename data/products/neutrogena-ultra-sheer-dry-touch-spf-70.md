Neutrogena Ultra Sheer Dry-Touch Sunscreen Broad Spectrum SPF 70 is a lotion-format face and body sunscreen built entirely on organic (chemical) UV filters, marketed for a fast-absorbing, non-greasy "Dry-Touch" finish.[^1]

> A Broad Spectrum SPF 70 sunscreen using an all-chemical filter set: avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene. Broad-spectrum sunscreen's health case, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, is well evidenced at the category level and set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Compared with the line's [[neutrogena-ultra-sheer-dry-touch-spf-55]], this version raises homosalate from 10% to 15%, the US permitted maximum, while avobenzone stays at 3%, so UVA coverage is unchanged and the difference is UVB-focused; going from SPF 55 to SPF 70 blocks only a small additional fraction of UVB rays, a diminishing-returns pattern general to high SPF numbers.[^1][^2]

## Summary

Ultra Sheer Dry-Touch is a longstanding sunscreen line from Neutrogena, a Kenvue-owned US drugstore skincare brand. This SPF 70 version is a lotion sunscreen positioned for the face and body, built around Neutrogena's "Dry-Touch" finish claim and its "Helioplex" filter-stabilization technology.[^1] Its core purpose is the same as any sunscreen's: broad-spectrum UV protection is the best-evidenced way to slow photoaging and lower the risk of sun-related skin cancer, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

What distinguishes it from the line's own [[neutrogena-ultra-sheer-dry-touch-spf-55]] is the UVB filter loading, not a different UVA approach. Per its FDA Drug Facts label, the actives are avobenzone 3%, homosalate 15%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 10%, an all-organic (chemical) filter set rated Broad Spectrum SPF 70.[^1] That is the same avobenzone, octisalate, and octocrylene percentages as the SPF 55 version, with homosalate raised from 10% to 15%, the US permitted maximum for that filter.[^1] [[avobenzone]], at its own US maximum of 3%, remains the formula's only dedicated UVA filter, so the higher SPF number does not add UVA protection ([the filter roles are detailed in The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1] The declared inactive ingredients include Fragrance, so this is not a fragrance-free formula.[^1] Target lists it at $9.89 for 3 fl oz ($3.30/fl oz) as of 2026-08-07, and corroborates the same four actives and percentages, water resistance up to 80 minutes, and non-comedogenic, PABA-free marketing.[^2]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No independent study of this specific product was located; the product-specific facts are its filter composition and FDA label rating.

The FDA Drug Facts label (revised October 17, 2025) lists the actives as avobenzone 3%, homosalate 15%, octisalate 5%, and octocrylene 10%, at SPF 70, Broad Spectrum.[^1] SPF is a UVB (sunburn) number; the SPF 70 rating is FDA-tested for UVB strength and does not by itself certify UVA (photoaging) protection, a separate axis described on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

Three of the four filters, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene, are UVB absorbers; UVA coverage rests on avobenzone alone.[^1] [[avobenzone]] at its 3% US maximum provides genuine UVA coverage once photostabilized, but it degrades under sunlight unless a co-filter holds it stable. [[octocrylene]], present here at its 10% US permitted maximum, serves that stabilizing role in addition to contributing its own UVB and short-wave UVA-II absorption. [[octisalate]] adds a weak, secondary UVB contribution at its 5% US maximum, and [[homosalate]] here sits at its full 15% US permitted maximum, higher than the 10% used in the line's [[neutrogena-ultra-sheer-dry-touch-spf-55]]. That higher homosalate loading is the formula's main lever for reaching SPF 70 rather than SPF 55; the UVA filter, avobenzone, is unchanged between the two versions.[^1] Measured against the global field, this remains the older US filter set: avobenzone is a weaker, less photostable UVA filter than the modern organic filters (bemotrizinol/Tinosorb S, bisoctrizole/Tinosorb M, the Mexoryl filters) standard in the EU, Korea, and Australia, a comparison detailed on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

> **Bottom line.** The FDA-tested SPF 70 Broad Spectrum rating reflects a higher homosalate load (15%, the US maximum) than the same line's SPF 55 version (10%), with octisalate and octocrylene unchanged and avobenzone unchanged at its own 3% maximum.[^1] Since UVB filter concentration and SPF do not scale linearly, going from SPF 55 to SPF 70 raises the fraction of UVB rays blocked only marginally, and UVA coverage, resting on avobenzone alone, is identical to the SPF 55 formula.

The label directs to "apply liberally 15 minutes before sun exposure" and to reapply "after 80 minutes of swimming or sweating; immediately after towel drying," and at least every 2 hours.[^1] Target's listing separately states the product is "water-resistant for up to 80 minutes," consistent with the label's reapplication direction.[^1][^2] How much protection any SPF delivers in practice depends on applying enough product, a point covered generally on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Dry-Touch" and "Helioplex."** Target's listing describes "Dry-Touch technology" for a "clean, ultra-light feel and non-shiny finish," and "Helioplex technology" for UVA/UVB protection.[^2] These are the brand's own names for its finish and filter-stabilization approach, as relayed by Target's product listing; the graded UV-filter protection above rests on the FDA label's active percentages, not on these trade names.
- **PABA-free, oxybenzone-free.** Composition points about which UV filters are absent, not efficacy claims; the label's active filters are avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene.[^1][^2]
- **"#1 dermatologist-recommended suncare brand" and non-comedogenic.** Target's listing states these; they are brand-recommendation and testing claims, not part of the FDA-graded UV-filter protection above.[^2]
