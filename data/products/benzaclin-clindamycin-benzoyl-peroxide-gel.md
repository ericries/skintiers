BenzaClin is the brand name for a prescription fixed-combination topical gel of clindamycin 1% (as phosphate) and benzoyl peroxide 5%, originally marketed by Dermik Laboratories, a business of sanofi-aventis U.S. LLC, and FDA-approved "for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris."[^dailymed] Each gram contains 10 mg (1%) clindamycin as phosphate and 50 mg (5%) benzoyl peroxide.[^dailymed]

> A legitimate fixed combination that genuinely beats clindamycin alone, in large part because adding benzoyl peroxide curbs the antibiotic resistance that undermines clindamycin monotherapy. But its edge over plain, cheap benzoyl peroxide is modest: in one of its own two registration trials the combination cut inflammatory lesions 63% versus 53% for benzoyl peroxide alone, and vehicle managed 42%. It is a sensible way to use topical clindamycin, but a generic clindamycin-benzoyl peroxide gel delivers the identical combination for less than the brand, and someone paying out of pocket could get much of the benefit from over-the-counter benzoyl peroxide. The general actives are owned by [[clindamycin-topical]] and [[benzoyl-peroxide]]; the broader condition is [[acne]].

## Summary

BenzaClin pairs a topical antibiotic, [[clindamycin-topical|clindamycin]], with [[benzoyl-peroxide]] in a single gel, each gram delivering 10 mg (1%) clindamycin as phosphate and 50 mg (5%) benzoyl peroxide.[^dailymed] Its FDA-approved indication, quoted above, is simply "the topical treatment of acne vulgaris," and the label sets an age floor of 12: "safety and effectiveness of this product in pediatric patients below the age of 12 have not been established."[^dailymed] It is applied twice daily, morning and evening, to affected areas after the skin is gently washed, rinsed with warm water, and patted dry.[^dailymed] The product is dispensed after reconstitution (the clindamycin component is mixed into the benzoyl peroxide gel base); this repackaged NDC (54868-6062-0) is supplied in 6 gram plastic jars, and the reconstituted gel is stored at room temperature, 25 degrees C (77 degrees F), with the label stating "do not freeze" and "refrigeration is not required."[^dailymed] It is prescription-only.

The rationale for the combination, and why it is a better way to use clindamycin than the antibiotic alone, is that benzoyl peroxide suppresses the *Cutibacterium acnes* antibiotic resistance that erodes clindamycin monotherapy over time; that general story is owned by [[clindamycin-topical]] and [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and is not re-derived here. The product-specific case rests on two randomized 10-week trials, described in full under [The Evidence](#the-evidence). Across them the fixed combination beat clindamycin alone, benzoyl peroxide alone, and vehicle, but the margin over benzoyl peroxide alone was modest, 14 percentage points in one trial and about 10 in the other, and in the second trial the vehicle response was unusually high.[^dailymed]

The most common adverse reaction was dry skin, reported in 12% of the BenzaClin group versus 6% on vehicle; application site reactions occurred in about 3% versus under 1% on vehicle.[^dailymed] This is a prescription drug; see a prescriber for use and dosing, and see [[acne]] for the broader condition context. Generic clindamycin-benzoyl peroxide gels containing the same actives are widely available and typically cost less than the brand.

## The Evidence

The general case for topical clindamycin as an acne antibiotic, its documented *C. acnes* resistance, and the case for benzoyl peroxide are covered on [[clindamycin-topical]] and [[benzoyl-peroxide]]; this section covers only what the BenzaClin label itself reports for this product.

The label describes two randomized controlled trials in patients with moderate to moderately severe facial acne vulgaris, each comparing the fixed BenzaClin combination against clindamycin 1% alone, benzoyl peroxide 5% alone, and vehicle, with treatment applied twice daily for 10 weeks. The label reports mean percent reductions in inflammatory, non-inflammatory, and total lesion counts.[^dailymed]

Study 1 (N=480, about 120 per arm), mean percent reduction in inflammatory lesion counts at 10 weeks:

| Arm | Inflammatory | Non-inflammatory | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| BenzaClin combination[^dailymed] | 46% | 22% | 36% |
| Benzoyl peroxide 5% alone | 32% | 22% | 28% |
| Clindamycin 1% alone | 16% | 9% | 15% |
| Vehicle | worsened 3% | worsened 1% | 0.2% |

Study 2 (N=287), mean percent reduction in inflammatory lesion counts at 10 weeks:

| Arm | Inflammatory | Non-inflammatory | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| BenzaClin combination (N=95)[^dailymed] | 63% | 54% | 58% |
| Benzoyl peroxide 5% alone (N=95) | 53% | 50% | 52% |
| Clindamycin 1% alone (N=49) | 45% | 39% | 42% |
| Vehicle (N=48) | 42% | 36% | 39% |

The label's summary claim is that "BenzaClin applied twice daily for 10 weeks was significantly more effective than vehicle in the treatment of moderate to moderately severe facial acne vulgaris."[^dailymed] The two trials tell a consistent but nuanced story: the fixed combination outperformed each single active and vehicle in both studies, and the gap over clindamycin alone was large (46% versus 16% in Study 1). But the gap over benzoyl peroxide alone, the cheap over-the-counter active, was much smaller, 46% versus 32% in Study 1 and 63% versus 53% in Study 2. In Study 2 the vehicle arm itself reduced inflammatory lesions 42%, nearly matching clindamycin alone (45%) and closing much of the distance to the combination, a reminder of how large placebo and regression effects can be in acne trials.

> **Bottom line.** The fixed combination clearly beat clindamycin monotherapy and vehicle, and adding benzoyl peroxide is the resistance-smart way to use topical clindamycin. But its incremental benefit over plain benzoyl peroxide, an inexpensive over-the-counter active, was modest in both trials, and a generic clindamycin-benzoyl peroxide gel supplies the identical combination for less than the brand.[^dailymed]

## What's In It

The active ingredients as declared on the FDA-approved prescribing information.[^dailymed]

Active, per gram:

- [[clindamycin-topical|Clindamycin]] 10 mg (1%), as clindamycin phosphate.[^dailymed]
- [[benzoyl-peroxide|Benzoyl peroxide]] 50 mg (5%).[^dailymed]

The label instructs that the product is applied twice daily, morning and evening, to affected areas after gentle washing, and warns that it may bleach hair or colored fabric because of its benzoyl peroxide component, and that it should not be used in combination with erythromycin-containing products because of its clindamycin component.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand marketing page was reviewed for this product; the claims below come from the FDA label's own characterization rather than promotional copy.

- **A fixed combination is better than clindamycin alone.** Supported by the label's own trials, where the combination cut inflammatory lesions 46% versus 16% for clindamycin alone in Study 1, and by the general rationale that benzoyl peroxide curbs the *C. acnes* resistance that undermines clindamycin monotherapy, covered on [[clindamycin-topical]].[^dailymed]
- **Framing the combination as a decisive upgrade for acne generally.** Overstated relative to plain benzoyl peroxide: the label's own increment over benzoyl peroxide 5% alone was 46% versus 32% (Study 1) and 63% versus 53% (Study 2), a modest margin, and vehicle alone reduced inflammatory lesions 42% in Study 2.[^dailymed]
