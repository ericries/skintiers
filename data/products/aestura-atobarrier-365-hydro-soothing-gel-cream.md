Aestura Atobarrier 365 Hydro Soothing Gel Cream is a light-textured, gel-cream moisturizer from [[aestura|Aestura]]'s Atobarrier 365 line, sold in a 60 ml / 2.02 oz size and marketed for instant cooling and quick hydration in sensitive skin.[^aestura][^target]

> A lighter, cooling gel-cream in the Atobarrier 365 line, positioned next to the richer [[aestura-atobarrier-365-cream|Atobarrier 365 Cream]] and the fluid [[aestura-atobarrier-365-lotion|Atobarrier 365 Lotion]]. The brand describes "visible ceramide capsules" in its marketing copy, and the declared ingredient list carries the synthetic pseudoceramide Hydroxypropyl Bispalmitamide MEA alongside cholesterol and free fatty acids, though it names no ceramide subtype such as ceramide NP, plus a glycerin and amino-acid humectant blend.[^aestura] General moisturizer evidence backs its hydration case (on [[moisturizing]]), while the ceramide-specific barrier-repair mechanism made for its siblings does not clearly carry over here without a named natural ceramide (on [[cholesterol]]).

## Summary

Aestura Atobarrier 365 Hydro Soothing Gel Cream is the lightest, gel-textured moisturizer in the South Korean brand [[aestura|Aestura]]'s Atobarrier 365 line, positioned for instant cooling and quick hydration in dry and sensitive skin, and directed for use on clean skin after toner and serum in the brand's Atobarrier 365 routine.[^aestura] Its hydration case rests on the general evidence for moisturizers and barrier-lipid ingredients on dry, sensitive skin, set out on [[moisturizing]].

What distinguishes this product from its siblings is a narrower ceramide profile rather than an edge: the brand's marketing copy for this SKU states "visible ceramide capsules melt softly upon application," and groups the formula's lipid capsules as three key components, ceramides, cholesterol, and fatty acids, the same physiologic-lipid trio Elias describes as the barrier's mortar on [[cholesterol]].[^aestura] The declared ingredient list names no ceramide subtype such as ceramide NP, but it does carry [[ceramides|Hydroxypropyl Bispalmitamide MEA]], a synthetic pseudoceramide (ceramide analog) that supplies the ceramide component behind that marketing claim, alongside cholesterol and the free fatty acids stearic, palmitic, myristic, and arachidic acid; that is a narrower ceramide profile than [[aestura-atobarrier-365-cream|Atobarrier 365 Cream]] and [[aestura-atobarrier-365-lotion|Atobarrier 365 Lotion]], which both list a named ceramide subtype, ceramide NP, explicitly.[^aestura] The rest of the formula is a glycerin, propanediol, and trehalose humectant base carrying an amino-acid and PCA blend (arginine, serine, citrulline, histidine HCl, alanine, threonine, glutamic acid, lysine HCl), ingredients modeled on the skin's own natural moisturizing factor.[^aestura] No published, independent trial tests this exact product; the brand cites its own uncontrolled test of 32 women, reported and quarantined under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

The brand states the formula contains less than 1% synthetic fragrance, a consideration for fragrance-avoidant [[atopic-dermatitis|eczema-prone]] skin ([see Tolerability](#tolerability)).[^aestura] Target lists the 2.02 oz size at $47.99 as of 2026-08-07 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy)).[^target]

## The Evidence

Moisturizers and physiologic-lipid ingredients have category-level evidence for improving barrier measurements and reducing eczema flares in dry, sensitive skin, set out on [[moisturizing]]. The narrower mechanism this product's own marketing invokes, that ceramides, cholesterol, and fatty acids act together as a barrier-repair mix, is set out on [[cholesterol]]: physiologic-lipid experiments found that a complete, equimolar three-lipid mixture was needed for normal barrier recovery, and that supplying only one or two of the three lipids delayed recovery rather than helping it.

No published, independently reviewed trial of Aestura Atobarrier 365 Hydro Soothing Gel Cream itself is cited here. The brand's own product page reports a single brand-commissioned moisturizing test on 32 participants with dryness and sensitivity concerns, with no stated methodology, testing lab, or year, and no control group, so it does not support a grade here; its figures are reported and weighed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^aestura]

The declared ingredient list supplies cholesterol, free fatty acids, and the synthetic pseudoceramide Hydroxypropyl Bispalmitamide MEA, which functions as the ceramide component behind the brand's "visible ceramide capsules" marketing even though no named ceramide subtype, such as ceramide NP, appears on the label.[^aestura] That narrower ceramide profile is why the ceramide-specific grade above is lower than the general moisturizing grade: the mechanism the brand's own marketing cites was demonstrated with natural ceramides, cholesterol, and fatty acids in an equimolar mixture, and this formula substitutes a synthetic ceramide analog for that named ceramide component.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on the manufacturer's product page.[^aestura] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

Barrier-lipid ingredients:

- [[cholesterol|Cholesterol]], one of the three physiologic barrier lipids
- stearic acid, palmitic acid, myristic acid, and arachidic acid, free fatty acids that are a second physiologic barrier-lipid class
- [[ceramides|Hydroxypropyl Bispalmitamide MEA]], a synthetic pseudoceramide (ceramide analog) that supplies the third physiologic-lipid class, ceramides, without being a named natural ceramide subtype such as ceramide NP; this is the ingredient behind the brand's "ceramide capsule" marketing

Humectant and NMF (natural-moisturizing-factor) ingredients:

- [[glycerin|Glycerin]], propanediol, and trehalose, humectants
- arginine, serine, PCA, citrulline, glycogen, histidine HCl, alanine, threonine, glutamic acid, lysine HCl, mannitol, and sucrose, an amino-acid and NMF-component blend

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, phenyl trimethicone, methyl trimethicone, 1,2-hexanediol, dicaprylyl carbonate, ammonium acryloyldimethyltaurate/VP copolymer, hydrogenated lecithin, hydrogenated polyisobutene, hydroxyethyl acrylate/sodium acryloyldimethyl taurate copolymer, cetearyl alcohol, acrylates/C10-30 alkyl acrylate crosspolymer, glyceryl caprylate, glyceryl stearate, tromethamine, disodium EDTA, ethylhexylglycerin, polyglyceryl-3 methylglucose distearate, sorbitan isostearate, tocopherol (vitamin E), acrylates/ammonium methacrylate copolymer, and silica.[^aestura]

The formula contains no parabens, formaldehyde or formaldehyde-releasing agents, phthalates, mineral oil, retinyl palmitate, oxybenzone, coal tar, hydroquinone, SLS/SLES sulfates, triclocarban, or triclosan, and the brand states it contains less than 1% synthetic fragrance.[^aestura] Unlike [[aestura-atobarrier-365-cream|Atobarrier 365 Cream]] and [[aestura-atobarrier-365-lotion|Atobarrier 365 Lotion]], the declared list carries no niacinamide and no named ceramide subtype such as ceramide NP, though it does carry the pseudoceramide Hydroxypropyl Bispalmitamide MEA noted above.

## Tolerability

The brand states the product underwent a 48-hour dermatologist-supervised patch test at a university hospital dermatology department, a non-comedogenic assessment measuring acne lesion counts and sebum on 20 acne-prone or sensitive-skin participants over 4 weeks, and a sensitive-skin panel test measuring irritation over 4 weeks.[^aestura] These are brand-reported test programs rather than independently published outcomes. The formula is not fragrance-free: the brand states it contains less than 1% synthetic fragrance, a consideration for fragrance-avoidant or reactive skin.
