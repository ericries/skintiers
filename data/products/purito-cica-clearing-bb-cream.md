Purito Seoul Cica Clearing BB Cream (Purito) is a tinted BB cream sold in a 30 ml bottle across six shades, built on a silicone base with titanium dioxide, zinc oxide, and two organic UV filters, plus centella asiatica extract, niacinamide, and panthenol.[^purito][^tsc]

> A light-coverage, silicone-based BB cream whose UV filters are not attached to a stated SPF or PA number on either the brand's own page or an independent retailer listing, so its real-world sun protection cannot be graded the way a rated sunscreen can; centella, niacinamide, and panthenol are present as recognized calming ingredients but at undisclosed concentrations, and sunscreen in general is commonly underapplied in practice ([[sunscreen-uv-filters]]).[^purito][^tsc]

## Summary

Cica Clearing BB Cream is a tinted, medium-coverage BB cream aimed at evening out skin tone while doubling as a light moisturizer, sold in six shades from Neutral Ivory to Deep Warm.[^purito][^tsc] The ingredient list carries two mineral UV filters (titanium dioxide, zinc oxide) and two organic ones (ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate/octinoxate, ethylhexyl salicylate/octisalate), the same classes of filter covered generally on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], but neither the brand's US product page nor an independent European retailer listing states an SPF or PA rating for it.[^purito][^tsc] Without a rated number, the product cannot be compared against other sunscreens on protection strength, and even a rated product's real-world protection depends heavily on how much is applied, a point covered generally on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

The formula's other headline ingredients are centella asiatica extract (with its triterpenes asiaticoside, madecassoside, asiatic acid, and madecassic acid), niacinamide, and panthenol, all recognized calming and skin-conditioning ingredients; the general evidence for centella and niacinamide is set out on [[centella-asiatica]] and [[niacinamide]].[^purito][^tsc] None of the three has a disclosed concentration in this product, and the ingredient order places niacinamide ahead of the color/tint pigments (mica, iron oxides) but well behind the silicone base, while centella asiatica extract and panthenol sit further down the list, near the preservative system ([see What's In It](#whats-in-it)).[^purito][^tsc]

As a cosmetic, a European retailer describes the product as delivering "medium coverage that's buildable and blendable" with a "velvety finish with a pinch of glow," while the brand's own page limits its claims to a natural finish and moisture retention.[^tsc][^purito] It lists at $12.00 for the 30 ml bottle on the brand's own US site, and the same product is also sold through a European retailer at a different, higher local price, which does not confirm the US figure but does confirm the product and its ingredient list independently.[^purito][^tsc]

The brand's "Retains Moisture," "Soothes Irritation," and vegan/cruelty-free positioning is weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## The Evidence

No published clinical trial tests this exact formulation, so its grades rest on general evidence and on the composition facts confirmed above. Centella asiatica's triterpenes have plausible, well-mapped mechanisms for wound healing and are anti-inflammatory in the lab, but the controlled clinical record is thin; that evidence, with its limits, is on [[centella-asiatica]]. Topical niacinamide is well tolerated with modest evidence for a handful of uses, mostly from small or manufacturer-run trials at concentrations this product does not disclose; that evidence is on [[niacinamide]].

On sun protection specifically, the product's filter set (titanium dioxide, zinc oxide, octinoxate, octisalate) covers both mineral and organic filter classes; how each filter class works and how much sunscreen in general is commonly underapplied in practice, a consideration that applies at least as much to a thin, blendable BB cream layered as makeup, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. The absence of a stated SPF or PA number here means its actual protection level is not established by either source checked.

## What's In It

The full INCI list as declared on the brand's own product page for shade #23 Natural Beige, corroborated on an independent retailer listing.[^purito][^tsc]

Actives and notable ingredients:

- [[niacinamide]], a form of vitamin B3, positioned ahead of the color/tint pigments (mica, iron oxides) but behind the silicone base
- Titanium Dioxide (CI 77891) and Zinc Oxide, mineral UV filters (also functioning as pigments/opacifiers in a tinted formula)
- Ethylhexyl Methoxycinnamate (octinoxate) and Ethylhexyl Salicylate (octisalate), organic UV filters
- [[centella-asiatica|Centella Asiatica Extract]], Madecassoside, Asiaticoside, Madecassic Acid, and Asiatic Acid, the plant's triterpene actives
- Allantoin, a soothing ingredient, and [[panthenol]] (provitamin B5), both lower in the list
- Adenosine and Tocopherol (vitamin E)

Base, texture, and pigment system: Water, Cyclopentasiloxane and Cyclohexasiloxane (silicones), Butylene Glycol and Glycerin (humectants), Lauryl Polyglyceryl-3 Polydimethylsiloxyethyl Dimethicone and Hydrogenated Poly(C6-14 Olefin) (silicone/emollient carriers), Mica, Sodium Chloride, Zinc Stearate, Dextrin Palmitate, Disteardimonium Hectorite, and Sorbitan Sesquioleate (pigment dispersion and texture agents), Caprylyl Glycol, Triethoxycaprylylsilane, Aluminum Hydroxide, Caprylhydroxamic Acid, and 1,2-Hexanediol (preservative/stabilizer system), Dimethicone/Vinyl Dimethicone Crosspolymer, and Iron Oxides (CI 77492, CI 77491, CI 77499) as the shade's colorants.[^purito][^tsc]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Retains Moisture" and "Soothes Irritation."** Brand positioning language on the product page; no independent trial of this formulation reports a moisture-retention or irritation outcome.[^purito]
- **"Natural and radiant finish" and "strengthens the skin barrier."** A retailer product description; the barrier-strengthening claim is not backed by a cited study of this product, and centella and niacinamide's general barrier-related evidence, at concentrations this product does not disclose, is on [[centella-asiatica]] and [[niacinamide]].[^tsc]
- **"Protects from both UVA/UVB rays."** The formula does contain recognized UVA/UVB filters, but without a stated SPF or PA rating the degree of protection this specific product provides is not established.[^tsc][^purito]
