The NuFACE Mini is the cheap entry point to microcurrent, $250 in the kit the brand currently sells, and it has been cleared twice under the same name. **Bottom line:** across nine years and two filings, what FDA was shown is this device's electricity compared against the company's own earlier device, and the indication quietly got bigger. Why a cleared stimulation claim is not a lifting claim is argued at [[nuface-trinity-microcurrent-device]] and not repeated here.

> **The indication grew between the two clearances.** In 2014 the Mini was "intended for facial stimulation and is indicated for over-the-counter cosmetic use", and the file adds that "The anatomical site for application of the NuFACE® Mini Device is the face."[^k2014] In 2019, under the same product name, it became "intended for facial and neck stimulation and is indicated for over-the-counter cosmetic use".[^k2019] The neck was added without either filing reporting an outcome measured on a neck, or on a face.

## Summary

Two clearances, both filed as Summaries under product code NFO: K133823, decided 2014-04-17 as an **Abbreviated** 510(k), and K191672, decided 2019-10-15 as a **Traditional** one.[^openfda] The current retail product is the MINI+ Starter Kit at $250.00, described by the brand as letting you "sculpt in just 5 minutes" with "3 intensities".[^brand] The 2019 file notes the device "requires the use of a conductive gel or medium during treatment" and offers "three microcurrent output levels".[^k2019]

## The Rubric

**Facial and neck stimulation, the thing actually cleared**
- **Effect size: minimal.** The cleared words describe an action, not a result. Across both filings the 2014 one is explicit about the basis: "The determination of substantial equivalence for the NuFACE® Mini is based on an assessment of non-clinical performance."[^k2014]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** What that assessment compared is worth listing, because it is all electricity: "This assessment included a comparison of the output of the NuFACE® Mini to that of the predicate", covering waveform type, waveform shape, maximum output voltage, maximum output current, output tolerance, pulse width and output frequency.[^k2014] Matching a predecessor's waveform is a real engineering result. It says nothing about skin.

**Lifting, sculpting and toning, the thing sold**
- **Effect size: minimal, graded on absence of evidence.** The brand says the Mini is "powered by the same professional-grade, FDA-cleared technology" that "sculpts and tones all 69 muscles in the face and neck to visibly firm, lift, and define".[^brand] Neither clearance measures lift, contour or muscle tone, and no source consulted here puts a number on any of them for this device.
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** The count of 69 muscles is the most specific thing in that sentence, and it describes anatomy rather than an effect.

## The Evidence

**Both clearances name the company's own device as the predicate.** The 2014 filing says the Carol Cole Company "is claiming substantial equivalence with its own device, the NuFACE® Trinity device, cleared as the NuFACE®D Plus under 510(k) K103472", and describes itself as "a new design based on the manufacturer's cleared device" whose "electrical output of the NuFACE® Mini is identical to the previously cleared NuFACE® Trinity Facial Toning Device".[^k2014] The 2019 filing again names a Trinity, "510(k) Number: K181008".[^k2019] Identical output to your own earlier product is exactly what an abbreviated pathway is for, and it also means the evidence base never grows.

**It is regulated as a pain-relief device.** Both filings sit under 21 CFR 882.5890, whose regulation name is "Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulator for Pain Relief", while the 2019 comparison table gives the device classification name as "Stimulator, Transcutaneous Electrical," "Aesthetic Purposes".[^k2019] A facial-toning gadget is therefore cleared under the nerve-stimulator-for-pain regulation, with an aesthetic classification sitting inside it. That is the same kind of mismatch as an LED device cleared under a laser regulation, documented at [[dpl-spotlite]], and it is why a product code or regulation number tells you how an agency filed something rather than what it does.

**An efficacy claim appears inside the regulatory filing itself.** The 2014 device description states the Mini "delivers soft wave micro-current in the millionths of an ampere and has the ability to increase facial contour and firm the skin and muscles".[^k2014] That sentence sits a few paragraphs above the one saying the equivalence determination rests on non-clinical performance. Both are in the same document, submitted by the same firm, and only one of them was tested.

**The retail name and the cleared name are not identical.** The clearances are for the "NuFACE Mini Device"; what the brand sells today is the MINI+.[^openfda][^brand] The overlap is much closer than the Lux naming gap at [[revive-light-therapy-lux-clinical]], and it is still not proof that the unit in the box is the unit in the file, so this page does not assert that it is.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. This is a cosmetic device cleared under a nerve-stimulation regulation; anyone with a pacemaker or other implanted electrical device, or who is pregnant, should read the manufacturer's contraindications before using any microcurrent device rather than taking a general page's word for it.

## Common Marketing Claims

Quarantined from the graded assessment above. All are the brand's own wording, cached 2026-10-22.[^brand]

- "sculpts and tones all 69 muscles in the face and neck to visibly firm, lift, and define". The headline claim, and none of those verbs appears in either cleared indication.
- "powered by the same professional-grade, FDA-cleared technology". Accurate in a narrow sense, since the clearances do exist and do cover this technology, and the thing cleared is stimulation for cosmetic use.
- "sculpt in just 5 minutes" and "3 intensities". A usage instruction and a specification.
