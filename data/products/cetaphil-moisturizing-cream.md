Cetaphil Moisturizing Cream is a fragrance-free cream moisturizer whose current US ingredient list is led by water, glycerin, and petrolatum, with panthenol and niacinamide as added barrier ingredients; it does not contain ceramides.[^1]

> A widely available, dermatologist-recommended fragrance-free moisturizer for dry and sensitive skin (see [[moisturizing]]). It is a plain glycerin-and-petrolatum cream with added panthenol and niacinamide, not a ceramide cream like [[cerave-moisturizing-cream]]. A small trial that added it to a topical corticosteroid found more clearing and a strong patient preference over the corticosteroid alone; a separate small trial of the cream by itself found a hydration gain but no significant change in water loss.[^4][^5]

## Summary

Cetaphil Moisturizing Cream is a fragrance-free cream made for dry, very dry, and sensitive skin. Like other basic moisturizers, its main job is a health one: slowing the water skin loses through its surface and helping a dry or damaged barrier hold onto moisture, the general case set out on [[moisturizing]]. It does that with an occlusive ([[petrolatum]]) and a humectant ([[glycerin]]), plus panthenol (pro-vitamin B5) and [[niacinamide]] (vitamin B3) as added barrier-supporting ingredients on the current US label.[^1] [What's In It](#whats-in-it) shows a plain moisturizer without [[ceramides]]: unlike [[cerave-moisturizing-cream]], which adds three ceramides and a slow-release "MVE" delivery system, this formula names no ceramides, a composition difference rather than a verdict on which performs better.[^1][^6] The soft, smooth feel it leaves on application is a secondary, cosmetic benefit rather than the point.

Two small, product-specific trials exist, with different designs and different results. A within-patient trial that added this cream, applied three times a day, to a topical corticosteroid found participants preferred the combined side over the corticosteroid alone 96% of the time and rated it "marked to excellent improvement" more often (70% vs 55%), though the trial was unblinded to patients and was rated high risk of bias (Hanifin 1998, [in The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^5] A separate small trial of the cream alone, tested alongside three other over-the-counter moisturizers, found it significantly improved skin's water-holding capacity (capacitance) but did not significantly change water loss (TEWL), and the trial was underpowered (Leshem 2020, [in The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^4] The broader, better-evidenced case that keeping dry and barrier-impaired skin moisturized reduces flares and spares topical steroids belongs to moisturizing as a category and is set out on [[moisturizing]].

The cream carries the National Eczema Association's Seal of Acceptance, a program that reviews a product's full ingredient list and safety-testing data and requires it to be fragrance-free ([see Tolerability](#tolerability)).[^2][^3] Board-certified dermatologists interviewed by TODAY (NBC) describe Cetaphil as "gentle and dermatologist designed" and among "the most commonly recommended skin care brands by dermatologists."[^7] It is inexpensive: a 16 oz jar is listed at $18.99 on Walgreens, and the cream is listed from $15.97 to $20.99 across Amazon, Walmart, and Target ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-07-31).[^8][^7]

## The Evidence

Keeping dry, barrier-impaired, and eczema-prone skin moisturized reduces flares and spares topical corticosteroids, one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines; that general case, including the roles of occlusives, humectants, and barrier lipids such as ceramides, is set out on [[moisturizing]]. What is specific to this product is narrower, and comes from two small trials with different designs.

Hanifin 1998, described in a later systematic evaluation of the Cetaphil trial evidence, was a 3-week, within-patient (split-body), investigator-blind trial in 80 adults with mild to moderate eczema (78 completed). One side of the body received desonide 0.05% lotion twice daily plus Cetaphil Moisturizing Cream three times daily; the other side received the corticosteroid alone. Participants preferred the combined side 96% of the time versus 4% for the corticosteroid alone, and investigators rated the combined side "marked to excellent improvement" more often than the corticosteroid-alone side (70% vs 55%, P<0.01). Adverse-event reports were lower on the combined side by the end of the trial. The reviewing paper classified the trial as high risk of bias because, unlike the investigators, patients were not blinded to which side got the extra cream.[^5]

> **Bottom line.** In a small trial, adding this cream to a topical corticosteroid cleared eczema more often and was strongly preferred over the corticosteroid alone, but patients could tell which side got the cream, so the comparison was not blinded to them.

Leshem 2020 was a within-patient, observer-blind, controlled trial of four over-the-counter moisturizers, including this cream, in atopic dermatitis. Across the moisturizer-treated forearms, "capacitance significantly improved," a measure of the skin's water content, while "mean TEWL improved in the treated forearm and worsened in the untreated one, but the difference was not significant." The authors reported "the study was underpowered as recruitment fell short," and the published results were not broken out per product.[^4]

> **Bottom line.** In a small trial that included this cream, moisturizing improved a hydration measure but did not significantly reduce water loss through the skin, and the trial was too small, and too pooled across products, to draw a firm conclusion about this cream alone.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on the manufacturer's current US product page.[^1] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system. Retail ingredient listings for this product are not always in sync (some show an earlier version of the formula), so the manufacturer's current US list is used here as the reference.[^1]

Barrier and hydration ingredients:

- [[petrolatum]], an occlusive that slows water loss
- [[glycerin]], a humectant
- panthenol (pro-vitamin B5) and [[niacinamide]] (vitamin B3)
- sweet almond oil and tocopheryl acetate ([[tocopherol|vitamin E]]), an emollient and antioxidant

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, dicaprylyl ether and [[dimethicone]] (emollients), glyceryl stearate and cetyl alcohol (emulsifiers), sunflower seed oil, PEG-30 stearate, pantolactone, dimethiconol, acrylates/C10-30 alkyl acrylate crosspolymer and carbomer (thickeners), propylene glycol, disodium EDTA (chelator), benzyl alcohol and phenoxyethanol (preservatives), and sodium hydroxide and citric acid (pH adjusters).[^1]

Not in the formula: no [[ceramides]] (unlike [[cerave-moisturizing-cream]], which adds three ceramides and an MVE delivery system) and no added fragrance.[^1]

## Tolerability

Cetaphil Moisturizing Cream holds the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance.[^2] The NEA describes the Seal as a program in which a manufacturer applies and submits a full account of ingredients, concentrations, and clinical safety-testing data; to qualify, a product "cannot contain any of the ingredients on the Ecz-clusion List in any amount" and "must be fragrance-free with no scent present in the formula," with the NEA's Seal Review Panel as "the ultimate decision maker in whether or not a product is accepted." The NEA notes that "eczema is different for everyone and a product that works for one may not work for another, or even for the same person over time."[^3]

Cetaphil labels the product dermatologist tested, hypoallergenic, and non-comedogenic.[^1] These are manufacturer label claims rather than independently tested outcomes.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Clinically proven to hydrate for 48 hours and fully restores skin's moisture barrier," "significant skin barrier improvement in 3 days with complete restoration in 1 week," and "97% of users reported that skin seems more hydrated over time."** These are manufacturer-reported figures from its own testing; neither published trial above measured a 48-hour, 3-day, or 1-week restoration endpoint.[^1][^4][^5]
- **"Defends against 5 signs of skin sensitivity."** A manufacturer positioning claim tied to Cetaphil's own sensitivity framework, not an outcome measured in the trials above.[^1]
