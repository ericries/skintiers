Vtama (tapinarof) Cream, 1% is a prescription-only, steroid-free topical drug made by Organon, an aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) agonist applied once daily to treat plaque psoriasis in adults and atopic dermatitis in adults and children 2 years and older.[^dailymed] The label states plainly that the specific mechanisms by which the cream exerts its therapeutic actions are unknown, even though its receptor target, AhR, is identified.[^dailymed]

> A branded, steroid-free prescription drug with its own randomized-trial base across two separate skin diseases. In two 12-week vehicle-controlled psoriasis trials it brought roughly 4 in 10 patients to clear or almost-clear skin, versus about 6% on vehicle; in two 8-week vehicle-controlled atopic dermatitis trials it brought 45% and 46% of patients to a comparable clear or almost-clear response, versus 14% and 18% on vehicle.[^dailymed] The label lists no contraindications, but folliculitis was common on treatment (20% of psoriasis-trial patients vs 1% on vehicle) and contact dermatitis and folliculitis were each a leading cause of stopping the drug.[^dailymed]

## Summary

Vtama is the brand name for tapinarof cream 1%, a prescription topical Organon markets for two separate FDA-approved indications: the topical treatment of plaque psoriasis in adults, and the topical treatment of atopic dermatitis in adults and pediatric patients 2 years of age and older.[^dailymed] It is applied once daily as a thin layer to affected areas, is not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use, and the label lists no contraindications.[^dailymed]

The active ingredient, tapinarof, is an aryl hydrocarbon receptor agonist, a mechanism class distinct from both topical steroids and topical calcineurin inhibitors; the label states the specific mechanisms by which the cream exerts its therapeutic actions are unknown, and the graded evidence and mechanism detail live on [[tapinarof]].[^dailymed] Because it is not a corticosteroid, it carries none of the skin-thinning or steroid-tachyphylaxis concerns tied to that class, though it has its own distinct side-effect profile described below.

Vtama's plaque psoriasis approval rests on two identically designed, 12-week, vehicle-controlled trials (PSOARING 1 and PSOARING 2), and its atopic dermatitis approval rests on two identically designed, 8-week, vehicle-controlled trials (ADORING 1 and ADORING 2); [the trial numbers are set out under The Evidence](#the-evidence).[^dailymed] General disease background for the two conditions it treats is covered on [[psoriasis]] and [[atopic-dermatitis]].

## The Evidence

**Plaque psoriasis: PSOARING 1 and PSOARING 2.** Two multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials (PSOARING 1, NCT03956355, and PSOARING 2, NCT03983980) randomized a combined 1025 adults with plaque psoriasis 2:1 to VTAMA cream or vehicle cream, applied once daily for 12 weeks to any lesion regardless of anatomic location.[^dailymed] At baseline, 82% of subjects had "Moderate" disease by the 5-point Physician's Global Assessment (PGA), with mean affected body surface area of 8% (excluding scalp, palms, and soles).[^dailymed] The primary endpoint was PGA Treatment Success, a PGA score of "Clear" or "Almost Clear" plus at least a 2-grade improvement from baseline. At week 12, treatment success was reached by 36% of the VTAMA group vs 6% on vehicle in PSOARING 1 (difference 29%, 95% CI 22% to 36%), and by 40% vs 6% in PSOARING 2 (difference 34%, 95% CI 27% to 41%).[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: against an inert vehicle cream, roughly 4 in 10 psoriasis patients using Vtama reached clear or almost-clear skin, versus about 1 in 20 on vehicle.

Of the subjects who achieved complete clearance (PGA 0) and had Vtama withdrawn, the median time to first worsening back to at least "Mild" disease was 114 days (95% CI 85 to 142) over up to 40 additional weeks of follow-up in the open-label PSOARING 3 extension.[^dailymed]

**Atopic dermatitis: ADORING 1 and ADORING 2.** Two multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials (ADORING 1, NCT05014568, and ADORING 2, NCT05032859) randomized a combined 813 adult and pediatric subjects 2 years of age and older (80% were 2 to 17 years old) 2:1 to VTAMA cream or vehicle cream, once daily for 8 weeks.[^dailymed] Baseline disease was "Moderate" in 87% of subjects by the validated Investigator's Global Assessment for atopic dermatitis (vIGA-AD), with mean affected body surface area of 16.8% and a mean baseline EASI score of 12.9.[^dailymed] The primary endpoint was vIGA-AD Treatment Success, a score of "Clear" or "Almost Clear" plus at least a 2-grade improvement. At week 8, treatment success was reached by 45% of the VTAMA group vs 14% on vehicle in ADORING 1 (difference 32%, 95% CI 23% to 40%), and by 46% vs 18% in ADORING 2 (difference 29%, 95% CI 19% to 38%).[^dailymed] Among subjects 12 and older with a baseline Peak Pruritus Numeric Rating Scale (PP-NRS) score of 4 or higher, a 4-point or greater improvement in itch was reached by 56% vs 34% in ADORING 1 and 53% vs 24% in ADORING 2.[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: against vehicle, roughly 45% of atopic dermatitis patients using Vtama reached clear or almost-clear skin over 8 weeks, versus 14 to 18% on vehicle, with a matching improvement in itch among older patients.[^adoring]

Among the subjects who reached complete clearance (vIGA-AD 0) and had Vtama withdrawn, the median time to first worsening back to at least "Mild" disease was 57 days (95% CI 30 to 79) over up to 48 additional weeks of follow-up.[^dailymed] That is notably shorter than the 114-day remission window seen after clearing psoriasis, indicating the atopic dermatitis benefit fades faster once the drug is stopped.

The full mechanism-of-action detail (an AhR agonist with an unknown specific mechanism, per the label) and any cross-trial evidence beyond this product's own approval trials are set out on [[tapinarof]].[^dailymed]

## What's In It

- **Active: tapinarof 1%** (10 mg per gram of cream), an aryl hydrocarbon receptor agonist.[^dailymed] See [[tapinarof]] for the graded mechanism and full evidence base.
- **Inactive ingredients:** benzoic acid, butylated hydroxytoluene, citric acid monohydrate, diethylene glycol monoethyl ether, edetate disodium, emulsifying wax, medium-chain triglycerides, polyoxyl 2 stearyl ether, polyoxyl 20 stearyl ether, polysorbate 80, propylene glycol, purified water, and sodium citrate dihydrate, as declared on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

The label lists no contraindications.[^dailymed] In the psoriasis trials, folliculitis occurred in 20% of Vtama-treated patients vs 1% on vehicle, and adverse reactions leading to discontinuation in more than 1% of patients were contact dermatitis (2.9%) and folliculitis (2.8%).[^dailymed] Two subjects (0.3%) using Vtama in the psoriasis trials developed urticaria.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Steroid-free."** This is accurate as a statement of drug class: tapinarof is an aryl hydrocarbon receptor agonist, not a corticosteroid, and the label confirms the mechanism is distinct from steroid action.[^dailymed] It is not, on its own, evidence that the drug is free of its own side effects; folliculitis and contact dermatitis were each common enough in trials to drive discontinuations.[^dailymed]
- **Once-daily application to any affected lesion.** The label's own dosing instruction is simply to apply a thin layer once daily to affected areas, with no body-surface-area dose cap stated, and the psoriasis trials dosed lesions regardless of anatomic location; the approval trials themselves enrolled patients with a defined, moderate range of BSA involvement (mean about 8% for psoriasis and about 17% for atopic dermatitis), not unlimited coverage.[^dailymed]
