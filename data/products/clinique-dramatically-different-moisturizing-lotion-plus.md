The Clinique Dramatically Different Moisturizing Lotion+ is the brand's iconic yellow daily face moisturizer, a fragrance-free occlusive-and-humectant lotion. Clinique lists the 4.2 fl oz bottle at $49.00.[^clinique]

> This is a well-formulated classic moisturizer: occlusives (mineral oil, petrolatum) slow water loss while humectants (glycerin, urea, sodium hyaluronate) pull in water, the standard moisturizing mechanism whose evidence is set out on [[moisturizing]]. It is genuinely fragrance-free, which is a plus for reactive skin, but it also contains lanolin alcohol, a known contact sensitizer for a minority of people, and three colorant dyes that give it the signature yellow, so "allergy tested" does not mean allergen-free. There is no published trial of this specific formula.

Clinique markets this as its "best-selling moisturizing lotion," a "dermatologist-developed nontoxic formula that strengthens and hydrates skin's moisture barrier, and leaves skin glowing," and states it is "dermatologist tested, allergy tested, and 100% fragrance free," "non-acnegenic," and "suitable for post-procedure skin."[^clinique] The general evidence for why occlusive and humectant moisturizers work, and how to use them, lives on [[moisturizing]]; this page covers what is specific to this product. It ships as a 4.2 fl oz bottle for $49.00 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-10-01).[^clinique]

## The Evidence

There is no independently published clinical trial of this specific formula. Its moisturizing job is the well-established occlusive-plus-humectant mechanism set out on [[moisturizing]]: the first four ingredients after water are [[mineral-oil]] and [[petrolatum]] (occlusives that slow transepidermal water loss) interleaved with [[glycerin]] (a humectant), and the formula also carries [[urea]] and sodium hyaluronate as further humectants. That is a sound moisturizer build, and the "strengthens and hydrates the moisture barrier" claim is consistent with it, though "nontoxic" and "leaves skin glowing" are marketing language rather than measured outcomes.[^clinique]

Two product-specific caveats are worth naming. First, the formula contains lanolin alcohol, a well-recognized contact allergen for a minority of users; "allergy tested" is a brand testing claim, not a guarantee the product is allergen-free, and lanolin-sensitive people should note it. Second, it contains added colorant dyes that produce the signature yellow but add nothing to performance (listed in full under What's In It below). It is, however, genuinely fragrance-free: no fragrance or parfum appears in the ingredient list, consistent with the brand's "100% fragrance free" claim.[^clinique]

> **Bottom line.** A classic fragrance-free occlusive-and-humectant moisturizer (4.2 fl oz, $49.00).[^clinique] It moisturizes on the standard, well-evidenced mechanism covered on [[moisturizing]], with no trial of this exact formula. The honest caveats are lanolin alcohol (a possible sensitizer) and three cosmetic dyes; the plus is that it is truly fragrance-free.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), as decoded by incidecoder: Water, Mineral Oil, Glycerin, Petrolatum, Stearic Acid, Glyceryl Stearate, Sesamum Indicum (Sesame) Oil, Urea, Lanolin Alcohol, Triethanolamine, Hordeum Vulgare (Barley) Extract, Cucumis Sativus (Cucumber) Fruit Extract, Helianthus Annuus (Sunflower) Seedcake, Propylene Glycol Dicaprate, Sodium Hyaluronate, Butylene Glycol, Pentylene Glycol, Trisodium EDTA, Phenoxyethanol, Yellow 6 (CI 15985), Yellow 5 (CI 19140), Red 33 (CI 17200).

Notable: the moisturizing work is done by the occlusives [[mineral-oil]] and [[petrolatum]] and the humectants [[glycerin]], [[urea]], and sodium hyaluronate, with stearic acid and glyceryl stearate as the emulsifier and emollient base. Lanolin alcohol is an emollient that is also a recognized contact sensitizer for some people. The barley, cucumber, and sunflower entries are low-level botanical extracts. Yellow 6, Yellow 5, and Red 33 are the colorant dyes behind the lotion's signature yellow. No fragrance is listed.
