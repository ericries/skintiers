Beauty of Joseon Matte Sun Stick is a Korean twist-up stick sunscreen, 18 g, rated SPF50 PA++++, built on the same family of modern organic UV filters as the brand's liquid Relief Sun and marketed around a mugwort-and-camellia botanical blend and a sebum-absorbing matte finish.[^1][^2]

> A broad-spectrum sunscreen in stick form, and broad-spectrum protection is the best-evidenced way to lower photoaging and sun-related skin-cancer risk ([[sunscreen-uv-filters]]). Its SPF50 PA++++ rating is a Korean-regulatory label claim rather than an independently lab-tested or FDA-tested figure, and unlike the brand's liquid Relief Sun, which Consumer Reports lab-tested directly, no independent test of this exact stick was found.[^1][^2] A stick is rubbed directly onto skin rather than dispensed as a measured dose, and real-world sunscreen application generally falls well below the tested laboratory amount regardless of format, an application-dose gap covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## Summary

Matte Sun Stick is a daily facial sunscreen from the Korean brand Beauty of Joseon, sold as an 18 g twist-up stick rather than the brand's more familiar liquid or cream sunscreens. Like Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics, its core purpose is health rather than cosmetics: broad-spectrum UV protection is the best-evidenced way to reduce photoaging and lower the risk of sun-related skin cancer, a case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

The filter set is built entirely on modern organic UV filters standard in Korea and the EU but outside the current US OTC sunscreen monograph, with one recent exception. [[ethylhexyl-triazone]] (Uvinul T 150) is the UVB filter; [[diethylamino-hydroxybenzoyl-hexyl-benzoate]] (Uvinul A Plus) is a dedicated long-wave UVA-I filter; and [[bemotrizinol]] (Tinosorb S) is a broad-spectrum photostable filter whose FDA regulatory history, only entering the US monograph in June 2026, decades after it became standard elsewhere, is detailed on that ingredient's own page. The label carries a PA++++ rating, the top of the graded UVA scale used in Korea and the EU, and SPF50, one notch below the SPF50+ label on the brand's liquid Relief Sun.[^1][^2]

What is distinct about this SKU is the format and the marketing, not a different level of independent testing. It is positioned around Artemisia capillaris extract ("mugwort") and camellia (Camellia japonica seed oil, Camellia sinensis leaf extract and seed oil), plus a silica- and boron-nitride-based texture system the brand markets as a sebum-absorbing "matte formula."[^1] Those botanical and texture claims are cosmetic positioning, separate from the graded UV-protection benefit, and are quarantined [below](#common-marketing-claims). No independent lab test of this specific stick was found; the brand's liquid Relief Sun, by contrast, was lab-tested head-to-head against a US reformulation by Consumer Reports, with results carried on [[beauty-of-joseon-relief-sun-rice-probiotics-spf50]]. Stick sunscreens are also rubbed directly onto skin rather than measured out, and real-world application of any sunscreen format tends to fall well below the tested dose, a general finding covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. It is inexpensive, listed at about $18 for 18 g, discounted to roughly $13 at the time of access, at [YesStyle](#where-to-buy) (accessed 2026-08-07).[^1][^2]

## The Evidence

Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, among the strongest clinical evidence in skincare; that evidence, and how the SPF (UVB) and PA/UVA axes differ, is set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. What is specific to this product is its filter set, its Korean label ratings, and the fact that no independent test of this exact stick was found. It is a cosmetic sunscreen sold in Korea and reaching US buyers through K-beauty retailers, not an FDA-reviewed OTC drug, and it carries no FDA Drug Facts panel.[^1][^2]

The label's SPF50 is a UVB (sunburn) rating and PA++++ is the top grade of the Persistent Pigment Darkening (PPD) based UVA scale used in Korea and the EU, a graded measure unlike the US system's single pass/fail "Broad Spectrum" test; both scales are explained on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. The formula pairs one UVB filter, ethylhexyl triazone, with two UVA filters, diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate and bemotrizinol, all modern organic filters standard in Korea and the EU.[^1][^2] Each filter's own absorption and stability data are covered on its own page: [[ethylhexyl-triazone]], [[diethylamino-hydroxybenzoyl-hexyl-benzoate]], and [[bemotrizinol]].

No product-specific lab test of this stick was found. That sets it apart from the same brand's liquid Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics, which Consumer Reports lab-tested directly against a US reformulation and scored 5 of 5 for both UVA and UVB protection, with an average tested SPF of 36 against a 50+ label, a result reported, cited, on [[beauty-of-joseon-relief-sun-rice-probiotics-spf50]]. No comparable independent test of the Matte Sun Stick's delivered SPF or PA was found.

> **Bottom line.** SPF50 PA++++ is a Korean-regulatory label claim built on a modern three-filter organic system, not an independently lab-tested or FDA-tested figure; no independent test of this specific stick was found, unlike the same brand's liquid Relief Sun.[^1][^2]

A stick format also raises a general, not product-specific, application concern. A stick is rubbed directly onto the skin rather than dispensed as a measured amount, the same practical gap that affects any sunscreen format; how far real-world application typically falls below the tested laboratory dose is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. No study isolating the applied dose of this stick specifically was found.

## What's In It

The ingredient list as declared on the importer listing, cross-checked against the INCIDecoder ingredient database.[^1] UV filters and other functional ingredients are linked to their own pages; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

UV filters (all modern organic filters; bemotrizinol's June 2026 US OTC monograph entry, and the others' continued absence from it, is detailed on each filter's own page):

- [[ethylhexyl-triazone]] (Uvinul T 150), a UVB filter
- [[diethylamino-hydroxybenzoyl-hexyl-benzoate]] (Uvinul A Plus), a UVA-I filter
- [[bemotrizinol]] (bis-ethylhexyloxyphenol methoxyphenyl triazine, Tinosorb S), a broad-spectrum filter

Other functional ingredients:

- Camellia japonica seed oil, Camellia sinensis leaf extract, and Camellia sinensis seed oil, the "camelia" positioning
- Artemisia capillaris extract, the "mugwort" positioning
- [[centella-asiatica]], [[hyaluronic-acid]], and [[panthenol]]
- A broader botanical blend: turmeric root, neem flower/leaf/bark, coralline algae, holy basil leaf, moringa seed oil, ivy gourd fruit, eggplant fruit, aloe flower, jojoba seed oil, bamboo, noni fruit, and mung bean seed extracts
- Amber powder, declared in the INCI between the eggplant fruit extract and water but not called out in the brand's marketing claims
- Silica and boron nitride, listed high on the ingredient list, the ingredients behind the sebum-absorbing "matte" texture claim
- [[glycerin]], a humectant

Base, texture, and preservative system: methyl methacrylate crosspolymer, synthetic wax, dibutyl adipate, coco-caprylate/caprate, isopropyl palmitate, caprylyl methicone, caprylyl trimethicone, ethylhexyl palmitate, vinyl dimethicone/methicone silsesquioxane crosspolymer, butyloctyl salicylate, microcrystalline wax, polysilicone-15, vinyl dimethicone, polyglyceryl-4 diisostearate/polyhydroxystearate/sebacate, water, 1,2-hexanediol, butylene glycol, propanediol, methylpropanediol, and ethylhexylglycerin.[^1]

Not in the formula: no zinc oxide or titanium dioxide (mineral filters) and no avobenzone or octinoxate; the whole filter system is modern organic filters, the same filter family as the brand's liquid Relief Sun though not an identical set.[^1][^2]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded UV evidence above:

- **"Mugwort" with "Vitamin A & C and minerals," "deeply moisturizes and smoothens skin."** The formula lists Artemisia capillaris extract along with camellia and green tea extracts.[^1] This soothing/nourishing positioning is a manufacturer claim, cosmetic and separate from the graded sun-protection benefit.
- **"Matte formula enables smooth and fresh application without clumping," sebum-control "long-lasting silky finish."** Silica and boron nitride are listed high on the ingredient list, ingredients used for oil absorption and soft-focus texture, consistent with a mattifying claim, though no independent sebum-control measurement of this product was found.[^1]
