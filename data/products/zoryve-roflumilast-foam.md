Zoryve topical foam, 0.3% is a once-daily, steroid-free phosphodiesterase-4 (PDE4) inhibitor from Arcutis, dispensed from a pressurized aerosol can and FDA-approved for seborrheic dermatitis and, separately, for plaque psoriasis of the scalp and body.[^dailymed] It is a distinct formulation from the sibling [[zoryve-roflumilast-cream]], which carries different indications (psoriasis and atopic dermatitis) at different strengths.

> A genuine, non-steroidal foam for a condition, seborrheic dermatitis, that is usually managed with antifungal shampoos or intermittent topical steroids. Its approval rests on real vehicle-controlled trial data: roughly 3 in 4 to 4 in 5 patients reached clear or almost-clear skin at 8 weeks, against roughly 4 in 10 to 6 in 10 on an inert foam. That is evidence against a placebo foam, not a head-to-head win over antifungals or steroids, and the full condition-level context lives on [[seborrheic-dermatitis]]. The foam base is flammable, a real label warning worth knowing before using it near an open flame or while smoking.

## Summary

Zoryve foam is regulated as a drug. The label lists the active as roflumilast, a PDE4 inhibitor, at 0.3% (3 mg per gram) in a white to off-white foam dispensed from 60-gram pressurized aluminum cans.[^dailymed] It carries two separate FDA indications in the same formulation: seborrheic dermatitis in patients 9 and older, and plaque psoriasis of the scalp and body in patients 12 and older.[^dailymed] Applied once daily to affected areas of scalp and/or body, rubbed in completely, with hands washed after application.[^dailymed]

Being non-steroidal is the product's central claim, and it is accurate: this is a PDE4 inhibitor, not a corticosteroid, so it avoids the skin-thinning and rebound concerns that limit how long and how often topical steroids can be used on the face and scalp. That distinguishes it from ketoconazole or selenium sulfide shampoos and from short-course topical steroids, the usual seborrheic dermatitis options, without this page claiming it beats either head-to-head, since the trials compared it to vehicle foam, not to those active comparators.

## The Evidence

Two labeled uses, graded above; the condition-level rankings live on the hub pages and are not repeated here.

**Seborrheic dermatitis (the lead use).** Two randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials, STRATUM (NCT04973228) and Trial 203 (NCT04091646), enrolled 683 adult and pediatric subjects 9 and older with seborrheic dermatitis of the scalp, face, and/or body, all with a baseline Investigator Global Assessment (IGA) of moderate or severe.[^dailymed] Subjects were randomized 2:1 to roflumilast foam 0.3% or vehicle foam once daily for 8 weeks. The primary endpoint, IGA treatment success at Week 8 (a score of "Clear" or "Almost Clear" plus a 2-grade improvement from baseline), was reached by 79.5% of the roflumilast group versus 58.0% on vehicle in STRATUM (treatment difference 20.6%, 95% CI 11.2% to 30.0%), and by 73.1% versus 40.8% in Trial 203 (treatment difference 33.8%, 95% CI 20.3% to 47.4%).[^dailymed] Among the 67% of STRATUM subjects with a baseline itch score (Worst Itch-Numeric Rating Scale) of at least 4, 62.8% of the roflumilast group achieved at least a 4-point itch reduction by Week 8, against 40.6% on vehicle, a treatment difference of 25.7% (95% CI 13.4% to 38.1%).[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: against an inert foam, once-daily roflumilast foam brought roughly 3 in 4 to 4 in 5 patients with moderate-to-severe seborrheic dermatitis to clear or almost-clear skin at 8 weeks, versus roughly 4 in 10 to 6 in 10 on vehicle, with a meaningful itch reduction as a secondary finding.

**Plaque psoriasis of the scalp and body (the second use).** Two further randomized, vehicle-controlled trials, ARRECTOR (NCT05028582) and Trial 204 (NCT04128007), enrolled 736 subjects 12 and older with mild-to-severe plaque psoriasis of the scalp and body, also randomized 2:1 to the same foam versus vehicle for 8 weeks.[^dailymed] This is the same active molecule and formulation as the seborrheic dermatitis indication, applied to a different disease; the full psoriasis treatment landscape and how this foam compares to other options is on [[psoriasis]].

Both indications share one label limit worth naming plainly: the comparator in every registration trial is an inert vehicle foam, not an active steroid, antifungal shampoo, or the cream version of this same drug, so nothing here demonstrates it outperforms the treatments patients are typically switching from.

## What's In It

- **Active: roflumilast 0.3%** (3 mg per gram of foam), a phosphodiesterase-4 (PDE4) inhibitor.[^dailymed]
- **Foam base:** ceteareth-10 phosphate, cetearyl phosphate, cetostearyl alcohol, diethylene glycol monoethyl ether, hexylene glycol, isopropyl palmitate, methylparaben, propylparaben, purified water, sodium hydroxide, and white petrolatum, as declared on the FDA label; hydrochloric acid may have been added to adjust pH.[^dailymed] The can is pressurized with propellant (butane, isobutane, and propane).[^dailymed]

The label's most common adverse reactions (at least 1% of subjects) in the seborrheic dermatitis trials were nasopharyngitis (1.5%), nausea (1.3%), and headache (1.1%); diarrhea and insomnia were reported in fewer than 1%.[^dailymed] The label carries one contraindication, moderate to severe liver impairment (Child-Pugh B or C), and one specific warning: the propellants are flammable, so the label directs patients to avoid fire, flame, and smoking during and immediately after application.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Steroid-free."** Accurate: the active, roflumilast, is a PDE4 inhibitor, not a corticosteroid, and the label carries none of the skin-thinning warnings that accompany topical steroids.[^dailymed]
- **"Once-daily."** Correct; the label directs once-daily application to affected areas of body and/or scalp.[^dailymed]
- **"Fast, visible relief."** The label's own trial data support meaningful improvement by Week 8 against vehicle, including a measured itch reduction in STRATUM; the sources reviewed here do not include an earlier timepoint at which superiority over vehicle was established, so "fast" is not a claim this page can independently verify.[^dailymed]
