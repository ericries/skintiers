La Roche-Posay Retinol B3 Serum is a $44.99, 1 fl oz evening leave-on serum pairing retinol with niacinamide (vitamin B3), sold at Target as "Hypoallergenic, For Sensitive and Mature Skin."[^target]

> Topical retinol has vehicle-controlled trial evidence for improving fine wrinkles and pigment in photoaged skin, and niacinamide has modest, preliminary evidence for barrier support; both are set out on [[retinol]], [[retinoids]], and [[niacinamide]]. No published trial tests this exact serum, and Target's listing, the only fetchable source for this formula, does not disclose a retinol percentage, describing it instead as "pure + gradual release retinol."[^target] That undisclosed strength is a point where a same-brand comparator, [[la-roche-posay-toleriane]], and a cheaper category comparator, [[trader-joes-retinol-night-serum|Trader Joe's Retinol Night Serum]] (which declares 0.3% on its own label), differ from this product.

## Summary

La Roche-Posay Retinol B3 Serum is a 1 fl oz leave-on facial serum priced at $44.99 on Target, positioned by the brand for evening use on the face and neck, "Use alone and always add a moisturizer and SPF to your routine when using this product."[^target] Target's listing describes it as "La Roche-Posay Pure Retinol Face Serum with Vitamin B3... Formulated with pure + gradual release retinol, vitamin B3, and glycerin."[^target]

The general case for topical retinol, including its evidence for fine wrinkles and pigment in photoaged skin, is set out on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]; niacinamide's evidence for barrier support is set out on [[niacinamide]]. What is distinct about this product is what it does not disclose: unlike [[trader-joes-retinol-night-serum|Trader Joe's Retinol Night Serum]], which declares 0.3% retinol on its own label, Target's listing for this serum gives no percentage for retinol, only the marketing description "pure + gradual release retinol" [see the evidence](#the-evidence).[^target] No product-specific trial was located.

## The Evidence

Topical retinol has category-level, vehicle-controlled trial evidence for improving fine wrinkles and pigment in aging skin; that evidence is set out in full on [[retinol]] and [[retinoids]]. Niacinamide's general evidence for supporting the skin barrier is modest and preliminary, set out on [[niacinamide]]. No published trial tests this exact serum.

What is verifiable about this product specifically is limited to its declared ingredient list and marketing copy on Target, the only source that could be fetched for this listing; La Roche-Posay's own US product page could not be retrieved. Target describes the retinol as "pure + gradual release retinol" and states the serum "visibly reduces the look of lines, wrinkles, & signs of premature sun damage,"[^target] but gives no concentration figure, so the retinol dose relative to the 0.1%-0.4% range with controlled-trial evidence cited on [[retinol]] cannot be determined from what was fetched. Niacinamide appears 6th of 21 ingredients on Target's declared list, ahead of the retinol/adenosine pairing, glycine soja (soybean) oil, and sodium hyaluronate that follow it, a composition fact consistent with a functional rather than trace inclusion, though INCI order alone does not establish a concentration.[^target]

> **Bottom line.** No trial tests this exact serum, and the only fetchable listing does not disclose a retinol percentage, describing the active only as "pure + gradual release retinol." The category-level case for retinol and for niacinamide is set out on [[retinol]], [[retinoids]], and [[niacinamide]].[^target]
