The Ordinary Multi-Peptide + HA Serum (sold for years as "Buffet") is a water-based leave-on serum whose ingredient list combines five peptide complexes with hyaluronic acid and a set of amino-acid natural-moisturizing-factor ingredients.[^1]

> Whether topical peptides reduce wrinkles is covered on [[peptides]]. A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of 19 peptide trials found a modest pooled effect on wrinkles that was largely driven by oral, not topical, peptides, with inconsistent effects on elasticity and skin density.[^2] The one widely cited human study behind one of this serum's peptides (Argireline) reported wrinkle-depth reduction "up to 30%," but it tested a 10% single-peptide emulsion, a far higher single-peptide dose than this blend's undisclosed level.[^3] The humectant base (glycerin, hyaluronic acid, amino acids) hydrates.

## The Evidence

Whether topical peptides signal skin to firm or smooth, and how well the evidence holds up, is covered on [[peptides]]. What is specific to this product is that it stacks several peptide technologies in one hydrating base and markets them for the appearance of aging.

Nukaly 2026 was a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials of peptides for skin aging, pooling both oral and topical products. "Nineteen RCTs involving 1,341 participants were analyzed. Peptides, particularly oral formulations, significantly improved hydration and brightness, with a modest pooled effect on wrinkle reduction (MD = 0.27, p = 0.04)." A subgroup analysis "indicated that this benefit was largely driven by oral polypeptides (MD = 1.5, p = 0.01)." The authors add that "while effects on elasticity and density were inconsistent, peptides were well tolerated," and conclude that "larger RCTs with standardized outcomes and histopathologic assessment are warranted."[^2]

> **Bottom line.** Across 19 trials, the measurable wrinkle benefit was small and came mostly from peptides taken by mouth, not applied to skin. Effects on firmness and skin density were inconsistent. This serum is a topical.

Blanes-Mira 2002 is the study behind Argireline (acetyl hexapeptide-8), one of this serum's peptides. In cells, "Argireline significantly inhibited neurotransmitter release with a potency similar to that of BoNT A, although as expected, it displayed much lower efficacy than the neurotoxin." On skin, "skin topography analysis of an oil/water (O/W) emulsion containing 10% of the hexapeptide on healthy women volunteers reduced wrinkle depth up to 30% upon 30 days treatment."[^3]

> **Bottom line.** The "up to 30%" figure often attached to Argireline comes from a single study of a 10% single-peptide emulsion.[^3] This serum blends acetyl hexapeptide-8 with four other peptides, lists it low in the ingredient order, and does not disclose its concentration.[^1]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] The peptides and humectants are linked or named; the rest is the base and preservative system.

Peptide technologies (all link to [[peptides]]):

- acetyl hexapeptide-8 (Argireline) and pentapeptide-18 (Leuphasyl), the pair the brand markets as ARGIRELOX
- palmitoyl tripeptide-1 and palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7, the pair sold as Matrixyl 3000
- palmitoyl tripeptide-38, sold as Matrixyl synthe'6
- dipeptide diaminobutyroyl benzylamide diacetate, sold as SYN-AKE
- acetylarginyltryptophyl diphenylglycine, sold as Relistase

Humectants and skin-identical ingredients:

- [[glycerin]], the second ingredient
- sodium hyaluronate crosspolymer and sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]])
- an amino-acid and sugar "natural moisturizing factor" set (glycine, alanine, serine, proline and others; trehalose, glucose, fructose, maltose; urea, sodium PCA, PCA, sodium lactate) with allantoin
- lactococcus ferment lysate, a bacterial ferment

Base and preservative system: water, butylene glycol and pentylene glycol (solvents), acacia senegal gum, xanthan gum and carbomer (thickeners), polysorbate 20, PPG-26-buteth-26 and PEG-40 hydrogenated castor oil (solubilizers), hydroxypropyl cyclodextrin, sodium chloride, citric acid and sodium hydroxide (pH adjusters), trisodium ethylenediamine disuccinate (chelator), ethoxydiglycol, and the preservatives sodium benzoate, caprylyl glycol, ethylhexylglycerin, phenoxyethanol and chlorphenesin.

The formula contains no [[retinoids]], no vitamin C ([[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]]), no [[niacinamide]] and no exfoliating acids, and no added fragrance or silicone.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the evidence above:

- **"five well-studied peptide technologies" that reduce "the appearance of crow's feet," improve "skin elasticity" and enhance "firmness."** The five peptide complexes appear in the ingredient list.[^1] The independent meta-analysis found the topical peptide effect on wrinkles small and mostly attributable to oral peptides, and effects on elasticity and density inconsistent.[^2]
- **The "up to 30%" wrinkle figure associated with Argireline.** It comes from a study of a 10% single-peptide emulsion, not this multi-peptide serum.[^3]
- **Ingredient trade names (Matrixyl 3000, Matrixyl synthe'6, SYN-AKE, ARGIRELOX, Relistase).** These are peptide-supplier brand names, not independent clinical outcomes for this serum.
