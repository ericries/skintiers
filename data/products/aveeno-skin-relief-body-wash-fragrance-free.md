## Summary

A fragrance-free gel body wash sold for "itchy, dry skin" and "formulated for sensitive skin", carrying an oat story the brand calls Triple Oat.[^brand] **Bottom line:** it is a cosmetic cleanser, not a treatment, and the three things worth knowing about it are that its oat content is not the monograph active that oat is graded for, that its primary surfactant is a sulfate, and that the only efficacy support offered anywhere on its page is a consumer-perception figure. Oat's own evidence lives on [[colloidal-oatmeal]], graded mid, and it was earned in leave-on products.

> **The same sub-line contains registered drugs. This is not one of them.** Querying openFDA's drug listing directory for the brand name "Aveeno Skin Relief" returns lotions, an overnight cream and a healing ointment, every one of them marketing category OTC MONOGRAPH DRUG with a declared active: dimethicone at 12 mg/mL and 12 mg/g, and glycerin at 400 mg/g in the ointment.[^ndc] No body wash appears among them. The wash declares no active ingredient at all, so when the carton and the lotion carry the same Skin Relief name, one of them is a drug making a skin-protectant claim and the other is soap.

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: minimal, for cleansing.** Glycerin sits second on the declared list, ahead of both surfactants, so the formula does carry humectant in quantity.[^brand] It is also a rinse-off, in contact with skin for the length of a shower, and the brand publishes no corneometry, no transepidermal water loss figure and no hydration measurement of any kind.[^brand]
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** The one efficacy number on the page is "9 out of 10 users agreed this product left itchy, dry skin feeling soothed & moisturized".[^brand] Users agreeing is self-reported perception. The page gives no sample size, no duration, no comparator and no definition of agreement, and a separate star rating of 4.3 from 2,311 reviews is brand-hosted feedback rather than a measurement.[^brand]
- **Effect size: unknown, for the oat claim.** The declared list carries three oat fractions in a row, Avena Sativa (Oat) Kernel Flour, Oat Kernel Oil and Oat Kernel Extract, which is what Triple Oat refers to.[^brand] None is declared as colloidal oatmeal, the over-the-counter skin protectant active, and a case-insensitive search of the brand page finds the word colloidal nowhere on it.[^brand] So the ingredient that carries oat's evidence here is not the one the brand is selling.
- **Composition note: the primary surfactant is a sulfate.** Sodium Laureth Sulfate is third on the list, ahead of [[cocamidopropyl-betaine]] in fourth, which makes the sulfate the lead cleansing agent rather than a minor one in a product positioned for sensitive skin.[^brand] Sodium laureth sulfate is the ethoxylated, milder relative of sodium lauryl sulfate and this page is not calling it harsh; the point is only that a shopper choosing this for reactive skin may assume a sulfate-free formula and would be wrong.
- **Effect size: not applicable; a record finding.** The composition claims are true as stated. The declared list contains no fragrance or parfum, no paraben and no colourant, matching "free of fragrances, parabens, dyes & soaps", and its only preservative is sodium benzoate.[^brand]

## The Evidence

There is no clinical trial of this product. The brand page offers one consumer-perception result and a star rating, and nothing read for this page measured an outcome on skin.[^brand]

What exists is evidence for an ingredient the product does not declare. Colloidal oatmeal is graded mid on [[colloidal-oatmeal]], and that work was done with leave-on vehicles. Reading it across to a wash requires two leaps the evidence does not make: that an oat flour, oil and extract blend behaves like colloidal oatmeal, and that a few seconds of contact before rinsing does what a cream left on the skin does. Neither is established here, which is why both oat-related grades on this page are unknown rather than low.

The useful comparison is internal. The Skin Relief lotion in the same sub-line is a registered over-the-counter drug whose active is dimethicone at 12 mg/g, a skin protectant.[^ndc] If the goal is relief of itchy, dry skin rather than washing, the leave-on member of the family is the one with a regulated active and a monograph claim behind it.

## What the aggregators say it contains

The ingredient list used throughout this page is the one Aveeno publishes on its own product page, read on 2026-10-31.[^brand] It is worth flagging that incidecoder, a widely used ingredient database, lists a materially different formula for a product of this name: it shows Quaternium-15, Tetrasodium EDTA, Myristyl Alcohol, a hydrolysed wheat protein derivative and a coriander, cardamom and myrrh extract blend, none of which appears on the brand's current list, and it places Sodium Laureth Sulfate fourth rather than third. The brand's current list instead carries sodium benzoate as the preservative and tetrasodium glutamate diacetate as the chelator. The most likely reading is that the aggregator is showing a superseded formula. This page follows the brand, and anyone cross-checking an Aveeno wash against a third-party database should check which formula they are reading. Quaternium-15 is a formaldehyde-releasing preservative, so the difference between the two lists is not cosmetic trivia for someone avoiding it.

## Common Marketing Claims

> The claims in this section are quoted from Aveeno's own product page and are **not** endorsed here. They are reproduced so a reader can see what is being asserted and what supports it.

- "Triple Oat Formula with oat flour, oat extract, and oat oil leaves itchy, dry skin soothed and feeling moisturized".[^brand] Soothed and *feeling* moisturized is the operative hedge; the support is the perception figure below.
- "9 out of 10 users agreed this product left itchy, dry skin feeling soothed & moisturized".[^brand] No sample size, duration or comparator is published.
- "allergy-tested & free of fragrances, parabens, dyes & soaps".[^brand] The second half is verifiable from the ingredient list and holds. Allergy-tested has no agreed definition and no protocol is given.
- "formulated for sensitive skin".[^brand] A formulation intent, not a result.
