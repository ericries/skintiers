## Summary

La Roche-Posay Toleriane Hydrating Gentle Cleanser is a mild, low-foaming cream cleanser made for normal to dry and sensitive skin. Its job is a health one: lifting the day's dirt, oil, and makeup without stripping the skin barrier that keeps moisture in and irritants out. It is built on a single mild amphoteric surfactant, [coco-betaine](#whats-in-it), rather than a high-foaming sulfate or a true soap, so the manufacturer labels it [sulfate free and soap free](#whats-in-it).[^1] It also carries a little of the brand's prebiotic thermal water plus [ceramide-3, niacinamide, glycerin, and panthenol](#whats-in-it), the barrier and humectant ingredients La Roche-Posay builds its Toleriane moisturizers around.

Two separate questions decide how much that is worth. The first is a category question: does a gentle, sulfate-free, low-pH cleanser protect the skin more than a harsher wash? On tolerability the category evidence leans yes. A study of 29 soaps and cleansers found that a product's irritation rose with its pH, and that sulfate-free syndet cleansers sat at the low-irritation end ([Baranda 2002](#the-evidence)).[^3] The best-evidenced routine for dry or barrier-impaired skin is leave-on moisturizing rather than a rinse-off wash, set out on [[moisturizing]] and for the barrier lipids on [[ceramides]].

The second question is whether this is a good example of a gentle cleanser, and the reputation is strong. NYT Wirecutter names it "Best for dry skin" and writes that "this milky liquid becomes a gauzy foam that efficiently cleans without robbing skin of precious moisture."[^2] Wirecutter also notes that eight of its ingredients that help skin hold moisture "are also in our CeraVe pick," placing it alongside [[cerave-hydrating-facial-cleanser]] and [[cetaphil-gentle-skin-cleanser]] as low-cost, fragrance-free, dermatologist-favored gentle cleansers.[^2] The recurring criticism is that it is plain: Wirecutter calls it "more dutiful than beautiful," with a "fragrance-free formula and weak suds" that are "far from an 'ahh-inducing' delight."[^2]

It is inexpensive. The manufacturer lists the 400 ml (13.52 fl oz) bottle at $19.99, with a 50 ml at $8.99, a 200 ml at $15.99, and a 400 ml refill at $17.99 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-07-28); Target lists the 13.5 fl oz bottle at the same $19.99.[^1][^4] The manufacturer's "maintains skin's natural moisture barrier and pH" positioning is a [label claim](#common-marketing-claims) the product-specific evidence does not establish.

## The Evidence

A cleanser's health-relevant task is to remove dirt, oil, and makeup with as little disruption to the skin barrier as possible; keeping dry or barrier-impaired skin moisturized is one of dermatology's better-evidenced routines, set out on [[moisturizing]] and, for the barrier lipids this cleanser carries, on [[ceramides]]. The [[van-zuuren-2017-emollients-moisturisers-eczema|2017 Cochrane review]] of 77 randomized trials found most moisturizers help barrier-impaired skin, and that no single one was shown to beat another. That evidence is for leave-on moisturizers rather than rinse-off cleansers.[^5]

What is specific to gentle cleansing is narrower. Baranda 2002 applied 8% emulsions of 29 soaps and liquid cleansers to the forearms of 30 people with sensitive skin for 5 consecutive days and scored the resulting irritation. "A significant correlation between pH and the IrIn [irritation index] of cleansers was found (P < 0.006)," and the low-irritation products were mostly sulfate-free syndet cleansers rather than true soaps.[^3]

> **Bottom line.** Across 29 cleansers, the more alkaline the product, the more it irritated sensitive skin, and sulfate-free syndet cleansers clustered at the low-irritation end. This is category evidence for gentle, non-alkaline washes, not a test of this product.[^3]

No published clinical trial isolates this exact cleanser. The closest product-specific assessment is hands-on review testing. NYT Wirecutter named it "Best for dry skin" among the cleansers it tested, reporting that it "efficiently cleans without robbing skin of precious moisture" and that its "light foam also flushed away easily." Wirecutter also observed that the "milky formula has a mere 19 ingredients, and eight of them (including glycerin, niacinamide, a ceramide, vitamin E, and panthenol) help skin maintain moisture," the same eight it identifies in its CeraVe pick.[^2]

> **Bottom line.** In review testing this cleanser washed without leaving dry skin stripped, and it shares eight barrier and humectant ingredients with a leading CeraVe cleanser. This is editorial testing, not a controlled trial of barrier function.[^2]

## What's In It

The ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the surfactant, texture, and preservative system.

Barrier and humectant ingredients:

- [[glycerin]], a humectant and the second ingredient
- ceramide NP (labeled ceramide-3), one of the [[ceramides]], a lipid that matches the skin's own barrier lipids
- niacinamide (vitamin B3), which at leave-on concentrations has good evidence for supporting the skin barrier and evening out tone; see [[niacinamide]]
- panthenol (provitamin B5), a humectant and skin-conditioning agent
- [[tocopherol]] (vitamin E), an antioxidant

Cleansing, texture, and preservative system: water (marketed as La Roche-Posay prebiotic thermal water), coco-betaine (a mild amphoteric surfactant) and polysorbate 60 (a nonionic surfactant and emulsifier), pentaerythrityl tetraethylhexanoate (an emollient ester), propanediol (a humectant and solvent), ammonium polyacryloyldimethyl taurate and sodium chloride (thickeners), disodium EDTA and trisodium ethylenediamine disuccinate (chelators), citric acid (a pH adjuster), and caprylyl glycol, pentylene glycol, ethylhexylglycerin, and chlorphenesin (the conditioning and preservative system).[^1]

The formula contains no added fragrance, no sulfate detergent such as sodium lauryl or laureth sulfate, no soap, no parabens, and no drying alcohol.[^1] Because ceramide-3, niacinamide, and panthenol sit in a product that rinses off, their listed presence is a fact about the formula, not the same as the leave-on doses studied for those ingredients.

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Maintains skin's natural moisture barrier and pH"** and **"helps restore skin comfort and retains essential moisture."** Manufacturer label claims.[^1] No trial isolates this cleanser; the closest category evidence is that gentle, non-alkaline cleansers irritate sensitive skin less than alkaline soaps (Baranda 2002).[^3]
- **La Roche-Posay Prebiotic Thermal Water and the skin microbiome.** The brand positions its thermal spring water, the aqua base of the formula, as supporting the skin microbiome.[^1] A positioning claim; no independent trial is cited here.
- **"94% said the product felt gentle on the skin"** and **"93% said the product rinsed easily from skin."** Manufacturer four-week consumer-study claims.[^1]
- **Awarded GQ's Best Face Wash for Sensitive Skin**, and an Allure Best of Beauty award shown on the packaging.[^1] Editorial awards reported by the manufacturer.
- **Fragrance free, non-comedogenic, allergy tested, dermatologist tested, and sensitive skin tested.** Manufacturer label claims, not independently tested outcomes.[^1]
