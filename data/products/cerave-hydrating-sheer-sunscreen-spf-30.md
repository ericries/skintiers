The CeraVe sunscreen that is not a mineral sunscreen. **Bottom line:** it is a hybrid, pairing zinc oxide with three organic filters, which makes it a different product from the mineral CeraVe sunscreens it sits beside on the shelf and is named almost identically to.

> **Read the actives before assuming.** The label lists "Homosalate 6% Octisalate 5% Octocrylene 5% Zinc oxide 7%".[^label] Three of those four sit in the group FDA put in Category III in 2019 and has still not resolved, which is a statement about the paperwork rather than about harm; [[what-otc-monograph-m020-settled-and-what-it-left-open]] tracks what has actually been settled since, and the answer is three filters out of fourteen.

## Summary

An over-the-counter drug product marketed by L'Oreal USA Products, Inc., label effective 2025-07-08, sold for face and body with 80 minutes water resistance.[^label] Its full label name carries the distinguishing word: "Hybrid Formula".[^label]

**The indication is the monograph one.** It "helps prevent sunburn" and, "if used as directed with other sun protection measures", "decreases the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging caused by the sun".[^label] Every compliant sunscreen says this; none of it is specific to this product.

**What else is in it, with a caveat about which list you read.** Both versions of the inactive list carry ceramide NP, ceramide AP, ceramide EOP, niacinamide and sodium hyaluronate, which is the CeraVe signature.[^label] Beyond those they diverge, because the FDA label's inactive-ingredient text and the carton artwork filed in the same label do not match.[^artwork] Inactive means exactly that either way: formulation components at undeclared concentrations, not tested treatments.

**No price.** None appears on the label and no retailer was fetched.

## The Evidence

**The useful work this page can do is telling two near-identical products apart.** CeraVe sells a Hydrating Mineral Sunscreen SPF 30 for face and a Hydrating Sheer Sunscreen SPF 30 for face and body. The first is mineral. The second, this one, is a hybrid containing [[homosalate]], [[octisalate]] and [[octocrylene]] alongside [[zinc-oxide]].[^label] Anyone avoiding organic filters, for whatever reason, would not get what they expected from the word CeraVe alone.

**The label disagrees with its own packaging about what is in the product.** The inactive-ingredient field of the FDA label lists an emollient system built on c12-15 alkyl benzoate, isohexadecane, dicaprylyl ether and PEG-30 dipolyhydroxystearate, and includes salicylic acid.[^label] The carton artwork filed in the same label record lists a different one, built on glycerin, coco-caprylate/caprate and butyloctyl salicylate, with oryza sativa bran wax, steareth-20 and benzoic acid, and no standalone salicylic acid.[^artwork] The active ingredients and the product name are identical on both. Which one is in the tube on a shelf today is not something this page can determine, so it names neither as the formula.

**No product-specific trial was found.** Sunscreens reach market through the OTC monograph, which requires SPF and broad spectrum testing to a standard method rather than clinical trials of the finished product, so the absence is structural rather than a lapse by this manufacturer. [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] covers why a regulatory route and an evidence base are different things.

**Directions, because they matter more than the formula.** The label says to "apply liberally 15 minutes before sun exposure" and to reapply "after 80 minutes or swimming or sweating", "immediately after towl drying" and "at least every 2 hours".[^label] The misspelling is the label's own. Sunscreen underperforms its SPF mostly through quantity and reapplication, which no formulation fixes.

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven unless it also appears in The Evidence above.

- **The CeraVe ceramide positioning.** Three ceramides appear in both versions of the inactive list and they are inactive ingredients in a sunscreen. No concentration is declared and no barrier benefit is tested for this product.[^label][^artwork]
- **Sheer and Hydrating, both words in the product name.** Cosmetic descriptors, not claims the label supports with data.
- **Developed with dermatologists.** Part of the registered brand name on the label itself rather than a finding about the product.[^label]
