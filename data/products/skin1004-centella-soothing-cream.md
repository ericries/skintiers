A cheap Korean gel-cream sold on centella, which is the weakest-evidenced thing in it. **Bottom line:** the brand's own key-ingredient line names Ceramide NP and cholesterol alongside the centella, and on this site ceramides grade better than centella does, so the quiet half of the formula is the half with the evidence. Centella's case is argued at [[centella-asiatica]] and the ceramide case at [[ceramides]]; neither is re-argued here.

> **The name sells the weakest ingredient in the tube.** [[centella-asiatica]] carries a `tier: weak` grade on this site, [[ceramides]] carries `tier: mid`. The product is called Madagascar Centella Soothing Cream and the packaging reads "MADE WITH PURE CENTELLA FROM MADAGASCAR".[^brand] A reader buying it for the centella is paying attention to the part of the label with the least behind it, and will probably still get a decent moisturizer.

## Summary

SKIN1004 sells it at **$7.50 for 30ml and $14.40 for 75ml**, both in stock on the brand's own storefront, against a listed compare-at price of $16.00.[^brand] The brand describes it as "A non sticky soothing gel formulated with Centella, Trehalose, and Ceramide NP that moisturizes the skin and helps support the skin natural barrier", with product benefits given as "Soothing, Hydrating" and skin types as "Normal, Sensitive".[^brand] Its published Key Ingredients are "Centella Asiatica Extract, Trehalose, Sodium Hyaluronate, Ceramide NP, Cholesterol".[^brand]

At roughly nineteen cents a millilitre in the larger size it is one of the cheapest ceramide-containing moisturizers this site covers.

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: minimal for the claim it is sold on.** Soothing is the lead benefit and centella is the lead ingredient, and centella is graded `tier: weak` here on its own page, [[centella-asiatica]]. No trial of this product exists, so there is nothing product-specific to raise or lower that.
- **Effect size: modest for the job it will actually do.** The brand's own list includes Ceramide NP and cholesterol, two of the three lipid classes a barrier cream is usually built around, plus sodium hyaluronate and trehalose as humectants.[^brand] Ceramides are graded `tier: mid` here, on [[ceramides]]. That grade belongs to the ingredient class and not to this tube.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary, and the limit is composition rather than claims.** The brand publishes five ingredients and calls them Key Ingredients. It does not publish a full ordered INCI on its own product page, checked by reading that page in full on 2026-11-11.[^brand] Without the order you cannot tell whether the ceramides are a meaningful fraction or a pinch at the end, which is the single thing that decides whether this works as a barrier cream.
- **Record finding: the retail name and the storefront name differ.** The product page title is "Madagascar Centella Soothing Cream" and the tube reads MADAGASCAR CENTELLA, while the brand's own product feed lists it simply as "Centella Soothing Cream".[^brand] Worth knowing when matching a tube to a listing.
- **What the tube itself states.** The brand's product photograph shows "75 ML / 2.53 FL.OZ." and "MADE WITH PURE CENTELLA FROM MADAGASCAR" on the 75ml size.[^brand] Pure centella from Madagascar is a sourcing claim, not a concentration, and the brand attaches no percentage to it anywhere on the page read.

## The Evidence

None specific to this product. What can be said is which of its ingredients have been studied and how well, and both of those arguments live elsewhere: [[centella-asiatica]] for the headline, [[ceramides]] for the barrier lipids, with the moisturizer field ranked at [[best-face-moisturizers-by-evidence]] and the ceramide products at [[best-ceramide-moisturizers]].

> **Bottom line.** At $14.40 for 75ml this is an inexpensive barrier moisturizer with a botanical name on the front. Buy it as a cheap ceramide-and-humectant cream and it is reasonable. Buy it because of the centella and you are buying the weakest claim on the label.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own product page and **not** endorsed here.

- "A non sticky soothing gel formulated with Centella, Trehalose, and Ceramide NP that moisturizes the skin and helps support the skin natural barrier."[^brand] Moisturizing and barrier support are reasonable for this combination of ingredients as a class. Soothing is the word doing the most work and the one with the thinnest support, since it rests on centella.
- "MADE WITH PURE CENTELLA FROM MADAGASCAR".[^brand] An origin claim. Nothing on the page read states how much centella the product contains.
