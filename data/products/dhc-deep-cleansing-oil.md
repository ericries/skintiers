DHC Deep Cleansing Oil is the classic Japanese olive-oil cleansing oil, used to dissolve makeup and sunscreen either as the first step of a [[double-cleansing|double cleanse]] or, as the brand positions it, a single cleansing step. DHC lists the 6.7 fl oz (200 mL) bottle at $31.50.[^dhc]

> A cleansing oil is judged by how well it removes what a water-based cleanser leaves behind, and this olive-oil-based one is built for exactly that: the trial evidence for oil cleansers clearing water-resistant sunscreen and long-wear makeup is on [[double-cleansing]]. The formula is short and classic: olive oil and a light emollient ester, an emulsifier so it rinses off, plus vitamin E, a licorice-derived soothing agent, and a little rosemary oil. The claim to read as marketing is the brand's "1-step" positioning and "clean and hydrated without any oily residue"; whether an oil cleanser alone is enough is a matter of what you are wearing, and the double-cleanse routine adds a water-based second step after the oil (see [[double-cleansing]]).

## Summary

DHC markets this as "the original Japanese 1-step oil cleanser" that "gently removes oil, dirt, and makeup, leaving your skin clean and hydrated without any oily residue."[^dhc] It is a liquid oil that turns milky and rinses off when you add water.

The formula is short: olive fruit oil (the emollient base DHC is known for), caprylic/capric triglyceride (a light spreading oil), sorbeth-30 tetraoleate (the emulsifier that lets an oil rinse clean), pentylene glycol, phenoxyethanol, vitamin E (tocopherol), stearyl glycyrrhetinate (a licorice-derived soothing ingredient), and rosemary leaf oil. The work is done by the oils and the emulsifier; the rest is preservation and a small soothing and antioxidant touch. It is listed at $31.50 for the 6.7 fl oz (200 mL) size at DHC ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-25).[^dhc]

## The Evidence

The product-specific claim worth checking is the removal one, and it is directionally right for the format: as set out on [[double-cleansing]], an oil-based cleanser removes water-resistant sunscreen that a foaming water-based cleanser leaves largely behind, so an oil cleanser is a sound first step on heavy-sunscreen or long-wear-makeup days. That controlled evidence is a single small study, and no independently published trial of this exact product is cited here; its case rests on the format. The brand's "1-step" and "no oily residue" claims are its own positioning, and the licorice-derived soothing agent and vitamin E are minor extras rather than the reason to buy a cleanser.

> **Bottom line.** A long-selling, sensibly built olive-oil cleansing oil whose real value is dissolving makeup and sunscreen, a job the double-cleanse evidence supports. It is a cleanser, not a treatment; if you do not wear water-resistant sunscreen or long-wear makeup, a single gentle cleanse does the same job, and whether it works as a true one-step cleanse depends on how much you are washing off.
