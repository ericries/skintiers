MetroGel is Galderma's brand-name formulation of metronidazole 1% gel, a prescription-only topical applied once daily to the face to treat the inflammatory lesions of [[rosacea]].[^dailymed]

> A once-daily, 1% branded formulation of metronidazole, a decades-old first-line rosacea topical. Its own FDA-cited approval trial (746 patients, 10 weeks) took inflammatory lesion counts down 50.7% against 32.6% for an inert vehicle, and brought about 4 in 10 patients to clear or almost-clear skin versus roughly 3 in 10 on vehicle.[^dailymed] Metronidazole overall is a well-evidenced, modest-effect rosacea treatment that has been outperformed by both azelaic acid 15% and ivermectin 1% in separate head-to-head trials; see [[metronidazole]] for that full comparative record.

## Summary

MetroGel is the Galderma brand of metronidazole 1% gel, prescribed once daily for the inflammatory lesions, the crops of red bumps (papules) and pus-topped spots (pustules), of [[rosacea-papulopustular|papulopustular rosacea]].[^dailymed] It is Rx-only; the FDA label carries no over-the-counter path.[^dailymed] Topical metronidazole is a long-standing, solidly evidenced rosacea treatment with a modest effect size, the reference topical against which newer options such as azelaic acid and ivermectin have been measured; the full cross-trial evidence base, including where metronidazole ranks against those newer topicals, is set out on [[metronidazole]].[^dailymed]

What this specific product adds is the once-daily 1% formulation that Galderma's own FDA submission tested: a randomized, vehicle-controlled trial of 746 rosacea patients over 10 weeks found inflammatory lesion counts fell 50.7% on the drug versus 32.6% on vehicle, and an Investigator's Global Assessment of clear or almost clear was reached by 38.42% of drug-treated patients versus 27.51% on vehicle ([the evidence](#the-evidence)).[^dailymed] The label states the mechanism by which metronidazole helps rosacea is unknown, despite the drug's classification as an antibiotic.[^dailymed]

MetroGel is dispensed only by prescription, applied once daily as a thin film to the affected areas after cleansing.[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

Topical metronidazole is a well-evidenced, modest-effect prescription treatment for the inflammatory lesions of papulopustular rosacea; the full trial base, including its standing against azelaic acid and ivermectin, is set out on [[metronidazole]].[^dailymed] The trial below is product-specific: it is the randomized, vehicle-controlled study the FDA label cites for this exact once-daily 1% gel.

A total of 746 subjects with rosacea, most with a baseline Investigator's Global Assessment (IGA) score of 3 ("moderate") and 8 to 50 inflammatory lesions, were treated with MetroGel or a matching vehicle gel once daily for 10 weeks.[^dailymed] Among the 557 subjects on MetroGel, mean inflammatory lesion counts fell from 18.3 at baseline to 8.9 at week 10, a 50.7% reduction; among the 189 subjects on vehicle, counts fell from 18.4 to 12.8, a 32.6% reduction, a 3.8-lesion difference between groups.[^dailymed] By the IGA scale, 214 of 557 MetroGel subjects (38.42%) reached clear or almost clear, versus 52 of 189 (27.51%) on vehicle.[^dailymed] The label does not report a p-value for these endpoints.[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: against an inert vehicle gel, once-daily MetroGel roughly halved inflammatory lesion counts and brought about 4 in 10 patients to clear or almost-clear skin, versus roughly 3 in 10 on vehicle.

## What's In It

- **Active: metronidazole 1%.** See [[metronidazole]] for the graded mechanism, comparative trial record, and evidence tier.[^dailymed]
- **Base and preservative system:** betadex, edetate disodium, hydroxyethyl cellulose, methylparaben, niacinamide, phenoxyethanol, propylene glycol, propylparaben, and purified water, as declared on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

The label directs patients to cleanse treated areas before application, apply a thin film once daily, and states the product is not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use; cosmetics may be applied after.[^dailymed] It carries a neurologic warning to reevaluate therapy if numbness or paresthesia of an extremity appears, and advises caution in patients with a history of blood dyscrasia.[^dailymed] The label states breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment.[^dailymed]

## Where to Buy

MetroGel is dispensed only by prescription; the FDA label lists it "Rx only," with no over-the-counter purchase path.[^dailymed] It is supplied in a 60 g tube (NDC 0299-3820-60), per the FDA label (accessed 2026-08-08).[^dailymed]
