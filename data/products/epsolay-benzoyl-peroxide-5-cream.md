Epsolay is a prescription cream that puts 5% benzoyl peroxide inside silica microcapsules, the first FDA-approved benzoyl peroxide product indicated for rosacea rather than acne.[^dailymed] Unencapsulated benzoyl peroxide has long been considered too irritating for the sensitized skin typical of [[rosacea-papulopustular|papulopustular rosacea]]; the encapsulation is the entire pitch, a slow-release shell meant to deliver the drug's antibacterial and keratolytic action for inflammatory lesions while limiting direct skin contact.[^dailymed][^bhatia]

> An FDA-approved rosacea drug backed by two real placebo-controlled trials, not a repurposed acne product. Roughly half of treated patients reached clear or almost-clear skin at 12 weeks versus about a fifth to a quarter on vehicle, a genuine and statistically significant effect.[^dailymed] The label's own mechanism section is candid that this is benzoyl peroxide doing what benzoyl peroxide does, bactericidal and keratolytic, and that "the precise mechanism of action in the treatment of rosacea is unknown."[^dailymed] It is brand-only with no generic, and GoodRx lists an average retail price above $600 for a single 30 g pump, so [cost is a real consideration](#where-to-buy).[^goodrx]

## Summary

Epsolay (benzoyl peroxide) Cream, 5% is a Galderma-marketed prescription drug, distributed by Mayne Pharma, indicated specifically for the inflammatory lesions (papules and pustules) of rosacea in adults.[^dailymed] It is a health-claim product, not a cosmetic: the FDA approval, dosing instructions, and evidence base all concern the medical treatment of a diagnosed inflammatory skin disease, and the label makes no cosmetic claims.

What differentiates Epsolay from ordinary benzoyl peroxide is formulation, not a new molecule. The label describes the active as "in a solid form that is incorporated into a microcapsule composed of silicon dioxide, cetrimonium chloride and polyquaternium-7," a silica shell that buffers the drug from direct skin contact and releases it gradually rather than exposing skin to the full concentration at once.[^dailymed] That microencapsulation is the mechanism by which a drug historically considered too irritating for rosacea patients became tolerable enough to earn an indication for the condition; a 2024 real-world pharmacovigilance analysis by the drug's own manufacturer found adverse events "generally mild and restricted to local irritation, pruritus, burning sensation, and erythema," consistent with the phase 3 trial data.[^poteate] The label itself is more conservative about mechanism, stating plainly that benzoyl peroxide is "an oxidizing agent with bactericidal and keratolytic effects but the precise mechanism of action in the treatment of rosacea is unknown."[^dailymed] Full grading of benzoyl peroxide as an ingredient is on [[benzoyl-peroxide]].

## The Evidence

The FDA approval rests on two identically designed, 12-week, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials (Trial 1, NCT03448939, and Trial 2, NCT03564119) in 733 adults with moderate-to-severe papulopustular rosacea, 89% scored moderate and 11% severe at baseline, with a mean inflammatory lesion count of 27.5.[^dailymed] Subjects applied EPSOLAY or a vehicle cream once daily; the co-primary endpoints were the proportion reaching IGA treatment success (a score of "clear" or "almost clear" with at least a two-grade improvement from baseline) at week 12, and the absolute change in inflammatory lesion count.[^dailymed]

Per the FDA label's Table 3, IGA treatment success at week 12 was 47.4% (Epsolay) versus 20.7% (vehicle) in Trial 1, and 49.2% versus 28.2% in Trial 2.[^dailymed] Mean inflammatory lesion counts fell 68.2% versus 38.3% in Trial 1, and 69.4% versus 46.0% in Trial 2, with the drug outperforming vehicle from week 4 onward on both endpoints.[^dailymed]

> Bottom line (FDA label data): roughly half of Epsolay-treated patients reached clear or almost-clear skin at 12 weeks, against about a fifth to a quarter on an inactive vehicle cream.

The same two trials were also published independently by the drug's developers and investigators (Bhatia and colleagues, 2023, in the Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology), reporting its own analysis of the identical trial population: IGA success of 43.5% versus 16.1% in Study 1 and 50.1% versus 25.9% in Study 2 (all P<0.001), with lesion-count reductions of -17.4 versus -9.5 and -20.3 versus -13.3, matching the label's absolute change figures.[^bhatia] The peer-reviewed report also notes the drug was "not compared with unencapsulated BPO," so the trials establish effectiveness against an inert vehicle, not a head-to-head advantage over ordinary benzoyl peroxide, and states there were no treatment-related serious adverse events, with 1.8% of Epsolay subjects (versus 0.4% on vehicle) discontinuing due to adverse events.[^bhatia] A 40-week open-label extension of the same subjects (NCT03564145) found IGA success sustained in the mid-to-high 60% range among subjects continuing treatment, with a favorable long-term safety and tolerability profile.[^werschler]

Two limits: the trial population was 93% Caucasian and 73% female with a mean age of 51, so applicability to a more diverse population is not separately established in these trials, and, as with Epsolay's peer-reviewed authors note, no trial directly compares encapsulated to unencapsulated benzoyl peroxide in rosacea, so the tolerability advantage claimed for encapsulation is plausible on mechanism but not proven by a controlled head-to-head study in this indication.[^dailymed][^bhatia]

## What's In It

- **Active: benzoyl peroxide 5%** (50 mg per gram of cream), in a solid form incorporated into a microcapsule composed of silicon dioxide, cetrimonium chloride and polyquaternium-7.[^dailymed] See [[benzoyl-peroxide]] for the graded mechanism and broader evidence base.
- **Inactive ingredients:** anhydrous citric acid, cetrimonium chloride, cetyl alcohol, cyclomethicone, edetate disodium, glycerin, hydrochloric acid, lactic acid, macrogol stearate Type I, mono and di-glycerides, phenoxyethanol, polyquaternium-7, purified water, silicon dioxide and sodium hydroxide, as declared on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

The label warns of hypersensitivity reactions, skin irritation/contact dermatitis, and photosensitivity, and notes the product "may bleach hair or colored fabric."[^dailymed] It is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity reactions to benzoyl peroxide.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **Microencapsulation's silica shells "provide a buffer between the drug and the skin," improving tolerability over unencapsulated benzoyl peroxide.** This is the manufacturer-affiliated framing repeated across peer-reviewed literature co-authored by the drug's developer, Sol-Gel Technologies.[^poteate] The mechanism is plausible and consistent with how the trials describe the tolerability data, but as the same authors' 2023 efficacy paper concedes, Epsolay was "not compared with unencapsulated BPO" in a controlled trial, so the specific tolerability advantage over ordinary benzoyl peroxide is not directly demonstrated by these studies.[^bhatia][^poteate]
- **Framed as a breakthrough because benzoyl peroxide was previously "too irritating" for rosacea.** This is a fair characterization of clinical practice, benzoyl peroxide has not historically been a mainstream rosacea treatment, and it is the first benzoyl peroxide product to earn an FDA rosacea indication.[^dailymed] The trial evidence supports that this specific encapsulated formulation is effective and reasonably well tolerated against vehicle; it does not establish that older, unencapsulated benzoyl peroxide products would have failed a similar trial in rosacea, since that comparison was not run.
