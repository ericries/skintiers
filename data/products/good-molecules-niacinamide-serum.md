Good Molecules Niacinamide Serum is an unscented, water-based serum built around 10% niacinamide, sold by the budget brand Good Molecules in a 1 fl oz (30 ml) bottle.[^1][^2]

> A very cheap, very simple 10% niacinamide serum. Topical niacinamide has modest evidence, much of it from small or manufacturer-run trials, for skin barrier support, facial hyperpigmentation, and mild inflammatory acne; that case sits on [[niacinamide]]. Its appeal here is not a distinctive formula but a meaningful dose of a real active in a nine-ingredient bottle at about $6 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-06). The 10% concentration sits above the 2% to 5% actually used in the trials behind niacinamide's evidence, and 10% is at the high end of what is sold, where some people get irritation.

## Summary

Good Molecules Niacinamide Serum pairs 10% [[niacinamide]], a form of vitamin B3, with a short list of humectants and thickeners in an unscented, vegan formula.[^2] It is listed for combination skin and positioned around pore appearance, tone, texture, and dullness.[^2] Topical niacinamide is well tolerated and has modest evidence, much of it from small or manufacturer-run studies, for supporting the skin barrier and lowering water loss in dry skin, for lightening facial hyperpigmentation, and for mild inflammatory acne; that general case, with its primary trials and their limits, is set out on [[niacinamide]].

What distinguishes this product is not the active but the formula and the price. The ingredient list runs to nine entries: water and niacinamide, three humectants and solvents (dipropylene glycol, glycerin, 1,2-hexanediol), a moisturizing amino acid (betaine), a thickener pair (ammonium acryloyldimethyltaurate/VP copolymer and xanthan gum), and one preservative (ethylhexylglycerin).[^2] There is no fragrance, no zinc, no exfoliating acid, and no second treatment active. At about $6 for 1 fl oz (30 ml), it is priced in the same narrow band as [[the-ordinary-niacinamide-10-zinc-1]], the other widely sold 10% niacinamide budget serum.[^2][^3]

The concentration is above the 2% to 5% niacinamide levels used in the trials behind its evidence, roughly 2% for barrier support and 4% to 5% for pigmentation and acne, summarized on [[niacinamide]]. A comparison already drawn for a similarly dosed budget serum on [[the-ordinary-niacinamide-10-zinc-1]] notes that going higher than the tested range is not shown to add benefit and raises the chance of irritation, a trade-off that applies equally to this product's own 10% label.

The product listing's directions read: "Apply a few drops to your skin in the morning and at night before oils, moisturizers, and creams. Suitable for daily use in AM/PM."[^2] The label's other claims are weighed against the evidence under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## The Evidence

Topical niacinamide is a well-tolerated active with modest evidence for supporting the skin barrier, lightening facial hyperpigmentation, and mild inflammatory acne; how well each use holds up, and how much of it comes from small or manufacturer-run trials, is set out on [[niacinamide]]. No published clinical trial tests this exact nine-ingredient, 10% niacinamide formulation, so its grades below rest on the category evidence for niacinamide at the concentrations that were actually studied, which were lower than the 10% on this label.[^2]

## What's In It

The ingredient list (INCI) as declared on the product listing.[^2] The active is linked to its own page; the rest is the base, texture, and preservative system.

Active:

- [[niacinamide]] 10%, a form of vitamin B3

Base, humectant, and preservative system: water, dipropylene glycol and 1,2-hexanediol (solvents), [[glycerin]] and betaine (humectants), ammonium acryloyldimethyltaurate/VP copolymer and xanthan gum (thickeners), and the preservative ethylhexylglycerin.[^2]

The formula is unscented and contains no vitamin C, no zinc, and no exfoliating acids.[^2]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **The 10% concentration.** The 10% strength is above the 2% to 5% at which niacinamide's cited trials were run (barrier support around 2%, pigmentation and acne at 4% to 5%), set out on [[niacinamide]].[^2]
- **"Niacinamide improves the appearance of enlarged pores, uneven skin tone and texture, and dullness."** Niacinamide's better-evidenced topical uses are barrier support, facial hyperpigmentation, and mild acne, set out on [[niacinamide]]; the pore and dullness claims are the product's own positioning and are not established for this product specifically.[^2]
- **"Reduces signs of moisture loss caused by a weak skin barrier function."** The barrier mechanism is real at a general level (on [[niacinamide]]), but no trial tests this exact 10% formulation.[^2]
