reVive Light Therapy Essentials is a small over-the-counter LED device from LED Technologies, Inc., cleared by the FDA twice under one trade name: K210965 for wrinkles and K210968 for acne.[^k965][^k968] The two clearances describe different light configurations, and only the acne version appears in the brand's current range.[^brand] The device category is on [[home-led-light-therapy]] and the brand on [[revive-light-therapy]].

> **These are the predicate devices behind the newer reVive unit, and following the chain is the point of this page.** The 2022 clearance on [[revive-light-therapy-wrinkle-and-acne-led-device]] rests on equivalence to these two. These two in turn rest on equivalence to three earlier LED Technologies devices.[^k965][^k968] And at every step the manufacturer's own summary says the conclusion comes from "nonclinical tests".[^k965][^k968] **Three generations of clearances, all against the same company's previous products, with no clinical efficacy study anywhere in the chain.**

## Summary

Two cleared configurations share the Essentials name. The wrinkle version "uses four types of LEDs: 605nm amber, 630nm red, 660nm red, and 880nm infrared".[^k965] The acne version "uses two types of LEDs: 630nm red and 415nm blue".[^k968] Both are described as an "over-the counter light emitting diode (LED) device, that emits energy for use in dermatology", the first "for the treatment of wrinkles" and the second "for the treatment of mild to moderate inflammatory acne".[^k965][^k968] The acne configuration is the blue-red pairing that the category evidence grades best, which is covered on [[home-led-light-therapy]] rather than repeated here.

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate inflammatory acne (K210968 configuration)**
- **Effect size: modest, and the grade belongs to the light rather than this unit.** 630nm red plus 415nm blue is the blue-red combination graded Level B with a Grade I recommendation in the 2026 GRADE review of home devices, the best grade in that review's acne section.[^home][^k968] Nothing in this clearance tests this device.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary for the device, mixed at the category level.** The clearance states its conclusions come from nonclinical tests.[^k968]

**Wrinkles (K210965 configuration)**
- **Effect size: minimal.** LED reached Level B for facial rejuvenation in the same review, behind home fractional nonablative lasers at Level A.[^home] This configuration adds 605nm amber and 880nm infrared to the red wavelengths, and no source consulted here reports what those achieved on this device.[^k965]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** Same nonclinical basis, and "treatment of wrinkles" is a broad claim that the clearance does not quantify.[^k965]

## The Evidence

**The clearance chain, laid out.** The acne Essentials names as its primary predicate K180447, listed as "reVive Light Therapy® LED Cleansing System (LED Technologies, Inc.)".[^k968] The wrinkle Essentials names two, K171390 as "dpl® II Panel (LED Technologies, Inc.)" and K180445 as "reVive Light Therapy® LED Cleansing System (LED Technologies, Inc.)".[^k965] Every predicate is a product of the same company. The 2022 device then cites these two as its predicates in turn. Substantial equivalence is a chain, and this one never leaves the manufacturer.

**What was actually tested, in the manufacturer's words.** Both summaries state that "Testing of the reVive Light Therapy® Essentials, included functional performance testing, software validation, testing, and user safety testing".[^k965][^k968] Both conclude that "The conclusions drawn from nonclinical tests demonstrate that the device is safe, as effective, and performs as well as the legally marketed devices".[^k965][^k968] The word nonclinical is the manufacturer's own, and it is the most useful word in either document. Functional, software and user-safety testing establish that a device works as built and does not hurt people. None of it measures a wrinkle or counts a lesion.

**Why this matters beyond one brand.** A reader comparing home LED devices sees "FDA cleared" on all of them and reasonably assumes a regulator checked whether they work. What a clearance checks is whether a device is equivalent to something already sold. When the predicates are the manufacturer's own earlier devices, and their predicates are too, the claim resolves to an internal comparison. The ladder of what the clinical literature actually supports is on [[home-use-skincare-devices-by-evidence]].

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"FDA cleared"**. True, twice over, and it means substantial equivalence to earlier LED Technologies devices on nonclinical testing.[^k965][^k968]
- **"treatment of wrinkles" and "treatment of mild to moderate inflammatory acne"**. The cleared indications verbatim, so legitimate labelling.[^k965][^k968] They state what the device may be sold for, not a measured result.
- **Wavelength claims**. Accurate as specifications.[^k965][^k968] For the acne version they happen to describe the better-evidenced blue-red configuration, which is a point in its favour at the category level.[^home]

## Where to Buy

**No price is stated because none was verified.** The brand's current range includes an Essentials Acne product, per its own storefront; no Essentials product matching the K210965 wrinkle configuration was found there.[^brand] Availability and pricing are worth checking directly.
