A 2009 over-the-counter LED device for acne, and its FDA file is worth reading for what counts as performance data. **Bottom line:** the clearance rests on equivalence to three earlier Omnilux lamps, and the only tests the Summary reports are two checks of whether people could understand the instructions. Whether LED helps acne at all is on [[home-led-light-therapy]], and which colour has the evidence is on [[led-colours-for-acne-by-evidence]].

> **Its measured results are about reading comprehension, not skin.** Under Performance Data the Summary offers an analysis of "the overall performance charactenstics for the device", then "The results of a self-selection test (carried out on a sample representative population using the product labeling) which showed a minimum comprehension rate for the cntical objectives of 93% at the 95% confidence level", then a usability test showing the same figure at 88%.[^k081307] Those numbers are real and they measure whether a shopper can follow the label. Checked by string search of the whole document, it contains no clinical study, no subject count for any skin outcome, and the words randomised, placebo and sham appear nowhere.[^k081307]

## Summary

Cleared 2009-01-16 as K081307, applicant Photo Therapeutics, Inc. of Carlsbad, California, product code **OLP**, a Traditional 510(k) published as a Summary.[^openfda] OLP is FDA's classification for an "Over-The-Counter Powered Light Based Laser For Acne" under 21 CFR 878.4810, and its official definition is almost the device's indication verbatim: the device "is intended to emit energy in the red and blue region of the spectrum, specifically indicated to treat mild to moderate acne vulgaris".[^olp]

**The cleared indication**, quoted in full: "The Omnilux Clear-U is intended to emit energy in the red and blue region of the spectrum, specifically indicated to treat mild to moderate acne on the face".[^k081307] The Summary adds that the target patient population matches the predicates but that "the Omilux Clear-U is designed for home use", the misspelling being the document's own.[^k081307]

**What it is physically.** Two outputs, "The red output of the Ommlux Clear-U is 633 + 6 nm, and the blue output is 415 + 5 nm", driven by a selector switch marked red, off and blue, an LED array assembly and air cooling, with a separate universal power supply.[^k081307] Treatment length is not fixed by the device: "Treatment time is controlled by the operator".[^k081307]

**No dose figure is published.** Checked by string search of the full Summary, it contains no occurrence of mW, J/cm, irradiance or energy density, so the wavelengths are the only optical numbers in it.[^k081307] That places it on rung 2 of [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]]: the basis is readable, the dose is not. The arithmetic you would need it for is on [[led-dose-j-per-cm2-and-wavelength]].

**No price is quoted here, and the reason is the product itself.** This clearance is from 2009 and the Omnilux name is now filed by a different company: openFDA records "Omnilux CLEAR" cleared in June 2021 as K210948, applicant Globalmed Technologies of Napa, California, under the same OLP code.[^openfda] Quoting a current price against a 2009 clearance would attach today's product to yesterday's file.

## The Rubric

**Mild to moderate facial acne (health)**
Graded modest on mixed evidence, inherited rather than earned. Blue light has the better of the colour evidence for acne and that case is made on [[led-colours-for-acne-by-evidence]]; this device contributes nothing to it, because its file reports no clinical study. The 415 nm blue output sits in the range those trials used, which is a statement about the hardware, not a result. Acne generally is on [[acne]].

**Anything this device itself demonstrated**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence, which is the honest reading of a Performance Data section built from a comprehension test. The two tests cited are about the labelling, and the equivalence argument is about similarity to three earlier lamps, which is a claim about technology rather than a measured outcome, as [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] sets out.

## The Evidence

**The manufacturer says in the file that the device is not a laser.** Under Names, the classification is given as "Laser Instrument, Surgical Powered - General and Plastic Surgery - Class II, OLP", immediately followed by: "Although this device is not a laser and is intended for OTC use, the manufacturer thinks this is the closest applicable classification name."[^k081307] That is unusually candid, and it is the same filing-versus-function mismatch documented on [[silkn-titan-allways]], where a radiofrequency device sits under a laser-surgical-instrument regulation.

**The Summary names the wrong device in its own title.** Its first line reads "510(k) Summary of Safety and Effectiveness for the Photo Therapeutics Limited Omnilux Plus", while every other part of the document, and the FDA clearance letter attached to it, concerns the Omnilux Clear-U.[^k081307] Omnilux Plus is not a hypothetical: it is a separate clearance, K043317 from March 2005 under a different product code.[^openfda] The header is a template leftover. It does not affect what was cleared, and it is the kind of thing you only see by reading the file.

**A dated predicate chain, and one name that does not match the register.** The three predicates named are "Ormnilux Revive (K030426), Omnilux blue (K030883), Omnilux revive/Omnilux blue combination (K043329)", all 2003 to 2005 clearances.[^k081307] openFDA records K030426 under the device name OMNILUX RED rather than Revive, so the marketed name and the registered name differ for that predicate.[^openfda] Worth knowing if you try to trace the chain yourself.

**The Summary was written two years before the decision.** It carries a preparation date in May 2007 amended that July, and the clearance letter is dated 16 January 2009.[^k081307] Nothing improper follows from that, but a reader should know the described device is a 2007 design.

**The disclosure record is genuinely good, and it is worth separating from the evidence.** All eight Omnilux filings in openFDA are published as Summaries rather than Statements, across both Photo Therapeutics entities and the 2021 Globalmed filing.[^openfda] Compare [[foreo-faq-led-masks]], where eight of ten are Statements that publish nothing. Full disclosure of a file that contains no clinical data is still better than a file nobody can read, and it is also not the same thing as evidence.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. Acne that scars, or that has not responded to over-the-counter treatment, is worth a dermatologist rather than another device; what has the strongest evidence is on [[acne]] and, for severe disease, [[isotretinoin]].

## Common Marketing Claims

**No brand marketing copy is quoted here.** This clearance is from 2009 and the device is not the product now sold under the Omnilux name, so no current brand page can be fairly quoted against it. The claims assessed above are the regulated ones in the FDA file.

**The registered name carries no claim at all.** FDA records the device name as simply OMNILUX CLEAR-U, with no severity qualifier and no mention of acne in it, while the cleared indication does carry a limit that retail listings tend to drop: mild to moderate acne on the face.[^openfda][^k081307] Any longer product name a retailer attaches is theirs, and none is quoted here because no current listing was fetched for a 2009 device.

## Where to Buy

Not addressed here. The 2009 device is superseded; the current Omnilux acne device is a separate 2021 clearance, K210948.[^openfda]
