La Roche-Posay Cicaplast Baume B5 (sold in the US as "Cicaplast Balm Vitamin B5 Soothing Therapeutic Cream for Dry & Irritated Skin") is a fragrance-free, multi-use balm labeled with 5% panthenol plus shea butter, glycerin, and madecassoside, positioned for cracked, chapped, or irritated skin on the face, body, and hands.[^1]

> Panthenol (provitamin B5) is a well-studied soothing and barrier-support ingredient rather than a treatment active in its own right: controlled trials show topical dexpanthenol speeds barrier repair and eases irritation-induced redness compared with a plain vehicle, and a separate dose-ranging trial found panthenol at 1% and 5% cut water loss, covered in full on [[panthenol]]. This balm's labeled 5% panthenol sits at the top of the directly tested range, so both the barrier-repair and hydration evidence line up with its actual concentration.

## Summary

Cicaplast Baume B5 is marketed as multi-purpose relief for "cracked, chapped, chafed skin," positioned for "daily relief and hydration for dry to very dry skin" on the face, body, and hands, and including use to "help treat and prevent diaper rash."[^1] Target's listing states it is accepted by the National Eczema Association and is "dermatologist tested, ophthalmologist tested," and suitable "for babies one week and up."[^1] It is sold in a 1.35 oz size for $14.29 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^1]

The label calls out 5% panthenol as the headline active, alongside shea butter and glycerin described as ingredients that "nourishes skin, reduces irritation signs," and madecassoside described as helping "reduces appearance of dry, cracked skin."[^1] Panthenol supporting barrier repair and reducing water loss is a well-evidenced use, covered on [[panthenol]]; at 5% this balm sits within, rather than above, the concentrations directly tested in that evidence, which is not true of every panthenol product on the market (see [[purito-panthenol-moisturizer]], labeled at 10%, double the highest tested dose).

The formula is [emollient- and occlusive-forward](#whats-in-it): water leads the ingredient list, followed by hydrogenated polyisobutene and dimethicone, then glycerin and shea butter, with panthenol sixth.[^1] It is fragrance-free, and Target's formula-attribute tags list it as "Oxybenzone-Free, Fragrance-Free, Paraben-Free, Oil-Free, Sulfate-Free, Non-Comedogenic."[^1] No product-specific clinical trial of this exact balm is cited below; the grades rest on the general panthenol evidence applied to its labeled concentration.

## The Evidence

No trial of this exact balm is cited here. Its labeled 5% panthenol falls inside the range covered by the general panthenol evidence on [[panthenol]]: controlled trials of topical dexpanthenol show faster barrier repair and less irritation-induced redness than a plain vehicle, and a separate dose-ranging trial found panthenol at 1% and 5% significantly reduced transepidermal water loss versus a vehicle, with 5% within the range tested. That page also notes much of the barrier-repair literature is manufacturer-linked (co-authored by employees of Bayer, which owns a competing dexpanthenol brand) and that the water-loss trial is a single, preliminary, instrumented study.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Helps treat and prevent diaper rash."** A manufacturer/retailer benefit statement, not a reported clinical trial result on the pages cited here.[^1]
- **Target's "Beauty Purpose" tag lists "Anti-Aging" alongside "Soothing, Moisturizing."** No controlled trial of panthenol or dexpanthenol against collagen synthesis, elastin, or wrinkle depth is established on [[panthenol]]; the evidenced endpoints are water loss, redness, and short-term wound recovery, not anti-aging.[^1]
- **"Madecassoside + Copper Zinc Manganese: reduces appearance of dry, cracked skin."** A retailer key-ingredient blurb; the fetched full ingredient list includes zinc gluconate and manganese gluconate but no copper compound.[^1]
- **"Dermatologist tested, ophthalmologist tested," "Accepted by National Eczema Association."** Formulation-review and testing designations, not a reported clinical-trial outcome for this balm on the pages cited here.[^1]
