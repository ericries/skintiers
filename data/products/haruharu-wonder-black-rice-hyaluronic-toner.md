Haruharu Wonder Black Rice Hyaluronic Toner is a Korean fermented-rice toner built on a water, betaine, and glycerin base, with hyaluronic acid, black rice (Oryza Sativa) extract, black bamboo extract, and Aspergillus ferment, sold in 30 ml, 150 ml, and 300 ml sizes and marketed to hydrate, brighten, and soothe.[^1]

> A watery Korean toner that leads with humectants, not a headline active at a stated strength. Hyaluronic acid appears undeclared for concentration or molecular weight, 11th on a 28-ingredient list behind water, betaine, glycerin, and propanediol; the general case for topical hyaluronic acid's hydration benefit is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. The brand's 40.43% moisture-improvement figure is its own unpublished 14-day test.[^1] The formula also lists alcohol and, from lavender oil, the fragrance allergens linalool and limonene near the end of the ingredient list.[^1]

## Summary

Black Rice Hyaluronic Toner is Haruharu Wonder's entry-level hydrating toner: a water-betaine-glycerin base carrying [[hyaluronic-acid]], black rice (Oryza Sativa) extract, black bamboo (Phyllostachys Pubescens shoot bark) extract, and a fermentation ingredient (Aspergillus ferment), sold in 30 ml, 150 ml, and 300 ml sizes.[^1] The general case for topical hyaluronic acid, reliable in placebo-controlled trials for raising a skin-hydration reading, thinner for wrinkle-appearance and dependent on molecular weight, is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. This toner does not declare the hyaluronic acid's concentration or molecular weight, and it is listed well down the ingredient panel, after water, betaine, glycerin, propanediol, and several plant extracts.[^1]

What distinguishes this toner from category peers such as Anua's Rice 70 Glow Milky Toner is the black rice and black bamboo framing rather than a differently evidenced active ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)). The brand's own 14-day test claims "40.43% moisture improvement" and "14.2% skin texture improvement," figures not tied to any published trial, sample size, or control arm; this and other label language is weighed under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1] It is listed at $18 for the 150 ml size on Haruharu Wonder's own site (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-16).[^1]

## The Evidence

Topical hyaluronic acid consistently raises a skin-hydration measurement in placebo-controlled testing, with a smaller and molecular-weight-dependent effect on fine-wrinkle appearance; the full case, including the split between low- and high-molecular-weight forms, is set out on [[hyaluronic-acid]]. Nothing specific to this product was found: no published trial tests Haruharu Wonder's exact formula, and the label does not state the hyaluronic acid's concentration or molecular weight, so it is not possible to say whether this toner's hyaluronic acid is the smaller, skin-penetrating type shown to affect wrinkle depth or the larger, surface-sitting type shown mainly to raise hydration readings.[^1]

No independent clinical evidence was found for black rice (Oryza Sativa extract) or black bamboo (Phyllostachys Pubescens shoot bark extract) as topical skincare actives; both sit mid-list behind the humectant base, and the brand's page does not cite a trial for either.[^1] The brand's unpublished 14-day test, "40.43% moisture improvement" and "14.2% skin texture improvement," is described on the product page without a linked published trial, sample size, or control arm.[^1]

> **Bottom line.** No trial isolates this product. Its hydration grade rests on hyaluronic acid's general, evidence-backed but concentration- and molecular-weight-dependent case, undermined here by the label not declaring either; its brightening and soothing claims rest on the brand's own unpublished 14-day test and framing around black rice and black bamboo, ingredients with no independent evidence found (see above).

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by the product-specific evidence above:

- **"Clinically proven results after 14 days: 40.43% moisture improvement and 14.2% skin texture improvement."** The brand's own unpublished test, described on its product page without a linked published trial, sample size, or control arm.[^1]
- **"Patented fermentation technology for deeper ingredient delivery."** A formulation and delivery claim not tied to any cited independent penetration or efficacy study.[^1]
- **"Soothing reinforcement from black rice and black bamboo."** No independent clinical evidence for either ingredient as a topical soothing agent was found.
- **"Free from: Artificial Fragrance" / "Free from: Drying Alcohol."** This SKU's own product page lists both under its "Free from" claims, but the same page's ingredient panel includes plain "Alcohol" (undifferentiated on-label) and Lavandula Angustifolia (lavender) oil carrying the declared fragrance allergens linalool and limonene; the page does not define "artificial" or "drying" against those two label entries, leaving the claim and the INCI unreconciled.[^1]
