Dear, Klairs EGF Blue Youth Activating Drop is a water-like ampoule built on two growth-factor peptides, named on the label sh-oligopeptide-1 (EGF) and sh-polypeptide-1 (FGF), sold in 10, 20 and 50 mL sizes.[^1]

> A growth-factor-peptide "anti-aging" ampoule marketed for skin renewal, texture, and pigmentation. Topical growth factors and exosomes, the marketing category this product belongs to, sit in the lowest, weakest-evidence tier on [[anti-aging]]; cosmetic peptides more broadly (a different peptide class from the growth-factor mimetics here) have only a modest, mostly preliminary effect on the look of aging, covered on [[peptides]]. No independent trial of this specific product, or of its two named peptides, was found (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence)).[^1]

## Summary

The Blue Youth Activating Drop is Dear, Klairs' EGF serum: a thin, water-like ampoule whose own product name, "EGF 10ppm + FGF 10ppm," identifies its two lead actives, declared on the ingredient list as sh-oligopeptide-1 and sh-polypeptide-1.[^1] The rest of the formula is short: water and butylene glycol as the base, a blueberry fruit extract, adenosine, and guaiazulene, in a light lecithin-emulsifier system with standard glycol preservatives; the ingredient list shows no added fragrance.[^1] Klairs markets it for skin texture, "anti-aging," collagen synthesis, and pigmentation, and for soothing "skin irritation caused by various external stressors."[^1] It is sold in 10, 20 and 50 mL sizes; the manufacturer's own product page does not list a price, and the product was not found in a Target search (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), both accessed 2026-08-07).[^1][^2]

Whether topical growth-factor peptides do much for skin aging is a separate and more skeptical question. Cosmetic peptides as a class are well-tolerated ingredients with a modest, mostly preliminary effect on the appearance of aging; that general case is on [[peptides]], though the growth-factor-mimetic peptides named here (EGF/FGF analogs) are a different mechanism from the signal, neurotransmitter-inhibiting, and carrier peptides that page grades. Growth factors and exosomes are their own heavily marketed anti-aging category, and the [[anti-aging]] goal page ranks that category in its lowest, weakest-evidence tier, describing the human evidence behind it as thin. No independent trial of this product, or of sh-oligopeptide-1 or sh-polypeptide-1 specifically, was found in this review; the soothing claim likewise rests only on the manufacturer's own marketing copy. The product-specific picture and its limits are in [The Evidence](#the-evidence); the brand's sales-count and front-label claims are separated out under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## The Evidence

Cosmetic peptides as a class are well-tolerated ingredients with a modest, mostly preliminary effect on the look of aging; how that evidence breaks down by peptide type is set out on [[peptides]]. Neither of the two INCI ingredients this product names as its actives, sh-oligopeptide-1 (EGF) and sh-polypeptide-1 (FGF), is itemized in that rubric: they are growth-factor-mimetic peptides, a different mechanism from the signal, neurotransmitter-inhibiting, and carrier peptides reviewed there.

Growth factors and exosomes are their own heavily marketed anti-aging category, separate from those peptide classes. The [[anti-aging]] goal page places that category in its lowest, weakest-evidence tier and describes the human evidence behind it as thin, pointing to a systematic review of platelet-derived exosomes that found the published literature on this class of ingredient scarce.

> **Bottom line.** Topical growth-factor products, including EGF/FGF-peptide serums like this one, sit in the weakest-evidence tier reviewed on [[anti-aging]]. No independent trial of this product or its two named growth-factor peptides was found in this review.

No manufacturer or independent clinical study of Blue Youth Activating Drop itself is cited on the brand's product page; the "1 Million+ Sold Globally" figure it does report is a sales count, not a clinical result, and is quarantined under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the manufacturer.[^1]

Growth-factor peptides and other named actives:

- sh-Oligopeptide-1 (EGF, epidermal growth factor) and sh-Polypeptide-1 (FGF, fibroblast growth factor), the two ingredients the product's own name ("EGF 10ppm + FGF 10ppm") credits with its renewal claims; see [[peptides]] for cosmetic-peptide evidence generally and [The Evidence](#the-evidence) for the growth-factor category specifically
- adenosine, marketed for anti-aging and soothing
- guaiazulene (an azulene the manufacturer credits with calming)
- Vaccinium angustifolium (blueberry) fruit extract

Base, texture, and preservative system: water and butylene glycol (solvent/humectant base), lecithin, sorbitan sesquioleate, and PEG-60 hydrogenated castor oil (emulsifiers), glycerin (humectant), 1,2-hexanediol, caprylyl glycol, and chlorphenesin (preservatives/solvents), and ethylhexylglycerin (preservative booster).[^1]

The ingredient list declares no added fragrance.[^1]
