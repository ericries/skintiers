PanOxyl Acne Foaming Wash Benzoyl Peroxide 10% (PanOxyl) is an over-the-counter foaming wash for the face, chest, and back formulated with benzoyl peroxide at 10%, the maximum concentration sold without a prescription.[^1]

> The maximum OTC strength of benzoyl peroxide (10%) in a rinse-off foaming wash. Benzoyl peroxide's own evidence shows higher concentrations are not meaningfully more effective than lower ones, only more irritating, and its trial base was built on leave-on gels rather than a wash rinsed off after a minute or two; see [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and [[acne]].

## Summary

PanOxyl Acne Foaming Wash Benzoyl Peroxide 10% is a single-active foaming cleanser built around [[benzoyl-peroxide]] at its top OTC concentration, positioned by the manufacturer as a "maximum-strength acne treatment wash that clears, treats, and prevents even your worst breakouts" for the face, chest, and back.[^1] Directions on the manufacturer's page call for wetting the area, massaging the wash in for 1-2 minutes, then rinsing thoroughly, applied once daily and "gradually increas[ing] to twice if needed or directed by your dermatologist."[^1]

Benzoyl peroxide is a well-evidenced, resistance-proof first-line acne treatment, but its effect size is modest and its concentration does not track its efficacy the way the "maximum strength" framing implies; the general grade and the trial finding that 5% and 2.5% BPO perform almost identically are laid out on [[benzoyl-peroxide]]. This product's own distinguishing feature is format, not concentration: it is a rinse-off wash rather than the leave-on gel or cream most of the acne evidence was generated with, which by definition shortens the active's contact time with the skin ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence)).

The [full ingredient list](#whats-in-it) is otherwise a standard foaming-wash base: water, cleansing/foaming agents, thickeners (carbomers), a preservative-adjacent glycol, and pH adjusters, with no other treatment actives.[^1][^2] Target lists the 5.5 oz bottle at $10.19 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^2]

## The Evidence

No trial isolates this product or its wash format. The general evidence for benzoyl peroxide as an acne treatment, including its head-to-head standing against adapalene and topical clindamycin and the finding that higher concentrations are not meaningfully more effective, is graded on [[benzoyl-peroxide]]. That evidence base was generated with leave-on gels and creams; acne washes as a category are noted separately as giving an active less contact time than the leave-on products their labels borrow from, since a wash is rinsed off within minutes rather than left on the skin (see [[acne]]).

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Kills over 99% of acne-causing bacteria in 15 seconds."** An in vitro/kill-time claim, not a clinical outcome measure; the graded clinical evidence for benzoyl peroxide is a modest effect on lesion counts, not a bacterial-kill percentage.[^1][^2]
- **"Dermatologist-recommended."** A brand positioning statement; no survey or trial data is reported on the pages cited here.[^1][^2]
- **"#1 Best-Selling Acne Wash in the U.S."** A sales claim on Target's listing, not an efficacy claim.[^2]
- **Allure Best of Beauty Award recipient.** Named on Target's listing without award-cycle, year, or category detail on the page cited here.[^2]
