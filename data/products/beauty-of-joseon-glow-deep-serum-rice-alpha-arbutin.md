Glow Deep Serum: Rice + Alpha-Arbutin is a Korean brightening serum from [[beauty-of-joseon|Beauty of Joseon]], the same brand behind [[beauty-of-joseon-relief-sun-rice-probiotics-spf50]], built on a rice bran water base carrying alpha-arbutin and niacinamide as its two functional actives.[^boj]

> The brand markets it for "dullness and dark spots," and its two actives, alpha-arbutin and niacinamide, are both established tyrosinase- and pigment-transfer-targeting brightening ingredients, covered on [[arbutin]] and [[niacinamide]]. Neither concentration is declared, alpha-arbutin sits seventh on the ingredient list and niacinamide eighth, and no published trial tests this exact formula.[^boj]

## Summary

Glow Deep Serum: Rice + Alpha-Arbutin is a lightweight, dropper-dispensed facial serum sold in 30 ml and 60 ml sizes, positioned by the brand as a nightly brightening step for "dullness and dark spots" and "brighter-looking skin over time."[^boj] The brand's own directions describe it as "recommended for evening use only."[^boj]

Its two functional actives are alpha-arbutin, a tyrosinase-inhibiting ingredient long used for hyperpigmentation and dark spots, and niacinamide, which has separate evidence for barrier support and for lightening facial hyperpigmentation; both are covered, with their general evidence base, on [[arbutin]] and [[niacinamide]]. Neither concentration is stated on the label. Alpha-arbutin appears seventh in the ingredient list, immediately ahead of niacinamide in eighth, which puts both above the halfway point of a roughly 30-ingredient formula but does not establish a specific percentage ([full list below](#whats-in-it)).[^boj]

The "Rice" half of the name refers mainly to the base: Oryza Sativa (rice) bran water is the first ingredient, standing in for the water most serums are built on, at an undeclared concentration. A separate rice seed extract also appears, but low in the list, grouped with several other grain and legume extracts (Job's tears, Coptis japonica root, soybean, barley, sesame, wheat, mung bean, corn) rather than as a standalone headline ingredient.[^boj]

No published trial tests this product, its rice bran water base, or this specific combination of alpha-arbutin and niacinamide. The evidence behind each active is general, established at other studied concentrations, and is set out on their own pages rather than being specific to this serum ([see below](#the-evidence)).[^boj] It is priced on the brand's own US site at $17.00, discounted to $10.20, as [listed there](#where-to-buy) as of 2026-08-07.[^boj]

## The Evidence

No published trial tests this product or its specific alpha-arbutin-and-niacinamide combination.[^boj] Arbutin is one of several tyrosinase-inhibiting agents, alongside hydroquinone, azelaic acid, and kojic acid, that a review of postinflammatory hyperpigmentation treatment describes as able to "effectively lighten areas of hypermelanosis"; the general case for arbutin is set out on [[arbutin]].[^davis] Topical niacinamide separately has modest, mostly small-trial evidence for lightening facial hyperpigmentation and supporting the skin barrier, set out in full on [[niacinamide]]. That niacinamide evidence was generated at declared concentrations, detailed on [[niacinamide]]; this serum states neither its niacinamide nor its alpha-arbutin percentage, so the general evidence for either active cannot be scaled to this specific formula.[^boj]

> **Bottom line.** Both of this serum's functional actives, alpha-arbutin and niacinamide, have established general brightening evidence covered on their own pages, but neither concentration is disclosed here and no trial tests this exact formula.[^boj][^davis]
