A deliberately low-strength glycolic acid exfoliant at $25 for 200 mL, which is a lot of product for the money. **Bottom line:** the glycolic percentage is published and the strength is honestly described as gentle, but two things are missing from the marketing that are present in the bottle: a second acid, and the pH. The acid evidence is at [[glycolic-acid]].

> **It is sold as an AHA and it contains a BHA too.** The name, the label and the page all say AHA; the bottle reads "GENTLE AHA EXFOLIATOR".[^brand] The brand's own ingredient list puts **betaine salicylate**, a salicylic acid derivative, ninth, four places after the glycolic acid.[^brand] No percentage is given for it and no marketing copy mentions it. That is not a safety problem, but anyone avoiding salicylates, or already using a BHA elsewhere, would want to know.

## Summary

KraveBeauty sells it at **$25.00 for 200 mL**, in stock on its own storefront, and the page title names the strength directly: "Kale-Lalu-yAHA: 5.25% Glycolic Acid Treatment".[^brand] The brand describes it as "A gentle yet effective exfoliator that smooths texture, fades discoloration, and unclogs pores", "Powered by 5.25% Glycolic Acid", and made for "All skin types but especially dull, sensitive skin."[^brand]

The full declared list runs 23 ingredients and opens "Aloe Barbadensis (Aloe Vera) Leaf Water, Water (Aqua/Eau), Butylene Glycol, Brassica Oleracea Acephala (Kale) Leaf Extract, Glycolic Acid, 1,2-Hexanediol, Potassium Hydroxide, Glycerin, Betaine Salicylate".[^brand] At 12.5 cents a millilitre this is among the cheapest exfoliants per volume this site covers.

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: modest, and the brand is upfront about why.** 5.25% is a low glycolic concentration and the brand says so in its own words: "We formulated Kale-Lalu-yAHA to contain a low percentage of glycolic acid to ensure it would be safe and gentle yet effective for all skin types."[^brand] [[glycolic-acid]] carries `tier: mid` here; a low concentration of a mid-graded acid is a modest proposition, honestly presented.
- **Evidence quality: solid for the ingredient, absent for the product.** No efficacy trial of this formula is cited anywhere on the page read.
- **Record finding: the clinical claim is about safety, and the brand says which.** The page lists "Clinically proven: Safe for sensitive skin." and, in smaller italics directly beneath it, "Based on an independent clinical study on 30 participants with sensitive skin."[^brand] That is a tolerability claim, not an efficacy claim, and it is labelled as one. It is the third KraveBeauty product on this site to print its own sample size unprompted, after [[kravebeauty-great-barrier-relief]] at 20 participants and the six SPF tests on [[kravebeauty-beet-the-sun-spf-40]].
- **Record finding: no pH, on a product whose active is pH-dependent.** Glycolic acid's behaviour depends on pH, and this formula contains potassium hydroxide, which is present to adjust it. So the pH was set deliberately and the figure was not published. Read the whole page and no pH appears. [[exfoliating-acid-products-that-publish-a-ph]] ranks the acid products that do publish one; this cannot join that list.
- **What the kale is doing.** Kale leaf extract is fourth, ahead of the glycolic acid, with spinach, parsley and apple extracts further down.[^brand] The brand's claim for them is a "boost of antioxidants and vitamins from Kale, Spinach, and Parsley".[^brand] They give the product its name and its colour; the exfoliation is the acids.

## The Evidence

None specific to this product beyond a 30-person tolerability study that is not cited. The ingredient arguments live at [[glycolic-acid]] and [[salicylic-acid]], the latter being the family betaine salicylate belongs to, and neither is restated here.

> **Bottom line.** Good value, honestly described strength, and a formula with one more acid in it than the name admits. If you are choosing an acid on pH, this one does not give you the number to choose with.

## Common Marketing Claims

> Quoted from the brand's own product page and **not** endorsed here.

- "A gentle yet effective exfoliator that smooths texture, fades discoloration, and unclogs pores."[^brand] Reasonable for a low-dose AHA as a class. Fading discoloration is the claim with the most distance between a 5.25% acid and the result people picture.
- "Clinically proven: Safe for sensitive skin."[^brand] A safety claim, correctly labelled, on 30 participants, with the study uncited.
- "Non-Comedogenic" and "Designed for Sensitive Skin".[^brand] Non-comedogenic is not a regulated term and no test is cited for it on the page read.
