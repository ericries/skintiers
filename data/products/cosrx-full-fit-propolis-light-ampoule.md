Full Fit Propolis Light Ampoule is a Korean glow-and-hydration serum from [[cosrx|COSRX]], the brand behind [[cosrx-advanced-snail-96-mucin-power-essence]], built almost entirely on propolis extract, the bee-hive resin the brand declares at 83.3% of the formula.[^cosrx]

> Propolis extract really is the first ingredient here, at a brand-declared 83.3%, in an unusually short eleven-ingredient formula.[^cosrx] But propolis itself is a bee resin whose replicated human trial evidence is narrow (cold sores, with one proprietary 0.5% extract), whose glow and anti-aging positioning rests on laboratory antioxidant data, and which is a documented contact allergen; the honest read is a pleasant, simple hydrating serum whose specific efficacy claims outrun the evidence, covered in full on [[propolis]].[^degroot][^mazzarello]

## Summary

Full Fit Propolis Light Ampoule is a dropper-dispensed 30 ml facial serum that COSRX positions for "glowing skin," describing it as an "intensive caring ampule to make your skin glowy and smooth without stickiness" for use after cleansing and toning, before moisturizer.[^cosrx] Its headline ingredient is propolis extract, which the brand declares at 83.3% and which sits first on the ingredient list, standing in for the water most serums are built on.[^cosrx]

The formula is unusually short: eleven ingredients in total, with the propolis base carried by a cluster of well-known humectants, glycerin, betaine, sodium hyaluronate (the salt form of [[hyaluronic-acid]]), and [[panthenol]] ([full list below](#whats-in-it)).[^cosrx] That composition supports the texture-and-hydration positioning: light, water-based humectant serums of this kind reliably make skin feel and look more hydrated, the kind of finish K-beauty marketing calls [[glass-skin|glass skin]], and this one adds no fragrance or declared essential oils.[^cosrx]

The propolis story itself is thinner than the percentage suggests. As set out on [[propolis]], the resin has genuine antibacterial, antifungal, and antiviral activity in the laboratory, but its replicated randomized human evidence is for a different job entirely, healing cold sores faster than aciclovir, and even that is tied to one proprietary 0.5% extract rather than propolis in general, whose composition varies with the plants the bees collected from.[^degroot] No published trial tests this ampoule, and the one human acne trial of topical propolis combined it with tea tree oil and Aloe vera, so nothing isolates what propolis alone does for blemishes.[^mazzarello] Propolis is also a recognized contact allergen carried on standard patch-test series, a real consideration for fragrance-sensitized skin even in a formula this simple.[^degroot]

It is mid-priced for a K-beauty serum: the brand's own US site lists the 30 ml bottle at $28.00 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-13).[^cosrx]

## The Evidence

No published trial tests this product.[^cosrx] The general evidence for propolis is set out on [[propolis]]; in brief, a dermatology review confirms the resin "has antibacterial, antifungal, and antiviral properties" in the laboratory, and the strongest human data are two randomized cold-sore trials of a single proprietary 0.5% extract (GH 2002 0.5%), which healed lesions a median one day faster than aciclovir 5%, a use this ampoule is not marketed for.[^jautova] For acne and blemishes, the closest human trial randomized 60 patients to a cream of 20% propolis combined with 3% tea tree oil and 10% Aloe vera, which beat 3% erythromycin cream, but the three-active design means the result cannot be attributed to propolis, and that cream is not this product.[^mazzarello] Propolis composition also varies by botanical source, so trial results with one extract do not transfer to another; the same review documents propolis as a contact allergen important enough to appear on standard patch-test series.[^degroot]

The hydration side of the formula needs less argument: glycerin, betaine, sodium hyaluronate, and panthenol are standard humectants whose general moisturizing evidence is covered on [[glycerin]], [[hyaluronic-acid]], [[panthenol]], and [[moisturizing]], though none is declared at a concentration here.[^cosrx]

> **Bottom line.** A short, fragrance-free humectant serum that should hydrate and add cosmetic glow like any well-made serum of its type; the 83.3% propolis headline adds laboratory-grade antioxidant and antibacterial activity, a narrow and unrelated human evidence base, and a documented contact-allergy risk, not a proven skin benefit specific to this bottle.[^cosrx][^degroot][^mazzarello]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), eleven ingredients in the order declared, as declared on the brand's own US product page (identical to the INCIDecoder listing).[^cosrx]

- **Propolis extract**, first on the list, brand-declared at 83.3%, the aqueous base of the formula; evidence covered on [[propolis]][^cosrx]
- **Butylene glycol** and **[[glycerin]]**, second and third, solvent and humectant
- **Betaine**, fourth, a humectant
- **1,2-hexanediol**, the preservative-humectant
- **Cassia obtusifolia seed extract**, a botanical polysaccharide extract with no declared concentration
- **Sodium hyaluronate**, the salt form of [[hyaluronic-acid]], a humectant, no declared concentration
- **Hydroxyethylcellulose** and **carbomer**, texture and thickeners
- **[[panthenol]]** and **arginine**, tenth and eleventh, conditioning humectants

No fragrance, essential oils, or drying alcohols are listed; the main sensitization consideration is propolis itself, a documented contact allergen.[^cosrx][^degroot]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by independent, product-specific evidence:

- **"83.3% Propolis Extract: Make your skin glowy and smooth without stickiness."** The percentage is the brand's own declaration and is consistent with propolis extract sitting first on the INCI list, but "glowy and smooth" is cosmetic positioning; no controlled human trial cited on [[propolis]] shows a glow, brightening, or anti-aging benefit from topical propolis.[^cosrx]
- **"Light texture with heavy benefits! A rich ampoule for glowing skin" and "Best selling ampoule that everyone loves, all year long!"** Popularity and texture claims, not measured outcomes.[^cosrx]
- **Anti-inflammatory and antibacterial positioning.** The brand's page tags the product with "anti-inflammatory" and "antibacterial"; propolis has that activity in laboratory assays, but the only human acne trial used a three-ingredient combination cream, so no human blemish benefit is isolable to propolis, let alone to this formula.[^cosrx][^mazzarello]
