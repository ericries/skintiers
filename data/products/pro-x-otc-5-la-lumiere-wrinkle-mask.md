The exception to almost everything this site has found about LED device filings. **Bottom line:** it publishes a full dose table and a placebo-controlled clinical trial with numbers, which no other LED clearance read here does; the published irradiance is also roughly a thousand times lower than its competitors'.

> **Two things at once, and they sit awkwardly together.** The file reports red light at 0.016 mW/cm2 and infrared at 0.0029 mW/cm2.[^k140471] For scale, the TheraFace clearance covered on [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]] lists red at 73 plus or minus 5 mW/cm2. The same file then reports a trial in which the treated group improved and a placebo group did not, at p<0.001.[^k140471] This page records both figures and does not try to reconcile them.

## Summary

Cleared 2015-01-15 as K140471, applicant La Lumiere, LLC, product code OHS, General and Plastic Surgery panel, filed with a Summary.[^openfda] Common name "Wrinkle Light Therapy System", classification name "Light Based Over- the-Counter Wrinkle Reduction".[^k140471]

**There is no clearance called illuMask Anti-Aging.** La Lumiere holds exactly two 510(k)s: this one and the acne mask K123999.[^openfda] A device-name search of openFDA for an anti-aging light therapy mask returns nothing.[^openfda] So the retail product sold under that name cannot be matched to a filing from FDA records alone, and this page covers the clearance rather than asserting which box it ended up in.

**What it is.** A lightweight mask placed over the face, with a controller, emitting "red light (620-630nm)" and "infrared light (855nm)", automatically stopping at the end of each cycle.[^k140471] Indicated "for use in the treatment of facial wrinkles" in "people with wrinkles on their face and who have Fitzpatrick skin types I, II, and/or III".[^k140471]

**You need two of them.** Each device runs 30 treatment cycles, the full regimen is 60 treatments, and the file says so outright: "therefore purchase of two (2) devices is needed to receive the full treatment regimen".[^k140471]

**No price.** The device is discontinued and no live listing was fetched.

## The Rubric

**Softening facial wrinkles in Fitzpatrick skin types I to III (cosmetic)**
Graded modest on preliminary evidence. The trial is described below and it is a real one, with a control arm and blinded assessors, which puts this filing ahead of every other LED clearance read on this site. It stays preliminary because it is a single manufacturer-run study reported inside its own regulatory submission, with no peer review, no publication and no replication.

**Anything for darker skin**
Graded unknown on anecdotal evidence. The indication stops at Fitzpatrick III and so did the trial. That is a limit worth stating plainly rather than letting a reader assume the result generalises.

## The Evidence

**The trial, as the file reports it.** "An 8-week clinical study was conducted with 50 subjects with Fitzpatrick Skin types I - III, 30 of which were randomly assigned into the Treatment Group and used the Pro X OTC 5, and 20 of which were randomly assigned into the Control Group and used a placebo mask."[^k140471] Subjects used the device 60 times over eight weeks, with a further evaluation three months later. Photographs were "evaluated by two independent, blinded, licensed physicians using the 9-point Fitzpatrick Wrinkle and Elastosis Scale".[^k140471]

**The numbers.** For the first physician the treated group went from a mean grade of 5.10 to 3.86 at eight weeks, "a difference of 1.24 points", and 3.83 at twenty weeks.[^k140471] For the second, 5.03 to 3.83 and then 3.90.[^k140471] The file states that "The Treatment Group statistically significantly outperformed the Control Group at all evaluation periods; 8-weeks and 20-weeks evaluation for both physicians, p<0.001", and that the control group "did not show statistically significant improvement in the Fitzpatrick Score at any evaluation period".[^k140471]

**What is still missing.** No confidence intervals and no standard deviations appear anywhere in the file, so the spread behind those means cannot be judged from it, and the clinical section reports no safety outcome at all.[^k140471] The study is unnamed, unregistered and unpublished as far as this file shows.

**Why it matters beyond this device.** [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]] ranks LED filings by what they disclose, and its recurring finding is that clinical data is absent across the category. This one is the counterexample, and it also predicates partly on [[illumask-acne-light-therapy-mask]], meaning the same firm filed a bare acne clearance and a data-rich wrinkle clearance eighteen months apart.

## Manufacturer Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven unless it also appears in The Evidence above. These are the submitter's statements in a 510(k) Summary, which FDA does not independently verify.

- **"the Pro X OTC 5 Device is effective in the treatment of facial wrinkles."** This is the submitter's own summary of its own analysis.[^k140471] The underlying numbers are above and are better than most; the sentence is still a manufacturer's conclusion.
- **The usability and self-selection studies.** Two layperson studies are reported, the usability one "with 20 participants randomly selected from the general public".[^k140471] These test whether people can read the box and use the device, not whether it works.
- **Standards conformance.** The file cites IEC 60601-1 3rd edition, IEC 60601-1-2 and IEC 62471.[^k140471] Those are electrical safety and photobiological safety standards, not efficacy evidence.
