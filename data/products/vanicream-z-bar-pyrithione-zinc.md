Vanicream Z-Bar is an over-the-counter medicated cleansing bar built around pyrithione zinc 2%, the Head & Shoulders antifungal active, in Vanicream's gentle, fragrance-free, sulfate-free base.[^1]

> An OTC drug, not a cosmetic soap: the FDA Drug Facts label lists the active as "pyrithione zinc 2%" with the purpose "Anti-dandruff, Anti-seborrheic dermatitis."[^1] Pyrithione zinc is a long-established antifungal against the Malassezia yeasts behind dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis, covered on [[zinc-pyrithione]]. What sets this bar apart is the format and base: a face-and-body cleansing bar (not a scalp shampoo) in Vanicream's signature fragrance-free, sulfate-free formula, which suits sensitive skin.

## Summary

This is the pyrithione zinc member of the Vanicream line. It is regulated as an OTC drug: the label states the active as "pyrithione zinc 2%," the purpose as anti-dandruff and anti-seborrheic-dermatitis, and the use as controlling and reducing the symptoms of dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis, used on affected areas in place of regular soap at least twice a week.[^1]

Pyrithione zinc's evidence sits at the ingredient-class level, set out on [[zinc-pyrithione]] and ranked on [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]], where it is a solid, established antifungal graded moderate, a step below ketoconazole and selenium sulfide on this site's head-to-head evidence. No trial of this exact bar was located, so its dandruff and seborrheic-dermatitis grade rests on that active plus its FDA monograph status.

## The Evidence

Two health uses, graded above:

**Dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis (the labeled use).** This is what the bar is approved and labeled for. Pyrithione zinc is an FDA OTC-monograph antifungal used the same way as [[ketoconazole]] and selenium sulfide for seborrheic dermatitis, with the class evidence and its limits set out on [[seborrheic-dermatitis]]. As a bar used on the face and body (rather than a scalp shampoo), it is a practical way to apply the active to seborrheic dermatitis on the face, chest, and back.

**Fungal acne (off-label, mechanistic).** Malassezia folliculitis responds to antifungals, and pyrithione zinc is antimicrobial against that yeast, so a 2% pyrithione zinc bar[^1] is commonly used off-label as a short-contact wash. But the documented [[fungal-acne]] options are azole antifungals and selenium sulfide; pyrithione zinc use rests on the shared mechanism rather than trials for this active, and it is not on the label.

> **Bottom line.** An established pyrithione zinc 2% antifungal in an unusually gentle, fragrance-free, sulfate-free bar, well suited to seborrheic dermatitis on the face and body and to off-label fungal-acne washing.[^1] Judge it as a solid, sensitive-skin-friendly delivery of a moderate-tier antifungal, not a stronger treatment than the ketoconazole and selenium sulfide options ranked above it.

## What's In It

As an OTC drug the label separates the active from everything else. Active ingredient: "pyrithione zinc 2%."[^1]

Inactive ingredients, verbatim from the Drug Facts label: "sodium cocoyl isethionate, stearic acid, coconut acid, sodium isethionate, water, petrolatum, sorbitol, cetearyl alcohol, propanediol, ceteareth-20, simethicone, glyceryl stearate, PEG-30 stearate, sorbic acid."[^1] This is a syndet (synthetic-detergent) bar built on sodium cocoyl isethionate rather than traditional soap, with no added fragrance or dye, consistent with Vanicream's sensitive-skin positioning.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **Fragrance-free, sulfate-free sensitive-skin cleanser.** Consistent with the label's inactive list (a mild sodium cocoyl isethionate syndet base, no fragrance); a formulation strength, not an added antifungal effect.[^1]
- **Off-label "fungal acne" use.** Recommended online on mechanistic grounds, but not on the product's OTC label and not documented for pyrithione zinc on [[fungal-acne]], whose evidence centers on other antifungals.
