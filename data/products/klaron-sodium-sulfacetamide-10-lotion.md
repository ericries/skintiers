Klaron is a prescription 10% sodium sulfacetamide lotion, distributed by Bausch Health US, LLC (manufactured by Bausch Health Companies Inc., a Product of Canada) and FDA-approved as follows: "Klaron Lotion is indicated in the topical treatment of acne vulgaris."[^dailymed] It contains sodium sulfacetamide alone, with no sulfur and no benzoyl peroxide, which sets it apart from the more heavily studied sulfacetamide/sulfur combination products.[^dailymed]

> Klaron is a genuine prescription antibacterial, not a cosmetic product, but its acne evidence is thin by modern standards. The label documents pharmacokinetics, not efficacy trial results, and no randomized vehicle-controlled trial of 10% sodium sulfacetamide monotherapy for acne is retrievable on PubMed. The mechanism and the fuller evidence picture, including where sulfacetamide (mostly in sulfur combinations) has and has not held up against modern comparators, live on [[sodium-sulfacetamide]]. The broader condition is [[acne]].

## Summary

Klaron Lotion, 10% contains 100 mg of sodium sulfacetamide per mL, "in a vehicle consisting of edetic acid, hydroxyethyl cellulose, lauramide DEA, methylparaben, PEG-400 monolaurate, propylene glycol, purified water, silicone emulsion, sodium chloride, sodium metabisulfite, and xanthan gum."[^dailymed] Dosing per the label is twice daily: "Apply a thin film to affected areas twice daily."[^dailymed] It is supplied in a single package size, a 4 fl oz (118 mL) bottle, NDC 0187-5198-04.[^dailymed]

This is a health claim, not a cosmetic one: sodium sulfacetamide is a sulfonamide antibacterial, and the label's own mechanism statement invokes "the Woods-Fildes theory, based on sulfonamides acting as a competitive inhibitor of para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) utilization, an essential component for bacterial growth."[^dailymed] Fuller context on how that translates into acne control, and how sulfacetamide compares with modern first-line actives, is on [[sodium-sulfacetamide]] and not re-derived here.

The label reports systemic absorption is low: "the percentage of the applied dose of Klaron Lotion excreted in the urine as sulfacetamide plus sulfanilamide ranged from 0.08 to 0.33%."[^dailymed] It carries an absolute contraindication for sulfonamide allergy and a black-letter warning about rare but severe sulfonamide reactions, detailed under Warnings below. It is prescription-only; see a prescriber for use and dosing, and see [[acne]] for the broader condition context.

## The Evidence

The label's clinical data for Klaron Lotion is limited to a general adverse-event statement: "In controlled clinical trials for the management of acne vulgaris, the occurrence of adverse reactions associated with the use of Klaron Lotion was infrequent and restricted to local events. The total incidence of adverse reactions reported in these studies was less than 2%."[^dailymed] The label does not report response rates, lesion counts, or any comparison against vehicle or an active comparator for the 10% lotion.

That silence matches the wider picture: [[sodium-sulfacetamide|sodium sulfacetamide is an older sulfonamide antibacterial whose modern controlled-trial support is thin, with no retrievable vehicle-controlled trial of this exact monotherapy and its one respectable randomized trial belonging to a sulfur combination product, not the plain lotion]], a fuller account best read there rather than repeated here.

> **Bottom line.** Klaron carries a genuine FDA acne indication and a plausible antibacterial mechanism, but the prescribing information itself supplies no efficacy numbers, only the general adverse-event statement quoted above, and no modern controlled trial of this monotherapy exists.[^dailymed] Approach it as an older, second-line option rather than a first-line acne treatment.

## Warnings

Klaron Lotion is contraindicated for use by patients having known hypersensitivity to sulfonamides or any other component of this preparation.[^dailymed] The label carries a class warning shared by topical sulfonamides: "Fatalities have occurred, although rarely, due to severe reactions to sulfonamides including Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, fulminant hepatic necrosis, agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, and other blood dyscrasias."[^dailymed]

Klaron Lotion also contains sodium metabisulfite, and the label warns: "Klaron Lotion contains sodium metabisulfite, a sulfite that may cause allergic-type reactions including anaphylactic symptoms and life-threatening or less severe asthmatic episodes in certain susceptible people."[^dailymed] The label instructs to keep away from eyes, for external use only, and to keep out of the reach of children.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **Effective for acne vulgaris.** The FDA indication is real, but the label supplies no efficacy data (response rates, lesion reductions, or a vehicle comparison) for the 10% lotion, only a statement that adverse reactions in controlled trials were infrequent and under 2%. Nothing on the label shows this product matches benzoyl peroxide, retinoids, or azelaic acid.[^dailymed]
- **A gentle, non-antibiotic option.** Sodium sulfacetamide is a sulfonamide antibacterial, not an antibiotic in the acne-specific-antibiotic class (clindamycin, doxycycline), which is a fair distinction, but it still carries antibacterial-class risks, including the sulfonamide hypersensitivity contraindication and the rare Stevens-Johnson-syndrome warning above.[^dailymed]
