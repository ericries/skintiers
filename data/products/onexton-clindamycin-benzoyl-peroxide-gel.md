Onexton is a prescription fixed-combination topical gel from Ortho Dermatologics (distributed by Bausch Health US, LLC), FDA-approved "for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in patients 12 years of age and older."[^dailymed] Each gram contains 12 mg (1.2%) clindamycin phosphate, equivalent to 10 mg (1%) clindamycin, and 37.5 mg (3.75%) benzoyl peroxide, applied as a pea-sized amount to the face once daily.[^dailymed]

> The pitch is convenience: the same clindamycin dose as [[benzaclin-clindamycin-benzoyl-peroxide-gel|BenzaClin]] with a lower benzoyl peroxide strength (3.75% instead of 5%), applied once a day instead of twice. The pairing itself is sensible, since adding [[benzoyl-peroxide]] curbs the antibiotic resistance that undermines [[clindamycin-topical|clindamycin]] used alone. But Onexton's single registration trial compared it only to vehicle, not to benzoyl peroxide alone, so its label cannot show what the combination adds over a cheap over-the-counter benzoyl peroxide product; BenzaClin's trials, which did make that comparison, found a modest margin. Generic clindamycin-benzoyl peroxide gels deliver the same actives for less. The broader condition is [[acne]].

## Summary

Onexton pairs the topical antibiotic [[clindamycin-topical|clindamycin]] with [[benzoyl-peroxide]] in a single once-daily gel: each gram contains 12 mg (1.2%) clindamycin phosphate, equivalent to 10 mg (1%) clindamycin, and 37.5 mg (3.75%) benzoyl peroxide in a white to off-white, opaque, smooth gel.[^dailymed] The FDA-approved indication is quoted above, and the age floor is explicit: "Safety and effectiveness of ONEXTON Gel in pediatric patients under the age of 12 years have not been evaluated."[^dailymed] The label directs patients to wash the face gently with a mild soap, rinse with warm water, pat dry, then "apply a pea-sized amount of ONEXTON Gel to the face once daily," and notes that use beyond 12 weeks has not been evaluated.[^dailymed]

Against [[benzaclin-clindamycin-benzoyl-peroxide-gel|BenzaClin]], the twice-daily clindamycin 1% / benzoyl peroxide 5% sibling, Onexton delivers the same clindamycin with a lower benzoyl peroxide strength at half the application frequency. The resistance-limiting rationale for combining an antibiotic with benzoyl peroxide is owned by [[clindamycin-topical]] and [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and is not re-derived here; the product-specific evidence is a single vehicle-controlled phase 3 trial, covered under [The Evidence](#the-evidence).

Reported adverse reactions in that trial were uncommon: "These adverse reactions occurred in less than 0.5% of subjects treated with ONEXTON Gel: burning sensation (0.4%); contact dermatitis (0.4%); pruritus (0.4%); and rash (0.4%)."[^dailymed] Local skin reactions (erythema, scaling, itching, burning, stinging) mostly peaked around Week 4 and were near or improved from baseline by Week 12.[^dailymed] It is prescription-only; see a prescriber for use and dosing, and see [[acne]] for the broader condition context. Generic clindamycin-benzoyl peroxide gels containing the same actives are available and typically cost less than the brand.

## The Evidence

The general case for topical clindamycin, its documented *Cutibacterium acnes* resistance problem, and the case for benzoyl peroxide are covered on [[clindamycin-topical]] and [[benzoyl-peroxide]]; this section covers only what the Onexton label reports for this product.

The label describes one registration trial: "The safety and efficacy of once-daily use of ONEXTON Gel was assessed in a 12-week multi-center, randomized, blinded trial in subjects 12 years and older with moderate to severe acne vulgaris. This trial evaluated ONEXTON Gel compared to vehicle gel."[^dailymed] The co-primary efficacy variables were mean absolute change from baseline at Week 12 in inflammatory and non-inflammatory lesion counts, and the percent of subjects with a 2-grade reduction from baseline on the Evaluator's Global Severity (EGS) score.[^dailymed]

Results at Week 12, from the label's Table 3:[^dailymed]

| Endpoint | Onexton Gel (N=253) | Vehicle Gel (N=245) |
|---|---|---|
| EGSS clear or almost clear[^dailymed] | 29% | 15% |
| 2-grade reduction from baseline | 35% | 17% |
| Inflammatory lesions, mean absolute reduction | 16.3 | 8.2 |
| Inflammatory lesions, mean percent reduction | 60.4% | 31.3% |
| Non-inflammatory lesions, mean absolute reduction | 19.2 | 9.6 |
| Non-inflammatory lesions, mean percent reduction | 51.8% | 27.6% |

The combination roughly doubled the vehicle response on every endpoint, a clear win over vehicle.[^dailymed] What the trial cannot answer is the question that matters for a buyer: how much the combination adds over benzoyl peroxide alone, an inexpensive over-the-counter active. There were no clindamycin-alone or benzoyl-peroxide-alone arms. The closest evidence comes from the BenzaClin registration trials of the 1%/5% pairing, where the combination's margin over benzoyl peroxide monotherapy was about 10 to 14 percentage points on inflammatory lesion reduction; details on [[benzaclin-clindamycin-benzoyl-peroxide-gel]].

> **Bottom line.** Once-daily Onexton beat vehicle convincingly on both co-primary endpoints in its single phase 3 trial, and combining clindamycin with benzoyl peroxide is the resistance-smart way to use the antibiotic. But the label offers no comparison against benzoyl peroxide alone, the sibling BenzaClin data suggest that increment is modest, and generic clindamycin-benzoyl peroxide gels supply the same pairing for less than the brand.[^dailymed]

## What's In It

The active ingredients as declared on the FDA-approved prescribing information.[^dailymed]

Active, per gram:

- [[clindamycin-topical|Clindamycin phosphate]] 12 mg (1.2%), equivalent to 10 mg (1%) clindamycin.[^dailymed]
- [[benzoyl-peroxide|Benzoyl peroxide]] 37.5 mg (3.75%).[^dailymed]

The label warns that the product may bleach hair or colored fabric because of its benzoyl peroxide component, advises limiting sun exposure, and says to avoid combining it with topical or oral erythromycin-containing products because of its clindamycin component.[^dailymed]

## Where to Buy

Prescription only; obtain through a dermatologist or prescriber. Onexton Gel is supplied as a 50 g pump (NDC 0187-3050-50); pharmacists dispense it with an 8-week expiration date, and patients store it at room temperature at or below 25 degrees C (77 degrees F), pump upright, without freezing.[^dailymed] No official list price appears on a durable primary source, and per house policy aggregator price-comparison sites are not cited, so no price is stated here. Generic clindamycin-benzoyl peroxide gels containing the same actives are available and generally cost less than the brand.

## Common Marketing Claims

No brand marketing page was reviewed for this product; the claims below come from the FDA label's own characterization rather than promotional copy.

- **Once-daily dosing works.** Supported by the label's trial: once-daily application beat vehicle on both co-primary endpoints, with a 60.4% versus 31.3% mean reduction in inflammatory lesions at Week 12.[^dailymed]
- **A lower benzoyl peroxide strength (3.75%) is still effective.** The trial shows 3.75% in this combination beats vehicle, but there was no 5% arm and no benzoyl-peroxide-alone arm, so the label cannot show the lower strength matches higher-strength products or that the combination beats plain benzoyl peroxide.[^dailymed]
