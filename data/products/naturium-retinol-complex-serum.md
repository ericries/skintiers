Naturium Retinol Complex Serum is a leave-on serum built around [[retinol]] paired with a second, plant-derived retinol alternative, [[bakuchiol]], sold by Naturium alongside its [[naturium-vitamin-c-complex-serum|Vitamin C Complex Serum]].[^naturium]

> A budget dual-retinoid serum. Retinol is fifth on the declared ingredient list and bakuchiol seventh, with neither dosed on the label.[^naturium][^target] Retinol has vehicle-controlled trial evidence for fine wrinkles and pigment (see [[retinol]]), and one independent trial found bakuchiol matched retinol on the same measures (see [[bakuchiol]]), but the manufacturer's own clinical support for this formula is an 8-week study not cited to any published journal, with no sample size, control arm, or peer review disclosed, detailed in [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^naturium] At $21 for 30 ml from the manufacturer, discounted to $14.69 at Target, it is inexpensive relative to single-form, dosed OTC retinol serums.[^naturium][^target]

## Summary

Retinol Complex Serum is a mass-market retinoid serum from Naturium, sold through Target and the brand's own site. Its manufacturer tagline describes it as an "advanced retinol serum formulated with a photostable, synergistic blend of encapsulated retinol, a natural bio-retinol and bakuchiol."[^naturium] The declared ingredient list names the active as plain "Retinol," fifth on the list, with no separate encapsulation ingredient and no disclosed percentage; [[bakuchiol]], the "natural bio-retinol" referenced in the tagline, follows two places later at seventh, also undosed.[^naturium] A Target listing for the same SKU independently confirms the identical ingredient order.[^target]

The general case for topical [[retinol]], a vitamin A precursor with vehicle-controlled trial evidence for fine wrinkles and pigment in aging skin, is mapped on its own ingredient page. [[bakuchiol]] is not a vitamin A derivative but has one independent randomized trial (n=44) matching it to 0.5% retinol on wrinkle area and pigmentation; the wider bakuchiol evidence outside that one trial is thinner, [set out in full on bakuchiol](#the-evidence). Neither ingredient carries a disclosed percentage on this product, unlike single-form retinol serums that state a concentration on the label.

The formula also includes murumuru seed butter, cotton fruit water, linseed oil, hops cone extract, sodium hyaluronate ([[hyaluronic-acid]]), and tocopherol ([[tocopherol|vitamin E]]), none dosed or independently tested as isolated actives in this formula.[^naturium] The manufacturer's benefit claims include being "clinically proven to improve the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles plus skin tone and texture in just 8 weeks"; that claim is not cited to any published journal, with no sample size, control arm, statistical result, or peer review disclosed, detailed in [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^naturium] The brand's own directions describe applying it "onto face, neck and chest, avoiding the eye area," followed by moisturizer and daytime SPF, for PM use 3 to 5 times weekly.[^naturium] It is listed at $21 for 30 ml on the manufacturer's site and $14.69 (discounted from $16.89) for the same size at Target (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^naturium][^target]

## The Evidence

Topical [[retinol]] is a well-studied vitamin A precursor with vehicle-controlled trial evidence for improving fine wrinkles and pigment in aging skin; that evidence is graded in full on its own page. What is specific to this product is that the manufacturer discloses neither a retinol percentage nor an encapsulation ingredient distinct from plain "Retinol," despite the tagline's reference to "encapsulated retinol."[^naturium]

The manufacturer's declared ingredient list runs water, propanediol, murumuru seed butter, cotton fruit water, retinol, glycerin, bakuchiol, then a series of plant extracts and the base/preservative system, with neither retinol nor bakuchiol's concentration disclosed.[^naturium] A Target listing for the same SKU records an identical ingredient order, independently confirming retinol at fifth and bakuchiol at seventh.[^target]

[[bakuchiol]] is graded on its own page: one independent randomized trial (n=44, 12 weeks) found 0.5% bakuchiol matched 0.5% retinol on wrinkle area and pigmentation, with retinol users reporting more scaling and stinging, but later systematic reviews found the wider bakuchiol evidence mostly unblinded, uncontrolled, or tested inside combination products. No trial isolates bakuchiol's contribution alongside retinol at the undisclosed doses used here.

> Bottom line. Both declared actives have real category-level evidence (retinol for fine wrinkles and pigment; bakuchiol as a comparable but less-replicated alternative), but neither is dosed on this label, so the formula's own strength relative to a single-form, dosed retinol serum is not verifiable from the label alone.

The manufacturer's "clinically proven" claim is attributed to an unnamed 8-week study, described only by its benefit outcome and duration, with no sample size, control or vehicle arm, statistical results, or peer review disclosed.[^naturium]

> Bottom line. The wrinkle, tone, and texture claim rests on a manufacturer-cited study not cited to any published journal, with no sample size, control arm, statistical result, or peer review disclosed, so it is not an independently verifiable trial.[^naturium]
