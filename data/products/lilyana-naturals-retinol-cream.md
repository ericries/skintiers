LilyAna Naturals Retinol Cream is a botanical-emollient face moisturizer built around a low, undosed-on-label amount of [[retinol]], sold by the brand [[lilyana-naturals|LilyAna Naturals]] and through Target.[^brand][^target]

> A "natural" retinol moisturizer, not a dosed retinol serum. The manufacturer states the formula holds "just under 1%" retinol, and retinol is eleventh on a 29-ingredient declaration, behind plain emollients and butters.[^brand] Retinol itself has vehicle-controlled trial evidence for fine wrinkles and pigment (see [[retinol]]), but the brand's claim that its "proprietary blend" matches "the results of a 2.5% Retinol content" is not backed by any published data, [detailed below](#the-evidence).[^brand] At $29.99 for 1.7 oz from the manufacturer, discounted to $17.59 at Target, it is mid-priced for an over-the-counter retinol moisturizer.[^brand][^target]

## Summary

Retinol Cream is a face moisturizer from LilyAna Naturals, sold on the brand's own site and through Target under the title "LilyAna Naturals Retinol Face Moisturizer."[^brand][^target] The manufacturer describes it as a "lightweight and nourishing" cream and states it contains "just under 1%" retinol, the highest concentration the brand says is legal without a prescription, adding that its "proprietary blend gives it the results of a 2.5% Retinol content."[^brand] Neither claim is independently corroborated; the Target listing does not state a retinol percentage or repeat the 2.5%-equivalence comparison.[^target]

The general case for topical [[retinol]], a vitamin A precursor with vehicle-controlled trial evidence for fine wrinkles and pigment in aging skin, is mapped on its own ingredient page. What is specific to this product is where retinol sits on the label: eleventh among 29 declared ingredients, after aloe vera leaf juice, water, sunflower seed oil, isopropyl palmitate, glyceryl stearate, cetyl alcohol, glycerin, stearic acid, jojoba oil, and shea butter.[^brand] Because INCI order generally runs by descending concentration, that position is consistent with a genuinely low, sub-1% dose diluted into a butter- and oil-heavy base, rather than a dosed retinol serum built around the active.

The product is titled "Retinol Cream with Hyaluronic Acid," but the ingredient declaration contains no sodium hyaluronate or other form of [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]]; the "hyaluronic acid" referenced is Caesalpinia spinosa gum, which the manufacturer's own listing labels "Botanical Hyaluronic Acid," a different plant-derived polysaccharide gum rather than hyaluronic acid itself.[^brand] The rest of the formula is a mix of plant extracts, none dosed or independently tested as isolated actives in this product: panthenol ([[panthenol|vitamin B5]]), willow bark, green tea, [[centella-asiatica|Centella asiatica]] (gotu kola), horsetail, wild geranium, and dandelion extracts, alongside tocopheryl acetate and tocopherol (vitamin E).[^brand] The manufacturer's brand-wide marketing states the line is made with "98% Natural & Naturally-Derived Ingredients" and is free of parabens, added fragrance, phthalates, SLS/SLES, and petroleum.[^brand] The manufacturer's own directions describe the cream as "suitable for use in the morning and at night," recommending sunscreen daily "as retinol increases sun sensitivity" and alternating with a non-retinol moisturizer if dryness occurs.[^brand] It is listed at $29.99 for 1.7 oz on the manufacturer's site and $17.59 for the same size at Target (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^brand][^target]

## The Evidence

Topical [[retinol]] is a well-studied vitamin A precursor with vehicle-controlled trial evidence for improving fine wrinkles and pigment in aging skin; that evidence is graded in full on its own page. What is specific to this product is that the manufacturer's percentage claim is neither shown on the label as an exact figure nor corroborated by a second source, and the brand's separate claim of "2.5%"-equivalent results is not tied to any cited study, sample size, or methodology.[^brand]

The declared ingredient list runs aloe vera leaf juice, water, sunflower seed oil, isopropyl palmitate, glyceryl stearate, cetyl alcohol, glycerin, stearic acid, jojoba oil, shea butter, then retinol, followed by vitamin E forms, panthenol, Caesalpinia spinosa gum, and a series of botanical extracts.[^brand] A Target listing for the same SKU independently lists retinol among the "key ingredients" without disclosing quantity, consistent with but not confirming the manufacturer's position and percentage claims.[^target]

> Bottom line. Retinol has real vehicle-controlled trial evidence for fine wrinkles and pigment at the category level, but this product's own retinol dose is stated only in the manufacturer's own words ("just under 1%"), placed eleventh on the label behind six bulk emollients, and its comparison to a 2.5% formula is unverified marketing rather than a tested claim.[^brand]
