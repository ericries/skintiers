Bioderma Sensibio H2O Micellar Water (Bioderma) is a fragrance-free, no-rinse micellar cleansing water for sensitive skin, first launched by Bioderma in 1995.[^1]

> A simple, 10-ingredient no-rinse cleanser built around micellar technology rather than an active ingredient; Bioderma's own small in-vivo tests report high makeup- and particle-removal rates and good tolerance, but no independently published or peer-reviewed trial of the finished product was found.[^1]

## Summary

Sensibio H2O Micellar Water is a no-rinse, alcohol-free, unfragranced cleansing water that Bioderma describes as "the original" micellar water, invented by the brand in 1995 and marketed as trusted by dermatologists.[^1] It works by micellar technology: surfactant micelles suspended in the water that Bioderma says are designed to lift away makeup, sebum, and particulate impurities without rubbing or rinsing.[^1] The formula is deliberately minimal, [10 named ingredients](#whats-in-it) built around water, a mild PEG-based surfactant, a cucumber extract, and a proprietary "D.A.F." tolerance complex, with no fragrance, alcohol, or parabens.[^1]

What distinguishes it from a plainer micellar water or toner is mainly formulation philosophy (few ingredients, a stated skin-friendly pH, and the D.A.F. complex) rather than a treatment active with independent trial evidence; this is a cleansing/makeup-removal product, not a leave-on treatment. Bioderma's own product page cites small, self-run "in vivo" studies for [specific removal and tolerance numbers](#the-evidence), but none of those studies appear to be published in an independent, peer-reviewed source, so they are reported here as company-run testing rather than external validation. It is mid-priced for a micellar water: the 16.9 fl oz bottle lists at $20.99 direct from Bioderma, with smaller and travel sizes also sold (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-23).[^1]

## The Evidence

No independently published clinical trial of this product was found; the figures below come entirely from Bioderma's own product page, which cites small, self-conducted "in vivo" studies without naming a journal, a registry, or independent verification.[^1] Bioderma reports: makeup removal of 99% in a 10-subject test (ages 25-49); removal of 99% of "fine particles" modeling pollution in an 11-subject test (ages 18-48); cleansing of 78% of heavy metals in a 20-subject test (ages 40-70); and, in a 400-subject observational study over 28 days, 90% of participants reporting a decrease in "unpleasant sensation."[^1] Bioderma also states the product has undergone "very good skin and eye tolerance" testing worldwide across more than 1,600 subjects, without disclosing the study design or a comparator.[^1]

> Bioderma's own small, self-run tests report high makeup/particle removal and good tolerance, but with no disclosed independent publication, so these numbers describe what the manufacturer reports, not an externally verified result.

The product's central mechanism, micelles that lift oil-soluble and particulate impurities into the water phase without needing to be rinsed off, is a well-established principle of surfactant chemistry rather than something unique to this formula; Bioderma states the micelles here are designed to be gentler than typical surfactant micelles by mimicking the composition of the skin's own cell membrane, a formulation claim rather than an independently tested one.[^1]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), as declared on Bioderma's product page.[^1]

- water
- PEG-6 caprylic/capric glycerides (mild nonionic surfactant, the primary cleansing agent)
- fructooligosaccharides (humectant/prebiotic-marketed sugar)
- mannitol (humectant/sugar alcohol)
- xylitol (humectant/sugar alcohol)
- rhamnose (sugar, part of Bioderma's proprietary "D.A.F." tolerance complex)
- cucumis sativus (cucumber) fruit extract (botanical, marketed for soothing)
- propylene glycol (solvent/humectant)
- cetrimonium bromide (cationic surfactant/preservative)
- disodium EDTA (chelator)

Bioderma notes this ingredient list reflects the most recent formulation on the page cited and advises checking the packaging for the version actually purchased.[^1] The formula carries no added fragrance and no drug-active ingredient; its ten-ingredient count and omission of alcohol and parabens are stated by Bioderma as deliberate formulation choices.[^1]
