What distinguishes it from other 10% azelaic products is the added 0.5% salicylic acid, a second, labeled acne active. Board-certified dermatologist Dr. Jenna Queller calls it "a reliable option for acne that also treats redness or post-breakout marks," and dermatologist Dr. Corey L. ## Summary

Paula's Choice 10% Azelaic Acid Booster is built on two acids: [[azelaic-acid|azelaic acid]], its second ingredient at a labeled 10%, and [[salicylic-acid|salicylic acid]] (BHA), listed further down the ingredient list at a labeled 0.5%.[^dermstore][^pceu] Azelaic acid has real controlled evidence for treating inflammatory acne and papulopustular rosacea, established at prescription strengths, the 20% cream AZELEX and the 15% gel FINACEA (see [[azelaic-acid]]).[^azelex][^finacea] It also has trial evidence for lightening melasma at prescription strength, detailed on [[azelaic-acid]]. This product's 10% is half that cream strength and below the gel strength, the same shortfall carried by [[the-ordinary-azelaic-acid-suspension-10|The Ordinary's]] and [[anua-azelaic-acid-serum|Anua's]] 10% azelaic products.[^dermstore]

What sets it apart from those other 10% options is the added 0.5% salicylic acid, an oil-soluble beta hydroxy acid that sits on the US OTC acne monograph at 0.5% to 2%. At 0.5% it lands at the low end of that range, a quarter of the 2% top strength Paula's Choice itself uses in its own [[paulas-choice-skin-perfecting-2-bha|BHA exfoliant]].[^dermstore] Salicylic acid's own controlled acne evidence, set out on [[salicylic-acid]], is real but ranks below benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids in current dermatology guidelines.

Two things follow. The evidence that a 10% azelaic cosmetic, even paired with a low-dose BHA, does as much as the 15-20% prescription strengths [is thin](#the-evidence), so the product's expected benefit is inferred from each ingredient rather than demonstrated in a trial of this combined formula. But among the site's 10% azelaic options, this is the only one that also carries a labeled, monograph-strength acne active alongside the azelaic acid. Dr. Jenna Queller, a board-certified dermatologist, calls it "a reliable option for acne that also treats redness or post-breakout marks," and adds that its lightweight, non-clogging texture "can be mixed into moisturizer for drier skin types." Dr. Corey L. Hartman, another board-certified dermatologist, says the azelaic-salicylic acid combination "helps treat skin after breakouts" and "helps prevent new ones."[^allure] Allure named it a "Best for Dark Spots" pick in its azelaic acid roundup.[^allure]

It is mid-to-premium priced for the category: Dermstore lists the 30 ml (1 fl oz) tube at $42 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-07-31).[^dermstore] The manufacturer's own general-brightening and "younger-looking skin" language is separated out under [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).

## The Evidence

Whether azelaic acid works, and for what, is covered on [[azelaic-acid]]. In short, it is FDA-approved as a prescription drug for inflammatory acne, as a 20% cream (AZELEX), and for papulopustular rosacea, as a 15% gel (FINACEA).[^azelex][^finacea] It also has controlled trial evidence for lightening melasma (a 20% cream beat hydroquinone 2%), covered on [[azelaic-acid]]. Whether salicylic acid works, and for what, is covered on [[salicylic-acid]]. In short, it is an FDA OTC acne active at 0.5% to 2%; its controlled acne evidence is real but ranks below benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids in current dermatology guidelines, as set out on [[salicylic-acid]]. The product-specific question is how this 10% azelaic, 0.5% salicylic formula sits against its strength peers.

Most azelaic acid sold without a prescription is formulated at about 10%, and retailer listings for this product state "Azelaic Acid 10%."[^dermstore] That is half the concentration of the 20% prescription cream AZELEX and below the 15% of the prescription rosacea gel FINACEA, the same shortfall carried by The Ordinary's and Anua's 10% azelaic products.[^dermstore][^azelex][^finacea] Both concentration figures on this product, azelaic acid 10% and salicylic acid 0.5%, are cosmetic label claims rather than independently assayed or FDA-reviewed figures; salicylic acid's 0.5% does fall inside the OTC acne monograph range, but at its floor rather than the 2% top strength Paula's Choice uses in its own BHA exfoliant.[^dermstore][^pceu]

> **Bottom line.** This formula carries azelaic acid at a labeled 10%, below the 15% gel and 20% cream strengths behind azelaic acid's controlled evidence, and salicylic acid at a labeled 0.5%, within the FDA's OTC acne range but at its lowest strength. Its expected benefit is inferred from each ingredient at those concentrations rather than shown in a trial of this combined formula (see [[azelaic-acid]] and [[salicylic-acid]]).[^dermstore][^azelex][^finacea]

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared by the retailer.[^dermstore] The ingredients with a functional role are linked or named; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

Active and supporting ingredients:

- [[azelaic-acid|azelaic acid]], labeled 10% and listed second on the INCI (after water)
- [[salicylic-acid|salicylic acid]] (BHA), labeled 0.5% and listed further down the list
- adenosine, a cell-communicating soothing ingredient
- Glycyrrhiza Glabra (licorice) root extract, a soothing and brightening botanical
- Boerhavia Diffusa root extract, an antioxidant botanical
- allantoin and bisabolol, soothing agents

Base, texture, and preservative system: water, C12-15 alkyl benzoate and caprylic/capric triglyceride (emollients), methyl glucose sesquistearate, cetearyl alcohol, and glyceryl stearate (emulsifiers and texture), [[glycerin]], propanediol, and butylene glycol (humectants), [[dimethicone]] and cyclopentasiloxane (silicones), xanthan gum and sclerotium gum (thickeners), and phenoxyethanol (preservative).[^dermstore]

The formula carries no niacinamide and no added fragrance.[^dermstore] The manufacturer lists the finished product's pH at 3.0 to 4.5.[^pceu]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing claims not established by a trial of this product:

- **"Resulting in smoother, more radiant, younger-looking skin."** An anti-aging claim on the manufacturer's page.[^pceu] A systematic review searching specifically for azelaic acid skin-aging trials found none (see [[azelaic-acid]]).
- **"brightens dark spots, and fades postacne marks" and "clarify uneven tone."** Azelaic acid's pigment evidence is specifically for melasma and post-acne pigmentation, where a 20% cream beat hydroquinone 2%; generic brightening of already-even skin is not what those trials measured (see [[azelaic-acid]]).
- **"10% Azelaic Acid" and "Salicylic Acid (BHA) 0.5%."** Concentrations stated in retailer and manufacturer copy, not independently assayed figures; this is a cosmetic, not an FDA-reviewed drug like the 20% cream AZELEX.[^dermstore][^azelex]
