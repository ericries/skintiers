## Summary

First Aid Beauty Ultra Repair Cream Intense Hydration is a rich cream marketed for dry, distressed, and eczema-prone skin on the face and body.[^1] Its main job is a health one: relieving the itch and irritation of eczema and rashes, and helping a dry, barrier-impaired complexion hold onto moisture. It does that with colloidal oatmeal declared as its active ingredient at 0.5%, an ingredient the FDA formally recognizes as an over-the-counter skin protectant for exactly that use; the general evidence for colloidal oatmeal, including randomized trials in atopic dermatitis, is set out on [[colloidal-oatmeal]]. Around that active sit an occlusive (dimethicone), a humectant (glycerin), shea butter, squalane, and a single ceramide (ceramide NP), the ingredient classes that do the demonstrable work in any moisturizer; that general case is set out on [[moisturizing]].

What distinguishes this product from a plainer drugstore moisturizer is that colloidal oatmeal is declared as an active drug ingredient rather than a cosmetic one. The 0.5% concentration here sits below the 1% used in the two published randomized trials of colloidal oatmeal in eczema, though above the FDA's 0.007% monograph minimum (see [The Evidence](#the-evidence) and [[colloidal-oatmeal]]). First Aid Beauty markets the cream as "accepted by the National Eczema Association"[^1] (see [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims)). The declared ingredient list also includes Eucalyptus Globulus Leaf Oil, an aromatic essential oil, and the manufacturer's page carries no fragrance-free claim.[^1] The formula carries a single ceramide, ceramide NP, not the multi-ceramide, ceramide-dominant system used in the randomized trial covered on [[ceramides]].

It sells at a premium: First Aid Beauty lists the 6 fl oz jar at $42 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-06), well above fragrance-free basics such as [[cerave-moisturizing-cream|CeraVe Moisturizing Cream]], which lists a 12 oz jar at about $15.

## The Evidence

Colloidal oatmeal is one of a short list of cosmetic ingredients the FDA formally recognizes as an over-the-counter skin protectant, at a minimum concentration of 0.007%, for temporarily relieving minor skin irritation and itching from rashes, eczema, and poison ivy, oak, or sumac. At a 1% concentration, two randomized trials found it non-inferior to a prescription barrier cream and effective at reducing eczema severity and improving barrier measurements against a plain moisturizer; that evidence, and its limits (both trials were manufacturer-run), is set out on [[colloidal-oatmeal]]. This cream declares colloidal oatmeal as its active ingredient at 0.5%, midway between the FDA's regulatory floor and the concentration tested in those trials.[^1]

No published trial has tested this exact cream. What is specific to this formulation is a single ceramide, ceramide NP, alongside shea butter, squalane, and dimethicone.[^1] The closest controlled test of a ceramide moisturizer, a randomized trial of a ceramide-dominant cream-and-cleanser regimen, improved a barrier measurement (transepidermal water loss) but did not reduce eczema severity more than a matched base without ceramides; that trial and its limits are set out on [[ceramides]].

> **Bottom line.** No study has isolated this exact cream's own effect. Its case rests on the FDA-recognized, trial-supported role of colloidal oatmeal, declared here at a concentration below what the published trials tested, and on the general evidence for moisturizing and barrier lipids covered on [[colloidal-oatmeal]], [[ceramides]], and [[moisturizing]].

Barrier and hydration ingredients:
