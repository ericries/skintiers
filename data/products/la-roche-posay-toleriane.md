## Summary

La Roche-Posay Toleriane Double Repair Face Moisturizer is a fragrance-free face cream led by niacinamide and ceramide NP (ceramide-3) as its named barrier actives, in a glycerin, squalane, and dimethicone base. [What's In It](#whats-in-it) records that the formula has no petrolatum, unlike [[cerave-moisturizing-cream]], and no added fragrance, parabens, or drying alcohol.[^1]

Moisturizing has strong evidence for barrier-impaired skin, a health effect rather than a cosmetic one: [[moisturizing]] maps that general case, and a Cochrane review of 77 randomized trials covering 6603 participants found that moisturizer use reduced eczema flares and lowered severity, with the authors reporting that "most moisturisers showed some beneficial effects." The same review "did not find reliable evidence that one moisturiser is better than another," a category-level finding [graded here as a notable effect on solid evidence for moisturizing as a category](#the-evidence).[^2]

What is specific to this formula, the ceramide-and-niacinamide combination, rests on a thinner, health-relevant case. A randomized, placebo-controlled trial of a different ceramide-dominant cream, funded by that cream's own maker, improved a barrier measurement (transepidermal water loss) against a vehicle but did not reduce eczema severity any more than the vehicle did, [graded here as a minimal effect on mixed evidence against a plainer emollient or CeraVe Moisturizing Cream](#the-evidence).[^3] The manufacturer's barrier-repair-in-one-hour and 48-hour hydration figures, along with its prebiotic thermal water microbiome positioning, are label claims rather than independently tested outcomes, set out in [Common Marketing Claims](#common-marketing-claims).[^1]

## The Evidence

The general evidence for moisturizers and ceramide creams, which a ceramide-and-niacinamide cream like this draws on, is on [[moisturizing]].

van Zuuren 2017 was a Cochrane systematic review of 77 randomized controlled trials (6603 participants) of emollients and moisturizers for eczema. Moisturizer use versus none reduced flares (risk ratio 0.40, 95% CI 0.23 to 0.70; 2 studies, 87 participants) and lowered eczema severity. The authors concluded that "Most moisturisers showed some beneficial effects, producing better results when used with active treatment …" On comparisons between one product and another, they reported: "We did not find reliable evidence that one moisturiser is better than another."[^2]

> **Bottom line.** Moisturizers as a class help barrier-impaired skin, and in this review no single moisturizer was shown to outperform another.

Spada 2021 was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=100, 28 days) of a ceramide-dominant cream against a vehicle, funded by the cream's maker (Ego Pharmaceuticals). The ceramide cream improved transepidermal water loss versus the vehicle (mixed-model P=.0342). For eczema severity (EASI), "this decrease was not significant between groups at day 28 (P = .7804)."[^3]

> **Bottom line.** A ceramide cream improved a barrier measurement, but it did not reduce eczema any more than a plain vehicle did. This was a trial funded by the cream's own maker.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI) as declared on the manufacturer's current US product page, transcribed verbatim and corroborated against the Target product listing.[^1] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system. Retail ingredient listings for this product are not always in sync (some third-party pages show an older or reformulated version), so the manufacturer's current US list is used here as the reference.[^1]

Barrier and hydration ingredients:

- [[glycerin]], a humectant
- squalane and [[dimethicone]], emollients that slow water loss
- [[niacinamide]] (vitamin B3)
- ceramide NP (labeled ceramide-3), one of the [[ceramides]], a barrier lipid

Base, texture, and preservative system: water (marketed as La Roche-Posay prebiotic thermal water), zea mays (corn) starch, ammonium polyacryloyldimethyl taurate and xanthan gum (thickeners), myristyl myristate (emollient), stearic acid, myristic acid and palmitic acid (fatty acids), potassium cetyl phosphate and glyceryl stearate SE (emulsifiers), sodium hydroxide (pH adjuster), and capryloyl glycine and caprylyl glycol (conditioning and preservative system).[^1]

Not in the formula: no petrolatum (unlike [[cerave-moisturizing-cream]]), no added fragrance, no parabens, and no drying alcohol.[^1]

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above. These are manufacturer claims, quarantined here and not treated as findings:

- **Barrier repair and hydration.** The manufacturer says the cream "Helps repair skin's barrier after 1HR" and "Provides 48-HR long-lasting hydration."[^1] The graded evidence here is category-level moisturization (see [[moisturizing]]), which in the Cochrane review did not show one moisturizer beating another.[^2]
- **Prebiotic thermal water and the skin microbiome.** The brand positions its La Roche-Posay Prebiotic Thermal Water as helping rebalance and support the skin microbiome; that thermal spring water is the aqua/water base of the formula.[^1] A positioning claim; no independent trial is cited here.
- **Suitable for sensitive skin, non-comedogenic, allergy-tested, and dermatologist-tested.** Manufacturer label claims, not independently tested outcomes.[^1]
