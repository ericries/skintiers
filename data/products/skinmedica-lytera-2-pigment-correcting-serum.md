SkinMedica Lytera 2.0 Pigment Correcting Serum is a hydroquinone-free brightening serum from [[skinmedica|SkinMedica]] (an AbbVie/Allergan brand), launched in 2017 as a reformulation of the original Lytera, and sold as a 2 fl. oz. (60 ml) bottle for $170.00 through authorized retailers.[^skinmedica-timeline][^beautysense]

> A four-active brightening serum built around tranexamic acid, phenylethyl resorcinol, and niacinamide, each individually studied for pigmentation, but not tested together in this exact combination or disclosed dose, [detailed under The Evidence](#the-evidence). At $170 for 60 ml, it competes on formulation breadth rather than proven superiority over its individual ingredients.

## Summary

Lytera 2.0 is positioned by SkinMedica as an "advanced" successor to the original Lytera Skin Brightening Complex, formulated "to address the appearance of stubborn skin discoloration for a variety of skin types and ethnicities" without hydroquinone.[^skinmedica-timeline] A retailer listing (Beautysense, an authorized Canadian SkinMedica retailer) describes it as "formulated to address the appearance of stubborn skin discoloration in a non-hydroquinone, retinol-free, paraben-free, fragrance-free, non-comedogenic formulation," directed for twice-daily use on face, neck, and chest of affected areas alongside SPF 20+ sun protection.[^beautysense]

The formula's disclosed active ingredients are tranexamic acid, phenylethyl resorcinol, niacinamide, and tetrapeptide-30, each with a plain-language function claim from the retailer's ingredient breakdown but no disclosed percentage.[^beautysense] [[Tranexamic-acid|Tranexamic acid]] and [[phenylethyl-resorcinol|phenylethyl resorcinol]] both have category-level trial evidence for hyperpigmentation, and [[niacinamide]] has its own separately graded evidence base; [that case is set out on those ingredient pages, not repeated here](#the-evidence). SkinMedica has not published a peer-reviewed or independently registered trial of this specific four-active serum, so this page cannot verify concentrations, and the cosmetic claim rests on the category-level evidence for its named actives rather than a trial of the finished product.

As of research, the product no longer appears in SkinMedica's own current US online catalog (its official product page returns a "Page Not Found" error, and it is absent from the brand's current 8-item Brighteners category listing), though it remains listed and sold by authorized third-party retailers.[^skinmedica-404][^skinmedica-brighteners]

## The Evidence

Whether tranexamic acid, phenylethyl resorcinol, and niacinamide individually improve hyperpigmentation is covered on their own ingredient pages and on [[hyperpigmentation]]: in short, each has published trial evidence supporting a brightening effect at studied concentrations, [set out in full there](#the-evidence). None of the three ingredient pages, nor SkinMedica's own marketing material, cite a trial of this exact four-active serum combining tranexamic acid, phenylethyl resorcinol, niacinamide, and tetrapeptide-30 together.

> **Bottom line.** The case for this serum rests on category-level evidence for its individual disclosed actives, not on a trial of the product itself; concentrations are undisclosed, so this page cannot confirm the actives are present at the doses those category trials tested.

## What's In It

The full ingredient list (INCI), declared on an authorized retailer's product page.[^beautysense] The ingredients with a functional role are linked to their own pages; the rest are the base, texture, and preservative system.

Actives, as named by the retailer's "Active Ingredients" breakdown (concentrations not disclosed):

- [[tranexamic-acid|Tranexamic Acid]], described by the retailer as helping "correct the appearance of hyperpigmentation"
- [[phenylethyl-resorcinol|Phenylethyl Resorcinol]], described as helping "support melanin balance to correct the appearance of hyperpigmentation"
- [[niacinamide|Niacinamide]] and Tetrapeptide-30, described as helping "prevent the appearance of new spots while visibly balancing the appearance of your skin tone"[^beautysense]

Full INCI order: Water, Butylene Glycol, Glycerin, Niacinamide, Polyacrylate-13, Phenylethyl Resorcinol, Tranexamic Acid, Vitis Vinifera (Grape) Flower Cell Extract, Plankton Extract, Tetrapeptide-30, Artemisia Capillaris Flower Extract, Vibrio Alginolyticus Ferment Filtrate, Alteromonas Ferment Extract, [[tocopherol|Tocopheryl Acetate]], Polyisobutene, Polysorbate 20, Caprylic/Capric Triglyceride, Hydroxyacetophenone, Phytic Acid, Diethylhexyl Syringylidenemalonate, Disodium EDTA, Ethylhexylglycerin, Phenoxyethanol.[^beautysense]

Niacinamide's position 4th of 23 ingredients (right after the base solvents) is consistent with it being a headline active by concentration, though the retailer does not disclose an exact percentage, and this page does not guess one.
