Prequel Sun Barrier Mineral Sunscreen is a fragrance-free, tinted mineral facial sunscreen from [[prequel]], rated Broad Spectrum SPF 50 PA++++ on a Drug Facts panel that lists zinc oxide 18.0% as its sole active ingredient.[^pdp][^target]

> At 18% zinc oxide, this carries roughly double the mineral load of EltaMD UV Clear (9% zinc oxide), a widely used US mineral sunscreen whose UVA reach is graded modest; the higher load and the label's PA++++ rating point to stronger UVA coverage from a single, photostable filter, covered generally on [[zinc-oxide]].[^pdp][^target] The Drug Facts panel names zinc oxide as the only active, so the "Chemical Boosters" promised in the product's own name are not a second UV filter: titanium dioxide, mica, and iron oxides also appear in the ingredient list, but as colorants, not actives.[^pdp] It is priced in line with premium US mineral sunscreens, at $22.00 for 1.7 fl oz on Prequel's own site and $21.99 at Target for the same size (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^pdp][^target]

## Summary

Sun Barrier Mineral Sunscreen is Prequel's all-mineral facial sunscreen, described on the brand's own product page as a "lightweight, semi-transparent mineral sunscreen with chemical boosters providing physical protection while strengthening the moisture barrier."[^pdp] Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen is among the best-evidenced routine skincare steps for slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer risk, a category-level case with randomized-trial support set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

The Drug Facts panel lists a single active, zinc oxide 18.0%, and rates the product Broad Spectrum SPF 50 PA++++; Target's listing for the same SKU independently corroborates both the 18.0% zinc oxide active and the SPF 50 PA++++ rating.[^pdp][^target] Because it carries a US Drug Facts panel, the SPF 50 figure is an FDA-tested rating under the US OTC sunscreen monograph, unlike sunscreens sold only under a foreign testing standard. At 18%, the zinc oxide load is substantially higher than lower-percentage US mineral sunscreens such as [[eltamd-uv-clear-spf-46]] (9% zinc oxide, whose UVA coverage is graded modest); [the filter comparison is set out below](#the-evidence).

Despite the product's own name, "Sun Barrier Mineral Sunscreen + Chemical Boosters," the Drug Facts panel names no second UV filter: titanium dioxide, mica, and iron oxides appear in the ingredient list as colorants (used for the formula's tinted, "peachy hue" finish the brand describes), not as declared actives.[^pdp] The "boosters" the name and marketing description refer to are a set of botanical antioxidant and soothing ingredients (bisabolol, ginger root extract, tetrahydrocurcuminoids, calendula, green tea leaf, chamomile, and centella asiatica), present per the brand's own ingredient panel but not independently tested for this formula.[^pdp]

The product is priced in line with premium US mineral sunscreens: Prequel lists the 1.7 fl oz (50 mL) bottle at $22.00, and Target lists the same size at $21.99 (see [Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^pdp][^target]

## The Evidence

Whether regular sunscreen use works, and how the SPF (UVB) and UVA axes differ, is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. What is specific to this product is its filter set and its FDA label rating.

The Drug Facts panel on Prequel's own product page lists a single active ingredient, zinc oxide 18.0%, at Broad Spectrum SPF 50, and the label separately carries a PA++++ rating (Water Resistant 40 minutes).[^pdp] Target's independent listing for the same SKU corroborates the same active (zinc oxide 18.0%), the same SPF 50 PA++++ rating, and the same 1.7 fl oz size.[^target] Zinc oxide is a photostable mineral filter with real broad-spectrum coverage, reaching further into long-wave UVA than titanium dioxide or most single organic filters; that general filter evidence is set out on [[zinc-oxide]]. At 18%, this formula's zinc oxide load is roughly double that of [[eltamd-uv-clear-spf-46]] (9.0% zinc oxide), a US mineral sunscreen whose UVA coverage is graded modest on the strength of that lower concentration.

Two things temper the UVA picture. The PA++++ mark is a Japanese/Korean-style graded UVA scale, not part of the FDA's own "Broad Spectrum" claim, which remains a single pass/fail test rather than a numeric UVA rating; neither the brand page nor Target's listing states that the PA rating itself was independently FDA-verified.[^pdp][^target] And "Chemical Boosters" in the product name is not a second UV filter: the only Drug Facts active listed is zinc oxide, and titanium dioxide, mica, and iron oxides in the ingredient list are declared for their tinting role rather than as actives.[^pdp]

> **Bottom line.** The FDA-tested Broad Spectrum SPF 50 rating and the 18.0% zinc oxide active are corroborated on two independent listings.[^pdp][^target] Zinc oxide's photostable, broad UVA reach makes this a genuinely higher-UVA-load mineral sunscreen than a lower-percentage product like EltaMD UV Clear (9% zinc oxide).[^pdp] The label's added PA++++ rating is a supplementary graded-UVA claim outside the FDA's own pass/fail Broad Spectrum test, and despite the "+ Chemical Boosters" name, no second UV filter appears on the Drug Facts panel.[^pdp][^target]
