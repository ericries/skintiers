Mirvaso is Galderma's brand-name formulation of brimonidine tartrate 0.33% gel, a prescription-only topical applied once daily to reduce the persistent facial redness of [[rosacea]].[^dailymed]

> A once-daily, 0.33% branded gel that constricts small facial blood vessels to blanch persistent redness for a matter of hours. Its own FDA-cited trials found a strict two-scale composite success in about a quarter to a third of treated patients versus roughly one in ten on an inert vehicle, but the effect fades within the day, it does nothing for the inflammatory bumps and pustules of rosacea, and the label warns some users get rebound erythema worse than their starting redness. See [[brimonidine]] for the full trial and rebound-rate record.[^dailymed]

## Summary

Mirvaso is the Galderma brand of brimonidine tartrate 0.33% gel, prescribed once daily for the "topical treatment of persistent (nontransient) facial erythema of rosacea in adults 18 years of age or older," per the FDA label.[^dailymed] It is Rx-only.[^dailymed] The drug works by direct vasoconstriction of small facial blood vessels rather than by any anti-inflammatory or antimicrobial action, so it addresses the background redness of [[rosacea-erythematotelangiectatic|erythematotelangiectatic rosacea]] specifically, not the inflammatory papules and pustules of the other rosacea phenotype, which are treated instead with topicals such as [[metronidazole]], [[azelaic-acid|azelaic acid]], or [[ivermectin-topical|ivermectin]].[^dailymed]

What this product adds is the specific 0.33% once-daily formulation that Galderma's own FDA submission tested: in two identical, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials, a strict composite success measure (a 2-grade improvement on both a clinician-rated and a patient-rated erythema scale) was reached by 31% of MIRVASO subjects versus 11% on vehicle at hour 3 of day 29 in one trial, and 25% versus 9% in the other ([the evidence](#the-evidence)).[^dailymed] The benefit is fast but temporary; the label states the effect "may begin to diminish hours after application."[^dailymed] A documented minority of users experience the opposite of the intended effect, a rebound where erythema returns worse than baseline once the drug wears off ([the evidence](#the-evidence)).[^dailymed] The full comparative and rebound-rate evidence for brimonidine as a molecule, including how it stacks up against the sibling drug oxymetazoline, is set out on [[brimonidine]].

Mirvaso is dispensed only by prescription, applied once daily as a thin layer to the face.[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

Brimonidine's mechanism, trial base, and documented rebound-erythema rate are covered in full on [[brimonidine]]; brimonidine is a well-evidenced, modest-effect, fast-onset but temporary treatment for the persistent redness of rosacea, with no demonstrated effect on inflammatory lesions.[^dailymed] The trial data below is the product-specific evidence the FDA label cites for this exact once-daily 0.33% gel.

Two identical, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials enrolled a total of 553 adults with moderate to severe persistent facial erythema of rosacea and treated them once daily for 4 weeks.[^dailymed] The primary endpoint was a strict composite: at least a 2-grade improvement on both the Clinician's Erythema Assessment and the Patient's Self-Assessment scales, measured at hours 3, 6, 9, and 12 on day 29.[^dailymed] Composite success at hour 3 was 31% for brimonidine versus 11% for vehicle in the first trial, and 25% versus 9% in the second, with brimonidine ahead of vehicle at every measured hour of the 12-hour day.[^dailymed] The label states the treatment effect "may begin to diminish hours after application," and separately warns that "some subjects in the clinical trials reported a rebound phenomenon, where erythema was reported to return worse compared to the severity at baseline," with postmarketing reports describing erythema on previously unaffected areas and, in some cases, spreading to the neck and chest.[^dailymed]

> Bottom line: against an inert vehicle gel, once-daily Mirvaso brought about a quarter to a third of treated patients to a strict two-scale redness-improvement threshold within a few hours, an effect the label itself says fades within the day, and which the label's own rebound warning says can reverse into worse-than-baseline redness in some users.

## What's In It

- **Active: brimonidine tartrate 0.33%** (equivalent to 3.3 mg brimonidine free base per gram). See [[brimonidine]] for the graded mechanism, comparative trial record, and rebound-erythema data.[^dailymed]
- **Inactive ingredients:** carbomer homopolymer type B, glycerin, methylparaben, phenoxyethanol, propylene glycol, purified water, sodium hydroxide, and titanium dioxide, as declared on the FDA label.[^dailymed]

The label directs application of a pea-sized amount once daily to each of five facial areas (central forehead, chin, nose, each cheek), for topical use only, not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use.[^dailymed] The most common adverse reactions in controlled trials (incidence greater than 1%) were erythema, flushing, skin burning sensation, and contact dermatitis.[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven unless it also appears in The Evidence above.

- **"A rosacea treatment," without qualification.** Mirvaso is FDA-approved only for persistent facial erythema. It carries no approval for the inflammatory papules and pustules of [[rosacea-papulopustular|papulopustular rosacea]] or for the visible blood vessels of erythematotelangiectatic rosacea.[^dailymed]
- **All-day redness control.** The label's own composite-success data shows brimonidine ahead of vehicle through the 12-hour measured day, but success on the strict two-scale threshold was reached by only a minority of treated patients even at its peak, and the label states the effect "may begin to diminish hours after application."[^dailymed]

## Where to Buy

Mirvaso is dispensed only by prescription; the FDA label lists it "Rx Only," with no over-the-counter purchase path.[^dailymed] It is supplied as a gel in 2 g, 30 g, and 45 g tubes, a 30 g pump, and a blister pack, per the FDA label (accessed 2026-08-07).[^dailymed]
