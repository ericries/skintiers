Neutrogena Hydro Boost Gel-Cream (fragrance-free), sold by Target under the title "Neutrogena Hydro Boost Water Gel Cream Face Moisturizer with Hyaluronic Acid for Normal to Dry Skin - Fragrance Free," is the fragrance-free sibling of [[neutrogena-hydro-boost-water-gel]], an oil-free gel-cream moisturizer marketed for normal-to-dry, combination, acne-prone, and sensitive skin.[^target]

> A fragrance-free humectant gel-cream for normal-to-dry skin. Its hydrating case rests on the well-evidenced general role of glycerin and hyaluronic acid as humectants (see [[glycerin]], [[hyaluronic-acid]], and [[moisturizing]]); glycerin, a higher-tier humectant on [[moisturizing]]'s own evidence ranking, is listed second in the ingredient list, ahead of sodium hyaluronate. Its only occlusive is a thin silicone (dimethicone) rather than petrolatum or ceramides, so [the case for it on dry or barrier-impaired skin specifically is thinner than for everyday hydration](#the-evidence). Unlike the fragranced Hydro Boost Water Gel, this SKU carries no added fragrance or dye.[^target]

## Summary

Neutrogena Hydro Boost Gel-Cream (fragrance-free) is Neutrogena's fragrance-free Hydro Boost moisturizer for normal-to-dry skin, sold by Target as "Neutrogena Hydro Boost Water Gel Cream Face Moisturizer with Hyaluronic Acid for Normal to Dry Skin - Fragrance Free," and recommended there for normal-to-dry, combination, acne-prone, and sensitive skin.[^target] It is the fragrance-free counterpart to [[neutrogena-hydro-boost-water-gel]], the same line's fragranced version built for normal-to-oily skin; this gel-cream instead targets the drier end of the Hydro Boost range and drops the added fragrance and Blue 1 dye that the water gel carries.[^target]

Its declared ingredient list opens water, glycerin, dimethicone, cetearyl olivate and sorbitan olivate (emulsifiers), then sodium hyaluronate, followed by a long block of amino acids, sodium PCA, sodium lactate, urea, and sugars that Target's listing describes as delivering hydration "with natural moisturizing factors including amino acids and electrolytes."[^target] Two things follow from that order. First, [[glycerin]], a longer-established and higher-tier humectant on [[moisturizing]]'s own evidence ranking, sits ahead of sodium hyaluronate in this formula, so glycerin is likely doing at least as much of the hydrating work as the headline hyaluronic acid. Second, the amino-acid and sugar block mirrors the skin's own natural moisturizing factors, a recognized humectant strategy, though this exact combination has not been tested in a published trial. The only occlusive in the formula is dimethicone, a lighter silicone rather than the petrolatum or ceramides that dermatology-focused creams such as [[cerave-moisturizing-cream|CeraVe Moisturizing Cream]] build around, so [its case for dry or barrier-impaired skin is thinner than for lighter, everyday hydration](#the-evidence).[^target]

No published trial has tested this exact gel-cream; its case rests on the general evidence for glycerin and hyaluronic acid as humectants ([see The Evidence](#the-evidence), and [[glycerin]], [[hyaluronic-acid]], and [[moisturizing]]). It is inexpensive: Target lists the 0.5 fl oz size at $9.97 ($19.94 per fluid ounce), with a 1.7 fl oz size also sold ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-07).[^target]

## The Evidence

Glycerin and hyaluronic acid, applied topically as humectants, reliably raise a measured skin-hydration reading in controlled testing; both cases are set out on [[glycerin]], [[hyaluronic-acid]], and [[moisturizing]], where glycerin sits in a higher evidence tier than hyaluronic acid. This product's declared ingredient list places glycerin second overall and sodium hyaluronate further down, after the emulsifiers, so glycerin's well-evidenced humectant effect is at least as relevant to this formula as the headline hyaluronic-acid claim.[^target]

No published trial has tested this exact gel-cream. What is specific to this formulation, beyond its humectant blend, is its occlusive: dimethicone, which sits lower on [[moisturizing]]'s own evidence-ranked tier of moisturizing ingredients than petrolatum or ceramides, and the formula contains neither of those stronger options.[^target] That composition fact, not a study result, is why the barrier-support grade above is lower than the hydration grade: the case for this exact gel-cream on dry or barrier-impaired skin is thinner than the case for it on normal-to-dry, everyday hydration, where a lighter occlusive plus multiple humectants is a more typical fit.

> **Bottom line.** No study has isolated this gel-cream's own effect. Its case rests on the general evidence for glycerin and hyaluronic acid as humectants, applied to a formula whose only occlusive (dimethicone) is lighter than the petrolatum- or ceramide-based options built for drier or barrier-impaired skin.
