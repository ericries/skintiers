A seven-ingredient ampoule sold entirely on centella. **Bottom line:** the brand publishes the full ingredient list for this one, which is unusually forthcoming, and that list puts centella fourth behind water, glycerin and butylene glycol. Glycerin grades better on this site than centella does, so the thing most likely to be doing the work is not the thing on the label. Centella's evidence is argued at [[centella-asiatica]] and glycerin's at [[glycerin]].

> **The brand names exactly one key ingredient, and it is fourth on its own list.** SKIN1004's page gives "Key Ingredients: Centella Asiatica Extract" and nothing else, then prints the full declaration: "Water, Glycerin, Butylene Glycol, Centella Asiatica Extract, 1,2-Hexanediol, Cellulose Gum, Ethylhexylglycerin".[^brand] Position is not concentration and this does not make the centella trivial. It does mean three things are present in larger amounts than the one the bottle is named after.

## Summary

SKIN1004 sells it in three sizes on its own storefront, all in stock: **$9.50 for 30ml, $13.50 for 55ml and $19.80 for 100ml**, against a listed compare-at price of $22.00.[^brand] The page describes it as "An all-rounder ampoule formulated to help soothe visible redness and comfort sensitive-looking skin, featuring high quality Madagascan Centella Asiatica extract", with benefits given as "Soothing, Hydrating" and skin type as "Suitable for all skin types, especially dry and delicate skin."[^brand] The bottle reads "MADE WITH PURE CENTELLA FROM MADAGASCAR".[^brand]

At under twenty cents a millilitre in the largest size, it is among the cheapest serums this site covers.

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: minimal for the claim it is sold on.** Soothing is the lead benefit, centella is the only named key ingredient, and [[centella-asiatica]] carries `tier: weak` here. No trial of this product exists.
- **Effect size: modest for what it most likely does.** [[glycerin]] carries `tier: good`, better than centella, and it is second on the brand's own list.[^brand] A cheap, well-made humectant serum is a reasonable thing to own; it is just not what the marketing is about.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary, but the disclosure is better than its sibling's.** This page can say where the centella sits because the brand prints the full declaration. Its own [[skin1004-centella-soothing-cream]] page cannot say the same thing about the ceramides in that product, because the brand publishes only a five-item Key Ingredients line there. Same brand, same storefront, two different levels of disclosure.
- **Record finding: the formula is short and there is nowhere to hide.** Seven ingredients: a solvent, two humectants, the extract, a preservative-booster, a thickener and a second preservative-booster.[^brand] Nothing in it is doing anything exotic, which is worth knowing before paying a premium for a centella name.
- **What the label states, and what it does not.** "MADE WITH PURE CENTELLA FROM MADAGASCAR" is a sourcing claim about the extract, not a strength.[^brand] No percentage for the centella appears anywhere on the page read.

## The Evidence

None specific to this product. The two ingredients that matter are argued elsewhere and not restated: [[centella-asiatica]] for the headline and [[glycerin]] for the humectant that outranks it both in the bottle and in this site's grading. [[best-face-moisturizers-by-evidence]] ranks the wider field.

> **Bottom line.** Nine dollars fifty for a short, clean humectant serum with centella in it. That is a fair deal. What it is not is a centella treatment, and the brand's own ingredient list is the thing that says so.
