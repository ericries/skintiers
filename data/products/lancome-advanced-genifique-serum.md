Advanced Genifique is Lancome's long-running, best-selling luxury serum, led by bifida ferment lysate (10% "Bifidus Prebiotic," by the brand's own disclosure) alongside ascorbyl glucoside (branded "Vitamin Cg"), sodium hyaluronate, and adenosine, in a base that carries alcohol denat and fragrance.[^lancome] It costs $220.00 for the single available size, 3.4 oz / 100 mL, on the brand's own site.[^lancome]

> The formula is built on a ferment plus three low-list supporting actives, none of them the ingredient classes with the deepest independent anti-aging evidence. Bifida ferment lysate itself carries only thin, largely in vitro support (see [[bifida-ferment-lysate]]). Lancome markets "Vitamin Cg" as a headline ingredient, but read against the INCI list that is ascorbyl glucoside, a weak vitamin C derivative that must first be converted by skin enzymes into actual ascorbic acid before it can act as an antioxidant, not the well-studied [[vitamin-c]] molecule itself (see [[ascorbyl-glucoside]]). Adenosine and sodium hyaluronate are hydrating and cosmetic-support actives with their own general evidence pages ([[adenosine]], [[hyaluronic-acid]]), but this product discloses no concentration for either. The serum contains no retinoid, the active class with the strongest independently replicated evidence for wrinkles and photoaging (see [[retinoids]]). Lancome's own clinical figures are unpublished consumer self-assessment surveys and brand-run grading rather than independent trials.

## Summary

Lancome markets Advanced Genifique as "a hydrating serum that strengthens the skin's protective moisture barrier for healthy, glowing skin," describing it as formulated with "10% Bifidus Prebiotic, Hyaluronic Acid, and Vitamin Cg" to "improve the look of dull skin and the signs of aging for visibly glowing skin," with results claimed "in just 7 days."[^lancome] Read against the ingredient list, that "Bifidus Prebiotic" is bifida ferment lysate, second on the list right after water, and "Vitamin Cg" is ascorbyl glucoside, further down the list; the general evidence for both is covered on [[bifida-ferment-lysate]] and [[ascorbyl-glucoside]].

The formula also carries sodium hyaluronate for hydration and adenosine, plus a yeast extract, further down the list ([full list below](#whats-in-it)).[^lancome] None of the actives has a disclosed concentration except the bifida ferment lysate, which the brand states is "10%." Notably absent is a retinoid, the active class with the strongest independent anti-aging evidence; neither ascorbic acid nor any stronger vitamin C derivative appears on the list, only the weak ascorbyl glucoside form. Alcohol denat sits fourth on the ingredient list, and the formula carries added fragrance plus the fragrance allergens limonene and citronellol.

It costs $220.00 for the only size Lancome sells it in, 3.4 oz / 100 mL ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-09-21).[^lancome]

## The Evidence

No independently published, controlled trial of this exact serum was located. Lancome's own product page cites unpublished, brand-run testing without a named journal or control arm: a "4-week consumer study of 1,127 women" in which "89% agree skin looks more radiant" and "89% agree skin texture looks smoother" after one week, rising to "90% agree overall skin appearance & quality looks improved" after four weeks, alongside separate brand-run clinical grading claiming improvements of "+27% radiance," "+48% firmness," "+42% evenness," "+21% smoothness," and "+50% elasticity," and reductions of "-42% fine lines," "-46% wrinkles," and "-14% pores" after 8 weeks.[^lancome] These are self-reported consumer-survey percentages and brand-graded clinical parameters, not a peer-reviewed, placebo-controlled trial.

The ingredient-level evidence is set out on the linked ingredient pages. Bifida ferment lysate's independent evidence is thin: a 2023 in vitro study found it changed barrier-gene and antioxidant markers in cultured skin cells (not human skin), and a 2022 trial found benefit for acne only as one of four actives in a combination product, so the result cannot be credited to bifida ferment lysate alone (see [[bifida-ferment-lysate]]). Ascorbyl glucoside has no antioxidant activity of its own until skin enzymes convert it to ascorbic acid; the human trials that exist test it combined with other actives or as a concentrated complex, never the plain molecule alone at typical serum strength (see [[ascorbyl-glucoside]]). Adenosine's topical anti-wrinkle evidence comes from two small trials run or funded by companies selling the product tested, both measuring surface appearance rather than a biological change in skin (see [[adenosine]]). Sodium hyaluronate's hydration mechanism is well established generally (see [[hyaluronic-acid]], tier: strong), but this product does not disclose its concentration, so the strength of that general evidence cannot be assumed to transfer at whatever dose is used here.

The product contains no retinoid, the active category with the deepest base of independently replicated human trials for wrinkles, texture, and photoaging (see [[retinoids]]).

> **Bottom line.** A well-tolerated, cosmetically elegant hydrating and antioxidant-support serum built on a ferment and a trio of thin-evidence supporting actives, at a luxury price. The brand's own figures are unpublished consumer surveys and brand-graded clinical parameters, not independent trials. Readers looking for the best-evidenced topical anti-aging active should start with [[retinoids]], which this serum does not contain.

## Common Marketing Claims

Label and marketing terms not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Strengthens the skin's protective moisture barrier" and "visibly hydrates, plumps and evens tone... in just 7 days."** Rests on sodium hyaluronate at an undisclosed concentration plus the supporting actives; no independently published trial measures barrier strength or a 7-day timeline for this exact serum.[^lancome]
- **"Vitamin Cg... a water-soluble, stable vitamin C derivative."** Accurate as a chemistry description, but "Vitamin Cg" is Lancome's marketing name for ascorbyl glucoside, a derivative that must be converted by skin enzymes into ascorbic acid before it has antioxidant activity, and no human trial has tested it alone at this formula's strength (see [[ascorbyl-glucoside]]).[^lancome]
- **"10% Bifidus Prebiotic... helps provide a favorable environment for good bacteria."** Bifida ferment lysate is a dead-cell fermentation byproduct, not a live probiotic, so it cannot recolonize or rebalance a microbiome the way this phrasing implies; see the probiotic-versus-postbiotic distinction on [[probiotics]] and the thin independent evidence on [[bifida-ferment-lysate]].[^lancome]
- **"Patented until 2031... Over 170 beauty awards worldwide."** Patent status and industry beauty awards describe the brand's intellectual property and marketing recognition, not independently verified clinical efficacy.[^lancome]
- **"90% agree overall skin appearance & quality looks improved," "+27% radiance," "+50% elasticity," and similar figures.** Self-reported consumer-survey percentages and brand-graded clinical parameters from unpublished, brand-run testing, not a peer-reviewed controlled trial.[^lancome]
