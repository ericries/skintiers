Anua PDRN Caffeine Rejuvenating Eye Cream is an eye-area moisturizer sold exclusively at Ulta for $20, built around two headline actives, polydeoxyribonucleotide (PDRN, listed on labels as sodium DNA) and caffeine, neither of which has controlled trial evidence for a leave-on eye cream.[^dray]

> A trending-ingredient eye cream whose own on-camera review from a board-certified dermatologist calls the headline ingredient "a gimmick": PDRN's real evidence base is injected or wound-healing research, not topical eye-area cosmetics, and isolated topical caffeine has no controlled trial for dark circles or puffiness either. The reviewing dermatologist still liked the product, but credited that to its moisturizing base and niacinamide, not to PDRN or caffeine specifically.[^dray]

## Summary

[[anua|Anua]], a Korean skincare brand, markets this cream as a "PDRN caffeine rejuvenating eye cream," sold only through Ulta at a $20 retail price for a small tube.[^dray] Both name-brand actives sit at the center of current skincare-trend marketing: PDRN (polydeoxyribonucleotide, a mixture of DNA fragments usually sourced from salmon or trout milt and declared on ingredient lists as sodium DNA) is the ingredient behind injectable "salmon DNA" treatments like Rejuran, [graded in full on [[polydeoxyribonucleotide-pdrn]]](#the-evidence); caffeine is a common eye-cream ingredient marketed for de-puffing and brightening, [graded in full on [[caffeine]]](#the-evidence).[^dray]

Board-certified dermatologist Andrea Suarez (Dr Dray) reviewed this specific product on video and, while she liked using it, was explicit that she would not recommend buying it for the PDRN: "I would not go buying this because the PDRN is going to change the game... PDRN is a gimmick. PDRN in this is not going to do anything."[^dray] She was more measured on caffeine, calling it "a reasonable ingredient to pursue" for its antioxidant properties while cautioning that its vasoconstrictive, de-puffing effect on dark circles is only a "potential" benefit that does not address the age-related bone and fat-compartment changes that cause most under-eye hollowing.[^dray] Her overall verdict credited the product's feel to its non-headline ingredients: glycerin, hydroxyethylurea, and sodium hyaluronate for hydration, niacinamide (which she called "the workhorse of the skin care industry"), adenosine, and hydrolyzed collagen for film-forming and moisturizing, plus the absence of fragrance, which she considers a plus for the delicate eyelid area.[^dray]

Both PDRN and caffeine are cosmetic-only claims here; neither addresses a disease process, and this cream carries no FDA drug indication.

## The Evidence

This cream has not been tested in a published clinical trial. The case for either headline active rests on category-level evidence graded on their own pages, not on a trial of this specific formula.

PDRN's well-characterized mechanism, activating the adenosine A2A receptor to promote healing and fibroblast activity, comes almost entirely from injected and wound-application research; the one topical, leave-on human trial identified for the ingredient tested a proprietary, penetration-engineered variant (PDRN-850K), not the standard PDRN typical of mass-market products, [full trial detail on [[polydeoxyribonucleotide-pdrn]]](#the-evidence). Reviewing this product specifically, Dr Dray put it plainly: "Your skin is an incredibly effective barrier... DNA fragments aren't something that it notoriously accepts with open arms. So, simply putting them on the skin doesn't mean a whole lot is going to happen."[^dray]

Caffeine's periorbital evidence is also thin at the category level: no isolated-ingredient, placebo-controlled trial exists for dark circles or puffiness, and every supportive study bundles caffeine with several other actives, [full detail on [[caffeine]]](#the-evidence). Dr Dray's own framing matches that gap: she described caffeine's under-eye benefit as resting on a vasoconstrictive effect that is "potential," not established, and noted that most under-eye discoloration and hollowing comes from structural, age-related changes that "a cream" cannot fix.[^dray]

> Bottom line: the reviewing dermatologist's read matches the category-level evidence graded on [[polydeoxyribonucleotide-pdrn]] and [[caffeine]]. Neither ingredient has controlled trial support for what this product is marketed to do, and no trial exists on the finished product itself.

## What's In It

Anua has not published a full INCI ingredient list for this product that was accessible for this review; the brand's own site did not surface this product, and no incidecoder listing was found.[^dray] The ingredients below are the ones a board-certified dermatologist identified by name while reviewing an in-hand tube on video, not a complete, ordered INCI declaration:[^dray]

- [[polydeoxyribonucleotide-pdrn|PDRN]] (sodium DNA), the headline, name-brand active
- [[caffeine|Caffeine]], the second headline active
- Glycerin, hydroxyethylurea, and sodium hyaluronate, humectants
- [[niacinamide]], described in the review as offering "multiple evidence-based benefits"
- [[adenosine]], described as hydrating and anti-inflammatory
- Hydrolyzed collagen, a film-forming, moisturizing ingredient (not a source of replacement collagen in skin)
- No added fragrance, per the review

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"PDRN caffeine rejuvenating."** The product name itself makes PDRN the headline claim. The reviewing dermatologist directly disputes that framing for a topical: "PDRN is a gimmick... PDRN in this is not going to do anything," distinguishing PDRN's real, injected/wound-healing evidence from what a leave-on eye cream can plausibly deliver.[^dray] The category-level evidence, including one industry-run trial of a specially engineered topical PDRN variant, is graded on [[polydeoxyribonucleotide-pdrn]].
- **Caffeine for de-puffing and brightening under-eyes.** A widely used eye-cream rationale (vasoconstriction and antioxidant activity). No isolated-ingredient controlled trial supports it for dark circles or puffiness, [graded on [[caffeine]]](#the-evidence); the reviewing dermatologist called the mechanism only a "potential" benefit and noted structural causes of under-eye hollowing are unaffected by any cream.[^dray]
- **Implicit "you need a dedicated eye cream" positioning.** Standard for the eye-cream category. The same reviewing dermatologist pushed back on this specifically for this product: "the main value of this product is as an eyelid moisturizer... You don't need a special moisturizer in a small tube to do that. The moisturizer you use on your face, you can use around the eyes."[^dray]
