Avene Cicalfate+ Restorative Protective Cream (Avene) is a fragrance-free barrier cream built around an oil-and-wax occlusive base plus a copper-zinc sulfate complex, positioned for compromised skin including dry, sensitive, or damaged areas.[^1]

> An occlusive, wax-and-oil barrier cream for compromised skin, positioned as a daily moisturizer or for use on minor damaged-skin situations such as sunburn, cuts, and diaper areas.[^1] Its base sits in the same well-evidenced occlusion-and-humectant category as other barrier creams, covered on [[moisturizing]]; no independent trial of this exact cream is cited here, and its "clinically proven to accelerate the skin recovery process by 4X" and "reduces skin discomfort by 80% within 48 hours" marketing figures rest on the manufacturer's own claim as relayed by a retailer, not on a source this page could independently verify, so they are [quarantined below](#common-marketing-claims) rather than graded.

## Summary

Avene Cicalfate+ Restorative Protective Cream is a fragrance-free cream for compromised skin, marketed for use as a daily moisturizer or on minor damaged-skin situations: sunburn, cuts, scrapes, stitches, bug bites, and diaper areas, and described as suitable for infants, children, and adults.[^1] Target's listing gives the [full ingredient list](#whats-in-it): Avene thermal spring water, caprylic/capric triglyceride, mineral oil, glycerin, hydrogenated vegetable oil, zinc oxide, propylene glycol, polyglyceryl-2 sesquiisostearate, PEG-22/dodecyl glycol copolymer, aluminum stearate, aquaphilus dolomiae ferment filtrate, arginine, beeswax, copper sulfate, magnesium stearate, magnesium sulfate, microcrystalline wax, tromethamine, and zinc sulfate.[^1]

The base is oil-and-wax occlusive: mineral oil is the third-listed ingredient, followed further down by hydrogenated vegetable oil, beeswax, and microcrystalline wax, with glycerin as a humectant.[^1] Layered onto that base is a copper-zinc sulfate complex (zinc oxide, zinc sulfate, copper sulfate) and Avene Thermal Spring Water.[^1] The listing does not name sucralfate among the ingredients, which distinguishes this "Cicalfate+" formula, as sold on this US listing, from any sucralfate-containing product elsewhere in Avene's Cicalfate range; only the ingredients actually declared on this listing are reported here.

Target's listing states the cream is free of parabens, sulfates, alcohol, and silicones, and is fragrance-free, non-comedogenic, and hypoallergenic, and that it has been accepted by the National Eczema Association.[^1] These are formulation and formulation-review designations, not a clinical trial of this cream; no product-specific trial is cited [below](#the-evidence). Occluding and moisturizing compromised, dry, or barrier-impaired skin is a well-evidenced dermatology approach covered on [[moisturizing]], where the occlusive/humectant mechanism is set out.

It sits at a mid-to-premium price for a barrier cream. Target lists the 1.3 fl oz size at $19.59 and the 3.3 fl oz size at $30.99 ([see Where to Buy](#where-to-buy), accessed 2026-08-08).[^1]

## The Evidence

No independently verified trial of this cream is cited here. Its composition matches the general moisturizing evidence: a leave-on cream built on mineral oil, hydrogenated vegetable oil, beeswax, and microcrystalline wax as occlusives, with glycerin as a humectant.[^1] Occluding and moisturizing dry, barrier-impaired, and compromised skin this way is one of the better-evidenced routines in dermatology; that evidence and the occlusive/humectant mechanism are set out on [[moisturizing]]. The manufacturer's claims that the product is "clinically proven to accelerate the skin recovery process by 4X" and reduces skin discomfort "by 80% within 48 hours," as relayed on Target's listing, rest on the manufacturer's own study or studies, which this page could not independently fetch or verify, so they are reported only as [quarantined marketing claims](#common-marketing-claims), not as graded evidence.[^1]
