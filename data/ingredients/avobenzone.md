Avobenzone (INCI butyl methoxydibenzoylmethane) is the ingredient that does most of the UVA-blocking work in US sunscreens, the kind of ultraviolet light most linked to wrinkles and deeper skin damage. It is an organic (chemical, not mineral) sunscreen filter and the main UVA filter in United States over-the-counter sunscreens, absorbing across the UVA band but breaking down in sunlight unless a co-filter holds it stable.[^beasley][^akintilo] It is a dedicated UVA filter, not a UVB one, so it contributes little to a product's SPF and is combined with UVB filters such as [[octinoxate]], [[octisalate]], [[homosalate]], and [[octocrylene]]; SPF is a UVB metric and UVA is a separate axis, both explained on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use is one of the few skincare practices with randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, and avobenzone is the UVA component of that protection in most older US formulas; the health benefit is established for the practice, not for avobenzone in isolation, and lives on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^beasley][^akintilo] Two questions shape how it is graded: how well it actually covers UVA once photodegradation is accounted for, and what its measured systemic absorption means.

> Avobenzone is the primary UVA filter in US over-the-counter sunscreens and, at its 3% maximum, covers the long-wave UVA that mineral filters attenuate less, but it degrades under sunlight unless a co-filter such as octocrylene stabilizes it, and maximal-use trials show it is absorbed into plasma above the FDA's safety-testing threshold, an absorption the agency has asked for more data on and that the trial authors have not linked to any harm.[^beasley][^akintilo][^matta2019][^matta2020][^fda2019]

## The Rubric

The grade below is for avobenzone's job in a sunscreen, ultraviolet-A photoprotection, framed relative to the other filters it is measured against. Systemic absorption and safety are a separate axis and are graded separately, not folded into the photoprotection grade.

**Ultraviolet-A photoprotection**
- **Effect size: notable when photostabilized, modest when it is not.** Avobenzone is the workhorse UVA filter in the US OTC monograph and covers long-wave UVA I, the part of the spectrum where the mineral filter titanium dioxide weakens. In model formulations at its 3% maximum, avobenzone provided, in the words of one characterization study, "true broad-spectrum protection against UVA wavelengths >360 nm."[^beasley] The limit is photostability: avobenzone loses absorbance under UV, and a dermatology review calls it "highly unstable," which is why formulators add octocrylene "in an attempt to retard its degradation."[^akintilo] Small losses are tolerable, since "small losses (<20%) of avobenzone did not significantly reduce a product's protective effects," but a poorly stabilized formula can degrade further in use.[^beasley] It is a weaker and less photostable UVA filter than the newer organic filters standard in Europe and Asia (bemotrizinol/Tinosorb S, bisoctrizole/Tinosorb M, and the Mexoryls), and it complements rather than beats mineral [[zinc-oxide]] on long-wave UVA. "Notable UVA coverage once the formula holds it stable, but photodegradation-dependent, and short of the modern filters (Tinosorb, Mexoryl) it is measured against."
- **Evidence quality: solid for the spectral and photostability characterization; the health payoff is inherited, not filter-specific.** The UVA coverage and its photoinstability are well documented across independent laboratory and formulation studies and a regulatory characterization.[^beasley][^akintilo][^chatelain][^sayre] What is not demonstrated for avobenzone on its own is a clinical-endpoint benefit; the randomized evidence that broad-spectrum sunscreen slows photoaging and lowers skin-cancer burden is for the practice of regular use, covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], and avobenzone earns its place as the UVA filter delivering that spectrum in older US products.[^beasley]

**Systemic absorption and safety (a separate axis, not an efficacy grade)**
- Under the FDA's own maximal-use trials, avobenzone is absorbed through skin into the bloodstream at levels above the 0.5 ng/mL plasma threshold above which the agency recommends further nonclinical safety testing.[^matta2019][^matta2020][^fda2019] In its 2019 proposed sunscreen rule the FDA placed avobenzone, along with eleven other organic filters, in "Category III," meaning the record lacks "sufficient data to support positive GRASE determinations," and requested more safety data; only [[zinc-oxide]] and titanium dioxide were proposed as generally recognized as safe and effective.[^fda2019] No health harm from this absorption has been demonstrated, and the authors of both absorption trials state their results "do not indicate that individuals should refrain from the use of sunscreen."[^matta2019][^matta2020] The absorption itself is well established (two randomized trials); its clinical significance is unresolved.

## What We Actually Know

Avobenzone is a well-characterized organic UVA filter: at its 3% US maximum it attenuates long-wave UVA that titanium dioxide covers less well, and it is the dominant UVA filter in older US sunscreens.[^beasley][^akintilo] Its central weakness is photostability. It degrades under UV unless a co-filter such as octocrylene or bemotrizinol stabilizes it, a problem documented across formulation studies.[^chatelain][^sayre][^akintilo] Two randomized FDA maximal-use trials showed avobenzone is absorbed into plasma above the 0.5 ng/mL threshold that triggers a call for further FDA safety testing, which is why the agency lists it as Category III and has asked for more data; the trial authors emphasized that this absorption is not a reason to stop using sunscreen, and no harm has been shown.[^matta2019][^matta2020][^fda2019] The health value of using it, slowing photoaging and reducing skin-cancer burden, is the value of regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use and is documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], not in any avobenzone-specific clinical-endpoint trial.

## How It Works

Avobenzone is a dibenzoylmethane derivative, an organic (chemical) filter that absorbs a UV photon and re-emits the energy as heat rather than scattering light the way mineral filters do. Its absorption is concentrated in the UVA range (about 320 to 400 nm), strongest in the long-wave UVA I region above 360 nm, and it provides little coverage of UVB (about 290 to 320 nm), which is why it is paired with UVB filters and adds little to a product's SPF, a UVB-only number.[^beasley][^akintilo] The molecule sits in equilibrium between an enol and a keto form; on absorbing UV the excited state can follow a path that degrades the molecule instead of returning it intact, so absorbance falls with continued light exposure. A review states the underlying problem plainly for the whole class: "UVA filters, however, are rare and not sufficiently photostable."[^chatelain] Degradation is worse in some mixtures. When avobenzone is combined with the UVB filter octinoxate, the two undergo, per one study, "concomitant photolysis of avobenzone and octinoxate," each speeding the other's breakdown.[^sayre] Formulators counter this by adding photostabilizers: octocrylene is added "in an attempt to retard its degradation," and the broad-spectrum filter bemotrizinol (Tinosorb S) "prevented the photodegradation of AVB in a concentration-dependent way" in an experimental formulation.[^akintilo][^chatelain]

## The Evidence

Primary evidence, by question.

**Spectral coverage.** A characterization study of UVA protection tested model sunscreens containing 3% avobenzone, 5% zinc oxide, or 5% titanium dioxide and reported that avobenzone and zinc oxide "provide true broad-spectrum protection against UVA wavelengths >360 nm," the long-wave UVA I band that drives photoaging, whereas the titanium dioxide formulation was weaker there.[^beasley] The same study found that "small losses (<20%) of avobenzone did not significantly reduce a product's protective effects," setting a tolerance for the photodegradation that follows.[^beasley] A US dermatology review similarly identifies avobenzone as the one organic UVA option in the older US filter set: "avobenzone is the sole chemical approved by the FDA to afford UVA protection."[^akintilo]

**Photostability.** Avobenzone's photoinstability is documented across formulation studies. The review calls it "highly unstable" and notes octocrylene is added "in an attempt to retard its degradation."[^akintilo] In an experimental oil-in-water sunscreen exposed to filtered xenon-arc radiation, adding the broadband filter Tinosorb S (bemotrizinol) "prevented the photodegradation of AVB in a concentration-dependent way, leading to a sustained SPF and UVA ratio even after irradiation with doses of up to 30 MED."[^chatelain] A separate photochemical study found that avobenzone and the UVB filter octinoxate destabilize each other, reporting "concomitant photolysis of avobenzone and octinoxate" that outpaced the expected harmless photoisomerization.[^sayre]

**Systemic absorption.** Two randomized FDA maximal-use trials measured how much sunscreen filter enters the bloodstream. In the first (24 healthy volunteers, four products applied at 2 mg/cm2 to 75% of body surface four times daily for four days), avobenzone reached geometric mean maximum plasma concentrations of 1.8 to 4.3 ng/mL across the four products, and concentrations "greater than 0.5 ng/mL were reached for all 4 products after 4 applications on day 1."[^matta2019] The second trial (48 participants, 44 completing; four spray and lotion formulations) found avobenzone maximum plasma concentrations of 7.1 ng/mL (coefficient of variation 73.9%) for the lotion and about 3.3 to 3.5 ng/mL for the sprays, again above the 0.5 ng/mL threshold on day 1.[^matta2020] The 0.5 ng/mL figure is the level below which the FDA may waive some nonclinical safety testing; exceeding it triggers a request for further study, not a finding of harm, and the authors of both trials wrote that the results "do not indicate that individuals should refrain from the use of sunscreen."[^matta2019][^matta2020]

**Regulatory status.** Avobenzone is a permitted US OTC sunscreen active at a maximum concentration of 3%.[^fda2019] In the FDA's 2019 proposed sunscreen rule the agency proposed that avobenzone and eleven other organic filters be classified "Category III," because "the public record does not currently contain sufficient data to support positive GRASE determinations" for them, and it requested additional safety data, with systemic absorption among the reasons; by contrast it proposed that zinc oxide and titanium dioxide (up to 25%) are Category I, generally recognized as safe and effective.[^fda2019] Avobenzone therefore remains available and widely used while its long-term safety data are still being sought.

## Uses

Well-supported: as the ultraviolet-A filter in a broad-spectrum sunscreen, where avobenzone covers long-wave UVA I that mineral filters attenuate less and supplies the UVA half of the protection that, used regularly, slows photoaging and lowers skin-cancer burden.[^beasley][^akintilo] That health benefit is a property of regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], and depends on the rest of the formula keeping avobenzone photostable, typically with a co-filter such as octocrylene.[^akintilo][^chatelain] Its practical limits are two: it degrades under UV unless stabilized, and it is absorbed systemically above the FDA's testing threshold, which is why the agency has requested more safety data.[^sayre][^matta2019][^matta2020][^fda2019] It offers little UVB protection on its own and is not used as a standalone filter.[^beasley]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Full-spectrum / complete UVA protection."** Avobenzone does absorb across the UVA band, including the long-wave UVA I where mineral filters weaken.[^beasley] But the protection actually delivered depends on photostabilization, because avobenzone degrades under UV and is destabilized outright by some co-filters such as octinoxate.[^akintilo][^sayre] A US "Broad Spectrum" label is a single low-bar UVA threshold, not a graded UVA strength, and US products built on avobenzone allow more UVA through than European products built on newer filters; that landscape is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^akintilo]
- **"Chemical sunscreens are toxic / hormone disruptors."** The demonstrated fact is systemic absorption above the FDA's 0.5 ng/mL testing threshold, shown in two randomized trials.[^matta2019][^matta2020] That threshold triggers a request for more data, not a finding of harm; the FDA classified avobenzone Category III (insufficient data), not unsafe, and the trial authors stated the results "do not indicate that individuals should refrain from the use of sunscreen."[^fda2019][^matta2019][^matta2020] No health harm from avobenzone at label use has been demonstrated.
- **"Photostable formula."** Photostability is achievable, but it is a property of the finished product's co-filters and stabilizers, not of avobenzone itself, which a review calls "highly unstable" on its own.[^akintilo] Stabilizers such as octocrylene and bemotrizinol can hold it stable in testing.[^akintilo][^chatelain]
