Some of the best-evidenced treatment on this site is a drug you inject, not a cream you buy. Dupilumab is an FDA-**approved** biologic for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis and for prurigo nodularis, with three randomised placebo-controlled trials behind the skin indication. It is a prescription systemic medicine and it is not an alternative to a routine; it is what the [[atopic-dermatitis]] and [[prurigo-nodularis]] hubs route severe disease toward.

> **It is approved, not cleared, and that distinction is the whole point.** Dupilumab holds BLA 761055, a biologics licence to Regeneron Pharmaceuticals with an original approval date of 28 March 2017.[^fda] Approval required the agency to review efficacy and safety data on this molecule. Compare the home devices on this site, which are *cleared* by equivalence to something already on the market and in several cases contain no clinical data at all, as [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]] and [[home-rf-devices-by-whose-evidence-the-file-rests-on]] set out.

## The Rubric

**Moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (health)**
Graded notable on gold-standard evidence. Three randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials are summarised in the label itself. At week 16, the proportion reaching an Investigator's Global Assessment of clear or almost clear with at least a 2-point improvement was 38% on dupilumab against 10% on placebo in SOLO 1 and 36% against 9% in SOLO 2; EASI-75, meaning at least 75% improvement in eczema severity, reached 51% against 15% and 44% against 12%.[^label] Added to topical corticosteroids in CHRONOS, EASI-75 was 69% against 23%.[^label] Those are large absolute differences from placebo in blinded trials, which almost nothing else graded on this site can claim.

**Itch in atopic dermatitis (health)**
Graded notable on gold-standard evidence, and worth separating from the skin score because itch is what patients actually report. At least a 4-point improvement on the Peak Pruritus numeric rating scale was reached by 41% against 12% in SOLO 1, 36% against 10% in SOLO 2, and 59% against 20% in CHRONOS.[^label]

**Prurigo nodularis (health)**
Graded notable on gold-standard evidence, from two more placebo-controlled trials. The composite endpoint, both at least a 4-point Worst Itch improvement and an IGA PN-S of 0 or 1 at week 24, was met by 38.7% against 9.2% in PRIME, a difference of 29.6% (95% CI 16.4 to 42.8), and 32.1% against 8.5% in PRIME2, a difference of 25.5% (95% CI 13.1 to 37.9).[^label] Itch alone did better: 60.0% against 18.4% and 57.7% against 19.5%.[^label] The confidence intervals are published, which is more than most evidence this site grades comes with.

**Anything cosmetic**
Not graded, because there is nothing to grade. Dupilumab has no wrinkle, pigment, texture, pore or anti-ageing indication and no trial here measures one. It is a treatment for inflammatory disease.

## How It Works

Per the label, "Dupilumab is a human monoclonal IgG4 antibody that inhibits interleukin-4 (IL-4) and interleukin-13 (IL-13) signaling by specifically binding to the IL-4Rα subunit shared by the IL-4 and IL-13 receptor complexes."[^label] Blocking one shared receptor subunit shuts down two cytokines at once, which is why one drug now carries nine separate indications across skin and airway disease.

The contrast with a topical is not a matter of strength. A ceramide moisturiser works on the barrier from outside, as [[skin-barrier-repair]] describes; dupilumab intervenes in the signalling that drives the inflammation. Those are different levels of the same disease, and the label is explicit that they coexist: "DUPIXENT can be used with or without topical corticosteroids."[^label]

## The Evidence

**What the approved skin indications actually say.** For atopic dermatitis it is "for the treatment of adult and pediatric patients aged 6 months and older with moderate-to-severe AD whose disease is not adequately controlled with topical prescription therapies or when those therapies are not advisable", and for prurigo nodularis simply "for the treatment of adult patients with prurigo nodularis (PN)".[^label] Read the AD wording closely: it is positioned after topical prescription therapy has failed or is unsuitable, not as a first step.

**The nine indications, because the breadth is itself informative.** Atopic dermatitis, asthma, chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps, eosinophilic oesophagitis, prurigo nodularis, COPD with an eosinophilic phenotype, chronic spontaneous urticaria, bullous pemphigoid and allergic fungal rhinosinusitis.[^label] One receptor blockade reaching that many diseases is a statement about how central IL-4 and IL-13 signalling is, and it is also why the safety profile is drawn from a very large pool rather than from eczema trials alone.

**Side effects are specific and eye-related.** In the pooled atopic dermatitis monotherapy trials the conjunctivitis cluster occurred in 10% on dupilumab against 2% on placebo, and injection site reactions in 10% against 5%; oral herpes ran 4% against 2%.[^label] In the CHRONOS arm on background topical steroids, blepharitis was 5% against 1% and the keratitis cluster 4% against 0.[^label] The label's own precaution is to "Advise patients to promptly report new onset or worsening eye symptoms".[^label] Eye inflammation is the characteristic problem with this drug and it is not rare.

**There is no boxed warning, and that is a real difference from the other systemic this site covers.** Checked by string search of the full label on 2026-10-27, no boxed warning appears anywhere.[^label] [[isotretinoin]], by contrast, carries pregnancy requirements that structure its entire use. Dupilumab's precautions are hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis, the eye findings, eosinophilic conditions, and not stopping corticosteroids abruptly.[^label] Serious, but a different order of constraint.

**Where the site's own study pages go further.** Comparative and subgroup work is on [[kim-2026-dupilumab-prurigo-nodularis-atopic-subgroup]], [[warren-2025-advantage-lebrikizumab-ciclosporin-refractory-atopic-dermatitis-rct]] and [[gupta-2025-hand-eczema-monotherapies-network-meta-analysis]]; itch-specific outcomes are on [[yosipovitch-2026-itch-relief-and-quality-of-life-improvement-with-abro]]. This page does not restate them.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. Dupilumab is a prescription injection with an eye-inflammation signal and a hypersensitivity precaution, started and monitored by a clinician. Nothing here is a reason to seek it or to avoid it; the point of the page is that when someone says eczema treatment has no good evidence, this is the counterexample.

## Common Marketing Claims

**No marketing copy is quoted here.** Dupilumab is a prescription biologic, and this page is built from its FDA label and approval record rather than from any promotional material, so no brand claim is assessed.

**Biologic, as a shelf word.** Worth separating from this drug entirely: cosmetic products that borrow biologic, peptide-signalling or cytokine language are not doing what a monoclonal antibody does, and none of them has a label like this one. Growth-factor and peptide claims are graded on [[peptides]] and [[copper-peptides]].
