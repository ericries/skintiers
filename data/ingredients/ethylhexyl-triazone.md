Ethylhexyl triazone (INCI ethylhexyl triazone; also called octyl triazone, and sold by BASF as Uvinul T 150) is an organic ultraviolet-B sunscreen filter authorized in the European Union at up to 5% but not part of the United States over-the-counter sunscreen monograph, where the FDA has said the available data are insufficient to classify it.[^eu][^fda2015][^latha] It absorbs in the ultraviolet-B band (roughly 290 to 320 nm), the wavelengths that cause sunburn and most of the DNA photodamage behind keratinocyte skin cancers, and it adds little coverage of ultraviolet-A (320 to 400 nm), so it is combined with dedicated UVA filters such as [[avobenzone]] or [[zinc-oxide]] rather than used alone; SPF is a UVB metric and UVA is a separate axis, both explained on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^baker][^tsuchiya] Regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, and ethylhexyl triazone supplies the UVB half of that protection in the non-US products that contain it; the health benefit is established for the practice, not for this filter in isolation, and is documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^baker] Two questions shape how it is graded: how effectively it absorbs UVB and how photostable it is, and what its unusually large molecule means for skin penetration and systemic safety.[^couteau][^scalia]

> Ethylhexyl triazone is a photostable organic UVB filter authorized in the European Union at up to 5% and common in sunscreens sold outside the US; in vitro it ranks among the more effective UVB absorbers, it covers the UVB band and adds little UVA, and its large 823-Da molecule was expected to limit skin uptake, though an in vivo study found its stratum-corneum penetration no lower than that of smaller filters. The FDA has said the available data are insufficient to classify it, so it is absent from the US over-the-counter monograph.[^couteau][^baker][^scalia][^eu][^fda2015]

## The Rubric

The grade below is for ethylhexyl triazone's job in a sunscreen, ultraviolet-B photoprotection, framed relative to the other UVB filters it is measured against. Skin penetration and systemic safety are a separate axis and are graded separately, not folded into the photoprotection grade.

**Ultraviolet-B photoprotection**
- **Effect size: notable.** Ethylhexyl triazone is an organic UVB filter that absorbs across the sunburn-causing UVB band.[^baker][^tsuchiya] In an independent in vitro comparison of 18 UV filters authorized in the EU, each tested at its maximum permitted concentration, octyltriazone ranked in the upper half by measured protective efficacy, scoring 7.80 on the study's scale, above homosalate (4.33), octisalate (3.12) and bisoctrizole (3.68), and below octocrylene (10.41), octinoxate (10.42) and bemotrizinol (20.0).[^couteau] Its limit as a photoprotectant is that it is a UVB filter only: it does little for UVA, the deeper-penetrating band that drives photoaging, so it does not by itself make a product broad-spectrum and is paired with a UVA filter such as [[avobenzone]] or [[zinc-oxide]].[^baker][^tsuchiya] "Notable as a UVB absorber, among the more effective UVB filters per unit at its 5% cap, but UVB-only and no substitute for a dedicated UVA filter."
- **Evidence quality: solid for the spectral classification and in vitro efficacy; the clinical health payoff is inherited, not filter-specific.** That it is a photostable UVB filter is documented across independent photochemical and formulation studies.[^baker][^tsuchiya][^stiefelskin][^couteau] What is not demonstrated for ethylhexyl triazone on its own is a clinical-endpoint benefit; the randomized evidence that regular broad-spectrum sunscreen slows photoaging and lowers skin-cancer burden is for the practice of regular use, covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], and this filter earns its place as the UVB component delivering part of that protection in the products that contain it.[^baker]

**Skin penetration and systemic safety (a separate axis, not an efficacy grade)**
- Ethylhexyl triazone is a large molecule (molecular weight 823.1 Da), and its size was expected to keep it from crossing skin; the authors of one photochemical study note these large filters are "suspected to be less likely to penetrate the skin than some of the smaller approved filters."[^scalia][^baker] The in vivo data complicate that expectation. A tape-stripping study in human volunteers found that 21.9% (SD 4.9) of the applied dose diffused into the stratum corneum, the skin's dead outer layer, which was "not significantly different" from the smaller filters octinoxate (22.2%) and avobenzone (20.5%); uptake into that outer layer is not the same as reaching living tissue or blood, but it is not the low figure the molecule's size would predict.[^scalia] An in vitro permeation study estimated whole-body systemic exposure of 933 micrograms per kilogram bodyweight per day for ethylhexyl triazone at 5%, and identified a margin-of-safety concern (a margin below 100) for its comparator benzophenone-3 but not for ethylhexyl triazone.[^hojerova] In model studies of reactivity toward skin proteins, ethylhexyl triazone was the least reactive of seven filters, in the order "EHT < EHMC < EHS < BP-3 < OCR < DBM < BM-DBM," a low signal for the protein-binding step involved in skin sensitization.[^stiefelprot] The FDA has not found these and the other submitted data sufficient to classify the ingredient: it placed octyl triazone in the category meaning "the data are insufficient to classify the active ingredient" as generally recognized as safe and effective, so it stays out of the US monograph while remaining permitted in the EU at up to 5%.[^fda2015][^eu]

## What We Actually Know

Ethylhexyl triazone is a well-characterized, photostable organic UVB filter. It absorbs the sunburn-causing UVB wavelengths, ranks among the more effective UVB filters in an independent in vitro comparison of EU-authorized filters, and does little for UVA, so it is used alongside a UVA filter rather than on its own.[^couteau][^baker][^tsuchiya] It is one of the more photostable filters in use: its photodeactivation runs through fast internal conversion that lets it cycle many times, and on skin it showed the smallest loss of the six filters in one photostability study.[^baker][^stiefelskin] It is a large molecule (823.1 Da), which was expected to limit skin uptake, but an in vivo tape-stripping study found its penetration into the stratum corneum no lower than that of smaller filters, while an in vitro study estimated its systemic exposure and did not flag the margin-of-safety concern it raised for benzophenone-3.[^scalia][^hojerova] It is authorized in the European Union at a maximum of 5%; in the United States the FDA reviewed it under a time-and-extent application and concluded the data are insufficient to classify it, so it is not in the US over-the-counter sunscreen monograph.[^eu][^fda2003][^fda2015] The health value of using it is the value of regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer burden, documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], and it depends on the rest of the formula supplying UVA coverage.[^baker]

## How It Works

Ethylhexyl triazone is a symmetrical triazine-derived organic (chemical) filter that absorbs a UV photon and returns the energy to the environment as heat rather than scattering light the way mineral filters do. Its absorption is concentrated in the UVB range (about 290 to 320 nm), which is why it protects against sunburn and UVB-driven DNA damage but adds little to a product's UVA coverage and is paired with a UVA filter.[^baker][^tsuchiya] Time-resolved spectroscopy describes the mechanism that makes it efficient: after UVB excitation the molecule undergoes "ultrafast internal conversion" back to the ground state, "enabling multiple absorption and recovery cycles, as one would anticipate from a highly efficient filter."[^baker] A fraction of excited molecules does not return cleanly. A separate study found that "a significant portion of the S1 molecules undergoes intersystem crossing to the T1 state," and the same work reported that ethylhexyl triazone "did not exhibit significantly antioxidative properties by quenching singlet oxygen," so it is a UV absorber rather than an antioxidant.[^tsuchiya] Its size (823.1 Da) also matters for behavior on skin, and is the basis for the expectation that it penetrates poorly.[^scalia][^baker]

## The Evidence

Primary evidence, by question.

**Spectral coverage and efficacy.** Independent photochemical studies classify ethylhexyl triazone as a UVB filter: one femtosecond-spectroscopy paper describes it as "an approved ultraviolet-B (UV-B) chemical filter for commercial sunscreens," and another studies it among the "UV-B absorbers."[^baker][^tsuchiya] An in vitro study of 18 sun filters authorized in the EU, each at its highest permitted concentration, ranked them "in ascending order of efficacy" and placed octyltriazone (7.80) above homosalate (4.33), 4-methylbenzylidene camphor (4.22), bisoctrizole (3.68) and octisalate (3.12), and below octocrylene (10.41), octinoxate (10.42), iscotrizinol (12.58) and bemotrizinol (20.0).[^couteau] The ranking is a per-formulation measure at each filter's legal maximum, so ethylhexyl triazone sits among the stronger UVB filters but not at the top of the field.

**Photostability.** Time-resolved measurements attribute ethylhexyl triazone's efficiency to fast internal conversion "enabling multiple absorption and recovery cycles," while also detecting "long-lived photoproducts" trapped in excited states, so it is efficient rather than perfectly clean.[^baker] Tested on mammalian skin under UV, it was the most stable of the six filters compared: the differences in recovery between glass and skin ran "BP-3 showed the greatest differences ... followed by EHS > BM-DBM > OCR > EHMC > EHT," with ethylhexyl triazone showing the smallest loss.[^stiefelskin]

**Skin penetration and systemic exposure.** Ethylhexyl triazone is described as "a widely used high-molecular-weight (823.1-Da) UV filter," and an in vivo tape-stripping study measured how much reaches the stratum corneum: "the percentage of the applied EHT dose diffused into the stratum corneum was 21.9 +/- 4.9%," which was "not significantly different from that of the smaller-molecular-weight" octinoxate (22.2 +/- 7.6%) and avobenzone (20.5 +/- 3.7%); encapsulating it in lipid microparticles cut that penetration by 45.7%.[^scalia] An in vitro permeation study through porcine skin estimated systemic exposure doses under in-use conditions of "4200 and 933 microgram/kg bodyweight/day for BP3 and EHT" (whole-body) and 136 and 30 (face), and found the margin-of-safety concern (a margin below 100) for benzophenone-3 but not for ethylhexyl triazone.[^hojerova] In model reactions with skin proteins, ethylhexyl triazone was the least reactive of the seven filters tested, in the order "EHT < EHMC < EHS < BP-3 < OCR < DBM < BM-DBM."[^stiefelprot]

**Regulatory status.** In the European Union, ethylhexyl triazone is a permitted UV filter under Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, Annex VI, reference number 15 ("2,4,6-Trianilino-(p-carbo-2'-ethylhexyl-1'-oxy)-1,3,5-triazine"), at a maximum concentration in ready-for-use preparations of 5%.[^eu] In the United States it is not in the over-the-counter monograph. The FDA found octyl triazone (up to 5%) "eligible for consideration" for the monograph through a time-and-extent application in 2003, then, under the Sunscreen Innovation Act, tentatively classified it in the category where "the data are insufficient to classify the active ingredient or combination of ingredients as GRASE and not misbranded, and additional data are necessary," a determination it applied to octyl triazone alongside bemotrizinol, bisoctrizole, drometrizole trisiloxane, amiloxate and diethylhexyl butamido triazone.[^fda2003][^fda2015]

## Uses

Well-supported: as a UVB filter in broad-spectrum sunscreens sold outside the United States, where ethylhexyl triazone supplies UVB absorption alongside a UVA filter and contributes the UVB half of the protection that, used regularly, slows photoaging and lowers skin-cancer burden.[^couteau][^baker] That health benefit is a property of regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], and depends on the rest of the formula covering UVA.[^baker] Its practical limits are that it is UVB-only and does not make a product broad-spectrum on its own, and that it is not a permitted active in US over-the-counter sunscreens.[^tsuchiya][^fda2015]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Broad-spectrum protection."** Ethylhexyl triazone absorbs UVB, not UVA, so on its own it does not provide broad-spectrum coverage; the broad-spectrum status of any product that contains it comes from a separate UVA filter such as [[avobenzone]] or [[zinc-oxide]].[^baker][^tsuchiya]
- **"The strongest / most powerful UVB filter."** It is among the more effective UVB filters in an independent in vitro ranking, but several others scored higher at their legal maximums, including octocrylene, octinoxate, iscotrizinol and bemotrizinol.[^couteau]
- **"Too large to penetrate skin, so it stays on the surface."** Its 823-Da size did predict low penetration, but an in vivo study found its uptake into the stratum corneum (21.9%) was no lower than that of smaller filters such as octinoxate and avobenzone.[^scalia]
- **"FDA-approved sunscreen filter."** It is authorized in the EU at up to 5%, but the FDA has not classified it, concluding "the data are insufficient," and it is not in the US over-the-counter monograph.[^eu][^fda2015]
