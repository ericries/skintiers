Rendered animal fat, sold online as a natural facial moisturiser. **Bottom line:** two separate reviews have now gone looking for human evidence and both reported that it is not there, and a 2025 analysis found that most of the social media promoting it carried financial bias and came from people without healthcare credentials. That is a statement about the evidence base, not about whether fat moisturises skin. If you want an occlusive with a real evidence base, [[petrolatum]] has one.

> **The claim and the evidence are not the same size.** A 2024 PRISMA scoping review screened 147 studies, included 19, and found them to be "comparative studies, basic science studies, and animal studies", concluding that "there are significant research gaps in how it can be used on human skin."[^scoping] A 2025 cross-sectional analysis of tallow claims on YouTube, Instagram and TikTok put it more bluntly: "evidence remains insufficient to support these claims."[^social]

## The Rubric

- **Effect size: unknown, because nobody has measured it on human faces.** The scoping review's own summary of what it found is that "more research is needed that is geared towards tallow as a cosmetic product in humans."[^scoping] No trial of topical tallow on human facial skin is identified by either review, so there is no effect to size.
- **Evidence quality: weak, and both reviews say so independently.** One is a scoping review following PRISMA-ScR that searched EMBASE and PubMed; the other is a cross-sectional study of social media claims by a dermatology group.[^scoping][^social] They were asking different questions and arrived at the same answer.
- **Record finding: the safety side is the least settled part, not the most.** People often assume a traditional fat is at least harmless. The scoping review reports the opposite of certainty: the objective asking about side effects "had the most discrepancies between the sources", and it closes by saying "there needs to be more research with controlled variables on the side effects of topical tallow."[^scoping] So *it cannot hurt* is not supported either.
- **Record finding: who is making the claims, measured rather than asserted.** The 2025 analysis found that "Claims of efficacy for acne, atopic dermatitis, and psoriasis were prevalent but largely lacked cited evidence", and that "The majority of the posts promoting beef tallow across platforms demonstrated financial bias and were uploaded by individuals lacking credentials in healthcare."[^social] That is a finding about the information environment, which is the part a reader actually encounters.
- **What is genuinely true about it.** It is "a solid fat derived from animals with a high content of triglycerides and essential fatty acids".[^social] A triglyceride-rich solid fat will behave as an occlusive and will feel moisturising. What does not follow is any of the condition-specific claims attached to it, and the second review notes that "most existing evidence for tallow use is based on studies examining its ingredients" rather than the material itself.[^social]

## The Evidence

Two reviews, no human facial trial between them. The scoping review screened 147 studies and included 19, none of which it characterises as a controlled human cosmetic trial.[^scoping] The social media analysis went looking for the evidence behind the claims being made and reported that the claims largely lacked cited evidence at all.[^social]

This page does not conclude that tallow does nothing. It concludes that the question has not been answered, which is a different and more honest position, and it is the reason the grade here is `weak` rather than anything lower. Whether an occlusive helps your skin is covered at [[skin-barrier-repair]]; [[petrolatum]] is the one with decades of data behind it.

> **Bottom line.** A plausible occlusive with an implausible amount of confidence attached to it online. Nothing published supports the acne, eczema or psoriasis claims, and the side-effect literature is the most contradictory part of the file rather than the most reassuring.

## Common Marketing Claims

> These are the claim types the 2025 analysis found circulating, quoted from that paper rather than from any one seller, and **not** endorsed here.

- **Efficacy for acne, atopic dermatitis and psoriasis.** The analysis found such claims "prevalent but largely lacked cited evidence."[^social] Those are three distinct medical conditions and none of them has a tallow trial behind it.
- **Natural and sustainable as a reason to prefer it.** The 2025 paper names that framing as the driver of the trend, "driven by the increasing demand for natural and sustainable beauty solutions".[^social] Neither word is a claim about whether it works on skin.
- **Traditional use as evidence.** The scoping review records tallow's long history "as a neutral cooking fat, ingredient in soaps, biofuel product, and now ingredient in skincare products".[^scoping] A long history in soap and biofuel is not evidence for a facial moisturiser.
