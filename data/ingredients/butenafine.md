Butenafine is an over-the-counter topical antifungal sold at 1% as Lotrimin Ultra. On the Bayer label its purpose is "Antifungal" and its stated uses are that it "cures most athlete's foot between the toes" and "cures most jock itch and ringworm".[^dailymed]

> **It is the only antifungal on this site that is FDA-approved rather than monograph-accepted, and its label is unusually candid about where it has not been shown to work.** The DailyMed marketing category is **NDA, application number NDA021307**, not an OTC Monograph citation.[^dailymed] That puts it on the top rung of [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]], while [[clotrimazole]] and [[tolnaftate]] sit a rung below under monograph M005, all three sold in the same aisle and two of them under the same Lotrimin brand. The candour is in the Uses section itself: after claiming it cures most athlete's foot between the toes, the label adds that "Effectiveness on the bottom or sides of foot is unknown".[^dailymed]

## The Rubric

**Athlete's foot between the toes**
- **Effect size: large for this specific site, and the label says "cures".** OTC Drug Facts wording is tightly regulated, so "cures most athlete's foot between the toes" is a meaningful claim.[^dailymed] The Cochrane review of topical treatments for fungal foot infections pooled placebo-controlled trials and reported "butenafine RR 0.33 (95% CI 0.24 to 0.45)" for treatment failure.[^cochrane]
- **Evidence quality: solid, and better supported than its shelf-mates.** This product went through a New Drug Application rather than riding a monograph, so the FDA reviewed evidence for this drug.[^dailymed] The Cochrane figure is class-level and foot-specific.[^cochrane]

**Athlete's foot on the sole or sides of the foot**
- **Effect size: unknown, by the manufacturer's own statement.** "Effectiveness on the bottom or sides of foot is unknown" appears in the Uses section of the label itself.[^dailymed] Moccasin-type tinea pedis on the sole is a common presentation, and this is the label declining to claim it.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable; this is a stated absence rather than a weak result.** Few consumer labels say this plainly, and it is the single most useful line on the carton.

**Speed, against the other OTC options**
- **Effect size: a genuine practical advantage for the interdigital case.** The directions allow "twice a day for 1 week (morning and night), or once a day for 4 weeks" for athlete's foot between the toes.[^dailymed] Compare the four-week course on [[clotrimazole]] and [[tolnaftate]]. A one-week option matters because antifungal courses are abandoned.
- **Evidence quality: label-grade, and indication-specific.** Jock itch and ringworm get a different schedule, "apply once a day to affected skin for 2 weeks".[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

**The Cochrane number needs the same caveat as every other drug in that table.** The review's placebo-controlled pools are separate, one per drug class, so butenafine's RR 0.33 cannot be read against azoles at 0.30 or tolnaftate at 0.19 as though they were compared head to head.[^cochrane] The only genuine head-to-head the review reports is allylamines against azoles, "RR 0.63 (95% CI 0.42 to 0.94) in favour of allylamines", and butenafine is not in it.[^cochrane] The fuller version of this trap is on [[tolnaftate]].

**Where it sits chemically.** Butenafine is a benzylamine, a class related to but distinct from the allylamines, which is why it is pooled separately from [[terbinafine]] in that review.[^cochrane] Nothing cited here establishes that one outperforms the other.

**Nails and scalp are excluded.** The label says "Do not use on nails or scalp", alongside not in or near the mouth or eyes, and not for vaginal yeast infections.[^dailymed] That exclusion matches [[clotrimazole]] and [[tolnaftate]], and the reason is the same: those sites need different drugs and usually a prescriber.

**A brand trap, again.** Bayer publishes four separate Lotrimin Ultra labels for this one active, covering athlete's foot, jock itch, ringworm and an unqualified version.[^dailymed] And the wider Lotrimin family spans several different antifungals, which is set out on [[clotrimazole]]. Read the active-ingredient line, not the brand.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. The label's own stopping rule applies: if irritation worsens, stop, and a rash that is not clearly tinea can look very like it.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"cures most athlete's foot between the toes"**. Regulated Drug Facts wording, supported by an NDA review and by class-level randomised evidence.[^dailymed][^cochrane] Note the two qualifiers the brand itself kept: "most", and "between the toes".
- **"Effectiveness on the bottom or sides of foot is unknown"**. Also the label's own wording, and a limitation rather than a claim.[^dailymed]
- **"cures most jock itch and ringworm"**. Label wording, on a two-week schedule rather than the one-week interdigital option.[^dailymed]
