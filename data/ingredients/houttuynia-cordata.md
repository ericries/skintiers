Houttuynia cordata, marketed in skincare as "heartleaf," is a flowering plant long used in East Asian traditional medicine as an oral heat-clearing and detoxifying remedy for respiratory and urinary complaints; in Korean beauty products its extract or distilled water is used as the base of toners, essences, and pads sold for calming sensitized or acne-prone skin.[^yang]

> No published human trial tests Houttuynia cordata extract applied to skin for a soothing, redness, or acne outcome. What supports the marketing is laboratory work: a mouse model of atopic dermatitis treated with a purified polysaccharide fraction, and cell-culture assays of the essential oil showing anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, antioxidant, and wound-healing activity.[^huang][^sangthong] The plant's actual traditional and pharmacological record is chiefly an oral one, for respiratory and urinary conditions, not a topical one.[^yang]

## What It Is and How It Works

Houttuynia cordata (also called fish mint or chameleon plant) is classified in a 2024 pharmacology review as "an important heat-clearing and detoxifying traditional Chinese medicine" that the review says "effectively clears away heat and toxins, eliminates carbuncles, and drains pus," with diuretic and detoxicating uses; the review's focus throughout is on the plant's role in "respiratory disease control," and it does not discuss topical or dermatological application.[^yang] The same review names the plant's three main constituent classes as "volatile oils, flavonoids, and alkaloids," proposing "Houttuynia sodium, methyl nonyl ketone, quercetin, and quercitrin" as its quality-marker compounds, and attributes to the plant "antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, antiviral, antioxidant, antitumor, and immunity-enhancing pharmacological effects" generally, not specifically on skin.[^yang]

The mechanistic case for a skincare use comes from two 2025 laboratory studies, neither in humans. In an MC903-induced mouse model of atopic dermatitis, a purified Houttuynia cordata polysaccharide fraction reduced "epidermal thickness, skin lesions, mast cell infiltration, inflammatory cytokines," restored skin barrier proteins, and suppressed inflammatory signaling through the MAPK pathway, alongside supporting work in cultured mouse macrophages (RAW264.7) and human keratinocytes (HaCaT); it also shifted skin lipid composition, "upregulating phosphatidylcholine (PC) and downregulating triglyceride (TG), lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC)."[^huang] Separately, an in vitro study of Houttuynia cordata essential oil (HCEO) found it suppressed nitric oxide production in LPS-stimulated macrophages with an IC50 of 1.176 ± 0.084 micrograms/mL, inhibited tyrosinase activity (IC50 48.00 ± 0.01 micrograms/mL), and showed antioxidant activity by DPPH (IC50 13.25 ± 0.09 mg/mL) and FRAP (17.44 ± 0.16 mg TEAC/g) assays; a 400 micrograms/mL concentration performed best in a cell-based wound-healing assay, and oil-in-water emulsions containing HCEO stayed physically stable for eight weeks, which the authors framed as evidence of "potential as a natural ingredient for advanced skincare formulations."[^sangthong] Both are preclinical signals (mouse, isolated cells, or physical-chemistry assays), not evidence from human skin.

## The Evidence

**Atopic dermatitis, mouse model.** Huang and colleagues induced dermatitis-like lesions in mice with MC903 and treated them with a purified Houttuynia cordata polysaccharide fraction, reporting reduced epidermal thickness, mast cell infiltration, and inflammatory cytokines, and restored skin barrier proteins, with supporting mechanism work in macrophage and keratinocyte cell culture.[^huang] The tested material is a purified polysaccharide extract, not the whole-plant water or oil used in commercial "heartleaf" toners, and the study is in mice.

**Antibacterial and anti-acne activity, in vitro.** Sangthong and colleagues tested Houttuynia cordata essential oil in cell-free and cell-culture assays and reported it "inhibited the growth of Staphylococcus aureus, Candida albicans, Staphylococcus epidermidis, and Cutibacterium acnes," alongside anti-inflammatory (nitric-oxide suppression), antioxidant, anti-melanogenic (tyrosinase inhibition), and wound-healing activity, plus eight-week emulsion stability data supporting cosmetic formulation.[^sangthong] These are laboratory assays (isolated bacteria, cultured cells, physical stability testing), not a trial on human skin or acne lesions.

**Traditional and pharmacological use.** The 2024 review of the plant's chemistry and pharmacology centers its use on "respiratory disease control," describing the heat-clearing, detoxifying, pus-draining traditional application; it does not identify a topical dermatological tradition or cite a skin-directed clinical trial.[^yang]

## The Rubric

**Skin soothing / redness-calming ("heartleaf" toners, cosmetic)**
- **Effect size: not established in humans.** The soothing positioning behind Korean "heartleaf" toners and pads rests on the mouse atopic-dermatitis result (a purified polysaccharide, not the whole extract used commercially) and on cell-culture anti-inflammatory signals from the essential oil.[^huang][^sangthong] No cited human trial measures redness, transepidermal water loss, or a soothing outcome from a topical Houttuynia cordata product. Products carrying the extract, such as [[anua-heartleaf-77-soothing-toner]] and [[anua-heartleaf-77-clear-pad]], report only the manufacturer's own device-measured figures, not an independent trial.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** One mouse model plus in vitro cell assays; no human topical trial identified.[^huang][^sangthong]

**Acne-prone / oily skin positioning (cosmetic)**
- **Effect size: not established in humans.** Houttuynia cordata essential oil inhibited growth of the acne-associated bacterium Cutibacterium acnes and other skin microbes in a cell-free laboratory assay, alongside anti-inflammatory activity in cultured macrophages.[^sangthong] That is a plausible in vitro signal, well below the clinical trial record behind [[benzoyl-peroxide]] or [[azelaic-acid]] for acne, and no human study of comedone count, sebum, or lesion count with this ingredient is cited here.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** In vitro antibacterial and anti-inflammatory assays only.[^sangthong]

**Barrier support and lipid metabolism (health, preclinical)**
- **Effect size: mouse-model only.** The polysaccharide fraction improved measured skin-lipid composition and restored barrier proteins in the MC903 mouse model, a genuine health-relevant mechanism (barrier repair, lipid metabolism) rather than a purely cosmetic endpoint, but demonstrated only in mice, with a purified fraction rather than the extract sold in cosmetics.[^huang]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** Single animal study.[^huang]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Calms and soothes irritated or sensitized skin."** The supporting laboratory work is real (a mouse dermatitis model and cell-culture anti-inflammatory assays),[^huang][^sangthong] but no cited human trial tests a topical Houttuynia cordata product for soothing or redness. Better-evidenced comparators for topical soothing include [[niacinamide]] and [[colloidal-oatmeal]].
- **"Fights acne and balances oily skin."** Cutibacterium acnes growth inhibition is shown in a cell-free laboratory assay of the essential oil, not in a human acne trial.[^sangthong] The controlled clinical evidence for topical acne treatment is far stronger for [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and [[azelaic-acid]].
- **"Traditional Korean herbal remedy for skin."** The plant's documented traditional and pharmacological literature centers on oral use for respiratory and urinary conditions in Chinese medicine, not a topical skin tradition.[^yang]
