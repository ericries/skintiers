Dapsone (INCI name Dapsone, chemically 4-(4-aminophenyl)sulfonylaniline, C12H12N2O2S) is a synthetic sulfone with antibacterial and anti-inflammatory properties, formulated as a once-daily 7.5% gel or a twice-daily 5% gel (Aczone) and prescribed for [[acne]] vulgaris, particularly the inflammatory papules and pustules.[^pubchem][^thiboutot] Two identically designed phase 3 vehicle-controlled trials of the 7.5% formulation put its effect at a modest but statistically significant edge over vehicle, and a pooled analysis of the original 5% registration trials found notably better response in women than men.[^thiboutot][^tanghetti-gender] Because dapsone is a sulfone related structurally to the oral drug that can trigger hemolysis in glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency, dedicated crossover safety trials specifically tested G6PD-deficient patients and found no clinical evidence of drug-induced hemolytic anemia.[^piette]

> In a pooled analysis of two identical phase 3 trials (n=4,340), dapsone gel 7.5% reduced inflammatory lesions by 54.6% versus 48.1% for vehicle (p<0.001), and 29.8% of dapsone-treated patients reached "clear or almost clear" versus 21.1% on vehicle (p<0.001).[^thiboutot]

## The Rubric

**Inflammatory acne vulgaris (topical, once-daily 7.5% gel)**
- **Effect size: modest, statistically significant but a narrow absolute margin over vehicle.** In the pooled phase 3 analysis (n=4,340), dapsone gel 7.5% produced a 54.6% reduction in inflammatory lesions versus 48.1% for vehicle, and a 45.1% reduction in noninflammatory lesions versus 39.4% for vehicle (all p<0.001), and Global Acne Assessment Score success (clear or almost clear) was reached by 29.8% of dapsone patients versus 21.1% of vehicle patients.[^thiboutot] Vehicle alone already achieved roughly 21% success and a 48% inflammatory-lesion reduction, so a meaningful share of the response in any dapsone trial is the vehicle/adherence effect; dapsone's true added benefit is the roughly 9-point gap in success rate and 6.5-point gap in inflammatory-lesion reduction.
- **Evidence quality: strong for statistical significance, industry-funded throughout.** The evidence rests on two identically designed, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, multicenter 12-week phase 3 trials (NCT01974141, NCT01974323) with a very large combined enrollment, plus an earlier, separately registered pair of 5% gel registration trials (n=3,010) that established the same directional effect.[^thiboutot][^draelos-2007] Both generations of trials were conducted or funded by the drug's manufacturer (Allergan for the 7.5% program), which does not invalidate the randomized design but means independent replication is thin.

**Response by sex (topical, 5% gel, twice daily)**
- **Effect size: substantially larger in women.** In a pooled analysis of the original 5% gel registration trials (n=2,898 dapsone/vehicle combined), dapsone-treated women had a 46.6% mean reduction in total lesion counts versus 35.8% in men (p<0.0001), and 48.6% of women reached clinical success versus 34.4% of men (p=0.0003).[^tanghetti-gender] This is a real, reproducible sex difference in a large post hoc dataset, not a marketing invention, and later post hoc work on the 7.5% formulation reported the same female-superiority pattern.[^tanghetti-female]
- **Evidence quality: moderate; conclusions come from post hoc, not pre-specified, subgroup analyses.** Both the 5% and 7.5% sex-stratified findings are post hoc analyses of trials that were not designed or powered to test sex as the primary variable, so the effect size should be read as a strong hypothesis-generating signal rather than a confirmed, independently pre-registered result.[^tanghetti-gender][^tanghetti-female]

**Hematologic safety in G6PD deficiency (topical)**
- **Effect size: no clinically significant hemolysis detected.** In a dedicated double-blind, randomized, vehicle-controlled crossover trial in 64 G6PD-deficient subjects, a 0.32 g/dL mean hemoglobin drop occurred at 2 weeks on dapsone gel but was "not accompanied by changes in other laboratory parameters, including reticulocytes, haptoglobin, bilirubin, and lactate dehydrogenase levels, and was not apparent at 12 weeks"; the largest individual hemoglobin drops were 1.7 g/dL on vehicle and 1.5 g/dL on dapsone, and "no clinical signs or symptoms of hemolytic anemia were noted."[^piette] Topical dapsone's systemic absorption is far lower than oral dapsone (roughly 100-fold lower exposure, per pharmacokinetic work summarized in a contemporaneous review), which is the pharmacologic basis for this reassuring safety margin.[^stotland]
- **Evidence quality: solid for the specific question tested, but a single trial and a 12-week horizon.** The G6PD safety trial is well-designed (randomized, double-blind, crossover, vehicle-controlled) and directly answers the question that matters for this drug class, but it is one trial of modest size (64 subjects) over 12-week exposure windows; standard prescribing guidance still recommends caution and G6PD-deficient patients should discuss this history with their prescriber rather than treat the trial as a blanket clearance.

## How It Works

Dapsone is 4,4'-diaminodiphenyl sulfone (4-(4-aminophenyl)sulfonylaniline, C12H12N2O2S), a synthetic sulfone antibiotic long used orally for leprosy and dermatitis herpetiformis before its topical reformulation for acne.[^pubchem] In acne, its proposed dual mechanism is antibacterial activity against Cutibacterium acnes and anti-inflammatory action, including inhibition of neutrophil chemotaxis and myeloperoxidase-mediated inflammatory pathways, which distinguishes it mechanistically from purely antibacterial topical agents; this anti-inflammatory route is the rationale for its particular use in inflammatory (papulopustular) rather than purely comedonal acne.[^thiboutot] The topical gel formulation uses a solvent-microparticle system designed to deliver a clinically active dose to skin while limiting systemic absorption; a phase I pharmacokinetic comparison found systemic exposure to dapsone was roughly two orders of magnitude lower after topical Aczone than after an equivalent oral dapsone dose, which is why topical use largely avoids the hemolytic and methemoglobinemia risks associated with oral dapsone dosing.[^stotland]

## The Evidence

**Two identical phase 3 trials of dapsone gel 7.5%, once daily, pooled (topical).** Thiboutot et al. pooled two randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, multicenter, 12-week trials (NCT01974141, NCT01974323) enrolling 4,340 patients aged 12 and older with moderate acne (20 to 50 inflammatory lesions, 30 to 100 noninflammatory lesions), randomized 1:1 to dapsone gel 7.5% or vehicle once daily. Global Acne Assessment Score success rates were "29.8 percent and 21.1 percent for patients who received dapsone gel, 7.5% and vehicle, respectively (p<0.001)," with inflammatory lesion reduction of -54.6% versus -48.1% and noninflammatory lesion reduction of -45.1% versus -39.4% (both p<0.001); dermal tolerability scores for stinging/burning, dryness, scaling, and erythema were "similarly low" between dapsone and vehicle.[^thiboutot] *A large, well-controlled pair of trials shows dapsone beats vehicle at a statistically robust but numerically modest margin, and it is well tolerated at the skin level, comparable to vehicle gel.*

**Original 5% gel registration trials, twice daily (topical).** Draelos et al. ran two identically designed 12-week randomized, double-blind studies of twice-daily dapsone gel 5% versus vehicle in 3,010 patients aged 12 and older. Dapsone-treated patients achieved superior results on the investigator's global acne assessment (p<0.001) and mean percentage reduction in inflammatory, noninflammatory, and total lesion counts (all p<0.001), with reductions favoring dapsone apparent by 2 weeks and statistically significant by 4 weeks; "no clinically significant changes in laboratory parameters, including hemoglobin, even among glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase-deficient patients, were observed."[^draelos-2007] *This is the original approval-supporting evidence for the 5% formulation, establishing both efficacy and an early safety signal in G6PD-deficient patients within the same trial population, though the drop-out and adverse-event reporting depth of the original abstract is limited.*

**Sex-stratified pooled analysis of 5% gel registration trials (topical, post hoc).** Tanghetti et al. pooled the same DAP0203/DAP0204 5% gel trials (2,898 patients: 1,453 dapsone, 1,445 vehicle) and stratified by sex. Dapsone-treated women had a 46.6% mean reduction in total lesion counts versus 35.8% in men (p<0.0001), and clinical success (Global Acne Assessment Scale 0 or 1) was reached by 48.6% of women versus 34.4% of men (p=0.0003).[^tanghetti-gender] *A substantial, statistically significant sex gap in response, though it is a post hoc subgroup finding from trials not originally designed to test sex as the primary variable.*

**G6PD-deficient crossover safety trial, 5% gel (topical).** Piette et al. ran a double-blind, randomized, vehicle-controlled crossover trial in 64 G6PD-deficient subjects 12 years or older with acne, each receiving 12 weeks of dapsone gel and 12 weeks of vehicle (2-week washout) in randomized order. A 0.32 g/dL mean hemoglobin decrease occurred at 2 weeks during dapsone treatment but "was not accompanied by changes in other laboratory parameters... and was not apparent at 12 weeks"; the number of subjects with a 1 g/dL hemoglobin drop was similar between dapsone and vehicle at both 2 and 12 weeks, the largest individual drops were 1.7 g/dL (vehicle) and 1.5 g/dL (dapsone), and "no clinical signs or symptoms of hemolytic anemia were noted."[^piette] *A purpose-built safety trial in the exact population of concern found no clinical hemolysis signal over 12 weeks, which is reassuring but does not eliminate the standard prescribing caution around G6PD status for this drug class.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Especially effective for women's acne."** Genuinely supported by data: a large post hoc pooled analysis found a statistically significant and clinically meaningful sex gap in favor of women on the 5% gel (46.6% vs 35.8% lesion reduction, p<0.0001), and a separate post hoc analysis reported the same pattern for the 7.5% gel.[^tanghetti-gender][^tanghetti-female] The caveat is that both are post hoc subgroup analyses, not pre-specified primary endpoints, so the magnitude should be treated as a strong signal rather than a confirmed, independently replicated effect size.
- **"Safe even if you have G6PD deficiency."** Reasonably supported for the topical gel specifically, not for dapsone as a drug class in general: a dedicated crossover trial in 64 G6PD-deficient subjects found no clinical or laboratory evidence of hemolytic anemia over 12 weeks of twice-daily use.[^piette] That trial is single, modest in size, and 12 weeks long; it does not license casual use without medical supervision, and G6PD status should still be disclosed to the prescriber, since the reassurance is specific to the low systemic absorption of the topical formulation, not a blanket statement about dapsone's underlying hemolytic risk.
