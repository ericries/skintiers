Sodium lauroyl isethionate is a mild anionic surfactant (the isethionate ester of lauric acid) used as a main cleansing agent in syndet ("synthetic detergent") beauty bars, such as the [[dove-sensitive-skin-beauty-bar|Dove Sensitive Skin Beauty Bar]], and in some liquid cleansers.[^ananth]

> Sodium lauroyl isethionate is a cleansing-base ingredient, not an active: it lifts oil and dirt, and its selling point is low irritation, not any visible improvement to skin. Its relevance is mildness. Isethionate-type surfactants interact less strongly with skin proteins than harsher surfactants do, and a syndet bar built on them can be formulated near skin-normal pH (about 5.5) rather than the alkaline pH of true soap, which a mild-cleansing review links to less stratum-corneum swelling and barrier disruption.[^ananth] Judge it as one of the gentler ways to build a cleansing bar, but not an ingredient that treats skin.

## The Rubric

Sodium lauroyl isethionate is a functional cleansing agent, so it is graded on what it is actually asked to do, not on a skin-treatment claim.

**Gentle cleansing / low irritation (its role)**
- **Effect size: modest, and a formulation property, not a skin benefit.** As a mild anionic isethionate it cleanses with less of the protein interaction that drives after-wash tightness and irritation from harsh surfactants; a mild-cleansing review notes that "harsh surfactants in cleansers can cause damage to skin proteins and lipids, leading to after-wash tightness, dryness, barrier damage, irritation, and even itch," and that surfactants "that interact minimally with both skin lipids and proteins are especially mild."[^ananth] Cleanser pH matters too: the same review reports that high-pH (pH 10) cleansing can increase stratum-corneum swelling, while cleansers "close to SC-normal pH 5.5" are potentially less damaging.[^ananth]
- **Evidence quality: descriptive and mechanistic.** The mildness case rests on surfactant-mechanism and cleanser-pH research on the mild-surfactant class generally, largely from cleanser-industry researchers, not on controlled efficacy trials of sodium lauroyl isethionate itself.[^ananth]

**Any active skin benefit (hydration, barrier, anti-aging, acne)**
- **Effect size: none demonstrated.** Sodium lauroyl isethionate is a rinse-off surfactant with no cited evidence that it hydrates, repairs the barrier, or treats any skin condition. Being gentler is about doing less harm during the wash, not adding a benefit afterward.
- **Evidence quality: none.** No controlled trial cited here tests sodium lauroyl isethionate for a skin-improvement endpoint.

## The Evidence

Sodium lauroyl isethionate earns its place as a mild, low-irritation cleansing surfactant, the kind of synthetic detergent that lets a beauty bar clean at a skin-friendly pH instead of the alkaline pH of soap; the general case for mild cleansing and near-skin pH is set out in the cleanser-and-barrier literature.[^ananth] It does nothing visible to skin on its own; a cleanser built on it is judged by the whole formula, not by this one ingredient.
