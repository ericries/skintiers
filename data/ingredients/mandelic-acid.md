Mandelic acid is an alpha-hydroxy acid (AHA) with a larger molecule than glycolic acid, which places it on the slower-penetrating, gentler side of the exfoliating-acid strength axis alongside [[lactic-acid]]; see [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]] for how it lines up against the rest of the family. Its clinical evidence is thinner than glycolic or lactic acid's: almost every controlled trial is a small in-office peel study, most from Indian dermatology departments, testing [[acne]] or [[hyperpigmentation]] rather than daily leave-on use.

> Alone at 45%, mandelic acid peels matched a 30% salicylic acid peel for acne, with fewer side effects.[^dayal] Combined with salicylic acid, a mandelic-containing peel outperformed a 35% glycolic acid peel on active acne lesions and hyperpigmentation in one small trial that a Cochrane review later rated low-quality evidence.[^garg][^cochrane] But in the one trial that tested mandelic acid alone against another gentle AHA, lactic acid, for hyperpigmentation, lactic acid produced significantly better results, higher satisfaction, and less irritation.[^malviya]

## The Rubric

Grades differ by use and by comparator; mandelic acid is graded separately depending on whether it was tested alone or combined with salicylic acid.

**Acne (chemical peels, 30% to 45%)**
- Effect size: modest, and roughly level with salicylic acid peels rather than clearly ahead of them. In a randomized trial of 50 patients with mild-to-moderate acne, six sessions of 45% mandelic acid peels over 12 weeks performed about the same as 30% salicylic acid peels on the Michaelsson acne score: "there was no significant difference between the two peels in improving MAS and percentage decrease in MAS," though salicylic acid worked better on noninflammatory lesions and mandelic acid on inflammatory lesions.[^dayal] Combined with salicylic acid at 20%/10%, a separate 44-patient trial found the combination peel more effective than a 35% glycolic acid peel for active acne lesions (P<.001).[^garg] A Cochrane review folded a 40-participant subset of that same comparison into its acne meta-analysis and found "no difference in PGA when comparing glycolic acid peel to salicylic-mandelic acid peel (RR 1.06, 95% CI 0.88 to 1.26; 1 study, 40 participants; low-quality evidence)."[^cochrane] Glycolic acid's own acne evidence is graded on [[glycolic-acid]]. "Modest; level with salicylic acid alone, and only shown ahead of glycolic acid as part of a salicylic-acid combination rather than in isolation, in a comparison Cochrane calls low quality."
- Evidence quality: preliminary. Two small RCTs (n=44 and n=50), both from Indian dermatology departments; the trial that beat glycolic acid tested mandelic acid mixed with salicylic acid, not alone, and that comparison is rated low-quality evidence by Cochrane.[^dayal][^garg][^cochrane]

**Melasma and hyperpigmentation**
- Effect size: mixed, and dependent on whether mandelic acid was tested alone or combined with salicylic acid. A 90-patient trial split three ways found 35% glycolic acid and a 20%/10% salicylic-mandelic peel produced statistically similar reductions in melasma severity (62.36% versus 60.98% by MASI score, P=.876 between them), both ahead of a phytic acid peel, after all three groups were primed for four weeks with hydroquinone and tretinoin.[^sarkar] The same 44-patient trial that favored the salicylic-mandelic combination for acne also found it reduced post-acne hyperpigmentation more than glycolic acid (P<.001).[^garg] The result reverses when mandelic acid is tested alone: in a 70-patient trial for periorbital hyperpigmentation, a 30% lactic acid peel produced significantly better outcomes than a 30% mandelic acid peel, with 50% of the lactic-acid group achieving more than 30% improvement on a visual-analog pigmentation scale (P=.001) and higher patient satisfaction (100% versus 83.9%, P=.011).[^malviya] "Mixed; a salicylic-mandelic combination matched or beat glycolic acid for pigmentation in two Indian trials, but the one trial testing mandelic acid alone against a comparably gentle AHA, lactic acid, found lactic acid worked better."
- Evidence quality: preliminary. Three small RCTs (n=44 to n=90), all in Indian patient cohorts, unblinded; the two results favorable to mandelic acid test it only as part of a salicylic-acid combination, not isolated.[^garg][^sarkar][^malviya]

**Photoaging and skin texture**
- Effect size: modest on one instrumented measure, from a study with no comparator arm. In an open-label trial, 24 patients applied a mandelic acid formulation to the face twice daily for four weeks; measured with a Cutometer, lower-eyelid skin elasticity increased 25.4% (P=.003) and firmness increased 23.8% (P=.029).[^jacobs] "Modest; a measured elasticity and firmness gain in one small, uncontrolled, single-arm trial of a formulation the authors themselves developed, not tested against a vehicle or another acid."
- Evidence quality: weak. A single open-label study (n=24) with no vehicle or active-comparator arm, using the treating practice's own proprietary formulation.[^jacobs]

**Tolerability relative to other AHAs**
- Effect size: mixed, and better supported against harsher actives than against its closest AHA sibling. Mandelic acid produced fewer adverse effects than 30% salicylic acid in the head-to-head acne trial,[^dayal] and the salicylic-mandelic combination was reported as better tolerated than glycolic acid in both Indian trials that compared them.[^garg][^sarkar] But the one trial to test mandelic acid alone against lactic acid, the AHA closest to it in molecule size, found irritation "more frequent in the mandelic acid group" alongside the lower satisfaction score.[^malviya] "Mixed; better tolerated than salicylic acid, and, in combination, than glycolic acid, but not shown to be gentler than lactic acid, its nearest AHA comparator, in the one trial that tested them head to head."
- Evidence quality: preliminary. Each comparator-specific finding rests on a single small trial.[^dayal][^garg][^sarkar][^malviya]

## What We Actually Know

Mandelic acid's controlled evidence is smaller and more mixed than the evidence behind [[glycolic-acid]] or [[lactic-acid]]. Tested alone at 45% against 30% salicylic acid for acne, it performed about the same on the Michaelsson acne score, with salicylic acid ahead on noninflammatory lesions and mandelic acid ahead on inflammatory ones, and "adverse effects were lesser with MA peels."[^dayal] Combined with salicylic acid, it outperformed glycolic acid on active acne lesions and post-acne hyperpigmentation in one 44-patient trial (P<.001 for both),[^garg] and matched glycolic acid for melasma severity in a 90-patient trial where both peels reduced MASI scores by about 60% (P=.876 between them).[^sarkar] A Cochrane review that pooled a subset of the acne comparison rated it low-quality evidence.[^cochrane] The picture changes when mandelic acid is tested alone rather than in combination: in the one trial that pitted it against lactic acid, the AHA closest to it in molecule size, for periorbital hyperpigmentation, lactic acid won on efficacy, satisfaction, and irritation.[^malviya] A single small, uncontrolled, open-label trial reports improved measured skin elasticity and firmness from a proprietary mandelic acid formulation, with no vehicle or comparator arm.[^jacobs]

## How It Works

As an alpha-hydroxy acid, mandelic acid works by the mechanism established for the AHA class: it weakens the bonds holding dead surface cells (corneocytes) together, so the outer skin layer sheds faster.[^kornhauser] Its molecule is larger than glycolic acid's, which is the basis for classing it, alongside [[lactic-acid]], on the slower-penetrating and gentler side of the exfoliating-acid strength axis; see [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]] for that comparison across the acids the site covers. The trials above test mandelic acid almost entirely as an in-office peel at 30% to 45%, either alone or combined with salicylic acid, rather than as a leave-on daily cosmetic.[^dayal][^garg][^sarkar][^malviya]

## The Evidence

**Acne, mandelic acid alone versus salicylic acid.** A randomized trial assigned 50 patients with mild-to-moderate facial acne vulgaris to six fortnightly sessions of either 30% salicylic acid or 45% mandelic acid peels over 12 weeks, graded with the Michaelsson acne score and clinical photographs.[^dayal] "Both agents showed almost equal efficacy in improving mild-to-moderate AV. Salicylic acid was found better in treating noninflammatory lesions, while MA had an upper hand in treating inflammatory lesions. Overall, there was no significant difference between the two peels in improving MAS and percentage decrease in MAS. However, adverse effects were lesser with MA peels."[^dayal] The authors concluded 45% mandelic acid "was found to be equally effective as 30% SA peel in mild-to-moderate facial AV. However, safety and tolerability of MA peel were better than SA peel."[^dayal]

**Acne and hyperpigmentation, mandelic acid combined with salicylic acid versus glycolic acid.** Forty-four patients with facial acne and post-acne scarring and hyperpigmentation were split between six fortnightly sessions of 35% glycolic acid peels or a 20% salicylic/10% mandelic acid combination peel.[^garg] "Both the agents were effective, but SMPs had a higher efficacy for most active acne lesions (p<.001) and hyperpigmentation (p<.001). Side effects were also lesser with SMPs."[^garg] The authors concluded both were "effective and safe in Indian patients, with SMPs being better for active acne and post-acne hyperpigmentation."[^garg] A Cochrane systematic review of topical acne treatments later pooled a 40-participant subset of this comparison and found "no difference in PGA when comparing glycolic acid peel to salicylic-mandelic acid peel (RR 1.06, 95% CI 0.88 to 1.26; 1 study, 40 participants; low-quality evidence)."[^cochrane]

**Melasma, mandelic acid combined with salicylic acid versus glycolic acid.** Ninety patients with melasma were randomized into three groups of 30, each primed for four weeks with 4% hydroquinone and 0.05% tretinoin cream before fortnightly peels for 12 weeks: 35% glycolic acid, a 20% salicylic/10% mandelic acid combination, or a phytic acid combination.[^sarkar] By MASI score, "there was a decrease in MASI score in all 3 groups but it was statistically significantly lower in Group A than Group C (p=.00), and it was also statistically significantly lower in Group B than Group C (p=.00) but there was no statistically significant difference between Groups A and B (p=.876)."[^sarkar] The reduction was "62.36% reduction in GA group, 60.98% reduction in SM group, and 44.71% in phytic acid group."[^sarkar] The authors concluded glycolic acid and the salicylic-mandelic peel "are both equally efficacious and a safe treatment modality for melasma in Indian skin, and are more effective than phytic acid peels," and that "salicylic-mandelic peels are better tolerated and more suitable for Indian skin."[^sarkar]

**Periorbital hyperpigmentation, mandelic acid alone versus lactic acid alone.** Seventy patients with periorbital melanosis (dark circles) were randomized to three sessions of either 30% mandelic acid or 30% lactic acid peels, two weeks apart, with efficacy assessed by pigmentation grade, a visual-analog scale, and patient satisfaction.[^malviya] "The lactic acid group exhibited a significantly greater reduction in pigmentation (50% of patients with more than 30% improvement in VAS, P=0.001)," and "patient satisfaction was higher in the lactic acid group (100% satisfied vs. 83.9% in the mandelic acid group, P=0.011)."[^malviya] Side effects included "mild erythema and swelling, with exfoliation more common in the lactic acid group and irritation more frequent in the mandelic acid group."[^malviya] The authors concluded "both 30% mandelic and lactic acid peels effectively treated" the condition, "with lactic acid showing better outcomes and patient satisfaction," and that "lactic acid may be better for sensitive skin due to milder side effects."[^malviya]

**Skin texture and elasticity, mandelic acid alone, uncontrolled.** Twenty-four patients applied a mandelic acid formulation, developed by the treating practice, to the face twice daily for four weeks, with lower-eyelid skin viscoelasticity measured weekly by Cutometer.[^jacobs] "After four weeks of topical mandelic acid treatment, the elasticity of lower eyelid skin increased 25.4% (P=.003). Skin firmness increased 23.8% (P=.029)."[^jacobs] There was no vehicle or active-comparator group.[^jacobs]

## Uses

Supported on preliminary evidence: mandelic acid peels at 45% perform about the same as 30% salicylic acid peels for mild-to-moderate acne, with fewer reported side effects.[^dayal] Combined with salicylic acid, mandelic-containing peels have outperformed glycolic acid peels for active acne and hyperpigmentation in one small Indian trial each, though a Cochrane review rated the acne comparison low-quality.[^garg][^cochrane] For melasma, a salicylic-mandelic combination peel matched, but did not beat, a 35% glycolic acid peel when both were layered onto a hydroquinone-and-tretinoin regimen.[^sarkar] Not supported as clearly gentler than its closest AHA sibling: the one trial testing mandelic acid alone against lactic acid for hyperpigmentation found lactic acid produced better pigment reduction, higher satisfaction, and less irritation.[^malviya] The general evidence for chemical exfoliation and photoaging shared across the AHA family is graded on [[glycolic-acid]] and [[lactic-acid]]; acne and hyperpigmentation as conditions are covered on [[acne]] and [[hyperpigmentation]].

## Common Marketing Claims

None of the claims below is established beyond what The Evidence above supports.

- **"Gentler than glycolic acid, ideal for darker or sensitive skin."** No trial in the evidence above tested mandelic acid alone against glycolic acid alone; the trials showing better tolerability used mandelic acid combined with salicylic acid.[^garg][^sarkar] The one trial testing mandelic acid alone against another gentle AHA found it caused irritation more often than lactic acid, not less.[^malviya]
- **"Virtually no irritation."** The same trial found irritation "more frequent in the mandelic acid group" than in the lactic acid comparison group.[^malviya]
- **"Clinically proven anti-aging."** The only controlled data on skin texture and elasticity come from a single 24-person, uncontrolled, open-label trial of a formulation its own authors developed, with no vehicle or comparator arm.[^jacobs]
