Carnosine is the N-(beta-alanyl) derivative of L-histidine, a naturally occurring dipeptide found in muscle that scavenges reactive oxygen species and the unsaturated aldehydes produced by lipid peroxidation.[^pubchem] It is marketed in serums and creams as an "anti-glycation" [[anti-aging]] active, on the premise that it blocks the sugar-protein cross-links (advanced glycation end-products, AGEs) blamed for stiffened, discolored collagen, but almost none of that marketing rests on a trial of living human skin.

> The only topical human-skin data for carnosine come from a manufacturer-funded study exposing skin explants (excised tissue, not living volunteers) to an artificial glycation trigger, not a controlled trial of people using the product.[^narda] A small oral-supplement RCT reported cosmetic skin-appearance improvements, but it used a proprietary multi-ingredient formulation from a company selling that same product, not carnosine alone.[^babizhayev]

## The Rubric

**Topical anti-glycation (ex vivo human skin, not a living-subject trial).** ISDIN's own research group applied 0.2% carnosine, plain or in a facial cream, to human skin explants chemically glycated with methylglyoxal, and reported the cream reduced two AGE markers more than the aqueous carnosine solution alone.[^narda] This shows carnosine can blunt AGE formation in a piece of excised skin tissue under an artificial glycation challenge; it does not show a facial cream slows visible skin aging in a person over time, and three of the five authors (Narda, Trullas, Granger) are ISDIN employees testing ISDIN's own cream, with a fourth author (Krutmann) disclosed as a Scientific Advisor to ISDIN and a fifth author (Laurent Peno-Mazzarino, of Laboratoire BIO-EC) affiliated with the contract lab that performed the explant testing.[^narda] No independent replication and no living-subject trial were found.

**Combination "anti-glycation" formulas (ex vivo, carnosine as one of several actives).** A separate study tested a multi-active sun-protection product (TPF50) containing carnosine alongside two other antioxidants (arazine and ergothionine) plus DNA-repair enzymes and physical sunscreen filters, on irradiated human skin biopsies, and reported the combined formula reduced UV-induced protein and DNA damage markers more than comparator products.[^emanuele] Because carnosine was never tested in isolation in that formula, this does not isolate what carnosine itself contributed.

**Oral carnosine supplementation, cosmetic skin appearance.** A 4-month randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial gave 42 subjects a proprietary "non-hydrolized carnosine" oral supplement (Can-C Plus) for 3 months and reported continuous, significant improvement in overall skin appearance and a reduction in fine lines versus placebo, with the improvement fading after supplementation stopped.[^babizhayev] The trial is small, tests a branded multi-ingredient oral product rather than carnosine alone, and its lead author is affiliated with the company that markets that same Can-C product, an unresolved conflict of interest. It is oral, not topical, so it does not support a topical carnosine serum's marketing claims.

Across all three lines of evidence, no independent, living-subject, placebo-controlled trial of a topical carnosine product against a vehicle control was found. The anti-glycation mechanism is plausible from tissue and cell-culture work, but the skin-specific human case for a topical carnosine product rests on manufacturer- or vendor-affiliated studies using ex vivo tissue, combination formulas, or an oral proprietary blend, not the isolated topical ingredient in a controlled human trial. Compare the deeper, larger, independently replicated evidence base behind related actives such as [[peptides]].

## The Evidence

**Ex vivo human skin, ISDIN facial cream.** Human skin explants were glycated with methylglyoxal in culture, then treated with 0.2% carnosine in aqueous solution or in ISDIN's facial cream. The aqueous solution reduced carboxymethyl-lysine (CML) by 64% in the epidermis and 41% in the dermis, and pentosidine by 48% and 42% respectively; the facial cream reduced CML by 150% in the epidermis and 122% in the dermis, and pentosidine by 108% and 136% respectively, versus the glycated, untreated control.[^narda] The authors (three ISDIN employees, an ISDIN Scientific Advisor, and a fifth author from the contract lab that ran the explant testing) concluded "topically applied carnosine protects against the glycation induced by MG" in this explant model.[^narda] *An excised-tissue, manufacturer-run experiment found carnosine cream blunted a chemically induced glycation marker in skin explants; it is not a trial of people using the product over time.*

**Ex vivo human skin biopsies, multi-active sun-protection formula.** A three-active antioxidant blend containing carnosine (with arazine and ergothionine), combined with DNA-repair enzymes and SPF 50 sunscreen filters in one product (TPF50), was compared against other DNA-repair/antioxidant/sunscreen products on irradiated human skin biopsies; TPF50 "showed the best efficacy in reducing" cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers, 8-oxo-7,8-dihydro-2'-deoxyguanosine, and protein carbonylation among the products tested.[^emanuele] *Carnosine's individual contribution cannot be separated from the other two antioxidants and the DNA-repair enzymes in this combination formula.*

**Oral supplement RCT.** In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 4-month trial (3 months of dosing followed by a 1-month washout), 42 subjects took an oral non-hydrolyzed carnosine/carcinine supplement (Can-C Plus) or placebo; skin parameters assessed with a Visioscan imaging device "showed a continuous and significant improvement in the active group during the 3 months of supplementation as compared to placebo," including "improvement of the overall skin appearance and a reduction of fine lines."[^babizhayev] *A small, industry-affiliated oral trial reported a cosmetic skin-appearance benefit versus placebo for a proprietary supplement, not for topical carnosine.*

## Common Marketing Claims

None of the claims below is established by a controlled, living-subject trial of topical carnosine alone.

- **"Anti-glycation, reverses AGE-related skin aging."** The only supporting data are an ISDIN-run ex vivo explant experiment using an artificial glycation trigger, not a trial showing a topical product changes glycation or visible aging in living skin over time.[^narda]
- **"Clinically proven to reduce wrinkles."** The oral-supplement trial that reported a fine-lines improvement tested a proprietary multi-ingredient pill (Can-C Plus), not a topical carnosine serum or cream, and its lead author is affiliated with the company selling that supplement.[^babizhayev]
