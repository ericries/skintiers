Ergothioneine (L-ergothioneine) is a naturally occurring sulfur-containing derivative of the amino acid histidine, "synthesised only by some fungi and bacteria" and reaching humans through diet, with the highest food levels in certain mushrooms and Spirulina.[^halliwell2018][^cheah2021] The body takes it up through a dedicated transporter, OCTN1 (encoded by the gene SLC22A4), retains it in tissues, and excretes very little; the leading academic reviewers of the molecule, Cheah and Halliwell, note that "ET has found its way into various cosmetics and topical skin products."[^halliwell2018][^cheah2021]

> The cell-level antioxidant credentials are real: ergothioneine has "powerful antioxidant and cytoprotective properties in vitro," skin keratinocytes make the OCTN1 transporter, and cultured skin cells that accumulate it are measurably protected from UV-induced oxidative damage.[^halliwell2018][^markova][^hseu] The case for a visible benefit from a topical cosmetic is far thinner: no controlled human trial of a topical ergothioneine product on skin appearance was found, the one in-vivo topical experiment (rat skin, 1993) reported no protective effect and attributed the failure to the charged molecule's inability to enter the stratum corneum, and the sole human trial of ergothioneine itself, an oral one, found mostly non-significant changes in oxidative-damage biomarkers.[^broeke][^cheah2017]

## The Rubric

**Cellular antioxidant biology (mechanism; in vitro and animal).** This is the strong layer. Halliwell and Cheah's reviews describe a molecule with "powerful antioxidant and cytoprotective properties in vitro" that is "avidly taken up from the diet by humans and other animals through a transporter, OCTN1, and accumulates to high levels in certain tissues," with evidence that the body may raise OCTN1 at sites of tissue injury.[^halliwell2018] The US FDA has granted it Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS) status "to be used as a food additive and supplement."[^cheah2021] The same reviewers add a caveat the marketing omits: "although ET has potent antioxidant properties in vitro, protection against oxidative stress may not be its only, or even the primary physiological role."[^cheah2021]

**Protection of cultured skin cells from UV damage (in vitro; partly industry-affiliated).** Consistent cell-model results, none of them a trial of living human skin. Researchers at AGI Dermatics reported that epidermal keratinocytes synthesize OCTN1, internalize and accumulate ergothioneine, and show reduced "reactive oxygen species and DNA, protein, and lipid damage" under solar-simulating UV stress in culture.[^markova] An academic group in Taiwan found ergothioneine at 125-500 nM protected human HaCaT keratinocytes from UVA irradiation via the Nrf2/ARE antioxidant pathway, and that silencing Nrf2 or OCTN1 "showed no such protective effects," tying the protection to the transporter.[^hseu] A Korean group including authors from the skin science research team of cosmetics maker It's Hanbul Co. reported ergothioneine-pretreated keratinocytes protected co-cultured fibroblasts from UVB-driven senescence and collagen-degradation signaling.[^ko] A mouse study (whose senior author co-founded an ergothioneine production company) reported reduced lipid-peroxidation markers and inflammatory factors in UV-exposed skin.[^li2024] These are cell and animal endpoints (ROS, DNA-damage markers, apoptosis), which sit on the mechanism side of the ledger rather than demonstrating a health or appearance outcome in people.

**Visible skin benefit from a topical product (human; cosmetic claim).** Not established. No controlled human trial of a topical ergothioneine product on skin appearance was found in the sources here. The single in-vivo topical experiment located, a 1993 rat study of UV-protective thiols, reported that "Ergothioneine, mesna and penicillamine did not show any effect; probably, as a result of their charge they are not able to enter the stratum corneum."[^broeke] A multi-active sunscreen product (TPF50) containing ergothioneine alongside [[carnosine]] and DNA-repair enzymes reduced UV-induced damage markers in human skin biopsies, but the combination design cannot isolate ergothioneine's contribution.[^emanuele] Topical antioxidants with human clinical-trial evidence for skin outcomes include [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]], a well-studied brightening antioxidant, and [[niacinamide]], which has modest human evidence for barrier support and pigmentation.

**Oral supplementation (human; oxidative-damage biomarkers).** In the first administration of pure ergothioneine to healthy human volunteers, Cheah, Halliwell and colleagues found it "avidly absorbed and retained by the body" with under 4% excreted in urine, and observed "some decreasing trends" in biomarkers of oxidative damage and inflammation, "However, most of the changes were non-significant." Their conclusion: ergothioneine "may function as a major antioxidant but perhaps only under conditions of oxidative stress."[^cheah2017]

## The Evidence

**Keratinocyte uptake and UV protection (in vitro, industry).** A team at AGI Dermatics reported that epidermal keratinocytes, both as resident skin cells and in culture, synthesize the OCTN1 transporter and use it to internalize and accumulate ergothioneine, which "reduces the levels of reactive oxygen species and DNA, protein, and lipid damage in keratinocytes subjected to solar-simulating UV oxidative stress," limits apoptosis, and increases cell viability.[^markova] *Skin cells can take up and use ergothioneine as an antioxidant in culture; this is a company-affiliated cell study, not a trial of a product on skin.*

**UVA protection via Nrf2 (in vitro, academic).** Hseu and colleagues at China Medical University treated human HaCaT keratinocytes with 125-500 nM ergothioneine before 15 J/cm2 UVA irradiation and found increased cell viability, suppressed ROS and DNA damage (comet assay), and reduced apoptosis, mediated by Nrf2/ARE-driven antioxidant genes; silencing Nrf2 or OCTN1 with siRNA "showed no such protective effects."[^hseu] *An independent lab confirmed nanomolar ergothioneine protects keratinocytes from UVA in culture, and that the protection runs through its dedicated transporter and the cell's own antioxidant machinery.*

**Keratinocyte/fibroblast co-culture, UVB (in vitro, industry-affiliated).** Ko and colleagues, several affiliated with the skin science research team of It's Hanbul Co., pretreated keratinocytes with ergothioneine before UVB, then co-cultured them with fibroblasts: ROS, proapoptotic protein cleavage, and inflammatory cytokines (IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha) fell in the keratinocytes, and the downstream fibroblasts showed less senescence, lower collagen-degradation gene expression, and increased procollagen type I synthesis.[^ko] *In a dish, protecting the top-layer cells also protected the collagen-making cells beneath them; the collagen finding is a culture endpoint, not a wrinkle outcome.*

**Topical application in vivo (rat, 1993).** In the only in-vivo topical test found, van den Broeke and Beyersbergen van Henegouwen screened seven topically applied thiols for protection against UVA-induced photobinding of phototoxic drugs to rat epidermal biomacromolecules: "Ergothioneine, mesna and penicillamine did not show any effect; probably, as a result of their charge they are not able to enter the stratum corneum."[^broeke] *When actually applied to intact living skin, ergothioneine did nothing in this model, and the authors' explanation, poor penetration of a charged hydrophilic molecule, is a standing question for any ergothioneine cream.*

**Combination product on human skin biopsies.** An irradiation study of TPF50, a product combining SPF 50 physical sunscreens, liposome-encapsulated DNA-repair enzymes, and an antioxidant complex of carnosine, arazine, and ergothioneine, reported it "showed the best efficacy" among compared products at reducing UV-induced cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers, 8OHdG, and protein carbonylation in human skin biopsies.[^emanuele] *Ergothioneine was one of six-plus actives; the study cannot say what it contributed.*

**Oral human trial (biomarkers).** Cheah and colleagues gave pure oral ergothioneine to healthy human subjects and tracked pharmacokinetics and damage biomarkers: uptake was avid and retention long, with less than 4% urinary excretion, and biomarkers of oxidative damage and inflammation (allantoin, 8-hydroxy-2'-deoxyguanosine, 8-iso-PGF2alpha, protein carbonylation, C-reactive protein) showed "some decreasing trends" of which "most of the changes were non-significant."[^cheah2017] *The body clearly wants this molecule, but the one human trial could not show it lowering oxidative damage in healthy people; the authors suggest any antioxidant role may appear only under oxidative stress.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"A potent antioxidant."** True at the level of chemistry and cell culture, where the descriptor comes from: Markova and colleagues call it "a potent and stable low-molecular-weight antioxidant."[^markova] A cell-assay property is a mechanism finding; no controlled human trial of a topical ergothioneine product showing a visible skin benefit was found, and the descriptor does not transfer to a cream unproven at that step.[^broeke]
- **"Protects skin from UV damage."** The support is keratinocyte-culture and mouse data.[^markova][^hseu][^li2024] The one experiment that applied ergothioneine to intact living skin found no protective effect and pointed to its inability to cross the stratum corneum.[^broeke]
- **"FDA-recognized safe."** The GRAS designation is real but is for use "as a food additive and supplement," a regulatory safety status for eating it, and says nothing about what it does in a serum.[^cheah2021]
