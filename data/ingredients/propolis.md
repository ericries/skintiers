Propolis, sometimes labeled "bee propolis extract" or "bee glue," is the resinous material honeybees gather from plant buds and use to seal and sterilize their hives; in skincare it is used as an antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory botanical, and its chemical makeup varies so widely by geography and plant source that two propolis extracts can behave like different ingredients.[^degroot][^bankova] The strongest clinical evidence is for one narrow use, herpes labialis (cold sores), where a specific proprietary extract healed lesions faster than topical aciclovir in two randomized trials; a separate and equally important health fact is that propolis is a recognized contact allergen that appears on standard patch-test series.[^jautova][^arenberger][^vanoers][^degroot]

> The best human evidence for propolis is two randomized trials of one 0.5% proprietary extract (GH 2002) that healed cold-sore lesions a median one day faster than aciclovir 5% (p<0.0001), plus a non-randomized pilot in diabetic foot ulcers; against that sits its status as a documented contact allergen, positive in 28% of 200 consecutive patients tested for suspected contact dermatitis, and the fact that propolis composition depends heavily on its plant source, so results with one extract do not transfer to another.[^jautova][^arenberger][^henshaw][^vanoers][^bankova]

## The Rubric

Grades differ by use, and safety is treated as its own axis. Framing is relative and names comparators. Because propolis composition varies by botanical origin, a grade earned by one studied extract does not automatically carry to a jar labeled "propolis" from a different source.[^bankova]

**Herpes labialis / cold sores (health)**
- **Effect size: notable, for one specific extract.** Two randomized trials of a 0.5% propolis special extract (GH 2002) found it reached complete crusting or re-epithelialization a median one day sooner than aciclovir 5% cream, the standard over-the-counter topical antiviral, and beat it on pain, burning, and swelling.[^jautova][^arenberger] "Notable, at least matching and on healing time beating aciclovir 5% in two RCTs, but shown only for the proprietary GH 2002 extract, not propolis in general."
- **Evidence quality: solid but narrow.** Two multicenter controlled trials of roughly 400 patients each, one double-blind and one single-blind, both positive.[^jautova][^arenberger] Short of gold-standard because both test the same single proprietary extract, one trial lists an industry-affiliated author, and no trial of a generic or differently sourced propolis is cited here.[^arenberger]

**Diabetic foot ulcer / wound healing (health)**
- **Effect size: modest and promising, not established.** In a human feasibility study, ulcers treated with topical propolis shrank by a mean 41% at week 1 versus 16% in matched controls (P<0.001), and 63% versus 44% by week 3 (P<0.05).[^henshaw] "Promising in one non-randomized pilot against matched standard-care controls; not a controlled standard of care."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** A single non-randomized human pilot (n=24 propolis vs 84 matched controls) supported by animal wound models; no randomized human wound trial is cited here.[^henshaw]

**Acne (health / cosmetic)**
- **Effect size: not established for propolis on its own.** The one human acne trial tested a cream of 20% propolis combined with 3% tea tree oil and 10% Aloe vera, which outperformed 3% erythromycin cream; because three actives were combined, the result cannot be attributed to propolis.[^mazzarello] "Untested as a single agent; the one human acne trial used a three-ingredient combination, so any effect is not isolable to propolis."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** One small double-blind trial (n=60, 20 per arm) of a combination product, plus laboratory activity against acne-associated bacteria.[^mazzarello]

**Antioxidant / "anti-aging" / brightening (cosmetic)**
- **Effect size: none demonstrated in controlled human trials.** Propolis extracts show antioxidant activity in laboratory assays, but no controlled human trial establishing an anti-aging or brightening skin benefit is cited here.[^degroot][^bankova] No graded effect can be assigned. "Laboratory antioxidant activity only; no controlled human anti-aging endpoint."
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** In vitro antioxidant data without a controlled human cosmetic outcome cited here.[^degroot]

**Contact allergy (safety, a separate axis, not an efficacy grade)**
- Propolis is a recognized skin sensitizer and appears on standard patch-test series. In a prospective study of 200 consecutive patients tested for suspected contact dermatitis, 56 (28%) had one or more positive reactions to Brazilian propolis, and the authors concluded the reactions were largely allergic, with a strong link to prior fragrance sensitization.[^vanoers] Earlier testing at similar rates found that "for most, no clinical relevance could be found," so the positive-test rate overstates how often propolis is the actual cause of a patient's dermatitis; the sensitizing potential itself is well documented.[^vanoers][^degroot]

## What We Actually Know

The one use with replicated randomized evidence is cold sores, and even there the evidence is tied to a single proprietary extract. Two controlled trials of a 0.5% propolis special extract (GH 2002) found it healed herpes labialis lesions a median one day faster than aciclovir 5% cream, one starting in the vesicular stage and one in the papular/erythematous stage, both at p<0.0001.[^jautova][^arenberger] A non-randomized human pilot found topical propolis shrank diabetic foot ulcers faster than matched standard-care controls, a health signal that has not been confirmed in a randomized trial.[^henshaw] For acne, the only human trial combined propolis with tea tree oil and Aloe vera, so it cannot show what propolis alone does.[^mazzarello] Two honest limits run through all of it: propolis composition "depends on the plant sources they are derived from," so an extract studied in one trial is not the same material as another jar of propolis, and propolis is itself a documented contact allergen carried on patch-test series.[^bankova][^vanoers][^degroot]

## How It Works

Propolis is a mixture, not a single active. A dermatology review describes it as "the resinous substance that bees collect from living plants" and reports that "it has antibacterial, antifungal, and antiviral properties," the activities behind its use on cold sores and wounds.[^degroot] European poplar-type propolis is characterized chiefly by flavonoids and phenolic-acid esters, including caffeic acid esters, which carry much of its antimicrobial and antioxidant activity and are also its principal contact allergens.[^degroot][^bankova] The central complication for any propolis claim is variability: because bees collect resin from whatever plants are nearby, composition "depends on the plant sources they are derived from," and it changes with the local flora, bee species, and climate, so Brazilian green propolis, European poplar propolis, and stingless-bee propolis differ in both their active and their allergenic constituents.[^bankova] The proposed anti-inflammatory mechanism in wound healing includes reduced matrix metalloproteinase activity and lower bacterial burden in the wound, both measured in the diabetic foot ulcer pilot.[^henshaw]

## The Evidence

Primary evidence, by use.

**Herpes labialis (health).** Jautová and colleagues ran a double-blind randomized trial in patients whose cold sores were all in the vesicular phase at inclusion, comparing a lip cream with 0.5% propolis special extract GH 2002 (199 patients) against aciclovir 5% (198 patients), each applied five times daily.[^jautova] Complete crusting or epithelialization was reached after a median 3 days with propolis versus 4 days with aciclovir (p<0.0001), with significant advantages for propolis on every secondary symptom, and "no allergic reactions, local irritations or other adverse events occurred."[^jautova] A second, independent single-blind randomized trial by Arenberger and colleagues enrolled patients in the earlier erythematous/papular stage (189 propolis, 190 aciclovir) and found the predefined healing endpoint at a median 4 days with propolis versus 5 with aciclovir (P<0.0001); the authors concluded the 0.5% propolis balm was "superior in the treatment of episodes of herpes labialis over 5% aciclovir cream."[^arenberger] Both trials test the same proprietary GH 2002 extract, and the Arenberger trial lists an author from a life-sciences consultancy, so the result is specific to that extract rather than to propolis in general.[^jautova][^arenberger]

**Diabetic foot ulcer (health).** Henshaw and colleagues applied topical propolis at each clinic review for 6 weeks to 24 patients with diabetic foot ulcers of at least 4 weeks' duration, comparing healing with a matched control cohort of 84 patients treated recently at the same center.[^henshaw] Ulcer area was reduced by a mean 41% in the propolis group versus 16% in controls at week 1 (P<0.001) and 63% versus 44% at week 3 (P<0.05); post-debridement wound-fluid active MMP-9 and bacterial counts were also lower in the propolis group (both P<0.001), and the authors called topical propolis "a well-tolerated therapy for wound healing."[^henshaw] This is a non-randomized feasibility study against a historical matched cohort, and the authors state that a multi-site randomized controlled trial "now appears to be warranted," so it is a signal rather than proof.[^henshaw]

**Acne (health / cosmetic).** Mazzarello and colleagues randomized 60 patients with mild-to-moderate acne to a cream containing 20% propolis, 3% tea tree oil, and 10% Aloe vera (PTAC), to 3% erythromycin cream, or to placebo (20 per arm), assessing lesions at baseline, 15, and 30 days.[^mazzarello] The combination cream was "better than ERC in reducing erythema scars, acne severity index, and total lesion count," but because propolis was combined with two other actives, including tea tree oil, the trial cannot show what propolis alone contributed.[^mazzarello]

**Contact allergy (safety).** van Oers and colleagues patch-tested 200 consecutive patients suspected of contact dermatitis with a Brazilian propolis dilution series (10%, 3.3%, and 1% in petrolatum); 56 (28%) had one or more positive reactions, and the pattern of concentration responses led the authors to conclude the reactions were largely allergic rather than irritant, with "a strong association with fragrances."[^vanoers] The background to that study notes that earlier testing at rates above 20% had found that "for most, no clinical relevance could be found," so a high patch-test positivity rate does not mean propolis is that often the cause of a given patient's rash; the point that propolis is a genuine sensitizer, however, is consistent across the contact-dermatitis literature.[^vanoers][^degroot]

## Uses

Best supported for herpes labialis, where two randomized trials of one 0.5% proprietary extract found faster lesion healing than aciclovir 5% cream, though the result is specific to that extract rather than to propolis generally.[^jautova][^arenberger] An early health signal exists for diabetic foot ulcer healing, from a single non-randomized human pilot that its own authors said warrants a randomized trial.[^henshaw] For acne, the only human trial tested propolis inside a three-ingredient combination, so propolis alone is untested there; salicylic acid and benzoyl peroxide carry the better-evidenced acne case, covered on [[salicylic-acid]] and [[benzoyl-peroxide]].[^mazzarello] Antioxidant and general "anti-aging" or brightening positioning rests on laboratory activity rather than controlled human skin trials cited here.[^degroot][^bankova] Running alongside all uses is a safety consideration: propolis is a documented contact allergen carried on standard patch-test series, more likely to matter for people already sensitized to fragrances.[^vanoers][^degroot]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Clinically proven to heal / soothe."** The clinical proof is narrow: two randomized trials of a specific 0.5% propolis extract (GH 2002) for cold sores, and one non-randomized pilot for diabetic foot ulcers.[^jautova][^arenberger][^henshaw] A jar of propolis serum or "bee glue" essence from a different botanical source is not the material those trials studied, because propolis composition "depends on the plant sources they are derived from."[^bankova]
- **"Antibacterial superfood for skin / fights acne."** Propolis does have antibacterial and antiviral activity in the laboratory, and a review states "it has antibacterial, antifungal, and antiviral properties."[^degroot] But the one human acne trial combined it with tea tree oil and Aloe vera, so its acne benefit is not isolable; the better-evidenced topical acne agents are covered on [[salicylic-acid]] and [[benzoyl-peroxide]].[^mazzarello]
- **"Antioxidant / anti-aging glow."** Antioxidant activity is a laboratory finding; no controlled human trial cited here shows propolis reduces wrinkles or ages skin more slowly.[^degroot][^bankova] Any anti-aging framing is extrapolation from in vitro data.
- **"Gentle, natural, and safe."** Propolis is a recognized contact allergen on standard patch-test series, positive in 28% of 200 patients tested for suspected contact dermatitis in one study, with a strong link to fragrance co-sensitization.[^vanoers][^degroot] Natural origin does not make it low-risk for sensitized skin.
