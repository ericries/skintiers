Acetyl Octapeptide-3, marketed under the trade name SNAP-8, is a synthetic eight-amino-acid peptide (acetyl-Glu-Glu-Met-Gln-Arg-Arg-Ala-Asp-NH2, C41H70N16O16S) built by extending the C-terminus of [[argireline-acetyl-hexapeptide-8]]'s six-amino-acid sequence with two additional residues (Ala-Asp).[^pubchem] It is sold as a longer, supposedly more potent version of the same "topical Botox" pitch, marketed to soften expression lines by blocking the SNARE-complex machinery neurons use to release acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction. Unlike Argireline, whose mechanism and a wrinkle-depth effect were at least published by the peptide's own developers in a peer-reviewed journal, SNAP-8 has no PubMed-indexed paper isolating the ingredient at all: the only two indexed clinical studies that mention it test it as one of several actives in a combination microneedle patch, so no clinical evidence exists that credibly isolates SNAP-8's own contribution to any skin outcome.[^shin][^avcil]

> The only PubMed-indexed clinical data touching acetyl octapeptide-3 come from a 12-week trial of a hyaluronic-acid microneedle patch combining it with four other actives (arginine/lysine polypeptide, palmitoyl tripeptide-5, adenosine, and seaweed extracts), which reported "the fine lines/wrinkles showed 25.8% noticeable decrease" versus baseline, with no arm testing acetyl octapeptide-3 alone or against a matched vehicle.[^avcil]

## The Rubric

**Expression-line and fine-line reduction (topical, isolated ingredient)**
- **Effect size: not established. No trial isolates SNAP-8.** Every PubMed-indexed clinical study that includes acetyl octapeptide-3 delivers it as one ingredient among several inside a hyaluronic-acid microneedle patch, so no published number can be attributed to SNAP-8 specifically rather than to its co-formulated actives or to the microneedle delivery mechanism itself.[^shin][^avcil]
- **Evidence quality: weak.** Two small industry-affiliated studies exist (24 and an unspecified small monocentric cohort, both from the peptide-patch manufacturer Raphas Co.), neither placebo/vehicle-controlled for the ingredient itself, and neither independently replicated.[^shin][^avcil] No independent academic group appears to have tested the isolated peptide in humans, and no origin paper analogous to Argireline's Blanes-Mira 2002 study establishing in vitro neurotransmitter-inhibition potency for this specific octapeptide is indexed in PubMed.

**The "stronger topical Botox" claim relative to Argireline**
- **Effect size: unverifiable from the peer-reviewed literature.** Manufacturer marketing describes SNAP-8 as more effective than acetyl hexapeptide-8 at inhibiting neurotransmitter release, but no PubMed-indexed study directly compares the two peptides' potency or clinical effect head to head, so this claim cannot be adjudicated against a primary published source.
- **Evidence quality: absent.** The comparative potency claim exists only in non-peer-reviewed manufacturer technical literature, which does not meet this site's bar for a primary source.

## How It Works

Acetyl octapeptide-3 extends the Ac-Glu-Glu-Met-Gln-Arg-Arg-NH2 sequence of [[argireline-acetyl-hexapeptide-8]] by two residues (Ala-Asp) at the C-terminus, a modification the manufacturer designed to more closely mimic the SNAP-25 protein fragment that forms part of the SNARE complex neurons use to dock and fuse synaptic vesicles before releasing acetylcholine.[^pubchem] The underlying biological logic is the same one described in depth on the Argireline page: botulinum toxin cleaves SNARE-complex proteins directly at the neuromuscular junction, paralyzing the targeted muscle, while these peptides are proposed to compete with or block SNARE-complex assembly when applied topically. For Argireline, that mechanism has at least a published in vitro demonstration and an independent skin-penetration study (which found the peptide barely crosses the stratum corneum); for acetyl octapeptide-3, no such isolated in vitro or penetration study appears in PubMed, so whether the modified sequence behaves as claimed, or whether it penetrates skin at all, has not been independently confirmed in the peer-reviewed literature.[^pubchem]

## The Evidence

**Dissolving microneedle patch with acetyl octapeptide-3, vitamin C derivative, and lysophosphatidic acid (topical, combination).** Shin and colleagues at Raphas Co. fabricated a dissolving microneedle patch (DA-MNP) combining a hyaluronic-acid polymer backbone with acetyl octapeptide-3, L-ascorbic acid 2-glucoside, and sodium cyclic lysophosphatidic acid, and compared it to a hyaluronic-acid-only placebo patch in 24 healthy subjects, applied to opposite eyes overnight for 28 days. The active patch group "showed an effective eye wrinkles improvement and better anti-aging of skin, with reduced TEWL, enhanced skin elasticity and lifting, and no adverse effects" relative to the HA-only placebo.[^shin] *The active patch beat a matched HA-only placebo, but the patch bundles three actives plus the microneedle delivery system itself, so none of the improvement can be attributed to acetyl octapeptide-3 specifically.*

**Hyaluronic-acid microneedle patch with five bioactives (topical, combination).** Avcil and colleagues, in a monocentric 12-week clinical study, tested hyaluronic-acid microneedle patches loaded with arginine/lysine polypeptide, acetyl octapeptide-3, palmitoyl tripeptide-5, adenosine, and seaweed extracts, applied at the outer eye corner and on the volar forearm of subjects with aged skin. The product was "tolerated excellently" with no reported skin reactions, and instrumental measurement found "the fine lines/wrinkles showed 25.8% noticeable decrease," alongside a 15.4% improvement in hydration and 14.2%/12.9% increases in dermal density and thickness versus baseline; the authors concluded the ingredients "might possibly be acted synergistically."[^avcil] *A five-active combination patch improved wrinkle appearance, hydration, and dermal density over 12 weeks against baseline, with no vehicle-only control arm and no way to isolate acetyl octapeptide-3's individual contribution, and the authors themselves frame the result as a synergistic, not single-ingredient, effect.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"A stronger, longer-lasting version of Argireline / topical Botox."** The structural logic (extending the SNARE-mimicking sequence) is plausible, and it mirrors a real modification described in patent and manufacturer literature, but no PubMed-indexed study has independently tested acetyl octapeptide-3's neurotransmitter-inhibiting potency in vitro or compared it head to head with acetyl hexapeptide-8, so the "stronger" claim rests on unpublished manufacturer data rather than peer-reviewed evidence.
- **"Clinically proven to reduce wrinkles."** Two small industry-affiliated microneedle-patch studies did find wrinkle improvement in the patches containing SNAP-8, but both tested it bundled with two to four other actives inside a novel delivery system, and neither isolates or vehicle-controls for the peptide itself, so "clinically proven" overstates what the published data can support for this specific ingredient.[^shin][^avcil]
