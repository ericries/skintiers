4-Butylresorcinol (INCI name, also called rucinol or 4-n-butylresorcinol) is a synthetic resorcinol derivative used in leave-on cosmetics as a tyrosinase inhibitor for melasma, age spots, and other hyperpigmentation, typically formulated around 0.1% to 0.3%.[^pubchem][^khemis] In a head-to-head biochemical comparison it was by far the most potent inhibitor of human tyrosinase among the tested skin-lightening agents, and the one independent, vehicle-controlled clinical trial of an isolated 0.3% rucinol serum found a statistically significant reduction in melasma pigmentation after 12 weeks.[^kolbe][^khemis]

> Against isolated human tyrosinase, 4-n-butylresorcinol inhibited the enzyme at an IC50 of 21 μmol/L, versus about 500 μmol/L for kojic acid and a millimolar-range IC50 for arbutin and hydroquinone, a roughly 25-fold potency edge over kojic acid.[^kolbe] In the one randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trial of the isolated ingredient, a 0.3% rucinol serum applied to one side of the face significantly outperformed its vehicle on the other side after 12 weeks in 32 women with melasma (P=0.027), with further improvement over an additional 12-week open-label extension.[^khemis]

## The Rubric

**Melasma and hyperpigmentation (topical, isolated ingredient)**
- **Effect size: modest to notable, in the one trial that isolates it.** In a split-face design, clinical pigmentation score was significantly lower on the rucinol 0.3%-treated side than the vehicle-treated side after 12 weeks (P=0.027); chromametry confirmed skin was significantly lighter and less yellow, with a trend toward less redness, and 78% of patients rated the product's efficacy good or fair.[^khemis] A separate forearm study cited in an industry review found twice-daily 4-n-butylresorcinol visibly reduced age spots over 8 weeks versus untreated vehicle-only control spots, and a second study found it outperformed two related resorcinol analogs (4-hexylresorcinol and 4-phenylethylresorcinol).[^kolbe] "Modest to notable, and directly comparable in design quality to the better topical melasma agents: one randomized, vehicle-controlled split-face trial reached significance for the isolated ingredient, a stronger design than kojic acid's evidence base."
- **Evidence quality: mixed to solid, but resting on one clinical trial.** The clinical case rests on a single independent, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled split-face trial (32 women, 12-week primary endpoint plus a 12-week open-label extension), reinforced by strong, consistent biochemical and reconstructed-skin-model potency data from a separate industry (Beiersdorf) research group and corroborated by an independent, no-conflict-of-interest 2024 pharmacology review describing rucinol as one of the most active tyrosinase inhibitors among resorcinol-scaffold compounds.[^khemis][^kolbe][^beaumet] Short of gold-standard because only one clinical trial isolates the ingredient, and it has not been independently replicated.

## How It Works

4-Butylresorcinol is a resorcinol derivative, chemically 4-butylbenzene-1,3-diol (C10H14O2), a structural analog of hydroquinone and arbutin that swaps the para-diol motif of hydroquinone for a meta-diol (resorcinol) ring carrying a butyl side chain.[^pubchem] In a biochemical assay on human tyrosinase, the enzyme that catalyzes the rate-limiting step of melanin synthesis, 4-n-butylresorcinol inhibited activity with an IC50 of 21 μmol/L, described by its authors as "by far the most potent inhibitor of human tyrosinase" among hydroquinone, arbutin, kojic acid, and 4-n-butylresorcinol.[^kolbe] In a reconstructed pigmented skin model (MelanoDerm), it also had the lowest IC50 (13.5 μmol/L) for inhibiting melanin production of the compounds tested.[^kolbe] The Khemis trial's authors additionally cite prior research that rucinol inhibits tyrosinase-related protein-1 (TRP-1), a second melanogenesis enzyme, alongside tyrosinase itself, a dual mechanism proposed to explain its clinical activity.[^khemis] A 2024 independent structure-activity review notes that resorcinol's meta-dihydroxy arrangement "demonstrates resistance to [tyrosinase]-mediated oxidation" compared with catechol- and hydroquinone-type inhibitors, which can themselves act as alternative enzyme substrates rather than true inhibitors.[^beaumet]

## The Evidence

**Melasma, isolated 0.3% rucinol serum versus vehicle, split-face (topical).** A prospective, single-center, double-blind, randomized, vehicle-controlled, split-face trial gave 32 women with melasma two identical tubes, one containing 0.3% rucinol serum and one the vehicle, applied to opposite halves of the face twice daily for 12 weeks (phase 1), with a broad-spectrum SPF 60 sunscreen used daily by all subjects; 28 patients completed phase 1. Clinical pigmentation score was significantly lower on the rucinol side than the vehicle side after 12 weeks (P=0.027), and chromametry found skin "significantly lighter and less yellow, with a strong trend towards reduced redness" on the rucinol side. In an optional 12-week open-label extension (phase 2, 26 completers) where both sides received rucinol, the side previously treated with vehicle showed a significant improvement, and the already-treated side improved further. The product showed good tolerability and acceptability, and 78% of patients rated its efficacy as good or fair.[^khemis] *A vehicle-controlled trial of the isolated ingredient found a statistically significant, if modest, pigment reduction after 12 weeks, with skin lightening confirmed instrumentally and further gains on continued use.*

**Enzyme and reconstructed-skin-model potency, comparative (in vitro).** A Beiersdorf research group compared 4-n-butylresorcinol against hydroquinone, arbutin, and kojic acid on isolated human tyrosinase and in the MelanoDerm pigmented skin-equivalent model. On the enzyme assay, arbutin and hydroquinone had millimolar-range IC50s, kojic acid was about 10-fold more potent (roughly 500 μmol/L), and 4-n-butylresorcinol was the most potent by a wide margin (IC50 21 μmol/L). In MelanoDerm, arbutin was the weakest (IC50 above 5000 μmol/L) and 4-n-butylresorcinol again the strongest (IC50 13.5 μmol/L), ahead of hydroquinone (below 40 μmol/L, which the authors attribute to a different, non-tyrosinase mechanism) and kojic acid (above 400 μmol/L).[^kolbe] *In a direct comparison, 4-butylresorcinol was the most potent tyrosinase inhibitor tested, both on the isolated enzyme and in a living-skin model, well ahead of hydroquinone, kojic acid, and arbutin.*

**Combined depigmenting serum with tranexamic acid, niacinamide, and hydroxy acids (topical).** A 35-subject open trial from ISDIN (whose employees authored the study) tested a gel serum combining tranexamic acid, niacinamide, 4-butylresorcinol, phytic acid, and a hydroxy-acid blend, applied twice daily for 84 days. Brown spot count fell 28.4% and melanin index fell 31.1% by day 84 versus baseline (both P<0.001), with improvements maintained a month after stopping. Because five actives were combined and the study had no separate vehicle or single-active arm, 4-butylresorcinol's individual contribution cannot be isolated, and the funding source is the ingredient's marketer.[^furmanczyk] *A multi-active, industry-funded combination product improved pigmentation over 12 weeks, but the design cannot attribute any of that effect specifically to 4-butylresorcinol.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"The most powerful tyrosinase inhibitor available."** The comparative enzyme and skin-model data support a genuine potency edge over hydroquinone, kojic acid, and arbutin in the one published head-to-head study, but that study came from an industry laboratory (Beiersdorf) rather than an independent academic group, and it did not test newer inhibitors such as thiamidol.[^kolbe][^beaumet]
- **"Clinically proven for melasma."** One randomized, vehicle-controlled trial of the isolated ingredient found a statistically significant effect versus vehicle, a real result, but it is a single trial (32 enrolled, 28 completed phase 1) that has not been independently replicated.[^khemis]
