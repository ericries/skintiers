Cocamidopropyl betaine (INCI name, CAPB) is a synthetic amphoteric surfactant made by reacting coconut-oil-derived fatty acids with dimethylaminopropylamine (DMAPA), used as a mild secondary or co-surfactant in cleansers, shampoos, and body washes to reduce the irritation of harsher anionic surfactants like sodium lauryl sulfate while boosting foam.[^pubchem][^jacob] It is genuinely gentler on skin than straight sulfate detergents in comparative testing, but it is also one of the most common allergens found in skin cleansers, and manufacturing-process impurities (amidoamine, DMAPA) are the presumed sensitizers.[^corazza][^warshaw]

> In a retrospective analysis of 32,945 North American Contact Dermatitis Group patients patch-tested for reactions to skin cleansers (2000-2014), cocamidopropyl betaine accounted for 9.5% of skin-cleanser-associated allergic reactions, and its related manufacturing impurity amidoamine for 5.5%.[^warshaw]

## The Rubric

**Mild cleansing co-surfactant (functional, not a skin-health active)**
- **Effect size: real but modest, a foam-and-mildness function rather than a treatment benefit.** In a comparative patch and irritancy study of eight surfactants commonly used in skin cleansers (105 patients), none of the eight, including cocamidopropyl betaine, produced measurable skin irritation on Draize-style assessment, and the authors concluded that "cosmetic companies' efforts to search for and market new products with very mild surfactants have been generally successful."[^corazza] Separately, an in vitro nociception assay found that anionic sulfate surfactants (sodium lauryl sulfate, sodium laureth sulfate) activated the pain-signaling TRPV1 receptor in a dose-dependent way, while cocamidopropyl betaine did not, offering a plausible mechanistic reason it feels gentler.[^lindegren] This is a formulation-mildness function, not a skin-condition benefit; it has no anti-aging, barrier-repair, or disease-modifying claim of its own.
- **Evidence quality: moderate, and undercut by the ingredient's own allergy signal.** The mildness data come from small comparative irritancy panels (105 and cell-line assays), not large randomized trials, because CAPB is a formulation ingredient rather than a therapeutic one.[^corazza][^lindegren] In the same Corazza study, none of the eight surfactant preparations tested produced allergic reactions, but cocamidopropyl betaine as included in the Italian SIDAPA standard series produced positive allergic patch-test reactions in 5 of 105 patients, a finding echoed at population scale by NACDG data.[^corazza][^warshaw]

**Contact allergy risk (topical exposure via cleansers, shampoos, personal-care products)**
- **Effect size: not small for a widely used ingredient.** Contact sensitization prevalence to CAPB is estimated at 3.0% to 7.2% in reviewed populations, high enough that the American Contact Dermatitis Society named it Allergen of the Year in 2004.[^jacob] In NACDG data, CAPB accounted for 9.5% of cleanser-associated allergens (with amidoamine at 5.5%), and in a 5-year retrospective eyelid-dermatitis clinic review, CAPB-related surfactants (dimethylaminopropylamine, oleamidopropyl dimethylamine, amidoamine) were among the top 10 clinically relevant allergens found on patch testing.[^warshaw][^alley]
- **Evidence quality: solid, from large multicenter patch-test registries.** The allergy data rest on tens of thousands of patch-tested patients across multiple independent contact-dermatitis research groups (NACDG, a metropolitan contact dermatitis clinic), not single small trials, making the allergen signal one of the better-established facts about this ingredient.[^warshaw][^alley][^jacob]

## How It Works

Cocamidopropyl betaine is manufactured by condensing coconut fatty acids (predominantly lauric acid, C12) with the amine dimethylaminopropylamine (DMAPA) to form an amidoamine intermediate, which is then reacted with sodium chloroacetate to install the betaine (carboxybetaine) head group; the resulting molecule carries both a positive (quaternary amine) and negative (carboxylate) charge, making it amphoteric/zwitterionic.[^jacob] As a coconut-derived product it is a mixture of fatty-chain-length homologs rather than a single pure compound; the C12 (lauric) homolog, lauramidopropyl betaine, has the molecular formula C19H38N2O3.[^pubchem] Functionally, its amphoteric charge lets it co-micellize with anionic surfactants such as sodium lauryl sulfate, diluting and buffering the harsher detergent's interaction with skin proteins and lipids, which is the accepted explanation for why sulfate/CAPB blends foam well but strip the skin barrier less than sulfates alone.[^jacob] A cell-based mechanistic study supports this: sulfate surfactants activated the pain receptor TRPV1 in a dose-dependent manner in neuroblastoma cells, while cocamidopropyl betaine did not trigger this response, consistent with lower irritant/nociceptive potential.[^lindegren] The allergy risk works through a separate pathway: incomplete purification during manufacturing leaves residual amidoamine and unreacted DMAPA in commercial CAPB, and these impurities, not the finished betaine molecule itself, are considered the primary drivers of delayed-type (T-cell-mediated) hypersensitivity reactions.[^jacob]

## The Evidence

**Comparative irritancy and sensitization of eight common cleanser surfactants (patch testing, human).** Corazza et al. patch-tested eight surfactants spanning anionic, cationic, amphoteric, and non-ionic classes, including cocamidopropyl betaine, in 105 patients, scoring allergic reactions by International Contact Dermatitis Research Group criteria and irritant potential by an amended Draize classification. None of the eight surfactants produced measurable skin irritation, and none of the eight preparations tested produced allergic reactions; the only positive allergic reactions came from cocamidopropyl betaine as tested in the Italian SIDAPA standard series, in 5 of 105 patients. The two newest mild anionics tested (sodium cocoyl glutamate and sodium lauroyl oat amino acids) and an amphoteric agent (disodium cocoamphodiacetate) were rated "most tolerated."[^corazza] *CAPB did not irritate skin in this panel, but it was the only surfactant to elicit positive allergic reactions, a direct illustration of the mild-but-allergenic trade-off.*

**North American Contact Dermatitis Group registry analysis of skin-cleanser-associated contact dermatitis (retrospective, 32,945 patients, 2000-2014).** Warshaw et al. analyzed 15 years of NACDG patch-test data; of 32,945 tested patients, 1,069 (3.24%) had an allergic patch-test reaction or irritant contact dermatitis associated with skin cleansers. Among more than 50 allergens implicated, cocamidopropyl betaine accounted for 9.5% of cleanser-associated allergic reactions and its manufacturing impurity amidoamine for 5.5%, ranking among the most common cleanser allergens alongside preservatives (methylchloroisothiazolinone/methylisothiazolinone, quaternium-15) and fragrance.[^warshaw] *At population scale across a 15-year, multi-center registry, CAPB and its amidoamine impurity are consistently among the top handful of allergens found in reactions to skin cleansers.*

**TRPV1 activation by shampoo and soap surfactants (in vitro, mechanistic).** Lindegren et al. measured calcium influx through the pain receptor TRPV1 in stably transfected neuroblastoma (SH-SY5Y) cells exposed to low concentrations of common cleanser surfactants. Anionic linear surfactants (sodium lauryl sulfate, sodium laureth sulfate, ammonium lauryl sulfate, sodium C12-15 pareth sulfate, N-lauroylsarcosine) concentration-dependently activated TRPV1; cocamidopropyl betaine, along with cationic and non-ionic surfactants tested, did not produce a TRPV1-mediated response.[^lindegren] *A cell-based assay offers a plausible mechanistic reason sulfate-free or CAPB-containing formulas feel less stinging than straight-sulfate cleansers, though this is an in vitro nociception proxy, not a clinical irritation trial.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Gentle, sulfate-free-friendly surfactant."** Fair as far as it goes: comparative irritancy testing found no measurable irritation from CAPB, and a mechanistic assay found it does not activate the same pain-signaling pathway as sulfate surfactants.[^corazza][^lindegren] But "gentle" refers only to irritant (chemical-burn-type) potential, not allergenicity, and CAPB is not itself a sulfate-free claim ingredient; it is routinely blended with sulfates to soften them.
- **"Hypoallergenic."** Not supportable. CAPB is one of the most consistently identified allergens in cleanser-related contact dermatitis, with sensitization prevalence estimated at 3.0% to 7.2% and NACDG registry data putting it among the top cleanser allergens, which led to its being named Allergen of the Year in 2004.[^jacob][^warshaw]
