Delgocitinib is a prescription topical Janus kinase inhibitor, sold as Anzupgo cream, "indicated for the topical treatment of moderate to severe chronic hand eczema (CHE) in adults who have had an inadequate response to, or for whom topical corticosteroids are not advisable".[^dailymed] Each gram of cream "contains 20 mg of delgocitinib", which is 2% by weight, and it is FDA-approved under NDA219155 with a marketing start date of 24 July 2025.[^dailymed] The condition it treats is on [[chronic-hand-eczema]].

> **Bottom line.** A genuinely new option for a condition with few, and the first topical JAK inhibitor approved for it. Two things are worth knowing before the headlines carry you away. First, **most people in the trials did not get clear skin**: 20% and 29% reached clear or almost clear at 16 weeks across the two trials, against 10% and 7% on vehicle.[^dailymed] The stronger result is **symptom relief**, where roughly 47% hit a 4-point itch improvement and 49% a 4-point pain improvement, against 20% to 28% on vehicle.[^dailymed] Second, unlike oral JAK inhibitors, this label carries **no boxed warning and no contraindications**, but it does warn about infection and about non-melanoma skin cancers.[^dailymed] It is prescription-only, capped by dose, and for the hands and wrists specifically.

## The Rubric

**Clearing moderate to severe chronic hand eczema**
- **Effect size: modest, and that word is doing real work.** The primary endpoint was clear or almost clear skin with at least a 2-point improvement at week 16. Delgocitinib beat vehicle in both trials, by 10 percentage points (95% CI 4% to 16%) in Trial 1 and 22 points (95% CI 16% to 29%) in Trial 2.[^dailymed] But the absolute response rates were 20% and 29%, so roughly three out of four treated people did not reach that endpoint.
- **Evidence quality: gold-standard for the indication.** Two randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled 16-week trials enrolling 960 adults, plus FDA approval via a full NDA rather than a monograph or an abbreviated application.[^dailymed] That is the strongest regulatory and trial footing any ingredient on this site can have. The grade reflects the approval and the trial design, not a large effect.

**Reducing hand eczema itch and pain**
- **Effect size: notable, and better than the clearance figures suggest.** For itch, 47% of treated subjects achieved at least a 4-point improvement in both trials, versus 23% and 20% on vehicle; for pain, 49% in both trials, versus 28% and 23%.[^dailymed] Mean baseline itch and pain scores were 7.1 and 6.7 on 11-point scales, so these were symptomatic patients with room to improve.
- **Evidence quality: gold-standard, from the same two trials.** These were measured outcomes in the registration trials rather than post-hoc observations.[^dailymed] If you are judging this drug by whether your hands stop hurting and itching rather than by whether they look clear, the case is stronger.

## The Evidence

**What the trials were.** Efficacy rested on "two randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, 16-week trials (TRIAL 1 [NCT04871711] and TRIAL 2 [NCT04872101]) which enrolled a total of 960 adult subjects with moderate to severe CHE who had a history of inadequate response to, or for whom topical corticosteroids were not advisable".[^dailymed] Subjects applied cream or vehicle twice daily to hands and wrists. Enrolled patients had moderate or severe disease by investigator assessment and a weekly average itch score of at least 4.[^dailymed]

**The two trials do not agree on magnitude, and the label does not hide it.** The vehicle-adjusted difference on the primary endpoint was 10 points in one trial and 22 in the other.[^dailymed] Part of that is the vehicle arm, which responded at 10% in Trial 1 and 7% in Trial 2, and part is the treated arm, at 20% and 29%. Two trials of the same drug landing that far apart is a reason to expect the real-world effect somewhere in between rather than at the better number.

**Who was studied.** Mean age 44.1 years, 64% female, 90% White, 4% Asian, 1% Black.[^dailymed] Hand eczema is not one disease, and the trials reflect that: subtypes were atopic hand eczema (35.9%), hyperkeratotic eczema (21.5%), irritant contact dermatitis (19.6%), allergic contact dermatitis (13.9%), vesicular hand eczema (9.1%), and contact urticaria or protein contact dermatitis (0.1%), with 28% of subjects carrying two or more overlapping subtypes.[^dailymed] The trials were not powered to tell you which subtype responds best.

**How it is thought to work, and the honest gap.** The label states delgocitinib "inhibits the activity of JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2)", which is what makes it a pan-JAK inhibitor rather than a selective one.[^dailymed] It then says plainly: "The exact mechanism of action of delgocitinib in the treatment of moderate to severe CHE is currently not known."[^dailymed] A mechanism story is not the same as a mechanism.

**Where it ranks against the alternatives.** A 2025 network meta-analysis of chronic hand eczema monotherapies ranked topical delgocitinib the most efficacious; that comparison, including its limitations, is on [[gupta-2025-hand-eczema-monotherapies-network-meta-analysis]] and is not repeated here. The comparators it is positioned against are on [[topical-corticosteroids]] and [[tacrolimus-topical]].

## Safety and Practical Limits

**No boxed warning, and no listed contraindications.** This matters because oral JAK inhibitors carry boxed warnings, and that association travels. The Anzupgo label's contraindications section reads "None".[^dailymed] Systemic JAK class warnings about mortality, major cardiovascular events, and thrombosis do not appear on it.

**It does carry two real warnings.** On infection: "ANZUPGO may increase the risk of infection. Eczema herpeticum was observed in a subject treated topically with ANZUPGO."[^dailymed] On skin cancer: "Non-melanoma skin cancers including basal cell carcinoma have been reported in subjects treated with ANZUPGO. Periodic skin examinations are recommended for all patients, particularly those with risk factors for skin cancer."[^dailymed] The label also directs that immunizations, including herpes zoster vaccination, be completed before starting.[^dailymed]

**Reported adverse reactions were uncommon but specific.** Those "reported in ≤ 1% of subjects were application site pain, paresthesia, pruritus, erythema, and bacterial skin infections including finger cellulitis, paronychia, other skin infections, leukopenia, and neutropenia".[^dailymed]

**Dose and site are capped.** "Do not use more than 30 grams per 2 weeks or 60 grams per month", and "Apply twice daily to skin of the affected areas only on the hands and wrists".[^dailymed] It is "For topical use only. Not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use."[^dailymed] Combining it "with other JAK inhibitors or potent immunosuppressants is not recommended".[^dailymed]

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. Delgocitinib is prescription-only, its indication is specifically moderate to severe chronic hand eczema after corticosteroids have failed or been ruled out, and nothing here is a reason to seek it for general dryness or for eczema elsewhere on the body.
