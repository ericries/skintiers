Dimethicone (INCI Dimethicone; polydimethylsiloxane) is a silicone polymer used in moisturizers as an occlusive and emollient. StatPearls lists it among the silicone occlusives, agents that "form a physical barrier over the skin that reduces water evaporation," and describes emollients as ingredients that "help to smooth and soften the skin by filling in gaps between skin cells."[^1] A dermatology review of moisturizers calls it "the second most common active agent in moisturizers today, following [[petrolatum]]."[^2]

## The Rubric

Grades below are relative and name comparators. They cover dimethicone's documented cosmetic function, not proof that any finished product performs.

**Occlusion and emollient feel**
- **Effect size: modest to notable (as a formulation agent).** Dimethicone is an occlusive and emollient with, per Kang and colleagues, "a moisturizing effect as well as a softening effect."[^2] Its practical advantage over [[petrolatum]] is cosmetic: the same review notes silicone has "good spreadability, is not sticky, induces fewer comedones and allergies, and has no irritating scent."[^2] The occlusive effect itself is the general benefit shared by moisturizers as a class; see [[moisturizing]].
- **Evidence quality: solid for classification, category-level for effect.** Its occlusive/emollient classification is stated in two authoritative dermatology references.[^1][^2] I found no controlled trial isolating dimethicone against another emollient, so the moisturizing benefit is inherited from the moisturizer category rather than shown for dimethicone specifically.

**Tolerability**
- **Effect size: notable (low irritancy).** Kang and colleagues describe dimethicone as "hypoallergenic, noncomedogenic and nonacnegenic."[^2]
- **Evidence quality: solid (review-level).** Stated in a peer-reviewed dermatology review of moisturizers,[^2] consistent with its long regulatory and clinical use.

## The Evidence

Dimethicone's role in skincare is functional rather than active. StatPearls classifies it as an occlusive silicone and gives the working definitions: occlusives "form a physical barrier over the skin that reduces water evaporation," and emollients "help to smooth and soften the skin by filling in gaps between skin cells."[^1] A review of moisturizers in inflammatory skin disease by Kang and colleagues states that "Dimethicone is the second most common active agent in moisturizers today, following petrolatum, due to its hypoallergenic, noncomedogenic and nonacnegenic properties," and that it has "a moisturizing effect as well as a softening effect."[^2] The general question of whether moisturizing helps, and how, belongs to [[moisturizing]]; the evidence above is for dimethicone's classification and tolerability, not for any effect beyond occlusion and emollience.

## Common Marketing Claims

Treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Silicones suffocate the skin, trap dirt, or cause breakouts."** The moisturizer review by Kang and colleagues describes dimethicone as "noncomedogenic and nonacnegenic" and notes silicones induce "fewer comedones and allergies."[^2] The "clogs pores / causes acne" framing is not supported by that source. Discounted.
