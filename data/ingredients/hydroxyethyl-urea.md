Hydroxyethyl urea (CAS 1320-51-0) is a small, water-soluble urea derivative formulated into moisturizers, cleansers, and hair-care products as a humectant and skin- and hair-conditioning agent.[^cir][^pubchem] It is a distinct molecule from urea itself and from the formaldehyde-releasing preservatives diazolidinyl urea and imidazolidinyl urea, despite the shared "urea" name; none of the identified literature on hydroxyethyl urea flags it as a formaldehyde source.[^cir] It is sold under the trade name Hydrovance by manufacturer Nouryon, which describes it as a "moisturizing agent" that "provides excellent moisturization, increases skin elasticity, and enhances tactile sensory properties of formulations" with a "non-tacky, non-greasy aesthetic," a formulator-facing marketing claim rather than an independently tested result.[^nouryon]

> The US Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) Expert Panel assessed hydroxyethyl urea in 2024 and concluded it "is safe in cosmetics in the present practices of use and concentration described in the safety assessment when formulated to be non-irritating," a regulatory safety finding rather than an efficacy claim.[^cir] No independent, isolated-ingredient efficacy trial of hydroxyethyl urea as a humectant was identified; the only clinical data located tested it as one of several ingredients in a brand-sponsored multi-active body lotion, which cannot isolate its individual contribution.[^cureus]

## The Rubric

**Regulatory safety status**
Well-established. The CIR Expert Panel, an industry-funded but independent scientific body that reviews cosmetic ingredient safety data, published its assessment in the International Journal of Toxicology, describing hydroxyethyl urea as "reported to function as a humectant and a hair and skin conditioning agent" and concluding it is "safe in cosmetics in the present practices of use and concentration described in the safety assessment when formulated to be non-irritating."[^cir] This establishes only that the ingredient is considered safe as formulated in current products, not that it delivers a specific benefit.

**Humectant/moisturizing efficacy (isolated ingredient)**
Effect size: not established by independent controlled data. The only human clinical study found that included hydroxyethyl urea tested it as one ingredient among spirulina, an alpha-hydroxy acid, Madhuca indica oil, and allantoin in a two-product kit (an exfoliating scrub plus a body lotion) manufactured and studied by the brand itself, with the study team's affiliations including the manufacturer.[^cureus] That design cannot attribute the reported hydration, redness, or texture changes to hydroxyethyl urea specifically. The manufacturer of the raw ingredient describes it, in its own marketing material, as providing "excellent moisturization" and a "non-tacky, non-greasy aesthetic," but this is an uncorroborated supplier claim, not an independently verified result.[^nouryon]

Evidence quality: thin. Hydroxyethyl urea has an independently reviewed safety record and formulation use as a supporting humectant, but no identified controlled trial isolates its own contribution to hydration or skin conditioning apart from a brand-affiliated multi-ingredient product study. See [[moisturizing]] for the general evidence on humectants and barrier-support ingredients.

## The Evidence

**CIR safety assessment.** The Expert Panel for Cosmetic Ingredient Safety reviewed available toxicology and use data and published its conclusion that hydroxyethyl urea "is safe in cosmetics in the present practices of use and concentration described in the safety assessment when formulated to be non-irritating."[^cir] The published abstract does not report a specific concentration-of-use range or an efficacy endpoint; it is a safety, not a performance, finding.

**Brand-sponsored multi-ingredient study.** A 2025 open-label study of the ThriveCo Bumps Eraser Kit, an exfoliating scrub and body lotion for keratosis pilaris, reported that skin hydration measured by Corneometer increased 131.75% (p<0.0001) over 45 days, alongside reductions in redness, roughness, and itchiness.[^cureus] The lotion's ingredient list included hydroxyethyl urea alongside spirulina, an alpha-hydroxy acid, Madhuca indica oil, and allantoin; the study authors include employees of Anveya Living, the kit's manufacturer, and the trial was not designed to isolate any single ingredient's effect.[^cureus] This is evidence about the finished product, not about hydroxyethyl urea.

**Distinguishing from other "urea" preservatives.** Hydroxyethyl urea is chemically and functionally separate from diazolidinyl urea and imidazolidinyl urea, preservatives that can release formaldehyde and are tracked separately as contact-allergy candidates in patch-test registries; a 2021 Spanish contact dermatitis registry study evaluating those two preservative ureas did not include hydroxyethyl urea.[^geidac] Readers should not infer allergy or formaldehyde-release concerns about hydroxyethyl urea from data on the differently-named preservative ureas.

## Common Marketing Claims

None of the claims below is established beyond what The Evidence above supports.

- **"Clinically proven to hydrate skin."** The one clinical hydration result identified tested a two-step body-care kit containing five different active ingredients, not hydroxyethyl urea alone, and was conducted with the product manufacturer's own staff among the authors.[^cureus]
