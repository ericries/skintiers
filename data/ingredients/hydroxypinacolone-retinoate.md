Hydroxypinacolone Retinoate (INCI Hydroxypinacolone Retinoate; also called HPR) is a synthetic ester of all-trans retinoic acid ([[tretinoin]]) used in cosmetics as a [[retinoids|retinoid]] alternative to [[retinol]]. The commercial raw material is sold by Grant Industries under the trade name Granactive Retinoid, an anhydrous blend of 10% HPR in 90% dimethyl isosorbide, so a label that reads "2% Granactive Retinoid" contains about 0.2% actual HPR (and 1.8% solvent), and "5% Granactive Retinoid" about 0.5% HPR. The Ordinary uses it in its Granactive Retinoid 2% Emulsion and Granactive Retinoid 5% in Squalane.

## The Rubric

Grades below are relative and name comparators. HPR's efficacy has been studied mainly in vitro and inside multi-ingredient formulas, not as an isolated ingredient in controlled human trials, so these grades describe a preliminary evidence base rather than a proven effect.

**Antiaging (photoaging, wrinkles)**
- **Effect size: unresolvable for HPR in isolation.** In a human foreskin fibroblast model, Wang and colleagues report that HPR combined with retinyl propionate at a 5:9 weight ratio "synergistically enhanced the expressions of type IV collagen, CRBP-I, and RARB genes,"[^1] a result consistent with retinoid-receptor activity. No controlled human trial isolates HPR's antiaging effect from the other actives it is formulated with, so its standalone effect cannot be graded against established retinoids such as [[tretinoin]] or [[retinol]].
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** The supporting work is in-vitro skin-model and cell data,[^1] part of it from cosmetic-industry-affiliated authors,[^1] and the human evidence tests HPR only within combination products.[^2][^3]

**Acne**
- **Effect size: unresolvable for HPR in isolation.** Bettoli and colleagues studied a fixed combination of HPR and retinol glycospheres as maintenance therapy after oral isotretinoin in 40 patients, reporting relapse in 6 of the 39 who completed (15.38%) over 12 months.[^2] The retinol component and the encapsulation technology are part of that formula, so the outcome cannot be attributed to HPR alone.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** Small, single-arm, combination-product data;[^2] no HPR-only randomized trial was located.

## The Evidence

HPR is a [[retinoids|retinoid]], one of the vitamin A derivatives that act on nuclear retinoic acid receptors. The general retinoid mechanism and its documented skin effects belong to [[retinoids]]. What sets HPR apart is chemical: it is an ester of all-trans retinoic acid ([[tretinoin]]) rather than an alcohol like [[retinol]], and its manufacturer states it acts on retinoid receptors without the retinol-to-retinaldehyde-to-retinoic-acid conversion that retinol undergoes in skin (see Common Marketing Claims).

The independent efficacy record is thin. In an in-vitro human fibroblast model, Wang and colleagues found that HPR and retinyl propionate together "synergistically enhanced the expressions of type IV collagen, CRBP-I, and RARB genes."[^1]
> Bottom line: in a cell model, an HPR-plus-retinyl-propionate mixture raised the expression of collagen and retinoid-pathway genes. That is laboratory evidence, not a clinical outcome, and it does not isolate HPR.

The human data comes from multi-ingredient formulas. Bettoli and colleagues treated 40 post-isotretinoin acne patients with a fixed combination of HPR and retinol glycospheres for 12 months and reported relapse in 6 of the 39 completers (15.38%), with no adverse events reported.[^2] A separate study compared a topical HPR-peptide product against a fractional CO2 laser for facial aging.[^3]
> Bottom line: these human studies pair HPR with retinol, peptides, or other actives, so they measure the formula, not HPR by itself.

Every published human study located tests HPR inside such a combination; no controlled human trial of HPR in isolation was found, and the antiaging evidence for HPR specifically is in-vitro skin-model work.[^1] On the current record, its standalone efficacy relative to established retinoids is unproven.

## Common Marketing Claims

Treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Binds retinoid receptors directly, with no conversion needed."** Grant Industries and retail descriptions present HPR as an ester of retinoic acid that acts on receptors without the retinol-to-retinaldehyde-to-retinoic-acid conversion step. In-vitro data are consistent with retinoid-receptor activity,[^1] but the "direct binding, no conversion" mechanism as a clinical advantage rests on manufacturer material rather than independent human evidence. The ingredient reference INCIDecoder, describing the same molecule, states that HPR is "not fully proven yet" (https://incidecoder.com/ingredients/hydroxypinacolone-retinoate). Discounted to the in-vitro evidence.
- **"The power of retinol without the irritation."** The claim that HPR matches retinoid efficacy while being gentler than [[retinol]] originates with the manufacturer. No independent head-to-head human trial isolating HPR against retinol was located, so the comparative-tolerance claim is unverified. Discounted.
