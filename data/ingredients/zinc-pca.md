Zinc PCA (zinc l-pyrrolidone carboxylate, INCI "Zinc PCA") is the zinc salt of pyrrolidone carboxylic acid, a component of skin's natural moisturizing factor, and it is formulated as a water-soluble humectant and astringent in moisturizers and oil-control products aimed at oily and acne-prone skin.[^pirot][^takino] Its cosmetic-chemistry rationale (humectancy, astringency, delivering zinc into skin) rests on ex vivo skin-penetration and in vitro cell-culture data rather than on human clinical trials of the ingredient itself.

> Zinc PCA has a plausible delivery mechanism, an ex vivo study found it penetrates human skin better than zinc oxide or zinc sulfate, and a manufacturer-funded in vitro study found it curbs UVA-driven collagen breakdown in cultured fibroblasts.[^pirot][^takino] Neither is a human trial, and no clinical study of Zinc PCA itself for its marketed uses (sebum control, oil-prone or acne-prone skin) was found, so the sebum-regulating and mattifying claims common on product labels are unverified.

## The Rubric

**Skin delivery of zinc (mechanism, not an outcome)**
- **Effect size: notable in the narrow sense measured.** In an ex vivo study on excised human skin, zinc from a Zinc PCA-containing emulsion showed higher percutaneous absorption than zinc from zinc-oxide or zinc-sulfate ointments over 72 hours (1.60% of the applied dose absorbed versus 0.36% and 0.34%).[^pirot] This measures how much zinc gets into skin, not any downstream skin benefit.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** A single ex vivo (excised human skin, in vitro diffusion cell) academic study comparing five formulations; it does not test a clinical endpoint.[^pirot]

**Photoaging and collagen support (cell-culture only)**
- **Effect size: unclear translation to real skin.** In cultured normal human dermal fibroblasts, Zinc PCA suppressed UVA-induced activator protein-1 (AP-1) activation and reduced production of matrix metalloproteinase-1, the collagen-degrading enzyme implicated in photoaging, and separately increased expression of the ascorbic-acid transporter SVCT2, leading to more type I collagen production by the cells.[^takino] This is isolated-cell biology, not a measured effect on human skin firmness or wrinkling, and it sits well below the demonstrated skin biology behind [[retinoids]].
- **Evidence quality: preliminary and manufacturer-funded.** A single in vitro cell-culture study from Ajinomoto, a manufacturer of Zinc PCA as a cosmetic raw material.[^takino]

**Sebum regulation and oily/acne-prone skin (the most common marketing use)**
- **Effect size: unresolvable.** No clinical or in vitro study measuring Zinc PCA's effect on sebum production, pore appearance, or acne was found on PubMed. The astringent and antimicrobial reputation this ingredient carries in cosmetic formulation is a traditional cosmetic-chemistry rationale (zinc salts are astringent), not a tested clinical claim for this specific compound.
- **Evidence quality: absent.** Leave it out rather than grade it; see the general zinc ointment evidence on acne if it exists as a separate topic.

## What We Actually Know

Zinc PCA is well characterized as a delivery form: an ex vivo human-skin study found it puts more zinc into skin than zinc oxide or zinc sulfate do, 1.60% of the applied dose absorbed after 72 hours versus 0.36% and 0.34%.[^pirot] What that delivered zinc then does in living human skin is mostly untested for this ingredient specifically. The only mechanistic efficacy data are a single manufacturer-funded cell-culture study, in which Zinc PCA blunted UVA-driven MMP-1 production and boosted collagen synthesis in isolated fibroblasts.[^takino] The claim that appears most often on product labels, sebum regulation for oily or acne-prone skin, has no clinical or laboratory study behind it that could be located.

## How It Works

Pyrrolidone carboxylic acid (PCA) is itself one of the components of skin's natural moisturizing factor, the mix of small hygroscopic molecules in the stratum corneum that helps hold water; Zinc PCA pairs that humectant with zinc, a metal traditionally used in astringent formulations. In the Pirot ex vivo study, zinc from the PCA salt penetrated excised human skin more readily than zinc from zinc oxide or zinc sulfate formulations, which the authors attribute to the physicochemical properties of the different zinc salts and their vehicles rather than to a shared "zinc effect".[^pirot] In the Takino cell-culture study, Zinc PCA acted at two points: it suppressed activation of the transcription factor AP-1, which in turn lowered production of matrix metalloproteinase-1 (the main collagen-degrading enzyme induced by UVA), and it separately increased expression of the vitamin C transporter SVCT2 in fibroblasts, which raised the cells' output of type I collagen.[^takino] Both findings are cell-culture observations from a single manufacturer-run study and have not been confirmed in human skin.

## The Evidence

Pirot F, Millet J, Kalia YN, Humbert P (Skin Pharmacology, 1996) compared percutaneous absorption and skin bioavailability of zinc and copper from five topical formulations, including a zinc 2-pyrrolidone-5-carboxylate (Zinc PCA) emulsion, zinc oxide ointment, and zinc sulfate ointment, applied to excised human skin in vitro for 72 hours.[^pirot] *After 72 hours, percutaneous absorption of zinc was 1.60% of the applied dose from the Zinc PCA emulsion versus 0.36% (zinc oxide) and 0.34% (zinc sulfate); all formulations raised zinc levels in skin and epidermis.*

Takino Y, Okura F, Kitazawa M, Iwasaki K, Tagami H (International Journal of Cosmetic Science, 2012, Ajinomoto-funded) treated cultured normal human dermal fibroblasts with Zinc PCA and UVA irradiation.[^takino] *Zinc PCA suppressed UVA-induced AP-1 activation and matrix metalloproteinase-1 production, and separately increased SVCT2 expression and type I collagen output, in isolated skin cells; the authors call it "a promising candidate for an anti-skin ageing agent" based on this in vitro work.*

No clinical trial of Zinc PCA for sebum control, acne, or any in-use human skin outcome was found on PubMed as of this analysis.

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Sebum-regulating" or "mattifying" for oily skin.** The most common claim on labels for Zinc PCA-containing products (often paired with niacinamide, as in [[the-ordinary-niacinamide-10-zinc-1]]); no clinical or laboratory study measuring Zinc PCA's effect on sebum was found.
- **"Anti-aging" or "boosts collagen."** Rests on one manufacturer-funded, cell-culture-only study, not a demonstrated effect on human skin.[^takino]
