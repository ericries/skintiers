Pimecrolimus cream 1% (Elidel) is a prescription-only topical calcineurin inhibitor approved as second-line therapy for mild to moderate [[atopic-dermatitis]] in patients 2 and older who have not responded to, or cannot tolerate, other topical treatments.[^fdalabel] It is milder-targeted than its sister drug [[tacrolimus-topical]], which is approved for moderate to severe disease, and a head-to-head trial found it less effective; it carries the same FDA boxed warning over a malignancy signal.

> Vehicle-controlled trials show pimecrolimus reduces atopic dermatitis flares and cuts the amount of topical corticosteroid needed over a year of use, and a 6-week trial found significant benefit over vehicle by the first assessment at day 8.[^eichenfield2002][^wahn2002] A three-trial, 1,065-patient head-to-head against tacrolimus ointment found tacrolimus significantly more effective and faster-acting, with similar overall safety.[^paller2005] The FDA's boxed warning over rare malignancy reports remains on the label; the largest postmarketing follow-up to date, a registry of 7,457 children followed for 26,792 person-years, found no statistically significant excess of malignancy overall, though the lymphoma point estimate was elevated with a wide confidence interval.[^fdalabel][^margolis2015]

## The Rubric

Pimecrolimus has one real use, steroid-sparing treatment of mild to moderate atopic dermatitis, and the same boxed-warning question that shadows [[tacrolimus-topical]].

**Mild to moderate atopic dermatitis, second-line steroid-sparing therapy (1% cream, ages 2+)**
- **Effect size: modest to notable, and weaker than tacrolimus ointment.** In two identically designed 6-week, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials pooling 403 children and adolescents with predominantly moderate atopic dermatitis, "significant therapeutic benefits relative to vehicle were observed in the pimecrolimus-treated group at the first efficacy assessment, 8 days after initial application," with pimecrolimus-treated patients showing significant improvement over vehicle across all efficacy measures; the authors concluded pimecrolimus cream 1% "appears to be a safe and effective alternative to currently used therapies for AD."[^eichenfield2002] A separate 1-year, double-blind, controlled trial randomized 713 children (ages 2 to 17) 2:1 to a pimecrolimus-based early-intervention regimen or a vehicle-then-reactive-corticosteroid regimen; the proportion completing the period with no flares was about twice as high with pimecrolimus (61.0% vs 34.2% at 6 months; 50.8% vs 28.3% at 12 months), and fewer pimecrolimus patients needed topical corticosteroid therapy (35.0% vs 62.9% at 6 months; 42.6% vs 68.4% at 12 months).[^wahn2002] Against its sister drug, a pooled analysis of three 6-week, investigator-blinded, randomized trials (1,065 patients) found tacrolimus ointment significantly more effective than pimecrolimus cream at study end in adults (54.1% vs 34.9% treatment success, P<.0001) and in children with moderate to severe disease (67.2% vs 56.4%, P=.04), with a faster onset of action; the authors concluded "tacrolimus ointment is more effective and has a faster onset of action than pimecrolimus cream in adults and children with AD; their safety profiles are similar."[^paller2005] "Modest to notable: pimecrolimus beats vehicle on flare prevention and steroid-sparing over a year, but a head-to-head trial found the mid-potency-steroid-matching [[tacrolimus-topical]] outperforms it and works faster, at similar safety."
- **Evidence quality: solid.** Multiple randomized, double-blind, vehicle- or active-controlled trials, including a 1-year flare-prevention design and a large three-trial head-to-head, plus FDA approval restricting the indication to mild-to-moderate disease and age 2 and up.[^eichenfield2002][^wahn2002][^paller2005][^fdalabel]

**The FDA boxed warning (malignancy signal)**
- **Signal strength: not confirmed as statistically significant in the largest follow-up so far, but not fully closed either.** The label carries a boxed warning stating that "although a causal relationship has not been established, rare cases of malignancy (e.g., skin and lymphoma) have been reported in patients treated with topical calcineurin inhibitors, including Pimecrolimus Cream, 1%," and on that basis instructs that continuous long-term use in any age group be avoided, restricts application to the areas of active atopic dermatitis, and bars use in children under 2.[^fdalabel] The PEER registry (the PEER registry was an FDA-required postmarketing study with a manufacturer-affiliated co-author), a longitudinal cohort of 7,457 children with a history of atopic dermatitis and pimecrolimus use followed for 26,792 person-years, recorded five malignancies (two leukemias, one osteosarcoma, two lymphomas, no skin cancers) against expected rates from the SEER cancer registry: an overall standardized incidence ratio of 1.2 (95% CI 0.5-2.8), a lymphoma SIR of 2.9 (95% CI 0.7-11.7), and a leukemia SIR of 2.0 (95% CI 0.5-8.2), none statistically significant; the authors concluded that "based on more than 25,000 person-years of follow-up, it seems unlikely that topical pimecrolimus as it was used in the PEER cohort to treat atopic dermatitis is associated with an increased risk of malignancy."[^margolis2015] The cohort was observational, not placebo-controlled, only 5 malignancies total were observed, and the lymphoma confidence interval (0.7 to 11.7) does not rule out a meaningfully elevated risk; it narrows but does not close the question the label still warns about.[^margolis2015]

## The Evidence

**Pimecrolimus versus vehicle, short term.** Two identically designed 6-week, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials pooled 403 children and adolescents with predominantly moderate atopic dermatitis. "Significant therapeutic benefits relative to vehicle were observed in the pimecrolimus-treated group at the first efficacy assessment, 8 days after initial application," and pimecrolimus-treated patients showed significant improvement relative to vehicle across all efficacy measures assessed; the trial was reported as well tolerated. The authors concluded pimecrolimus cream 1% "appears to be a safe and effective alternative to currently used therapies for AD."[^eichenfield2002]

**Pimecrolimus versus vehicle-then-reactive-steroid, long term (flare prevention).** A 1-year, controlled, double-blind trial randomized 713 children (ages 2 to 17) with atopic dermatitis, 2:1, to early treatment of the first signs and symptoms with pimecrolimus or with vehicle (both groups could add a moderately potent topical corticosteroid reactively if a flare occurred, and both used emollients). The proportion of patients who completed 6 or 12 months with no flares was roughly twice as high with pimecrolimus (61.0% vs 34.2% at 6 months; 50.8% vs 28.3% at 12 months), and fewer pimecrolimus patients required topical corticosteroid therapy (35.0% vs 62.9% at 6 months; 42.6% vs 68.4% at 12 months). Suspected drug-related adverse events were not significantly different between groups (24.7% vs 18.7%), serious adverse events were 8.3% vs 5.2%, application-site burning was 10.5% vs 9.3%, and grouped viral skin infections ran somewhat higher with pimecrolimus (12.4% vs 6.3%).[^wahn2002]

**Pimecrolimus versus tacrolimus, head-to-head.** Three multicenter, randomized, investigator-blinded, 6-week trials randomized 1,065 patients (adults and children) to tacrolimus ointment or pimecrolimus cream. Tacrolimus was significantly more effective by treatment-success rate at study end in adults (54.1% vs 34.9%, P<.0001), in the combined analysis (52.8% vs 39.1%, P<.0001), and in children with moderate to severe disease (67.2% vs 56.4%, P=.04); tacrolimus also showed a significantly higher success rate in children with mild disease as early as week 1 (39.2% vs 31.2%, P=.04) and a faster onset of action overall. In the pediatric studies (650 children), the incidence of adverse events did not differ significantly between drugs; adults on tacrolimus had more day-1 application-site reactions, with comparable rates thereafter. The authors concluded: "Tacrolimus ointment is more effective and has a faster onset of action than pimecrolimus cream in adults and children with AD; their safety profiles are similar."[^paller2005]

**The boxed warning and its evidentiary basis.** The FDA label states that "although a causal relationship has not been established, rare cases of malignancy (e.g., skin and lymphoma) have been reported in patients treated with topical calcineurin inhibitors, including Pimecrolimus Cream, 1%," restricts continuous long-term use in any age group, and states the product "is not indicated for use in children less than 2 years of age."[^fdalabel] The PEER cohort study (the PEER registry was an FDA-required postmarketing study with a manufacturer-affiliated co-author) followed 7,457 children with a history of atopic dermatitis and pimecrolimus use for 26,792 person-years and compared observed malignancies with SEER-based expected rates. Five malignancies were recorded (two leukemias, one osteosarcoma, two lymphomas, no skin cancers): an overall standardized incidence ratio of 1.2 (95% CI 0.5-2.8), a lymphoma SIR of 2.9 (95% CI 0.7-11.7), and a leukemia SIR of 2.0 (95% CI 0.5-8.2). The authors concluded that "based on more than 25,000 person-years of follow-up, it seems unlikely that topical pimecrolimus as it was used in the PEER cohort to treat atopic dermatitis is associated with an increased risk of malignancy."[^margolis2015] In infants 3 to 23 months old (below the approved age of 2, studied for label purposes), the labeled clinical trial data show higher rates on pimecrolimus than vehicle for pyrexia (32% vs 13%), upper respiratory infection (24% vs 14%), nasopharyngitis (15% vs 8%), gastroenteritis (7% vs 3%), and diarrhea (8% vs 0%), and the label notes children have higher detectable blood levels of the drug than adults over comparable treatment periods.[^fdalabel]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"A gentler, steroid-free alternative with no real downside."** Pimecrolimus avoids the corticosteroid skin-thinning risk, but a large head-to-head trial found it significantly less effective and slower-acting than [[tacrolimus-topical]] at similar overall safety, and the FDA label still carries a boxed warning restricting continuous long-term use.[^paller2005][^fdalabel]
