Zinc pyrithione (pyrithione zinc) is the classic Head & Shoulders anti-dandruff active, an FDA over-the-counter monograph ingredient recognized at 0.3 to 2 percent in wash-off products for controlling dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis.[^fda] It is antifungal and antimicrobial against the Malassezia yeast behind both.

> Zinc pyrithione is a long-established, well-tolerated antidandruff antifungal, the original mass-market one. It reliably controls dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis, but on this site it sits a notch below ketoconazole and selenium sulfide because its recent head-to-head trial evidence is thinner than theirs, which is why it is graded moderate on [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]].

## The Rubric

**Dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis (the Malassezia-driven scaling)**
- **Effect size: moderate and reliable, but a step below the leaders.** Zinc pyrithione reduces Malassezia and controls the flaking and itch of dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis. It is a genuine, effective first-line-adjacent option used the same way as the other antifungals, but the strongest modern comparative data on this site favor ketoconazole and selenium sulfide, so it ranks moderate on [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]].
- **Evidence quality: FDA monograph and long use, thinner recent head-to-heads.** Its standing rests on OTC-monograph recognition at 0.3 to 2 percent (wash-off) and decades of use, more than on recent randomized comparisons against the reference antifungals.[^fda] It is established and safe, just less sharply quantified here than its peers.

**Practical notes (not an efficacy grade)**
- Well tolerated and low-odor compared with selenium sulfide or coal tar, which is part of why it dominates the mass market. Used as a wash a few times a week.

## The Evidence

Zinc pyrithione is an FDA OTC-monograph anti-dandruff active at 0.3 to 2 percent when formulated to be applied and washed off after brief exposure.[^fda] Mechanistically it is antifungal and antimicrobial against Malassezia, the same target as ketoconazole and selenium sulfide, and it is grouped with them as a topical antifungal on [[seborrheic-dermatitis]]. Where it differs on this site is the depth of recent comparative evidence: ketoconazole has a Cochrane meta-analysis and selenium sulfide has a modern head-to-head against ketoconazole, while zinc pyrithione's ranking here rests more on monograph status and long use, so it is graded moderate rather than strong.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Clinically proven anti-dandruff."** Supported at the monograph-active level for dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis; the active is well established, even if this site holds less recent head-to-head data for it than for ketoconazole or selenium sulfide.[^fda]
- **Fungal acne use.** Plausible on mechanism (antifungal against Malassezia) but extrapolation; the [[fungal-acne]] evidence centers on azole antifungals and selenium sulfide, not pyrithione zinc.
