Clascoterone is a topical androgen receptor inhibitor and the active in Winlevi 1% cream, a prescription-only acne drug the FDA first approved in August 2020.[^dhillon][^fda] Chemically it is cortexolone-17alpha propionate, a molecule with the same four-ring steroid backbone as dihydrotestosterone (DHT) and spironolactone.[^kircik][^fda] A review in Expert Opinion on Pharmacotherapy describes it as "the first topical androgen antagonist developed to treat acne in both male and female patients and the first such agent to receive U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval for treatment of acne."[^kircik] Its evidence base is real but young: two identical foundational Phase 3 trials support the acne indication, and no long track record or head-to-head trial against the established topical retinoids exists yet.

## The Rubric

**Acne vulgaris (moderate to severe, facial)**
- **Effect size: modest.** In the two foundational trials, treatment-success rates on the Investigator's Global Assessment (IGA) scale were 18.4% and 20.3% with clascoterone versus 9.0% and 6.5% with vehicle (both P<.001), a roughly 10 to 14 percentage-point improvement over vehicle.[^hebert] That places it in the same modest band as the other FDA-approved topical acne actives graded on [[acne]] rather than above them; this profile has no head-to-head trial putting clascoterone directly against [[tretinoin]], [[adapalene]], or [[azelaic-acid]]. "Modest: a real, statistically significant improvement over vehicle, in the same range as other topical acne actives, without a comparative trial to place it above or below the established retinoids."
- **Evidence quality: solid but new.** Two identical, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled Phase 3 trials (1,440 patients) and FDA approval both support the indication.[^hebert][^fda] It falls short of the older actives' evidence base: the 2024 American Academy of Dermatology guideline, built on a GRADE systematic review, gives clascoterone only a "conditional" recommendation, the same tier as salicylic acid and azelaic acid, while reserving "strong" recommendations for benzoyl peroxide, topical retinoids, topical antibiotics, and oral doxycycline.[^aad] A post hoc subgroup analysis of the same two foundational trials, detailed on [[libecco-2026-efficacy-and-safety-of-clascoterone-cream-1-for-acne-are-]], found the effect held across adolescents, adults, males, and females, but it re-slices the same original trial data rather than adding an independent replication.[^libecco]

## What We Actually Know

Clascoterone has replicated, vehicle-controlled evidence that it improves facial acne: in two identical Phase 3 trials, IGA treatment success was 18.4% and 20.3% with clascoterone versus 9.0% and 6.5% with vehicle (both P<.001), with parallel reductions in inflammatory and noninflammatory lesion counts.[^hebert] It is mechanistically distinct from every other FDA-approved topical acne drug: it blocks the androgen receptor directly in the skin rather than acting as a retinoid, an antimicrobial, or an antibiotic, which the FDA label states plainly ("WINLEVI (clascoterone) cream is an androgen receptor inhibitor indicated for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris").[^fda] Two honest limits sit alongside that novelty. First, the trials enrolled patients as young as 9, but the FDA-approved indication is narrower, patients 12 years of age and older.[^fda] Second, the drug is new enough that no controlled trial compares it directly with [[tretinoin]], [[adapalene]], or [[benzoyl-peroxide]], and the AAD guideline reflects that thinner record with a conditional rather than strong recommendation.[^aad]

## How It Works

Clascoterone works locally rather than systemically. The FDA label describes it structurally as "cortexolone-17α propionate" and functionally as "an androgen receptor inhibitor," while adding directly that "the mechanism of action of WINLEVI cream for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris is unknown."[^fda] A separate FDA-approval review fills in the working hypothesis: it shares a four-ring backbone with DHT and spironolactone,[^kircik] and "the drug is believed to compete with the androgen dihydrotestosterone for binding to androgen receptors in the sebaceous gland and hair follicles to attenuate signalling necessary for acne pathogenesis."[^dhillon] Androgens drive acne by stimulating sebaceous glands to grow and overproduce sebum and by contributing to follicular inflammation; a review in Expert Opinion on Pharmacotherapy states that "topically applied clascoterone binds androgen receptors (ARs) in the sebaceous glands and hair follicles, interfering with the pathogenesis of acne."[^kircik] This mechanism is what distinguishes it from oral anti-androgens such as spironolactone, covered on [[pcos]]: those act on androgen receptors throughout the body, whereas clascoterone is designed to act where it is applied. That local design is not the same as zero systemic exposure. The FDA label reports measurable systemic absorption and a small, reversible signal: in a separate pharmacokinetic substudy, transient suppression of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis (a post-stimulation cortisol level at or below 18 mcg/dL) was seen in 1 of 20 adult subjects (5%) and 2 of 22 adolescent subjects (9%) after 14 days of twice-daily use; "all subjects returned to normal HPA axis function at follow-up 4 weeks after the end of treatment."[^fda]

## The Evidence

**The foundational Phase 3 trials.** Hebert and colleagues ran two identical, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled Phase 3 trials (CB-03-01/25 and CB-03-01/26; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02608450 and NCT02608476) from November 2015 to April 2018, enrolling 1,440 males and nonpregnant females aged 9 to 58 with moderate or severe facial acne (30 to 75 inflammatory and 30 to 100 noninflammatory lesions), applying clascoterone cream 1% or vehicle twice daily for 12 weeks.[^hebert] At week 12, IGA treatment success (clear or almost clear, with at least a 2-grade improvement) was 18.4% versus 9.0% in the first trial and 20.3% versus 6.5% in the second (both P<.001).[^hebert] Noninflammatory lesion counts fell by -19.4 versus -13.0 and -19.4 versus -10.8, and inflammatory lesion counts fell by -19.3 versus -15.5 and -20.0 versus -12.6, all favoring clascoterone at P<.001 in both trials.[^hebert] The authors reported that "adverse events rates were low and mostly mild; the predominant local skin reaction was trace or mild erythema."[^hebert] The FDA label's own efficacy table, restricted to the approved 12-and-older population, reports closely matching numbers: IGA success of 18.8% versus 8.7% and 20.9% versus 6.6% across the two trials.[^fda]

**Regulatory status.** The FDA's own approval history states that "in August 2020, clascoterone cream 1% received its first approval in the USA for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in patients 12 years of age or older," the first approval anywhere for the drug.[^dhillon] The current label carries no active-comparator claim: its indication is limited to acne vulgaris in patients 12 and older, with safety and effectiveness "not been established in pediatric patients under 12 years of age."[^fda]

**Guideline placement.** The 2024 AAD acne guideline, built on a GRADE systematic review, states "conditional recommendations are made for topical clascoterone, salicylic acid, and azelaic acid, as well as for oral minocycline, sarecycline, combined oral contraceptive pills, and spironolactone," while "strong recommendations are made for benzoyl peroxide, topical retinoids, topical antibiotics, and oral doxycycline."[^aad] That gap in recommendation strength, not any negative finding about clascoterone, reflects the newer and thinner evidence base behind it relative to decades-old actives.

**Age and sex subgroups.** A post hoc analysis of the same two foundational trials, pooling 1,232 of the original patients and detailed on [[libecco-2026-efficacy-and-safety-of-clascoterone-cream-1-for-acne-are-]], found clascoterone significantly outperformed vehicle on IGA treatment success in every subgroup examined (adolescent, adult, male, and female), with adults showing larger lesion-count reductions than adolescents and no significant male-female difference.[^libecco]

## Uses

Well-supported: moderate to severe facial acne vulgaris in patients 12 and older, where clascoterone is FDA-approved on the strength of two identical, vehicle-controlled Phase 3 trials, with an effect size in the same modest range as the acne actives compared on [[acne]].[^hebert][^fda] Its distinguishing feature is mechanism rather than magnitude: as a locally acting androgen receptor inhibitor it can be used in both males and females, unlike oral anti-androgens (spironolactone, combined oral contraceptives) that are limited by systemic hormonal effects and are typically reserved for women, covered on [[pcos]].[^kircik] Because androgen signaling in the sebaceous gland is the mechanism most directly implicated in hormonal and adult-onset acne, that local-androgen-blocking action is mechanistically relevant to the hormonal acne pattern described on [[pcos]], though no clascoterone trial cited here specifically enrolled a PCOS population. Not established: any comparative advantage over [[tretinoin]], [[adapalene]], [[azelaic-acid]], or [[benzoyl-peroxide]], since no head-to-head trial exists, and any use in patients under 12, where safety and effectiveness have not been established.[^fda]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"A hormone-free way to treat hormonal acne."** Misleading framing. Clascoterone works by blocking androgen receptors, the same drug class mechanism as oral anti-androgens; it is topical and locally acting rather than systemic, not "hormone-free."[^fda][^kircik]
- **"No systemic effects."** Not fully supported. The FDA label reports measurable systemic absorption and a reversible HPA-axis suppression signal in a small subset of subjects in a pharmacokinetic substudy (1 of 20 adults, 2 of 22 adolescents), even though the drug is designed to act locally.[^fda]
- **"Proven better than tretinoin or adapalene for acne."** Not supported. No cited trial compares clascoterone directly against an established topical retinoid, and the AAD guideline places it in a conditional rather than strong recommendation tier.[^aad]
