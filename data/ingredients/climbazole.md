Climbazole (INCI name, chemically 1-(4-chlorophenoxy)-1-imidazol-1-yl-3,3-dimethylbutan-2-one, C15H17ClN2O2) is an imidazole antifungal used in leave-on and rinse-off antidandruff scalp products, typically at 0.2% to 1% and sometimes at 2%, often alongside [[piroctone-olamine]] or zinc pyrithione.[^pubchem][^wigger] Its anti-*Malassezia* activity is well documented in vitro and on hair/skin models, but the human clinical case rests almost entirely on old, uncontrolled, or combination-product studies, and the one large, industry-run randomized trial that pitted a climbazole combination shampoo against a competitor's optimized single-active formula found climbazole's combination the *loser*, not the winner.[^schwartz]

> In a randomized, double-blind trial of 620 subjects, a "Potentiated" zinc pyrithione shampoo produced greater reductions in scalp flaking and epidermal histamine than a "Dual Active" shampoo combining zinc pyrithione with climbazole, at all four weekly assessments.[^schwartz]

## The Rubric

**Dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis of the scalp (topical, leave-in contact via shampoo)**
- **Effect size: plausible but not well quantified for climbazole alone.** The only report using climbazole as a solo active in human subjects is a small, open, apparently uncontrolled German study: a 0.65% climbazole shampoo used for 4 weeks in 30 volunteers with moderate to severe seborrheic scalp dermatitis produced "a successful reduction of dandruff, skin redness and itching in 80% of the volunteers and a mild improvement in 20%."[^wigger] There was no placebo or active comparator arm reported in the abstract, so the size of any true drug effect above vehicle washing and moisturizing cannot be estimated from this data. Combination studies pairing climbazole with piroctone olamine or zinc pyrithione report clinical improvement, but by design none of them isolate climbazole's individual contribution.[^youn][^turner]
- **Evidence quality: weak, and thinner than [[ketoconazole]] or zinc pyrithione.** There is no identified vehicle-controlled or ketoconazole-controlled randomized trial of climbazole used alone. The strongest-designed human trial that includes climbazole is a 620-subject randomized, double-blind, industry (P&G) trial, and it is a negative result for climbazole: a competitor's zinc-pyrithione-only shampoo with enhanced bioavailability beat the zinc pyrithione plus climbazole "Dual Active" shampoo on both flaking and epidermal histamine at every weekly timepoint.[^schwartz] The Unilever-funded companion literature showing benefit for a zinc pyrithione/climbazole dual-active shampoo is industry data from the ingredient's other major user, and compares the combination to a non-antidandruff shampoo baseline rather than to climbazole alone.[^turner]

**Facial seborrheic dermatitis (topical, combination product)**
- **Effect size: unquantifiable for climbazole alone; modest for the combination.** A single-center, open-label, split-face study applied a climbazole/piroctone olamine cream to one side of the face and a plain emollient to the other in 24 patients with mild-to-moderate seborrheic dermatitis for 4 weeks; the treated side showed lower measured sebum and erythema than the emollient side at week 4.[^youn] The study was not blinded, had no climbazole-only or piroctone-olamine-only arm, and the comparator was a bare emollient rather than a vehicle cream, so climbazole's specific contribution versus piroctone olamine's is not separable.
- **Evidence quality: weak.** One small (n=24), open-label, unblinded, industry-adjacent trial with a non-vehicle comparator and a combination product; no independent replication identified.[^youn]

## How It Works

Climbazole is an imidazole-ring antifungal, chemically 1-(4-chlorophenoxy)-1-imidazol-1-yl-3,3-dimethylbutan-2-one, in the same structural family as [[ketoconazole]] and other azole antifungals; imidazoles act by inhibiting fungal cytochrome P450-dependent 14-alpha-demethylase, blocking ergosterol synthesis in the fungal cell membrane.[^pubchem] Dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis are strongly associated with overgrowth of *Malassezia* yeast species on the scalp, and climbazole shows direct in vitro activity against them: minimum inhibitory concentrations for a climbazole/piroctone olamine cream against *Malassezia restricta*, *M. globosa*, *M. sympodialis*, and *M. slooffiae* ranged from 0.625 to 5 mg/mL.[^youn] A skin-penetration study using Franz diffusion cells found that under typical antidandruff-shampoo use conditions, less than 1% of an applied 1% climbazole solution actually permeated across human or porcine skin, with most of the compound instead retained in the outer epidermis, which the authors framed as consistent with a topical, non-systemic mode of action.[^pazalvarez] A hair-strand assay (hairs incubated with shampoo, rinsed, then plated with *Malassezia*) found that among tested actives, "1% climbazole proved to be particularly effective" at leaving an antifungal residue on hair, the proposed mechanism for a between-washes depot effect.[^mayser]

## The Evidence

**Climbazole 0.65% shampoo, uncontrolled, human seborrheic scalp dermatitis (topical).** An open study in 30 volunteers with moderate to severe seborrheic dermatitis of the scalp applied a 0.65% climbazole shampoo for 4 weeks after a 1-week washout; the published abstract reports "a successful reduction of dandruff, skin redness and itching in 80% of the volunteers and a mild improvement in 20%," with good cosmetic acceptability.[^wigger] *This is the closest available human trial of climbazole as a solo active, but as reported it lacks a vehicle or active-comparator control arm, so the result cannot distinguish a genuine antifungal effect from regular shampooing and moisturizing.*

**Zinc pyrithione/climbazole "Dual Active" vs "Potentiated" zinc pyrithione alone, randomized, double-blind (topical, 620 subjects).** Procter & Gamble researchers ran a randomized, double-blind clinical trial comparing a zinc-pyrithione shampoo engineered for maximized bioavailability against a "Dual Active" shampoo combining standard zinc pyrithione with climbazole, using scalp flaking and epidermal histamine as endpoints. In vitro and confocal-microscopy work showed the Potentiated ZPT formula delivered more antifungal potency and reached the lower hair follicle better than the climbazole-containing combination; clinically, "a 4-week treatment with the Potentiated ZPT formula resulted in superior clinical efficacy compared with the Dual Active product at all 4 weekly time points for both flaking and epidermal histamine endpoints."[^schwartz] *In the largest and best-controlled trial identified that includes climbazole, adding climbazole to zinc pyrithione did not win: an optimized single-active zinc pyrithione shampoo from a competitor outperformed it, a reminder that vehicle and delivery matter as much as which antifungal is listed on the bottle.*

**Zinc pyrithione/climbazole dual-active shampoo, industry (topical, in vitro and clinical, versus non-antidandruff baseline).** Unilever researchers showed that a zinc pyrithione/climbazole dual-active shampoo deposited both actives onto a model skin surface and reduced *Malassezia furfur* regrowth in vitro, and reported that clinical evaluation "demonstrated superior efficacy and retained superiority during a regression phase" when subjects reverted to a non-antidandruff shampoo, alongside improved hair-fiber smoothness from co-formulated silicones.[^turner] *This industry study shows the combination beats no antidandruff treatment at all, which is a much lower bar than the head-to-head loss against Potentiated ZPT reported by a competing manufacturer, and it does not isolate climbazole's contribution from zinc pyrithione's or the silicones'.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Clinically proven anti-dandruff active."** Antifungal activity against *Malassezia* in vitro is genuinely well supported, and one small uncontrolled human study of climbazole alone reported improvement in most subjects.[^wigger][^youn] But no identified trial isolates climbazole against a true vehicle placebo in humans, and the one large, well-controlled RCT that tested a climbazole combination found it inferior to a competitor's single-active formula, so "clinically proven" overstates what the isolated ingredient has actually shown.[^schwartz]
- **"Works with piroctone olamine/zinc pyrithione for enhanced results."** Combination products do show measurable improvement over no treatment or over a bare emollient in small industry or single-center studies.[^youn][^turner] Whether the combination beats either active alone, or beats a well-formulated single-active shampoo, is unresolved and was answered unfavorably for a climbazole combination in the one trial designed to test exactly that question.[^schwartz]
