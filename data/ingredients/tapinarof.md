Tapinarof (brand name VTAMA, chemically a stilbenoid, molecular formula C17H18O2) is a first-in-class, steroid-free topical aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) agonist formulated as a 1% cream and applied once daily, FDA-approved for plaque [[psoriasis]] in adults and for [[atopic-dermatitis]] in patients age 2 and up.[^pubchem][^jdd][^adoring] Two identical 12-week phase 3 trials (PSOARING 1 and 2) found a Physician's Global Assessment (PGA) response in 35.4% and 40.2% of tapinarof-treated psoriasis patients versus 6.0% and 6.3% on vehicle (P<0.001 both), and two 8-week phase 3 trials (ADORING 1 and 2) found a Validated Investigator Global Assessment for Atopic Dermatitis (vIGA-AD) response of 45.4% and 46.4% on tapinarof versus 13.9% and 18.0% on vehicle (both P<0.0001); the tradeoff is folliculitis, the signature adverse event across the program, reaching 22.7% in the year-long psoriasis extension and 12.1% in the year-long atopic-dermatitis extension.[^psoaring3][^adoring3]

> In the two phase 3 PSOARING psoriasis trials, "A PGA response occurred in 35.4% of the patients in the tapinarof group and in 6.0% of those in the vehicle group in trial 1 and in 40.2% and 6.3%, respectively, in trial 2 (P<0.001 for both comparisons)."[^psoaring]

## The Rubric

**Plaque psoriasis (mild to severe), topical monotherapy**
- **Effect size: notable versus vehicle, roughly a fivefold higher response rate.** Across PSOARING 1 and 2 (n=510 and n=515), PGA response (clear or almost clear, with at least a 2-point drop from baseline) occurred in 35.4% and 40.2% of tapinarof-treated patients versus only 6.0% and 6.3% on vehicle at week 12 (P<0.001 for both).[^psoaring] In the 40-week open-label PSOARING 3 extension, 40.9% of eligible patients reached complete clearance (PGA=0), and clearance persisted off-drug for a mean of 130.1 days;[^psoaring3] a Dermavant-affiliated review characterizes the long-term program as showing "no tachyphylaxis."[^jdd]
- **Evidence quality: strong for a topical, industry-run but well-designed and FDA-registered.** Two identical, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled phase 3 trials (NCT03956355, NCT03983980) with over 1,000 combined patients meet a high bar for a topical agent; all trials were funded by and largely authored with Dermavant Sciences (the drug's manufacturer), and the open-label extension has no control arm, so long-term durability numbers should be read as descriptive rather than comparative.[^psoaring][^psoaring3]

**Atopic dermatitis (moderate to severe), topical monotherapy, ages 2+**
- **Effect size: notable, roughly two to three times the vehicle response.** In the two 8-week ADORING trials (n=813 combined), vIGA-AD success (score of 0 or 1 with a 2-grade improvement) occurred in 45.4% versus 13.9% and 46.4% versus 18.0% (tapinarof vs vehicle, both P<0.0001); EASI75 was reached by 55.8% versus 22.9% and 59.1% versus 21.2% (both P<0.0001).[^adoring] Itch relief was fast: mean daily pruritus scores fell more with tapinarof than vehicle "as early as day 1, 24 hours after initial application," with the difference reaching significance by day 2 (P=0.0115).[^itch] In the 48-week ADORING 3 open-label extension, 51.9% of the 728 enrolled patients eventually reached complete clearance (vIGA-AD=0), with a mean treatment-free interval of 79.8 days after first clearing.[^adoring3]
- **Evidence quality: strong for the primary endpoint, but the trials were short (8 weeks) relative to a chronic, relapsing disease.** Two randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials with a shared, prospectively defined primary endpoint is a solid design, and the drug's approval down to age 2 broadens its relevance versus most topical AD options; the 8-week window is shorter than psoriasis's 12-week trials, and, as with psoriasis, the long-term extension (ADORING 3) is open-label and industry-funded with no control group.[^adoring][^adoring3]

## How It Works

Tapinarof is a small-molecule agonist of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR), a ligand-activated transcription factor expressed in keratinocytes and immune cells that regulates skin barrier genes and inflammatory signaling.[^jdd] A Dermavant-affiliated review states that tapinarof "specifically binds to AhR to decrease proinflammatory cytokines, decrease oxidative stress, and promote skin barrier normalization," and separately that it "modulates the expression of interleukin-17 and the skin-barrier proteins filaggrin and loricrin" in the psoriasis trial report; for atopic dermatitis, the same mechanism is framed as downregulating pro-inflammatory Th2 cytokines while upregulating skin-barrier components.[^jdd][^psoaring][^adoring] This single-target, non-steroidal mechanism is the basis for its marketing as a steroid-free alternative that avoids topical-corticosteroid-associated skin atrophy, though none of the phase 3 trials directly compared tapinarof to a corticosteroid.[^jdd]

## The Evidence

**PSOARING 1 and 2, plaque psoriasis, randomized double-blind vehicle-controlled (topical).** Two identical phase 3 trials randomized adults with PGA 2 to 4 psoriasis and 3 to 20% body-surface involvement 2:1 to tapinarof 1% cream or vehicle once daily for 12 weeks (510 patients in trial 1, 515 in trial 2). The primary endpoint, PGA response (score of 0 or 1 with at least a 2-point drop from baseline) at week 12, was met by 35.4% versus 6.0% in trial 1 and 40.2% versus 6.3% in trial 2 (both P<0.001); secondary endpoints (PASI75, PASI90, body-surface area change) moved in the same direction. Adverse events included folliculitis, nasopharyngitis, contact dermatitis, headache, upper respiratory infection, and pruritus.[^psoaring] *A clean, adequately powered, two-trial replication found roughly a fivefold higher clear/almost-clear rate than vehicle, with folliculitis as the signature side effect to watch.*

**PSOARING 3, 40-week open-label extension (topical).** Patients completing the 12-week phase 3 trials could enter 40 weeks of open-label, PGA-driven intermittent tapinarof with 4 weeks of follow-up; 763 of 833 eligible patients (91.6%) enrolled. Complete clearance (PGA=0) was reached by 40.9% of patients, and 58.2% of those entering with PGA 2 or higher reached PGA 0 or 1. Mean duration of the remittive effect (time off therapy before relapse in patients who cleared) was 130.1 days. Most frequent adverse events were folliculitis (22.7%), contact dermatitis (5.5%), and upper respiratory infection (4.7%).[^psoaring3] *Uncontrolled but reassuring: repeated intermittent use over a year produced deeper clearance than the 12-week trials and a roughly four-month drug-free remission in patients who cleared, without new safety signals, though nearly a quarter of patients on this schedule developed folliculitis.*

**ADORING 1 and 2, atopic dermatitis, randomized double-blind vehicle-controlled (topical).** Two 8-week phase 3 trials randomized 813 patients ages 2 and up with moderate to severe AD to tapinarof 1% cream or vehicle once daily. The primary endpoint, vIGA-AD success (0 or 1 with a 2-grade improvement) at week 8, was met by 45.4% versus 13.9% and 46.4% versus 18.0% (both P<0.0001); EASI75 was reached by 55.8% versus 22.9% and 59.1% versus 21.2% (both P<0.0001). Common adverse events (5% or more) were folliculitis, headache, and nasopharyngitis, mostly mild to moderate, with fewer discontinuations for adverse events on tapinarof than on vehicle.[^adoring] *A large, well-replicated pair of trials found roughly two to three times the vehicle response on both the investigator-graded and the more objective EASI75 endpoint, in a population that includes children as young as 2.*

**ADORING 3, 48-week open-label extension (topical).** Patients from ADORING 1, ADORING 2, a separate pharmacokinetics trial, and tapinarof-naive patients could receive up to 48 weeks of vIGA-AD-driven intermittent tapinarof; 728 enrolled. Overall, 51.9% eventually achieved vIGA-AD=0 and 81.6% achieved vIGA-AD 0 or 1 at least once; after first clearing, the mean treatment-free interval before needing to restart was 79.8 days. Most frequent adverse events were folliculitis (12.1%), nasopharyngitis (6.9%), and upper respiratory infection (6.9%).[^adoring3] *Extended, intermittent use produced high rates of at least transient clearance and roughly a ten-week average break between courses in patients who cleared, again without a control arm to benchmark against.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Steroid-free alternative for long-term use."** The steroid-free mechanism (AhR agonism rather than corticosteroid activity) is accurate, and PSOARING 3 and ADORING 3 support that repeated, intermittent use over 40 to 48 weeks does not show new safety signals or loss of efficacy.[^psoaring3][^adoring3] No trial directly compared tapinarof against a topical corticosteroid for either disease, so "as effective as steroids" is not something these data establish, only that tapinarof itself performs well against vehicle.
- **"Fast itch relief."** Supported for atopic dermatitis: pooled ADORING data found daily pruritus scores separating from vehicle within 24 hours and reaching statistical significance by day 2 (P=0.0115).[^itch] This is a real, trial-documented early effect, not a marketing exaggeration, though it was measured against vehicle, not against faster-acting prescription options like topical corticosteroids.
