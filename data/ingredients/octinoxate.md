Octinoxate (INCI ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate, also called octyl methoxycinnamate or OMC) is an organic sunscreen filter that absorbs ultraviolet-B, the sunburn wavelengths, and for decades was the most widely used UVB filter in the world.[^latha][^schlumpf] It absorbs across the UVB band (about 290 to 320 nm) and gives little coverage of UVA (about 320 to 400 nm), so it raises a product's SPF, a UVB metric, but supplies no meaningful UVA protection and is paired with UVA filters such as [[avobenzone]] or mineral [[zinc-oxide]]; SPF and the UVA axis are explained on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^latha][^chatelain] Its central weakness is photostability: its absorbance falls under sunlight, and when it is combined with avobenzone the two speed each other's breakdown.[^tarras][^sayre][^chatelain] The health value of using it is the health value of regular broad-spectrum sunscreen, which has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer burden and is documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], not in any octinoxate-specific clinical-endpoint trial.[^latha] Two health questions shape how it is graded: how much of it is absorbed into the body, and what its estrogenic activity in laboratory and animal tests means for people.

> Octinoxate is an effective UVB absorber and one of the most-used UVB filters worldwide, permitted in US sunscreens at up to 7.5% and in the EU at up to 10%, but it is photolabile (its absorbance drops under sunlight and it destabilizes avobenzone), covers only UVB and no UVA, is absorbed into the bloodstream above the FDA's safety-testing threshold, and is estrogenic in cell and rodent assays, though a human study at exaggerated exposure found no change in reproductive hormones.[^latha][^tarras][^sayre][^matta2020][^schlumpf][^janjua][^fda2019][^eu2009]

## The Rubric

The grade below is for octinoxate's job in a sunscreen, ultraviolet-B photoprotection, framed relative to the other filters it is measured against. Systemic absorption and endocrine activity are a separate axis, graded separately and not folded into the photoprotection grade.

**Ultraviolet-B photoprotection**
- **Effect size: notable for UVB, none for UVA, and photostability-limited.** Octinoxate is an efficient UVB absorber that contributes to a product's SPF, and it was long the default UVB filter in sunscreens worldwide.[^latha][^schlumpf] Its coverage is confined to UVB (about 290 to 320 nm); it does little in the UVA range (about 320 to 400 nm), which is why it is combined with a dedicated UVA filter such as avobenzone or zinc oxide.[^latha][^chatelain] The limit is photostability. In a study of sunscreen filters irradiated with a full day's UV dose, "the absorbance of 2-ethylhexyl 4-methoxycinnamate was reduced significantly," so the UVB protection it delivers falls during sun exposure unless the formula stabilizes it.[^tarras] The problem is worse in a common pairing: octinoxate and avobenzone undergo, per one photochemical study, "concomitant photolysis of avobenzone and octinoxate," each accelerating the other's breakdown, and a formulation study confirmed "AVB was shown to destabilize ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate (EHM)."[^sayre][^chatelain] "Notable UVB absorption and SPF contribution, but no UVA coverage and photolabile, degrading under sunlight and destabilized outright by avobenzone."
- **Evidence quality: solid for the spectral and photostability characterization; the health payoff is inherited, not filter-specific.** That octinoxate is a UVB absorber and that it loses absorbance under UV are documented across independent laboratory and formulation studies.[^latha][^tarras][^sayre][^chatelain] What is not demonstrated for octinoxate on its own is any clinical-endpoint benefit; the randomized evidence that broad-spectrum sunscreen slows photoaging and lowers skin-cancer burden is for the practice of regular use, covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], and octinoxate contributes the UVB half of that protection when a formula holds it stable.[^latha]

**Systemic absorption and endocrine activity (a separate axis, not an efficacy grade)**
- In an FDA maximal-use randomized trial, octinoxate was absorbed through skin into plasma above the 0.5 ng/mL threshold above which the agency recommends further nonclinical safety testing, reaching geometric mean maximum concentrations of 7.9 ng/mL (coefficient of variation 86.5%) for a nonaerosol spray and 5.2 ng/mL (CV 68.2%) for a pump spray, with the threshold surpassed on day 1 for all six filters tested.[^matta2020] Octinoxate is estrogenic in laboratory assays: it increased proliferation of estrogen-sensitive MCF-7 breast-cancer cells in vitro, and in immature rats fed the chemical it raised uterine weight with a median effective dose of 935 mg/kg/day, which led the authors to conclude that "UV screens should be tested for endocrine activity."[^schlumpf] A human study bears on what that means at realistic exposure: after a week of whole-body application of a 10% cream, octinoxate reached maximum plasma concentrations of about 10 ng/mL in women and 20 ng/mL in men and was detectable in urine, but the reproductive hormones FSH and LH were unchanged, and the small hormone differences seen "were not related to sunscreen exposure."[^janjua] The absorption and the in-vitro/animal estrogenicity are well established; no reproductive-hormone effect has been demonstrated in people, and the FDA classified octinoxate "Category III," meaning insufficient safety data rather than a finding of harm.[^matta2020][^schlumpf][^janjua][^fda2019]

## What We Actually Know

Octinoxate is a well-characterized organic UVB filter: it absorbs across the UVB band (about 290 to 320 nm), contributes to SPF, and provides essentially no UVA (about 320 to 400 nm) coverage, so it is combined with a UVA filter.[^latha][^chatelain] Its central weakness is photostability. Its absorbance drops under sunlight, and when it is paired with avobenzone the two break each other down, a problem documented across formulation and photochemical studies.[^tarras][^sayre][^chatelain] Under an FDA maximal-use trial it is absorbed into plasma above the 0.5 ng/mL threshold that triggers a call for further FDA safety testing, which is one reason the agency lists it as Category III.[^matta2020][^fda2019] It is estrogenic in cell and rodent assays, but a human study at exaggerated whole-body exposure found no change in reproductive hormones.[^schlumpf][^janjua] The health value of using it, slowing photoaging and reducing skin-cancer burden, is the value of regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], with octinoxate supplying UVB coverage when the formula keeps it stable.[^latha]

## How It Works

Octinoxate is a cinnamate ester, an organic (chemical) filter that absorbs a UV photon and re-emits the energy as heat rather than scattering light the way mineral filters do. Its absorption is concentrated in the UVB range (about 290 to 320 nm), the wavelengths that cause sunburn and most of the DNA photodamage behind keratinocyte cancers, so it adds to a product's SPF; it absorbs little in the UVA range (about 320 to 400 nm) and is combined with UVA filters to make a product broad-spectrum.[^latha][^chatelain] The molecule sits in equilibrium between a trans and a cis form, and absorbing UV can drive it toward the cis isomer and other photoproducts that absorb less, so its protective absorbance falls with continued light exposure; a study exposing sunscreen filters to a day's UV dose found "the absorbance of 2-ethylhexyl 4-methoxycinnamate was reduced significantly."[^tarras] A review states the broader problem for the whole field: "UVA filters, however, are rare and not sufficiently photostable," and octinoxate makes the pairing worse rather than better, because avobenzone and octinoxate destabilize each other on exposure.[^chatelain][^sayre] Formulators counter this with photostabilizers: the broad-spectrum filter bemotrizinol (Tinosorb S) showed "photoprotective properties toward both UV filters" when added to an avobenzone-plus-octinoxate formula.[^chatelain]

## The Evidence

Primary evidence, by question.

**Spectral coverage.** Octinoxate is an organic UVB filter. A dermatology review of sunscreening agents places it among the chemical filters and gives the UVB band it works in as "UVB (290-320)" nm, the sunburn wavelengths; it is not a UVA (320-400 nm) filter and supplies no meaningful long-wave protection on its own, which is why broad-spectrum products pair it with a UVA filter such as avobenzone or zinc oxide.[^latha][^chatelain] Because SPF is a UVB number, octinoxate raises SPF while leaving UVA coverage to other filters, a distinction covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^latha]

**Photostability.** Octinoxate loses absorbance under UV. Tarras-Wahlberg et al. irradiated films of common sunscreen filters with a full day's equivalent UV dose (100 J/cm2 UVA; 20 minimal erythema doses of UVB) and reported that "the absorbance of 2-ethylhexyl 4-methoxycinnamate was reduced significantly."[^tarras] In a commercial product containing avobenzone, oxybenzone, and octinoxate, Sayre et al. found the two filters degrade together, reporting "concomitant photolysis of avobenzone and octinoxate" that outpaced the expected harmless E/Z photoisomerization, and detected persistent free radicals by electron spin resonance.[^sayre] Chatelain and Gabard confirmed the same interaction from the other direction, writing that "AVB was shown to destabilize ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate (EHM)," and showed the broad-spectrum filter Tinosorb S (bemotrizinol) restored stability, showing "photoprotective properties toward both UV filters."[^chatelain]

**Systemic absorption.** In an FDA maximal-use randomized trial (48 participants, 44 completing; four spray and lotion products applied at 2 mg/cm2 to 75% of body surface, once on day 1 then four times daily on days 2 to 4), all six tested filters were absorbed into plasma above 0.5 ng/mL, with the threshold "surpassed on day 1 after a single application for all active ingredients." Octinoxate maximum plasma concentrations were 7.9 ng/mL (CV 86.5%) for the nonaerosol spray and 5.2 ng/mL (CV 68.2%) for the pump spray.[^matta2020] The 0.5 ng/mL figure is the level below which the FDA may waive some nonclinical safety testing; exceeding it triggers a request for further study, not a finding of harm, and the authors wrote that the results "do not indicate that individuals should refrain from the use of sunscreen."[^matta2020]

**Endocrine activity.** Schlumpf et al. tested six UV filters for estrogenicity. Octinoxate increased proliferation of estrogen-responsive MCF-7 human breast-cancer cells in vitro (five of the six filters were active, with median effective concentrations between 1.56 and 3.73 microM) and induced the estrogen-regulated pS2 protein; in an in-vivo uterotrophic assay in immature Long-Evans rats fed the chemical for four days, octinoxate raised uterine weight with a median effective dose of 935 mg/kg/day.[^schlumpf] The authors concluded that "UV screens should be tested for endocrine activity, in view of possible long-term effects in humans and wildlife."[^schlumpf] Whether this translates to people was tested directly by Janjua et al.: 32 volunteers (15 young men, 17 postmenopausal women) applied a cream containing 10% octinoxate (and 10% each of two other filters) to the whole body daily for a week. Octinoxate reached maximum plasma concentrations of about 10 ng/mL in women and 20 ng/mL in men and was detectable in urine, but "the reproductive hormones FSH, LH were unchanged," and the small differences in testosterone, estradiol, and inhibin B "were not related to sunscreen exposure."[^janjua] The absorption and the laboratory/animal estrogenicity are established; a reproductive-hormone effect in humans is not.

**Regulatory status.** Octinoxate is a permitted US OTC sunscreen active at a maximum concentration of 7.5%.[^fda2019] In the FDA's 2019 proposed sunscreen rule the agency placed octinoxate, along with eleven other organic filters, in "Category III," stating that "the public record does not currently contain sufficient data to support positive GRASE determinations" for them and requesting additional safety data, including on systemic absorption; only zinc oxide and titanium dioxide were proposed as generally recognized as safe and effective.[^fda2019] It is more broadly permitted abroad: the EU Cosmetics Regulation lists ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate (Annex VI, entry 12; CAS 5466-77-3) as an allowed UV filter at a maximum concentration of 10% in the finished product.[^eu2009] It therefore remains widely used worldwide while its long-term safety data are still being sought in the US.

## Uses

Well-supported: as the ultraviolet-B filter in a sunscreen, where octinoxate absorbs the sunburn wavelengths, raises SPF, and supplies the UVB half of broad-spectrum protection when the formula is stabilized.[^latha][^chatelain] That health benefit is a property of regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], and depends on the rest of the formula keeping octinoxate photostable and on a separate UVA filter, because octinoxate covers no UVA and degrades under sunlight, worst of all alongside avobenzone.[^latha][^tarras][^sayre][^chatelain] Its practical limits are three: it covers only UVB, it is photolabile, and it is absorbed systemically above the FDA's testing threshold and is estrogenic in laboratory and animal assays, which is why the agency has requested more safety data.[^matta2020][^schlumpf][^fda2019]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Broad-spectrum protection" (from octinoxate).** Octinoxate is a UVB-only filter; it covers about 290 to 320 nm and does little in the UVA range (320 to 400 nm), so any broad-spectrum claim rests on the product's other filters, not on octinoxate.[^latha][^chatelain] A US "Broad Spectrum" label is a single low-bar UVA threshold, explained on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^latha]
- **"Photostable / long-lasting protection."** Octinoxate loses absorbance under sunlight, and one irradiation study found "the absorbance of 2-ethylhexyl 4-methoxycinnamate was reduced significantly."[^tarras] In the common octinoxate-plus-avobenzone pairing the two degrade each other, so photostability is a property of the finished formula and its stabilizers, not of octinoxate itself.[^sayre][^chatelain]
- **"Chemical sunscreens are toxic / hormone disruptors."** The demonstrated facts are systemic absorption above the FDA's 0.5 ng/mL testing threshold and estrogenicity in cell and rodent assays.[^matta2020][^schlumpf] Neither is a finding of harm in people: the human study at exaggerated whole-body exposure found reproductive hormones unchanged, the FDA classified octinoxate Category III (insufficient data), not unsafe, and the absorption-trial authors stated their results "do not indicate that individuals should refrain from the use of sunscreen."[^janjua][^fda2019][^matta2020]
