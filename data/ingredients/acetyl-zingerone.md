Acetyl zingerone (AZ, sold under the trade name Synoxyl AZ) is a synthetic antioxidant built by condensing vanillin with acetylacetone and hydrogenating the result, giving a molecule that shares a phenolic ring with ginger's zingerone and with curcumin; it was developed and is patented by Sytheon, the same company behind [[tetrahexyldecyl-ascorbate|THD ascorbate]], and the two are frequently formulated together.[^meyer] Independently measured in-vitro work shows it is a genuine, photostable free-radical scavenger and iron chelator that raises collagen I output in cultured fibroblasts, but the human clinical case largely traces back to studies run or cited by the ingredient's own patent-holders, and the one clinical trial with an independently verifiable, published dataset tests AZ only as an add-on to THD ascorbate, not against a vehicle or on its own.

> Cell-based data from the compound's inventors show AZ is a real antioxidant: it neutralizes hydroxyl and peroxyl radicals, singlet oxygen, and peroxynitrite more effectively than vitamin E in their assays, stays largely intact under UV exposure where vitamin E degrades, and raises collagen I production 15-31% in cultured dermal fibroblasts.[^swindell][^meyer] The strongest human evidence with an independently checkable dataset is a small, sponsor-funded 8-week trial that found adding 1% AZ to 5% THD ascorbate improved wrinkle, pigment, and redness scores, while THD ascorbate used alone in the same trial did not; no arm tested AZ by itself or against a vehicle.[^afzal] Larger appearance and sun-redness improvements reported for AZ as a standalone ingredient exist only inside a review paper written by the compound's inventors, citing an unpublished company trial that is not separately published or independently retrievable.[^meyer]

## The Rubric

**Antioxidant activity, photostability, and dermal matrix effects (in vitro, mechanism)**
Swindell and colleagues, funded by Sytheon and including the company's CEO and research director as authors, tested AZ on its own (not combined with THD ascorbate) in cell-free and cell-based assays. AZ inhibited lipid peroxidation with an IC50 of 0.46 microgram/mL and scavenged hydrogen peroxide with an IC50 of 62.6 microgram/mL or better, stayed more than 80% intact after 4 hours of UVA/UVB exposure, and increased collagen I protein output in adult dermal fibroblasts by 15-31% across the concentrations tested, an effect THD ascorbate alone did not produce.[^swindell] Combined with THD ascorbate, AZ also more potently inhibited MMP-1, MMP-2, and MMP-3 enzyme activity than AZ alone.[^swindell] A review by the same company's researchers separately reports that AZ neutralized hydroxyl radicals, peroxyl radicals, peroxynitrite, and singlet oxygen 2.7 to 126 times more effectively than alpha-tocopherol (vitamin E) in their comparative assays, and inhibited ongoing UV-induced DNA damage (cyclobutane pyrimidine dimer formation) by about 82% in pigmented melanocytes in a separate in-vitro study from the same group.[^meyer] This is real, independently readable in-vitro data, but it comes entirely from the ingredient's patent-holder and has not been replicated by an outside lab.

**Facial photoaging appearance, added to THD ascorbate (topical, human RCT)**
- **Effect size: modest, and shown only in combination.** In a randomized, double-blind trial of 44 adults, a lotion with 5% THD ascorbate plus 1% AZ reduced wrinkle severity 3.72% (P=0.048) and redness intensity 14.25% (P=0.045) at 8 weeks, and pigment intensity 4.10% (P=0.0002), versus baseline-relative changes in the THD-ascorbate-only arm that went the other way: wrinkle severity up 4.48% and redness up 8.34% at 8 weeks.[^afzal] The trial did not include a vehicle or AZ-alone arm, so it shows that adding AZ improved on THD ascorbate alone in this design, not that AZ itself has an effect against an inactive control.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** One trial, with grant support from Sytheon and a co-author who is Sytheon's owner and CEO; no independent replication.[^afzal]

**Facial photoaging appearance and UV-related redness, as a standalone ingredient (topical, human)**
- **Effect size: reported as notable, but from an unpublished source.** A review co-authored by AZ's patent-holders reports that in an 8-week randomized, double-blind trial, a 1% AZ lotion outperformed its placebo lotion on wrinkle severity (down 25.7%, P=0.019), wrinkle volume (down 30.1%, P=0.003), dyspigmentation (down 25.6%, P=0.021), and redness intensity (down 20.7%, P=0.035), and that a 0.5% AZ lotion cut UV-induced redness by 33-39% (P<0.01) after simulated sun exposure over 7 days.[^meyer]
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** Both figures are attributed inside the review to Sytheon's own unpublished work: the UV-redness study is cited as "Chaudhuri, in preparation," and the placebo-controlled lotion trial is not separately retrievable as a standalone published paper. Neither can be checked against an independent methods section, so despite the specific percentages, this sits below the Afzal trial, which at least has its own peer-reviewed, independently indexed publication.[^meyer][^afzal]

## What We Actually Know

The most solidly established fact about AZ is chemical, not clinical: in cell-free and cell-based assays run by its patent-holder, it is a genuine, photostable antioxidant, inhibiting lipid peroxidation and scavenging hydrogen peroxide at low concentrations, staying largely intact under UV exposure where vitamin E does not, and raising collagen I output in fibroblasts on its own.[^swindell] Its main commercial role is stabilizing [[tetrahexyldecyl-ascorbate|THD ascorbate]], an oil-soluble vitamin C ester that the same company's own data show is otherwise "a poor antioxidant that degrades rapidly when exposed to singlet oxygen."[^swindell] The best human evidence with an independently checkable published dataset is a small, sponsor-funded 8-week RCT (44 participants) showing that adding AZ to THD ascorbate improved wrinkle, pigment, and redness scores where THD ascorbate alone did not, but that trial never tests AZ on its own or against a vehicle.[^afzal] Larger single-ingredient claims, that a 1% AZ lotion beat placebo on wrinkles, pigment, and redness, and that a 0.5% AZ lotion cut UV-triggered redness by a third or more, appear only inside a review written by AZ's own inventors, sourced to trials that are unpublished or not separately retrievable.[^meyer]

## How It Works

AZ is made by condensing vanillin with acetylacetone and then hydrogenating the intermediate, yielding a molecule built around the same 4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl ring found in ginger's zingerone and in curcumin, joined to a 1,3-dicarbonyl group.[^meyer] That dicarbonyl group lets AZ chelate iron in its di-keto form, which the inventors' review reports blocks iron-catalyzed hydroxyl radical formation more effectively than the classic chelator EDTA in their assay.[^meyer] Separately, AZ quenches singlet oxygen mostly by physical quenching, converting it back to ordinary oxygen without AZ itself being consumed, which the review credits for its photostability compared with vitamin E, which "completely degrades over an irradiation time course."[^meyer] Its role alongside THD ascorbate is protective rather than additive: Swindell and colleagues found THD ascorbate alone "reduced viability of keratinocytes exposed to oxidative stress" and triggered "unexpected activation of type I interferon signaling," a pro-inflammatory response, and that adding AZ "completely abrogated" the viability loss and blunted the interferon signal.[^swindell]

## The Evidence

**In-vitro antioxidant activity and dermal effects.** Swindell, Randhawa, Quijas, Bojanowski, and Chaudhuri, in a paper funded by Sytheon and disclosing that co-authors Chaudhuri (president and CEO, with ownership interest) and Randhawa (Director of Research and Innovation) work for the company, tested AZ alongside THD ascorbate (THDC) in cell-free and keratinocyte/fibroblast assays. AZ alone inhibited lipid peroxidation with an IC50 of 0.46 microgram/mL, more potent than the THDC+AZ combination in that assay, and AZ or the THDC+AZ combination scavenged hydrogen peroxide with an IC50 of 62.6 microgram/mL or lower. AZ "was stable under UVA/UVB light exposure, with >80% of AZ remaining following 4 h" of irradiation. In cultured adult dermal fibroblasts, "AZ elicited a significant 15-31% extracellular increase in COL I production at all concentrations tested," while "THDC alone [did] not significantly alter COL I protein levels"; the THDC+AZ combination increased COL I protein 12-27% at the highest concentrations and produced more potent inhibition of MMP-1, MMP-2, and MMP-3 activity than AZ alone. THDC alone "reduced viability of keratinocytes exposed to oxidative stress," an effect "completely abrogated by the addition of AZ to THDC."[^swindell]

**Facial photoaging, combined with THD ascorbate (human RCT).** Afzal and colleagues, with grant support from Sytheon and a co-author (Chaudhuri) who is Sytheon's owner and CEO, ran a double-blind RCT randomizing 44 healthy adults aged 30-65 to a lotion with 5% THD ascorbate plus 1% AZ or to 5% THD ascorbate alone, twice daily for 8 weeks, assessed by facial photography and colorimetry. "Average wrinkle severity was significantly decreased in the THDA-AZ group at Weeks 4 and 8 by 0.75% (p = 0.023) and 3.72% (p = 0.048), respectively, compared to the THDA group where wrinkle severity at Weeks 4 and 8 was increased by 7.88% and 4.48%." Facial pigment intensity fell 4.10% (P=0.0002) at week 8 in the combination group versus 0.69% in the THD-ascorbate-only group. Facial redness intensity fell 3.73% (P=0.0162) at week 4 and 14.25% (P=0.045) at week 8 in the combination group, while the THD-ascorbate-only group's redness rose 27.5% and 8.34% over the same weeks. The authors concluded "daily use of combined THDA and AZ may improve facial wrinkle severity, pigment intensity, and erythema to a greater extent than THDA," and noted "while THDA alone increases facial wrinkle severity and erythema, the addition of AZ reduces both."[^afzal] Neither arm was compared to an inactive vehicle.

**Standalone-ingredient claims, cited inside a review by AZ's inventors.** Meyer, Swindell, and Chaudhuri (funded by Sytheon; Chaudhuri discloses ownership as president and CEO, the other two disclose paid consulting) published a review in Antioxidants summarizing AZ's mechanisms and citing clinical figures the authors attribute to their own company's testing. They write that "AZ incorporated into a lotion at 1% was directly compared to its placebo lotion in a randomized double-blind clinical study where subjects applied either of the lotions twice daily to their face over a course of 8 weeks," reporting that versus placebo, the AZ lotion produced "significant reductions in wrinkle severity (-25.7%, p = 0.019), wrinkle volume (-30.1%, p = 0.003), dyspigmentation (-25.6%, p = 0.021) and redness intensity (-20.7%, p = 0.035)," and that over the same 8 weeks "AZ treatment... stimulated the skin-friendly Staphylococcus epidermidis by >20% vs. baseline, whereas placebo treatment remained practically unchanged." A separate figure, credited only to "Chaudhuri, in preparation," reports that a 0.5% AZ lotion "significantly reduced (p < 0.01) skin redness by 33% and 39%" after two simulated sun-exposure doses over 7 days. The review also cites the group's own earlier melanocyte work reporting that AZ "inhibited ongoing [delayed cyclobutane pyrimidine dimer] formation by about 82%" when added after UVA exposure in vitro, a marker of UV-induced DNA damage rather than a measured skin-cancer outcome.[^meyer] None of these three findings is available as a separately published, independently indexed paper in the sources fetched here.

## Uses

Established mainly at the mechanism level: AZ is an independently measurable, photostable antioxidant and iron chelator that raises collagen I output in cultured fibroblasts, and it protects [[tetrahexyldecyl-ascorbate|THD ascorbate]] from the oxidative degradation and pro-inflammatory signaling that the same lab's data show THD ascorbate causes on its own.[^swindell] The one human trial with an independently retrievable dataset shows a benefit only when AZ is added to THD ascorbate, not for AZ alone.[^afzal] Claims that AZ by itself improves facial wrinkles, pigmentation, and UV-triggered redness rest on figures reported only inside the inventors' own review, sourced to unpublished or non-independently-retrievable internal studies.[^meyer]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Clinically proven to reduce wrinkles and photoaging."** The clinical figures behind this claim for AZ used alone come from a study cited only inside a review written by the ingredient's patent-holders, credited to their own unpublished or not-separately-published trial; the one independently published RCT tests AZ only as an add-on to THD ascorbate, not alone.[^meyer][^afzal]
- **"A stabilized, better-performing form of vitamin C."** AZ itself is not a vitamin C derivative; it is a separate antioxidant that stabilizes THD ascorbate, an ascorbic acid ester, against oxidative degradation the ester would otherwise undergo on its own.[^swindell]
- **"Protects against UV-induced skin damage."** The DNA-damage protection figure (about 82% reduction in delayed cyclobutane pyrimidine dimer formation) is an in-vitro melanocyte finding from the compound's own inventors, not a measured reduction in skin cancer or a human DNA-damage endpoint.[^meyer]
