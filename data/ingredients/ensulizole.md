Ensulizole, INCI phenylbenzimidazole sulfonic acid (PBSA, 2-phenylbenzimidazole-5-sulfonic acid), is an organic ultraviolet-B sunscreen filter permitted in United States over-the-counter sunscreens at up to 4%, one of the few water-soluble organic filters, which lets it go into aqueous gels and sprays rather than the oil phase most other organic filters need.[^fda2019][^norman2025] A 2024 pharmaceutical-sciences paper describes it plainly as a "UVB sunscreen," and, like most of the older organic filters, it is combined with a dedicated UVA filter such as avobenzone, the main UVA filter in US sunscreens, covered on [[avobenzone]].[^rajamohan][^fda2019] Regular use of a broad-spectrum sunscreen has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing skin-cancer burden, and that health benefit belongs to the practice and the whole formula, not to ensulizole as a single filter; it is documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Two questions shape how it is graded: how much protection a single UVB co-filter actually delivers, and what its safety record looks like given a 2019 FDA data gap and newer human and environmental findings.

> Ensulizole is a water-soluble organic UVB filter used as a co-filter rather than a product's main filter, so it contributes only modestly to a formula's SPF and nothing to UVA coverage. The FDA still lists it "Category III," meaning the record lacked "sufficient data" for a positive safety determination as of 2019, but a 2025 industry-funded toxicology review reports low dermal absorption (0.26%) and safety margins over 100 at the US 4% maximum; a separate, independent 2025 case-control study found ensulizole among four UV filters elevated in the follicular fluid of women with diminished ovarian reserve, an association, not a demonstrated cause.[^fda2019][^norman2025][^tian2025]

## The Rubric

The grade below is for ensulizole's job in a sunscreen, ultraviolet-B absorption, framed relative to the other filters it sits alongside. Systemic exposure and safety are graded as a separate axis, not folded into the photoprotection grade.

**Ultraviolet-B photoprotection (as a secondary filter)**
- **Effect size: minimal on its own.** Ensulizole is one of twelve organic filters the FDA's 2019 proposed sunscreen rule groups together, and a 2024 formulation study of ensulizole treats it as a single "UVB sunscreen" active rather than a broad-spectrum one.[^fda2019][^rajamohan] Its water solubility is a formulation convenience, letting it sit in the aqueous phase of a gel or spray, but it does not by itself make the filter stronger; it still absorbs only in the UVB band and provides no UVA coverage, so a broad-spectrum product pairs it with a UVA filter such as avobenzone.[^fda2019] "Minimal as a standalone filter. It is a co-filter that adds some UVB absorbance and no UVA protection, and it is not the primary filter in any current mainstream US formula."
- **Evidence quality: thin.** No head-to-head clinical trial measuring ensulizole's contribution to a product's SPF or photoaging outcomes on its own was found. What is documented is its regulatory classification as a UVB filter and its inclusion in laboratory formulation research aimed at improving its photostability, not a clinical efficacy trial.[^fda2019][^rajamohan]

**Systemic exposure and safety (a separate axis, not an efficacy grade)**
- Two contrasting bodies of evidence exist. A 2025 industry-funded toxicology review (authored by Personal Care Products Council staff and consultants) reports an experimentally determined dermal absorption of 0.26% in humans, no evidence of bioaccumulation, non-sensitizing and non-phototoxic results in the studies it compiled, and safety margins "greater than 100" at the US 4% maximum use level.[^norman2025] Separately, in its 2019 proposed sunscreen rule the FDA placed ensulizole, with eleven other organic filters, in "Category III," because the record lacked "sufficient data to support positive GRASE determinations," and requested more data; the 2025 review is one response to that request but has not yet changed the FDA's classification.[^fda2019][^norman2025] An independent 2025 case-control study of 102 women found ensulizole was one of four UV filters (with octyl methoxycinnamate, UV-P, and UV-328) present at significantly higher concentrations in the follicular fluid of women with diminished ovarian reserve than in healthy controls; the study reports an association, and the correlations it highlights for ovarian-reserve biomarkers were driven by octyl methoxycinnamate, not ensulizole specifically.[^tian2025] No causal link between ensulizole and reduced fertility has been demonstrated. Ensulizole is also detected in surface water in Germany and the Baltic Sea, and a laboratory study found it altered antioxidant and stress-response biomarkers in blue mussels at environmentally relevant concentrations.[^winter2026][^pham2022]

## The Evidence

Primary evidence, by question.

**Regulatory status and use levels.** Ensulizole is a permitted US OTC sunscreen active at a maximum concentration of 4%.[^fda2019] A 2025 toxicology review states that "authorized maximum ensulizole usage levels in consumer end-use products worldwide range from 3% to 8%, with the maximum usage level limited to 4% in the United States, Canada, and Australia."[^norman2025] In the FDA's 2019 proposed sunscreen rule the agency proposed that ensulizole and eleven other organic filters be classified "Category III," stating "the public record does not currently contain sufficient data to support positive GRASE determinations for cinoxate, dioxybenzone, ensulizole, homosalate, meradimate, octinoxate, octisalate, octocrylene, padimate O, sulisobenzone, oxybenzone, or avobenzone," and requested additional safety data.[^fda2019]

**Toxicology and dermal absorption.** A 2025 Critical Reviews in Toxicology paper, authored by Personal Care Products Council staff and industry-affiliated toxicology consultants, compiled existing animal and human data on ensulizole. It reports "experimentally determined values of 4% for oral absorption in rats and of 0.26% for dermal absorption in humans," no bioaccumulation in rat studies "consistent with its high water solubility and low octanol/water partition coefficient," and that ensulizole is "non-(photo)sensitizing, non-phototoxic," with subchronic no-observed-adverse-effect levels of "100 mg/kg/day (dermal rabbit) and 1000 mg/kg/day (oral rat OECD 408 study)." Using the rat oral NOAEL, the authors calculated "margins of safety... >100" for sunscreen use up to the US 4% maximum.[^norman2025] A related 2025 mode-of-action paper by an overlapping author group evaluated ensulizole among six commonly used UV filters and found "no evidence of biologically relevant carcinogenic MOAs" and systemic exposure levels "well below concentrations at which they have biologic activity."[^cohen2025]

**Human biomonitoring and fertility signal.** A 2025 case-control study measured 16 UV filters in follicular fluid from 102 Chinese women, 34 with diminished ovarian reserve (DOR) and 68 controls. Ensulizole was one of four individual compounds "significantly higher" in the DOR group, alongside octyl methoxycinnamate, UV-P, and UV-328; the cumulative concentration of the ten most commonly detected filters was also markedly higher in the DOR group (median 178.96 ng/mL versus 23.93 ng/mL, p<0.001). The paper's reported correlations between filter concentration and ovarian-reserve biomarkers (anti-Mullerian hormone, antral follicle count) were specific to octyl methoxycinnamate, not ensulizole.[^tian2025] As a single case-control study, it establishes an association worth further investigation, not a demonstrated cause of reduced ovarian reserve from ensulizole exposure.

**Environmental presence.** A 2026 German Environment Agency study using targeted and non-target mass spectrometry detected ensulizole (PBSA), among other UV filters, in suspended particulate matter and water samples from German rivers.[^winter2026] A separate laboratory study exposed blue mussels (Mytilus edulis) to a range of ensulizole concentrations, including levels "commonly found in the Baltic Sea," for three weeks and reported that the filter "affected the antioxidant response, energy storage, and cell death-related processes in mussel tissues," with mussels at low, environmentally relevant concentrations surviving air exposure for a shorter time than controls.[^pham2022]

## Uses

Well-supported: as a secondary ultraviolet-B filter in a broad-spectrum sunscreen, where its water solubility allows formulation into aqueous gels and sprays and it adds some UVB absorbance alongside stronger UVB filters and a dedicated UVA filter such as avobenzone.[^fda2019][^norman2025] Its practical limits are that no clinical trial isolating its own contribution to SPF or photoaging outcomes was found, and it provides no UVA coverage, so it is not used as a standalone filter.[^fda2019] Its safety picture is mixed: an industry-funded 2025 toxicology review reports low dermal absorption and wide safety margins at the US 4% maximum, while the FDA's 2019 Category III classification and a 2025 independent case-control association with diminished ovarian reserve mean the ingredient's long-term safety record is not fully settled.[^norman2025][^fda2019][^tian2025]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Broad-spectrum protection" (attributed to ensulizole).** Ensulizole is a UVB-only filter and provides no UVA coverage.[^fda2019] Any broad-spectrum performance of a product containing it comes from its other filters, particularly a UVA filter such as avobenzone; that landscape is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^fda2019]
- **"Gentle, water-soluble, non-irritating filter."** A 2025 toxicology review reports ensulizole is non-sensitizing and non-phototoxic in the studies it compiled, but a general tolerability claim is not the same as a demonstrated absence of any human irritation reaction, and the review is industry-funded.[^norman2025]
- **"Chemical filters are toxic / hormone disruptors."** The FDA's Category III status reflects a data gap, not a finding of harm.[^fda2019] A 2025 case-control study found ensulizole elevated, alongside three other filters, in the follicular fluid of women with diminished ovarian reserve, an association that the study itself frames as needing further investigation, not a demonstrated cause of reduced fertility.[^tian2025]
