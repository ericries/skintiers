Alitretinoin is 9-cis-retinoic acid, a retinoid that exists in two very different medicines. In the United States the only FDA-approved alitretinoin product is **Panretin gel 0.1%**, a topical gel "indicated for the topical treatment of cutaneous lesions in adults with AIDS-related Kaposi's sarcoma (KS)", approved under NDA020886 with an initial US approval of 1999.[^dailymed] Separately, an **oral** 30 mg alitretinoin capsule is used for severe chronic hand eczema in some countries.

> **The thing worth knowing before anything else: the oral hand-eczema drug is not an American product.** A 2025 network meta-analysis describes oral alitretinoin 30 mg daily as approved by both the FDA and the EMA for chronic hand eczema.[^gupta] That is not borne out on the FDA side: a DailyMed search for alitretinoin returns **only Panretin gel**, with no oral alitretinoin label at all.[^dailymed] So if you have read that there is an approved oral pill for hand eczema, in the US there is not one you can be prescribed. The condition's actual options are on [[chronic-hand-eczema]] and [[best-chronic-hand-eczema-treatments]].

## The Rubric

**Cutaneous lesions of AIDS-related Kaposi's sarcoma (Panretin gel, the approved US use)**
- **Effect size: modest, and the appearance endpoint is weaker than the lesion endpoint.** Two vehicle-controlled trials measured lesion response at 35% versus 16% (Trial 1) and 36% versus 7% (Trial 2).[^dailymed] But the label also reports a separate FDA-reviewed **photograph response**, judging how the skin actually looked, at only **15% versus 4%** and **19% versus 2%**.[^dailymed] Nearly all responses were partial: "All responses were partial responses except 1% complete response for modified ACTG response in Trial 1."[^dailymed]
- **Evidence quality: gold-standard for the indication.** "PANRETIN GEL was evaluated in two multicenter, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled studies in adult patients with cutaneous lesions of AIDS-related KS", supporting a full NDA.[^dailymed] The grade reflects the trial design and approval, not a large effect.

**Severe chronic hand eczema (oral alitretinoin, not available in the US)**
- **Effect size: cannot be graded from a US primary source.** The comparative ranking that put it second to delgocitinib is on [[gupta-2025-hand-eczema-monotherapies-network-meta-analysis]] and is not repeated here.
- **Evidence quality: not assessable here.** There is no FDA label for an oral alitretinoin product to read, so this page does not grade the indication. Nothing on this site should be taken as a route to obtaining it.

## The Evidence

**What the approved product actually achieves.** Trial 1 enrolled 268 patients in the US and Canada, randomized to Panretin gel or matching vehicle for at least 12 weeks; Trial 2 was international, planned for 270 patients but "stopped early because of positive-interim results in the initial 82 patients", so its arms were small at 36 and 45.[^dailymed] Both reported the three endpoints above.

**The label is unusually candid about cosmetic outcome, and it is the most useful sentence on it.** It states: "Photographs of some patients revealed an erythematous and edematous response, leading to a cosmetically mixed outcome."[^dailymed] That is the explanation for the gap between the 35% lesion response and the 15% photograph response: the tumour shrank while the treated skin became red and swollen, so an observer judging appearance alone scored it as no better. For anyone reasoning about retinoids by analogy, this is a useful reminder that a measured lesion change and a visibly better result are not the same outcome.

**It works slowly.** "the cumulative percentage of patients who experienced a response was less than 1% at 2 weeks, 10% at 4 weeks, and 28% at 8 weeks".[^dailymed] A two-week trial of this drug would show essentially nothing.

**New lesions were common and were not counted against it.** About 50% of Trial 1 patients developed new lesions in untreated areas, and the response criteria deliberately excluded new and non-index lesions from counting as progression.[^dailymed] The reported response rates therefore describe the lesions that were treated, not the disease overall.

## Safety and Practical Limits

**Pregnancy.** The label states Panretin gel "can cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman", based on animal data and mechanism.[^dailymed] Retinoid teratogenicity is a class issue; the oral retinoid on this site with the fullest treatment of it is [[isotretinoin]].

**Contraindication.** It "is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity to retinoids or to any of the ingredients of the product".[^dailymed] There is no boxed warning on this label.

**Photosensitivity and an unusual interaction.** The label carries warnings for photosensitivity and for toxicity with DEET-containing products, so insect repellents matter here in a way they do not for most topicals.[^dailymed]

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. Panretin gel is a prescription treatment for a cancer-related skin lesion in people with AIDS, prescribed and monitored by a specialist. It is not a cosmetic retinoid, and the general retinoid case readers usually want is on [[retinoids]], [[tretinoin]] and [[adapalene]].
