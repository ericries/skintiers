Shea butter (INCI name Butyrospermum Parkii Butter, from the nut of the African shea tree, *Vitellaria paradoxa*) is a plant-derived triglyceride butter used as an occlusive/emollient in moisturizers, balms, and eczema-adjacent creams, dominated by oleic acid and stearic acid with a smaller unsaponifiable fraction of triterpenes.[^oleic][^stearic][^raffin] It appears almost exclusively as one ingredient among several occlusives, humectants, and botanical anti-inflammatories in published trials, so its clinical evidence is real but never isolated: every controlled study that reports a benefit is testing a multi-ingredient formula, not shea butter alone.[^jira][^draelos][^prakoeswa]

> In a split-body trial of 29 children (ages 2-15) with mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis, a moisturizer containing Butyrospermum parkii extract (with spent grain wax and argania oil) was compared to 1% hydrocortisone cream on opposite body sides for four weeks; both sides showed statistically significant SCORAD improvement by week 2 (p<0.001), with no significant difference between the two treatments by week 4.[^jira]

## The Rubric

**General moisturization / xerosis (topical, as part of an emollient formula)**
- **Effect size: real but not ingredient-specific.** Formulas containing shea butter alongside other occlusives (dimethicone), humectants (glycerin, hyaluronic acid), and barrier lipids (ceramides) have produced significant gains in skin hydration versus baseline and versus comparator moisturizers, including a 44% increase in corneometry-measured hydration after two weeks in one eczema study.[^draelos][^prakoeswa] Mechanistically this is unsurprising: shea butter's high oleic/stearic acid content is a classic occlusive emollient profile, similar in kind to other plant butters and oils.
- **Evidence quality: weak for shea butter specifically, moderate for the formulas it sits in.** No identified trial isolates shea butter against a shea-free vehicle; every positive result comes from a multi-ingredient cream, so the moisturizing benefit is attributable to the formula's occlusive/humectant system as a whole, not demonstrably to shea butter over any other emollient.[^draelos][^prakoeswa]

**Mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis, adjunct to (or comparator with) low-potency topical steroid**
- **Effect size: comparable to 1% hydrocortisone over 2-4 weeks in one small trial, but again as part of a blend.** In the Jirabundansuk 2014 split-body trial, a Butyrospermum parkii/spent grain wax/argania oil cream matched 1% hydrocortisone on SCORAD improvement by week 4 (p>0.05, no significant difference), and had a numerically (not statistically) higher remission rate.[^jira] Consistent with this, an intertrigo trial using a related shea-containing adsorbent lotion found anti-inflammatory efficacy statistically equivalent to 1% hydrocortisone for erythema and superior for pruritus reduction.[^leeyaphan]
- **Evidence quality: weak-to-moderate, small n, and confounded by co-actives.** The strongest trial (n=29) is a single-center, unblinded-for-treatment-side design with no vehicle-only arm; shea butter is one of three actives in the tested cream, so this cannot be read as "shea butter treats eczema," only "an emollient blend containing shea butter performed as well as a mild steroid in this small sample."[^jira] Pan-African dermatologist survey data note shea butter is among the emollients "widely available" and commonly used as first-line barrier support for atopic dermatitis in Sub-Saharan Africa, which reflects access and tradition more than trial evidence.[^otro]

**Anti-inflammatory / anti-itch effect from shea butter's unsaponifiables**
- **Effect size: plausible mechanism, not clinically quantified for shea alone.** Shea butter's unsaponifiable fraction (triterpene esters, phytosterols) has documented anti-inflammatory activity in animal and lipid-nanoparticle drug-delivery models, but those studies used shea butter as a carrier/vehicle for a separate anti-inflammatory drug (nimesulide), not as the active being tested for its own anti-inflammatory effect on skin.[^raffin]
- **Evidence quality: weak.** No human skin trial isolates shea butter's own anti-inflammatory contribution independent of its emollient/occlusive effect.

## How It Works

Shea butter is a plant lipid extracted from the nut of *Vitellaria paradoxa* (formerly *Butyrospermum parkii*), composed predominantly of triglycerides of oleic acid (C18H34O2) and stearic acid (C18H36O2), with smaller amounts of linoleic and palmitic acid, plus a 5-17% unsaponifiable fraction containing triterpene alcohols/esters and tocopherols.[^oleic][^stearic] As a topical emollient it works the way most fatty, semi-solid lipids do: it forms an occlusive film that slows transepidermal water loss and softens the stratum corneum, which is the mechanism behind essentially all of its reported moisturizing and barrier-support outcomes.[^prakoeswa] A separate line of research has used shea butter itself as a drug-delivery vehicle, exploiting its melting point and lipid composition to build nanoparticles that improve the bioavailability of a co-formulated anti-inflammatory drug (nimesulide); this demonstrates shea butter's pharmaceutical utility as a carrier, not a proven independent anti-inflammatory action on skin.[^raffin]

## The Evidence

**Shea-containing cream versus 1% hydrocortisone, split-body, childhood atopic dermatitis (topical, combination).** Twenty-nine children aged 2 to 15 with mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis had one body side randomized to a moisturizer containing spent grain wax, Butyrospermum parkii extract, and argania spinosa kernel oil, and the other side to 1% hydrocortisone cream, applied twice daily for four weeks. Both sides improved SCORAD significantly by week 2 (p<0.001); by week 4 the improvement was still present on both sides but "without being statistically significant different (p>0.05)" between them, and the shea-blend side had a numerically higher remission rate that did not reach significance.[^jira] *A small, unblinded, single-center trial found a shea-butter-containing emollient performed on par with a mild topical steroid for pediatric eczema over a month, but it cannot separate shea butter's contribution from the other two actives in the cream, and has not been independently replicated.*

**Botanical anti-inflammatory moisturizer, open-label, adult eczema (topical, combination).** Twenty-five adults with mild-to-moderate eczema used a moisturizer containing shea butter, dimethicone, glycerin, ceramides, allantoin, and bisabolol three times daily for two weeks, with investigator-assessed erythema, dryness, itching, and corneometry measured at baseline and week 2. There was a statistically significant improvement (P<0.001) across investigator-assessed signs, a 79% reduction in itching, and a 44% increase in corneometry-measured hydration (P<0.001).[^draelos] *A strong result, but open-label with no comparator arm and six other actives in the formula, so the effect cannot be credited to shea butter specifically.*

**Anti-inflammatory moisturizer versus non-anti-inflammatory moisturizer, double-blind, adult atopic dermatitis (topical, combination).** In a double-blind trial, adults with mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis used either a moisturizer containing shea butter plus other anti-inflammatory botanicals (bacterial lysate, allantoin, bisabolol, and plant extracts) or a moisturizer without those anti-inflammatory ingredients, for 14 days. Skin hydration rose from 35.97±6.04 to 66.06±15.84 in the anti-inflammatory group versus 40.74±10.94 to 56.12±8.34 in the control group, a significantly larger gain (p<0.05), and SCORAD also improved significantly more in the anti-inflammatory group.[^prakoeswa] *Shea butter was present as an emollient base in both study arms (it was also listed among the "anti-inflammatory ingredients" arm's occlusives), so this trial mainly demonstrates the value of adding other botanicals to a shea-containing base, not shea butter's own effect.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Deeply moisturizes and repairs the skin barrier."** Reasonably supported in direction: shea butter's fatty-acid profile is a legitimate occlusive emollient, and every multi-ingredient formula tested that contains it has produced significant hydration gains.[^draelos][^prakoeswa] The honest caveat is that no trial isolates shea butter from its co-ingredients, so "shea butter repairs the barrier" overstates what any single study actually shows about the ingredient alone versus other emollients.
- **"Anti-inflammatory, soothes eczema and irritated skin."** Partly supported by outcome, not by mechanism specific to shea. Shea-containing creams matched a low-potency steroid on SCORAD in one small pediatric trial and reduced erythema/pruritus comparably to hydrocortisone in an intertrigo trial, but these are blended-formula results, and shea butter's own anti-inflammatory unsaponifiables have only been studied as a drug-delivery vehicle for a separate anti-inflammatory drug, not tested alone on inflamed skin.[^jira][^leeyaphan][^raffin]
