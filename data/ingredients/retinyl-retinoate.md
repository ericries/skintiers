Retinyl retinoate (INCI Retinyl Retinoate) is a synthetic vitamin A derivative made by joining [[retinol]] and retinoic acid into a single ester, developed in Korea as a cosmetic [[retinoids|retinoid]]. Its originators describe it as an ester of all-trans retinoic acid (RA) and all-trans retinol, designed to have reduced toxicity due to blocking of the carboxyl end group of RA and higher skin regeneration activity than retinol.[^bjd] The synthesis paper reports it was made "with a condensing reaction between retinol and retinoic acid to improve the photo-stability," and showed "enhanced thermal stability and decreased photosensitivity, and exhibited decreased cell toxicity compared to that of retinol" in laboratory testing.[^synth] The composition and its use for wrinkles are covered by a patent assigned to the Korean cosmetics company Enprani Co. Ltd.[^patent]

## The Rubric

Grades below are relative and name comparators. They cover what the published trials measured, not proof that any finished product performs. The single most important caveat sits in the evidence-quality lines: every human trial and preclinical study to date shares authors and traces to the compound's patent holder, so nothing here has been independently replicated.

**Wrinkle improvement (versus retinol)**
- **Effect size: modest.** In small split-face trials the compound reduced wrinkle depth and roughness more than [[retinol]] at a comparable strength. The originating group reported that "the maximum roughness improvement rate (R2) for retinyl retinoate cream were 22% higher than that of retinol cream after 12 weeks" with 0.06% retinyl retinoate against 0.075% retinol.[^srt] The comparison is against an active retinoid rather than placebo alone, so the relevant quantity is the increment over an existing over-the-counter option. "Modest: beat 0.075% retinol on a roughness measure in small split-face studies, but the margin comes from one group's trials."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** The human evidence is two split-face trials (n=11 and n=22), each 8 to 12 weeks, both from the originating research group and its patent holder (Enprani).[^srt][^bjd][^patent] With no independent replication and small samples, the superiority-over-retinol finding is best treated as preliminary rather than established.

**Tolerability (irritation)**
- **Effect size: modest (lower irritancy, by design).** Esterifying the carboxyl end of retinoic acid is the stated route to reduced toxicity.[^bjd] Preclinically the molecule "exhibited decreased cell toxicity compared to that of retinol,"[^synth] and across the two human split-face trials no severe side-effects were observed.[^bjd]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** The support is in vitro toxicity assays plus adverse-event reporting in the same two small trials from the originating group; no study measured irritation in a head-to-head comparison, so the advantage is an unquantified, adverse-event-only signal, and it has not been measured against another modern derivative such as [[retinaldehyde]].[^synth][^bjd]

## The Evidence

The human evidence for retinyl retinoate is thin and comes from a single research group. The retinol comparison that the marketing rests on is a double-blind, randomized, controlled split-face study of 11 Korean women over 30 with periorbital wrinkles (Skin Research and Technology, 2011). Subjects applied 0.06% retinyl retinoate cream for 3 months, assessed every 4 weeks by global photodamage score, photographs, and replica image analysis. The authors report "a statistically significant improvement in facial wrinkles (P<0.05) in eleven volunteers," and that "the successive application of 0.06% retinyl retinoate cream for 3 months showed decreased depth and area of wrinkles in comparison with 0.075% retinol cream," with the visual and maximum-roughness (R2) improvement "22% higher than that of retinol cream after 12 weeks."[^srt]

An earlier report in the British Journal of Dermatology (2010) pooled two split-face studies in 46 Korean women. In the first, 24 patients completed a 12-week trial of 0.06% retinyl retinoate against placebo on the opposite side of the face; in the second, 22 patients completed an 8-week trial of 0.06% retinyl retinoate against 0.075% retinol. The authors state "the retinyl retinoate-treated wrinkles improved compared with wrinkles treated with placebo or retinol, as assessed by both the investigators and the subjects," with replica analysis showing significant improvements, especially in average roughness, and no severe side-effects observed.[^bjd] These are the same group's cohorts and share authors with the 2011 study, so they corroborate but do not independently confirm the retinol comparison.

The preclinical basis is a synthesis and in vitro study (Bioorganic & Medicinal Chemistry, 2008) from the same authors. It reports the hybrid "had enhanced thermal stability and decreased photosensitivity, and exhibited decreased cell toxicity compared to that of retinol," and that in receptor assays it "had higher inhibitory activity against c-Jun than retinol and showed superior effects on collagen synthesis compared to retinol."[^synth] The compound's composition and its cosmetic use for wrinkles are covered by patent US7173062B2, "Method for the improvement of skin wrinkles using retinyl retinoate," assigned to Enprani Co. Ltd, priority 2001 and granted in 2007, which describes retinyl retinoate as formed by condensing retinol and retinoic acid.[^patent]

The common thread is provenance. The clinical trials, the preclinical work, and the patent all originate with one group and the company that holds the composition rights, so the retinol-beating and low-irritation findings have not been reproduced by any independent laboratory. The comparison to retinaldehyde or hydroxypinacolone retinoate, the derivatives it is usually shelved beside, has not been tested at all; against those, its standing is unresolvable from the current evidence.

## Common Marketing Claims

Treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"More effective than retinol / a next-generation retinol."** The originating group's split-face trials did report better wrinkle and roughness outcomes than 0.075% retinol, including R2 "22% higher than that of retinol cream after 12 weeks."[^srt][^bjd] Because those results come from small trials (n=11 and n=22) by the compound's patent holder with no independent replication, the superiority claim is preliminary, not established. Discounted to preliminary.
- **"Photostable, won't degrade like retinol."** The synthesis paper reports "enhanced thermal stability and decreased photosensitivity" for the molecule in laboratory testing.[^synth] That is a measured chemistry property of the raw ingredient, not evidence that a finished product delivers more benefit; stability and clinical effect are separate questions.
- **"Gentle, low-irritation retinoid."** Supported only by in vitro "decreased cell toxicity"[^synth] and no severe side-effects in two small trials.[^bjd] No controlled irritation comparison against another modern derivative has been published.
