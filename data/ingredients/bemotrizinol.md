Bemotrizinol (INCI bis-ethylhexyloxyphenol methoxyphenyl triazine, trade name Tinosorb S) is a triazine-class organic sunscreen filter described in the chemistry literature as broad-spectrum and photostable, absorbing across both the UVB and UVA bands.[^pontes] It was the standard modern broad-spectrum filter in Europe, Australia, and much of Asia for roughly two decades before the FDA added it to the US OTC sunscreen monograph for the first time, in June 2026.[^fda2026]

> Bemotrizinol is described in the pharmaceutical-chemistry literature as "a broad-spectrum photostable organic UV filter," part of the triazine class whose defining properties are "photostability and UV broad-spectrum absorption (UVA and UVB)."[^pontes] In direct formulation testing it protected the photounstable UVA filter [[avobenzone]] from breaking down under simulated sunlight rather than degrading itself.[^chatelain] The FDA only added it to the US OTC sunscreen monograph, at concentrations up to 6%, as generally recognized as safe and effective (GRASE) in June 2026, decades after it entered use elsewhere.[^fda2026]

## The Rubric

The evidence splits cleanly: what bemotrizinol's own chemistry and formulation testing show, versus a US regulatory timeline that reflects market history rather than any efficacy or safety finding against it.

**Broad-spectrum UVB+UVA coverage and photostability**
- **Effect size: strong, as a photostabilizing broad-spectrum filter in mixed formulations.** A computational chemistry paper uses bemotrizinol as its case study of "a broad-spectrum photostable organic UV filter," noting that triazine compounds as a class are defined by "photostability and UV broad-spectrum absorption (UVA and UVB)."[^pontes] In direct formulation testing, adding bemotrizinol to an avobenzone-containing sunscreen "prevented the photodegradation of AVB in a concentration-dependent way, leading to a sustained SPF and UVA ratio even after irradiation with doses of up to 30 MED," and the same stabilizing effect held when avobenzone was combined with octinoxate.[^chatelain] Avobenzone is separately known to destabilize octinoxate in some formulations, the mirror image of avobenzone's own instability problem, covered on [[avobenzone]]. "Strong as a photostable broad-spectrum filter and as a stabilizer for avobenzone-based blends; the fetched studies characterize it through formulation testing and computation, not a standalone clinical SPF trial."
- **Evidence quality: solid experimental and computational, not clinical.** The photostability finding rests on a controlled formulation study (spectrophotometric SPF and UVA-ratio measurement plus chromatographic filter recovery after xenon-arc irradiation) corroborated by a separate TD-DFT computational characterization; no fetched human clinical trial isolates bemotrizinol's own SPF or UVA protection factor.[^chatelain][^pontes] The formulation study's authors were affiliated with Spirig Pharma, a Swiss sunscreen manufacturer that markets Tinosorb S-containing products, so it is not independent industry-free research; the underlying photostability characterization is corroborated only by the separate computational paper, not by an independent formulation trial.

**US regulatory availability (a status fact, not an efficacy grade)**
- Bemotrizinol sat outside the US OTC sunscreen monograph for years while a 2025 review of US sunscreen regulation named it among filters that "offer broader and more stable UV protection" than the older US-approved set, attributing the gap to "regulatory inertia" rather than a safety or efficacy finding against it.[^modern] The FDA's final administrative order amending Monograph M020, published in the Federal Register on June 10, 2026, added bemotrizinol at concentrations up to 6 percent as generally recognized as safe and effective, closing that gap.[^fda2026] Which other modern filters remain outside the US monograph, and why that matters for real-world UVA protection, is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

## How It Works

As an organic (chemical) filter, bemotrizinol absorbs UV photons and re-emits the energy as heat, the same general mechanism as other organic filters such as [[avobenzone]].[^pontes] The functional difference formulation testing demonstrates is stability under continued UV exposure: where avobenzone degrades under sunlight and typically needs a co-filter to hold its protection steady, bemotrizinol's addition to a formulation "prevented the photodegradation" of avobenzone across repeated UV doses rather than breaking down itself, which is why it is used as a stabilizing broad-spectrum co-filter rather than one that needs stabilizing.[^chatelain]

## The Evidence

**Photostabilization and broad-spectrum classification.** In an experimental oil-in-water sunscreen exposed to filtered xenon-arc radiation, adding bis-ethylhexyloxyphenol methoxyphenyl triazine (bemotrizinol, Tinosorb S) "prevented the photodegradation of AVB [avobenzone] in a concentration-dependent way, leading to a sustained SPF and UVA ratio even after irradiation with doses of up to 30 MED," and the same protective effect held when avobenzone was combined with ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate (octinoxate).[^chatelain] Octinoxate is separately known as a filter avobenzone can destabilize. The paper's own title describes bemotrizinol as "a new UV broadband filter."[^chatelain] Its authors were affiliated with Spirig Pharma, a Swiss sunscreen manufacturer that markets Tinosorb S-containing products, a limitation on the study's independence noted above. A separate computational chemistry paper (using time-dependent density functional theory to model the molecule) cites bemotrizinol as its worked case study of "a broad-spectrum photostable organic UV filter," within a triazine class whose defining physical-chemical properties are "photostability and UV broad-spectrum absorption (UVA and UVB)."[^pontes]

**US regulatory status.** The FDA's final administrative order amending Over-the-Counter Monograph M020 (Sunscreen Drug Products for Over-the-Counter Human Use), published in the Federal Register on June 10, 2026, added bemotrizinol at concentrations up to 6 percent as an approved, generally recognized as safe and effective, sunscreen active ingredient.[^fda2026] A 2025 review of US sunscreen filter regulation names bemotrizinol among filters that "offer broader and more stable UV protection" than the older US-approved set, states such filters "have demonstrated favorable safety and efficacy profiles but remain unavailable in the U.S. due to regulatory inertia," and frames the FDA's newer order-request pathway as the route to closing that gap, which the 2026 order did for bemotrizinol specifically.[^modern]

## Common Marketing Claims

None of the claims below is established beyond what The Evidence above supports.

- **"The best/superior UV filter."** The fetched studies show bemotrizinol resists photodegradation and stabilizes avobenzone in mixed formulations[^chatelain] and describe it generically as broad-spectrum,[^pontes] but no fetched head-to-head clinical trial compares its finished-product SPF or UVA protection factor against other modern filters (bisoctrizole, the Mexoryls) covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].
- **"Only just approved in the US because it isn't safe."** The 2026 FDA order classifies bemotrizinol as generally recognized as safe and effective, and the review literature attributes its long US absence to "regulatory inertia" in the approval process, not a safety finding against the filter itself.[^fda2026][^modern]
