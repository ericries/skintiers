Tris-biphenyl triazine (TBPT, INCI name Tris-Biphenyl Triazine, CAS 31274-51-8, sold under the BASF trade name Tinosorb A2B) is an organic ultraviolet filter formulated as an aqueous dispersion of nanoparticles rather than dissolved in the oil phase like most organic filters.[^pubchem][^naumov] It was the first new filter added to Annex VI of EU Cosmetics Regulation (EC) No. 1223/2009, the list of UV filters permitted in cosmetic products, since that regulation took effect in July 2013.[^couteau]

> A pharmaceutical-technology paper describes TBPT as "very effective... and very photostable," reporting that under a solar simulator its "SPF and UVA-PF do not vary after 2h of irradiation."[^couteau] A BASF-affiliated formulation-physics study found its protection is split by wavelength: in the UVB range TBPT works mainly by absorbing light, while across UVA I (340-400 nm), where the molecule itself absorbs little, the same study found its protection "is completely attributed to scattering" from the particles.[^sohn]

## The Rubric

**Broad-spectrum photoprotection and photostability**
- **Effect size: notable, with a particle-scattering mechanism unusual among organic filters.** A French university group testing the newly launched filter reported it "enables 2 SPF units and 1 UVA-PF units to be obtained respectively, by percentage of use" and found the SPF and UVA-PF "do not vary after 2h of irradiation in a solar simulator," describing it as "very effective... and very photostable."[^couteau] A BASF formulation-physics study of the same filter found that UVB protection is driven mainly by absorption, while in the UVA I band (340-400 nm) "the protection is completely attributed to scattering," and that scattering's share of the effect declines once use concentration exceeds about 3% because absorption then dominates.[^sohn] "Notable and photostable as a standalone filter per the one fetched independent formulation study; the mechanistic detail on how much of that protection is scattering versus absorption comes from the filter manufacturer's own lab."
- **Evidence quality: mixed independence.** The efficacy and photostability figures come from an academic French pharmacy-school group with no disclosed manufacturer affiliation,[^couteau] but the scattering-versus-absorption mechanism study was conducted by BASF Grenzach, the company that manufactures and sells Tinosorb A2B, and a separate BASF-authored review claims TBPT has "the highest efficacy of all cosmetic UV absorbers in the market," a superlative from an interested party that no independent head-to-head trial in the fetched sources confirms.[^sohn][^hueglin] No fetched source reports a human in vivo SPF or UVA-PF trial on a finished sunscreen product; the Couteau figures and the BASF scattering data are both in vitro/formulation measurements.

**Photostabilizer for avobenzone-containing formulations**
- **Effect size: supportive, in combination formulations only.** Independent Brazilian academic testing found that adding TBPT to formulations of avobenzone, octyl methoxycinnamate, and retinyl palmitate "improved the photostability of AVO and RP" under UVA exposure, though a different photostabilizer (ethylhexyl methoxycrylene) performed better at protecting retinyl palmitate specifically and at reducing the phototoxicity of the avobenzone/octyl methoxycinnamate combination.[^scarpin] A separate analytical-chemistry paper developing an HPLC assay for the pair states that combining avobenzone and TBPT "enables broad-spectrum photoprotection and improved photostability," corroborating the direction of the effect without adding new efficacy data of its own.[^miranda] General avobenzone photostability is covered on [[avobenzone]].
- **Evidence quality: one independent formulation study plus one analytical-methods paper**, neither a clinical trial; "improved" is not quantified against an avobenzone-alone control on human skin.

**Environmental and dermal-uptake safety signals**
- A 2026 non-animal hazard-and-risk screen of nine UV filters, run by a cosmetics manufacturer's safety group with an academic co-author, modeled predicted environmental concentrations against predicted no-effect concentrations and reported "acceptable environmental margins of exposure" for TBPT along with three other filters in that screen.[^pinto] Separately, a 2026 computational study modeling how UV filters bind human membrane transporters found TBPT formed one of the most stable predicted complexes in its dataset, with a solute-carrier transporter (SLC22A31), and the authors frame such stable binding as a way transporter interactions "may facilitate localized toxicological effects."[^xing] This is an in silico prediction, not a measured toxicology finding, and no fetched source reports human dermal absorption or systemic-exposure data for TBPT specifically.

## How It Works

TBPT is supplied as an aqueous dispersion of nanoparticles rather than dissolved in the sunscreen's oil phase, the formulation route BASF also used for the related filter [[bisoctrizole]].[^naumov] Photophysical modeling of the particles found they can pack into stacked molecular aggregates in water that shift and reshape the filter's UV absorbance spectrum compared to the same molecule dissolved in an organic solvent.[^naumov] The practical consequence, measured directly in a separate BASF study, is a mechanism split by wavelength: UVB protection comes mainly from the molecule absorbing light, while UVA I protection (340-400 nm), a band the molecule itself absorbs weakly, comes from the particles scattering light instead, a mechanism most dissolved organic filters do not rely on.[^sohn]

## The Evidence

**First launch efficacy and photostability data.** An academic paper from a French pharmacy faculty, published shortly after the filter's EU market launch, reported that TBPT was "the first filter to be registered on Annexe VI of 'Cosmetics Regulation' (EC) No. 1223/2009... since the regulation came into force in July 2013," found it "enables 2 SPF units and 1 UVA-PF units to be obtained respectively, by percentage of use," and reported the SPF and UVA-PF "do not vary after 2h of irradiation in a solar simulator," concluding it is "both very effective... and very photostable."[^couteau]

**Wavelength-dependent scattering mechanism.** BASF Grenzach researchers measured light transmittance of TBPT dispersions and finished formulations with and without an integrating sphere to isolate scattering from absorption. They found "in the UVB range the efficacy of TBPT is primarily provided by absorption with little scattering," while "in the UVAI range (340-400 nm), where TBPT is not absorbing[,] the protection is completely attributed to scattering," and that scattering's contribution fell once use concentration passed roughly 3% by weight as absorption began to dominate instead.[^sohn]

**Photostabilization of co-formulated filters.** A Brazilian academic study exposing avobenzone/octyl methoxycinnamate/retinyl palmitate mixtures to UVA radiation found that adding TBPT "improved the photostability of AVO and RP," alongside two other tested stabilizers, though it was not the most effective of the three at protecting retinyl palmitate or at reducing phototoxicity in the OECD 3T3 neutral red uptake assay.[^scarpin] A 2026 analytical-chemistry paper validating an HPLC method for quantifying avobenzone and TBPT together in formulations states the combination "enables broad-spectrum photoprotection and improved photostability," a claim consistent with, but not independently re-testing, the Scarpin findings.[^miranda]

**Aquatic and dermal-uptake safety screens.** A 2026 non-animal (in silico/in vitro) hazard-and-risk assessment of nine cosmetic UV filters, led by a Brazilian cosmetics company's product-safety group with a university co-author, modeled predicted environmental concentration against predicted no-effect concentration for water and sediment and reported "acceptable environmental margins of exposure" for TBPT among four of the nine filters screened.[^pinto] A separate 2026 computational study used molecular docking and dynamics simulations across nearly 24,000 UV-filter/transporter complex pairs and found TBPT formed a stable predicted complex with the human transporter SLC22A31 (binding affinity -10.8 kcal/mol, ligand RMSD held below 5 Å over 200 ns), a finding the authors frame as illustrating how "specific UVF-SLC interactions... may facilitate localized toxicological effects" without measuring any actual toxicological outcome.[^xing]

## Common Marketing Claims

None of the claims below is established beyond what The Evidence above supports.

- **"The most effective UV filter on the market."** A BASF-authored review states TBPT "exhibits the highest efficacy of all cosmetic UV absorbers in the market (measured by area under the UV spectrum)," but this is the filter manufacturer's own claim, and no fetched independent head-to-head trial compares TBPT's finished-product SPF or UVA protection factor against other modern filters such as [[bemotrizinol]] or [[bisoctrizole]].[^hueglin]
- **"Nanoparticle sunscreen filters are unsafe."** No fetched source reports a measured toxicological harm from TBPT's nanoparticulate form; the one fetched computational transporter-binding study is an in silico prediction framed by its own authors as a hypothesis about how such binding "may facilitate" localized effects, not a demonstrated one.[^xing] General UV-filter formulation and monograph status is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].
