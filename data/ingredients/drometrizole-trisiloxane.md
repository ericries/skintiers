Drometrizole trisiloxane (its trade name is Mexoryl XL) is a silicone-based organic UV filter, with a trisiloxane backbone containing three silicon atoms (molecular formula C24H39N3O3Si3), developed and patented by L'Oreal.[^pubchem][^turner] An independent 2025 review of international sunscreen filters reports it has "two peaks at 303 and 344 nm," giving it absorption in both the UVB and UVA bands from a single molecule, and groups it with the other newer international filters ([[avobenzone]] alternatives bemotrizinol and bisoctrizole, covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]) as offering "broader and more stable UV protection" than the current US filter set, with "favorable safety and efficacy profiles."[^turner] It is, per that same review, "patented by L'Oreal and used exclusively in their products," most often alongside the related water-soluble filter ecamsule (Mexoryl SX), and it is not part of the FDA's OTC sunscreen monograph.[^turner]

> Drometrizole trisiloxane absorbs across both UVB (peak 303 nm) and UVA (peak 344 nm) from a single molecule, a broader single-filter spectral range than [[avobenzone]]'s UVA-only coverage, and a 2025 independent review credits it with "broader and more stable UV protection" than the US filter set.[^turner] But the clinical data specific to the isolated filter are thin: the one formulation trial located was manufacturer-run by its inventor, L'Oreal,[^moyal] and as a proprietary L'Oreal filter it has never been submitted for, or added to, the FDA's OTC sunscreen monograph, so it does not appear in sunscreens sold in the US.[^turner]

## The Rubric

**Spectral coverage (UVB + UVA, on the underlying chemistry)**
- Effect size: notable. The dual-peak characterization (303 nm and 344 nm) comes from a single 2025 narrative review that also credits the filter with "broader and more stable UV protection" than the older US filter set.[^turner] An earlier, independent review classifies it more simply among the "organic UVA sunscreening agents," alongside ecamsule, bemotrizinol, and bisoctrizole, without detailing a separate UVB peak.[^latha] Both sources agree it functions as a broad-spectrum organic filter used internationally rather than in the US.
- Evidence quality: preliminary. This is a spectral/regulatory characterization synthesized in narrative reviews, not a controlled clinical-endpoint trial of the isolated filter.

**Photoprotection against UV-induced pigmentation (paired with ecamsule)**
- Effect size: modest, and reported only in combination. In an in vivo study measuring protection against solar-simulated-radiation-induced pigmentation, "protection against pigmentation correlates with the concentration of Mexoryl SX," and the results show "a synergetic effect of Mexoryl SX when associated with Mexoryl XL"; products with high UVA protection prevented pigmentation about as well as they prevented sunburn, while products with low UVA protection prevented pigmentation less well.[^moyal] This tests the filter pair and a complete formula, not drometrizole trisiloxane on its own.
- Evidence quality: preliminary and manufacturer-run. The trial was conducted by L'Oreal Recherche, the R&D arm of the company that patents both Mexoryl filters, and no independent replication was located.[^moyal]

**Skin tolerability**
- Case reports document allergic contact dermatitis to drometrizole trisiloxane in sunscreen users, including one patient with concomitant sensitivities to other UV filters and one, later diagnosed with frontal fibrosing alopecia, in whom the filter allergy was identified on patch testing.[^hughes][^pastor] These are individual case reports, not incidence data, so they establish that contact allergy to the filter occurs without indicating how common it is.

## How It Works

As an organic (chemical) filter, drometrizole trisiloxane absorbs UV photons and re-emits the energy as heat, the same general mechanism as [[avobenzone]] and the other organic filters, rather than scattering light the way mineral filters do. Its distinguishing features are chemical: a trisiloxane (silicone) backbone that makes it oil-soluble, unlike its usual formulation partner ecamsule (Mexoryl SX), which is water-soluble,[^turner][^pubchem] and two separate absorption peaks, at 303 nm (UVB) and 344 nm (UVA), rather than the single UVA-band peak typical of filters like avobenzone.[^turner] It is a proprietary L'Oreal molecule, patented and, per the 2025 review, "used exclusively" in L'Oreal-owned brands.[^turner] Because it was never submitted through the FDA's sunscreen monograph safety-and-efficacy testing process, it is absent from the FDA's list of permitted actives and does not appear in US-market sunscreens, even though it is used in the same brands' international formulas; a US OTC monograph sunscreen instead relies on older filters such as avobenzone, whose UVA coverage and photostability are comparatively weaker, as set out on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^turner]

## The Evidence

**Spectral coverage.** Turner and Torgerson's 2025 review of the gap between US and international sunscreen filters states that "TDSA has a peak UV absorption at 345 nm, whereas DT has two peaks at 303 and 344 nm," and groups drometrizole trisiloxane (DT) with bemotrizinol, bisoctrizole, and TDSA (ecamsule) as filters that "offer broader and more stable UV protection" and "have demonstrated favorable safety and efficacy profiles but remain unavailable in the U.S. due to regulatory inertia."[^turner] The review also states the two Mexoryl filters are "organic broad spectrum UV filters patented by L'Oreal and used exclusively in their products," and that DT "is the oil-soluble form" paired with the water-soluble TDSA.[^turner] An earlier review by Latha and colleagues classifies the same filter more simply: "ecamsule (terephthalylidene dicamphor sulphonic acid), dometrizole trisiloxane, bemotrizinol, and bisoctrizole are considered organic UVA sunscreening agents," without separately describing a UVB peak.[^latha]

**Pigmentation protection.** Moyal's 2004 study used the standardized SPF test method, adapted to measure delayed pigmentation, to compare sunscreens containing "Mexoryl SX and Mexoryl XL, and complete formula." It reported that "protection against pigmentation correlates with the concentration of Mexoryl SX" and found "a synergetic effect of Mexoryl SX when associated with Mexoryl XL"; sunscreens matched for SPF but differing in UVA protection differed in how well they prevented pigmentation, concluding that "SPF determination was not sufficient to account for the efficiency in preventing pigmentation and that UVA protection was an essential part of this prevention."[^moyal] The author is based at L'Oreal Recherche, the manufacturer of both filters tested.[^moyal]

**Contact allergy.** Two published case reports describe allergic contact dermatitis attributed to drometrizole trisiloxane in sunscreen users: one in a patient with concomitant sensitivities to other sunscreen filters,[^hughes] and one, more recently, in a patient subsequently diagnosed with frontal fibrosing alopecia.[^pastor]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Photostable."** The only independent characterization located describes drometrizole trisiloxane, alongside three other newer filters, as offering "broader and more stable UV protection" than the US filter set in a general comparison; this is not a dedicated instrumented photostability assay of the isolated molecule.[^turner]
- **"Protects against pigmentation/dark spots."** The pigmentation-protection data are for the filter combined with ecamsule in a full formula, from a single manufacturer-run L'Oreal study, not for drometrizole trisiloxane isolated or in a generic concentration.[^moyal]
