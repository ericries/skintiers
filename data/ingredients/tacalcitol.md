Tacalcitol (1α,24(R)-dihydroxyvitamin D3, INCI/chemical name, molecular formula C27H44O3) is a synthetic vitamin D3 analog used as a once-daily topical for stable plaque [[psoriasis]], formulated in Western markets as a 4 μg/g ointment (versus a twice-daily 2 μg/g formulation historically used in Japan).[^pubchem][^peters] It is chemically and mechanistically a peer of [[calcipotriene]] (calcipotriol) and was the active comparator arm in two large industry-sponsored trials of calcipotriol/betamethasone combination products, in which it consistently underperformed the combination and, in one trial, barely more than tripled the response rate of vehicle alone.[^ortonne][^langley]

> In a randomized, placebo-controlled, right-left comparison in 122 Caucasian patients, once-daily 4 μg/g tacalcitol ointment reduced the erythema/infiltration/desquamation score sum significantly more than placebo at every timepoint starting week 2 (P<0.0001) over 8 weeks, with local irritation in 12.3% of patients and no clinically relevant change in serum calcium.[^kerkhof]

## The Rubric

**Stable plaque psoriasis, once-daily monotherapy (topical)**
- **Effect size: real but modest, and clearly behind calcipotriol-based regimens in every head-to-head design available.** Against vehicle, tacalcitol produced a significantly greater reduction in the combined erythema/infiltration/desquamation score from week 2 through week 8 in a 122-patient placebo-controlled trial.[^kerkhof] In a larger 458-patient, investigator-blinded, 3-arm trial, 17.9% of tacalcitol-treated patients reached "clear or almost clear" at 8 weeks versus 5.5% on gel vehicle (P<0.001), but 39.9% on a calcipotriol/betamethasone two-compound gel (P<0.001 versus tacalcitol).[^langley] In a separate 501-patient trial where tacalcitol served as the 8-week active comparator to a calcipotriol/betamethasone regimen, mean PASI reduction with tacalcitol was 33.3% at week 4 and 38.4% at week 8, versus 65.0% and 59.0% for the comparison regimen (P<0.001 for both).[^ortonne] Tacalcitol beats doing nothing, clearly, but every trial that puts it against a calcipotriol-containing regimen shows it losing by a wide margin.
- **Evidence quality: moderate, resting on a handful of trials, some industry-funded, with the sponsor-favorable comparisons all coming from the calcipotriol manufacturer's own combination-product program.** The core placebo-controlled evidence is one randomized, double-blind, intraindividual trial (122 patients).[^kerkhof] The comparative trials showing tacalcitol's inferiority to calcipotriol/betamethasone (Ortonne 2004, n=501; Langley 2011, n=458) were designed to demonstrate the sponsor's combination product's superiority, which does not make the tacalcitol data wrong but means the comparator arm was not the trial's primary interest.[^ortonne][^langley] There is no large, independent, contemporary trial isolating tacalcitol against a modern comparator built around tacalcitol itself.

**Adjunct to PUVA phototherapy (topical, combination)**
- **Effect size: modest UVA-sparing benefit, similar to tazarotene.** In a 31-patient, observer-blinded, intrapatient trial, adding once-daily tacalcitol ointment or 0.1% tazarotene gel to four-times-weekly PUVA significantly reduced the cumulative UVA dose and number of exposures needed to clear lesions versus PUVA alone (P<0.01 for both combinations), with tacalcitol better tolerated than the higher-strength tazarotene.[^tzaneva]
- **Evidence quality: low, small single trial.** Twenty-four completers in one intrapatient comparison; no independent replication.[^tzaneva]

## How It Works

Tacalcitol is 1α,24(R)-dihydroxyvitamin D3 (C27H44O3), a synthetic analog of the active vitamin D hormone calcitriol that differs in side-chain hydroxylation from [[calcipotriene]] (calcipotriol), another vitamin D3 analog built on the same 1α,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 template.[^pubchem] Like other vitamin D3 analogs, it binds the vitamin D receptor to modulate gene transcription, inhibiting keratinocyte proliferation and promoting normal differentiation, the core defect in psoriatic plaques.[^kerkhof9603664] In hairless mouse skin, tacalcitol dose-dependently inhibited TPA-induced inflammatory cell infiltration (largely neutrophils), reduced expression of the chemokines MIP-2 and KC (the functional analog of human IL-8), and inhibited mast cell degranulation, evidence for an anti-inflammatory action layered on top of its antiproliferative effect.[^sato] A flow-cytometric study of psoriatic plaques treated with tacalcitol found a significant decrease in proliferating basal keratinocytes in verum-treated lesions that was not seen with placebo, consistent with an antiproliferative mechanism as the dominant clinical driver.[^glade] Systemically, tacalcitol shows no significant absorption through skin at labeled doses, and repeated 8-week application of the 4 μg/g once-daily ointment did not cause hypercalcemia or hypercalciuria in trials.[^petersreview]

## The Evidence

**Placebo-controlled, once-daily 4 μg/g ointment, right-left intraindividual (topical).** A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, right-left comparison in 122 Caucasian patients with psoriasis vulgaris tested once-daily 4 μg/g tacalcitol ointment against placebo ointment for 8 weeks. "The score sum of erythema, infiltration and desquamation was influenced significantly more by tacalcitol ointment than by placebo (P < 0.0001) at every control point, starting from week 2." Local skin irritation was reported by 12.3% of patients, discontinuation for irritation occurred in only one patient, and laboratory markers including serum calcium, phosphate, calcitonin, and parathormone showed no clinically relevant change.[^kerkhof] *A solid placebo-controlled trial establishing that tacalcitol works against vehicle from as early as week 2, with a favorable local and systemic safety profile, though it does not compare tacalcitol with any other active treatment.*

**Three-arm trial versus calcipotriol/betamethasone gel and vehicle (topical, 8 weeks).** A randomized, investigator-blinded trial in 458 patients with at least moderately severe psoriasis vulgaris compared once-daily calcipotriol/betamethasone dipropionate two-compound gel, once-daily tacalcitol ointment, and the gel vehicle alone. At week 8, "clear or almost clear" by Investigator's Global Assessment was reached by 39.9% on the combination gel, 17.9% on tacalcitol, and 5.5% on vehicle (P<0.001 for the combination versus both other arms). Adverse drug reactions were significantly less frequent with the combination gel than with either tacalcitol or vehicle.[^langley] *This is the closest thing to direct evidence that tacalcitol beats an inert vehicle in a large, contemporary trial, but the same trial shows a calcipotriol-based combination roughly doubling tacalcitol's response rate.*

**Active-comparator trial versus calcipotriol/betamethasone-then-calcipotriol (topical, 8 weeks).** A randomized, double-blind trial in 501 patients compared calcipotriol/betamethasone combination for 4 weeks followed by calcipotriol alone for 4 weeks, against tacalcitol as the active comparator for the full 8 weeks. Mean percentage PASI reduction was 65.0% versus 33.3% at week 4, and 59.0% versus 38.4% at week 8 (P<0.001 for both), favoring the calcipotriol-based regimen.[^ortonne] *A large, well-controlled trial in which tacalcitol was the losing active comparator, achieving roughly half the PASI reduction of the calcipotriol-based regimen at both timepoints.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"A convenient once-daily vitamin D treatment for psoriasis."** True as far as it goes: the 4 μg/g formulation is applied once daily and the placebo-controlled trial data support real efficacy over vehicle with good tolerability (irritation in about 12% of patients, no hypercalcemia at 8 weeks).[^kerkhof][^petersreview] The convenience claim doesn't address that its effect size trails calcipotriol-based regimens substantially in every head-to-head trial available.
- **"Comparable to standard vitamin D psoriasis therapy."** Not well supported by the trials that exist. In both large trials that pitted tacalcitol against a calcipotriol/betamethasone regimen, tacalcitol's response rate or PASI reduction was roughly half that of the comparator (17.9% vs 39.9% clear/almost clear; 38.4% vs 59.0% PASI reduction at week 8), a meaningful and consistent gap rather than statistical noise.[^langley][^ortonne]
