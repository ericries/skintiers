Solubilized Coal Tar Extract is a refined, water-soluble processed form of crude coal tar, sold by Neutrogena under the trade name Neutar and used as the active ingredient in OTC anti-dandruff, anti-seborrheic dermatitis, and anti-psoriasis shampoos.[^scheinfeld] It is labeled by its own percentage alongside the equivalent crude coal tar it delivers, for example "Solubilized Coal Tar Extract 5.00% (Coal Tar, 1.00%)," and current DailyMed labels show manufacturers standardizing it so the declared coal tar equivalent falls inside the FDA's 0.5% to 5% OTC monograph range for coal tar.[^tgel-es][^tgel-orig] This page covers the delivery form only; the disease evidence for coal tar's antiproliferative, anti-inflammatory action on dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis scaling belongs to [[coal-tar]] and is not repeated here.

> Current DailyMed OTC shampoo labels list the active ingredient as "Solubilized Coal Tar Extract 2.50% (Coal Tar, 0.50%)" in an original-strength product and "Solubilized Coal Tar Extract 5.00% (Coal Tar, 1.00%)" in an extra-strength product, a consistent 5:1 extract-to-coal-tar ratio; a 2004 case report describing prior Neutrogena T/Gel instead cites "4% neutar solubilized coal tar extract (1.0% coal tar)," a 4:1 ratio for the same 1.0% coal tar dose.[^tgel-orig][^tgel-es][^scheinfeld]

## The Rubric

**Scaling of dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis (as a coal-tar delivery vehicle)**
- **Effect size: inherits [[coal-tar]]'s grade, modest and established but not independently studied for this delivery form.** No identified clinical trial isolates solubilized coal tar extract shampoo against crude coal tar or vehicle; the ingredient's therapeutic case rests entirely on the fact that it is a standardized source of coal tar itself, dosed within the same 0.5% to 1.0% range covered by the FDA monograph.[^tgel-orig][^tgel-es]
- **Evidence quality: label and case-report level only, no controlled comparison.** The strongest primary evidence found is a single case report in which a patient's scalp plaques improved on a regimen that included "4% neutar solubilized coal tar extract (1.0% coal tar)" shampoo alongside topical betamethasone and calcipotriene, which cannot isolate the shampoo's contribution from the concurrent steroid and vitamin D analog treatment.[^scheinfeld] There is no randomized trial found that tests the solubilized extract on its own.

**Cosmetic elegance (less staining, odor, and mess than crude coal tar)**
- **Effect size: plausible but unverified.** Solubilizing coal tar into a clear or translucent shampoo base is the evident purpose of this processing step, and it is the reason such products can be marketed as gel-like liquids rather than the thick, tarry pastes and ointments crude coal tar preparations require. No comparative study of staining, odor, or user tolerability between solubilized-extract shampoos and crude coal tar formulations was found.
- **Evidence quality: none identified.** This is a manufacturing and formulation claim, not a tested clinical endpoint.

## How It Works

Solubilized coal tar extract is coal tar that has been processed into a soluble liquid concentrate so it can be blended uniformly into a shampoo base rather than applied as a crude, viscous tar. Whatever therapeutic action it has comes from the coal tar it contains, not a distinct mechanism: coal tar is understood to be antiproliferative and anti-inflammatory, slowing the excess keratinocyte turnover that drives scaling in dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, and psoriasis, a mechanism detailed on [[coal-tar]] and not re-derived here. The two DailyMed-listed OTC shampoos in current commerce label their active ingredient as a fixed multiple of the crude coal tar it delivers, 5 parts extract to 1 part coal tar, while the earlier Neutrogena T/Gel formulation described in a 2004 case report used a 4:1 ratio for the same 1.0% coal tar dose, indicating the "solubilized coal tar extract" percentage is a manufacturer-specific standardization rather than a fixed industry ratio, and the number that matters for potency is the parenthetical coal tar percentage, not the extract percentage.[^tgel-orig][^tgel-es][^scheinfeld]

## The Evidence

**Case report, combination regimen including 4% Neutar solubilized coal tar extract shampoo (topical, scalp).** A 40-year-old man with scalp and elbow/knee psoriasis was prescribed betamethasone valerate foam for the scalp plus "a tar shampoo contain-ing 4% neutar solubilized coal tar extract (1.0% coal tar) three times a week"; after 2 weeks on this scalp regimen "the plaques became much less keratotic," and his scalp was reported "almost clear" after further weeks of the combined regimen.[^scheinfeld] *This is a single uncontrolled case report with a concurrent topical steroid, so it cannot show what the shampoo alone contributed; it documents that this specific product exists and was used in practice, not that it works.*

**DailyMed labels, two current OTC coal tar shampoos (regulatory, not clinical).** Two Walgreens-brand shampoos currently on file with the FDA declare "Solubilized Coal Tar Extract 2.50% (Coal Tar, 0.50%)" and "Solubilized Coal Tar Extract 5.00% (Coal Tar, 1.00%)" as their active ingredient, both under the purpose "Anti-dandruff, anti-seborrheic dermatitis, anti-psoriasis" and both within the FDA's monograph-permitted 0.5% to 5% coal tar range.[^tgel-orig][^tgel-es] *This confirms the delivered coal tar dose is monograph-compliant and consistent across strength tiers of one product line, but a drug label is a regulatory declaration, not a clinical efficacy trial.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"A cleaner, less messy way to get coal tar's benefits."** The premise is plausible on its face, solubilizing coal tar into a shampoo base is what lets it be sold as a clear liquid rather than a tarry paste, but no comparative study of staining, odor, or tolerability against crude coal tar preparations was found, so this reads as a reasonable formulation rationale rather than a demonstrated advantage.
- **"[Brand]'s proprietary extract works better than plain coal tar."** Not supported. The active ingredient declared on the label is still coal tar at 0.5% to 1.0%, the same dose range and same FDA monograph that governs crude coal tar products; nothing in the sources found suggests the solubilization step changes potency, only deliverability.
