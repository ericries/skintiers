Arbutin is a glycoside of hydroquinone, chemically hydroquinone attached to a glucose molecule by a glycosidic bond, naturally present in the leaves of bearberry (genus *Arctostaphylos*) and other plants in the Ericaceae family.[^pubchem] Skincare formulations also use a separate compound, alpha-arbutin, studied in cream form at 5% to 7% for melasma.[^teera][^tanta] It is marketed as a gentler tyrosinase-inhibiting alternative to hydroquinone, but the enzyme data say it is not a stronger inhibitor, and the clinical comparisons against hydroquinone-based treatment are limited to one small trial.

> On isolated human tyrosinase, arbutin inhibits the enzyme about as weakly as hydroquinone itself, both needing millimolar concentrations against a target where a newer compound worked in the low micromolar range.[^mann] In the one independent, evaluator-blinded trial to test it clinically, a cream combining 5% alpha-arbutin and 2% kojic acid was statistically indistinguishable from a hydroquinone-based triple combination cream on objective melasma severity and melanin measures over 12 weeks, though the triple combination scored better on physician-rated improvement and caused more irritation.[^tanta]

## The Rubric

**Hyperpigmentation and melasma (topical)**
- **Effect size: modest, and not shown to beat an active comparator on the most objective measure.** In a split-face, randomized, evaluator-blinded pilot trial, 30 melasma patients had one side of the face treated with a cream combining 5% alpha-arbutin and 2% kojic acid and the other side with a triple combination cream (hydroquinone, tretinoin, and a corticosteroid) for 12 weeks. Melanin index and modified Melasma Area and Severity Index scores showed "no difference between groups" (mMASI p=0.344; melanin index p=0.268), while physician global assessment improved only on the triple-combination side (p=0.032).[^tanta] Combined with ten weekly Q-switched Nd:YAG laser sessions, a 7% alpha-arbutin solution was part of a regimen that produced excellent pigment clearance (more than 81% reduction) in 30% of 35 patients with refractory melasma, but the laser makes arbutin's own contribution impossible to isolate.[^polnikorn] "Modest, and only shown to be statistically indistinguishable from, not better than, an active hydroquinone-containing comparator in the one independent trial; the laser-combination data cannot separate what arbutin itself contributed."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** One small (30-patient), no-conflict-of-interest split-face RCT against an active comparator, no vehicle- or placebo-controlled trial of arbutin alone, and one uncontrolled case series combining arbutin with laser treatment.[^tanta][^polnikorn]

## How It Works

Arbutin is hydroquinone joined to a sugar by a glycosidic bond; the PubChem compound record describes it as "hydroquinone attached to a beta-D-glucopyranosyl residue at position 4 via a glycosidic linkage" and notes it is "functionally related to a hydroquinone."[^pubchem] Both compounds are presumed to act by inhibiting tyrosinase, the rate-limiting enzyme in melanin synthesis, but a screen of 50,000 compounds against recombinant human tyrosinase found that "hydroquinone and its derivative arbutin only weakly inhibited human tyrosinase with a half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) in the millimolar range," compared with an IC50 of 1.1 micromol/L for a newly identified compound (Thiamidol) tested in the same screen, a gap of roughly a thousandfold.[^mann] A review covering three decades of arbutin research reports that dermatitis from arbutin use is rare but flags a specific concern: caution is warranted "especially from the viewpoint that hydroquinone may be generated during product use," meaning arbutin-containing products are not guaranteed to be free of the compound they are marketed as a substitute for.[^boo]

## The Evidence

**Melasma, alpha-arbutin plus kojic acid versus a hydroquinone-based triple combination (topical).** A split-face, randomized, evaluator-blinded pilot trial assigned one side of the face in 30 melasma patients to a cream combining 5% alpha-arbutin and 2% kojic acid, and the other side to a triple combination cream (hydroquinone, tretinoin, and a corticosteroid), for 12 weeks plus 4 weeks of follow-up. Melanin index and modified Melasma Area and Severity Index scores showed "no difference between groups" (mMASI p=0.344; melanin index p=0.268). Physician global assessment improved only on the triple-combination side (p=0.032), but after treatment stopped, that side showed a "higher severity of recurrence" than the arbutin/kojic-acid side (melanin index p=0.004; mMASI p=0.045), and erythema and stinging were more frequent with the triple combination. Patient satisfaction did not differ between sides, and the authors declared no conflicts of interest.[^tanta] *Alpha-arbutin plus kojic acid matched a hydroquinone-based triple combination cream on the most objective pigmentation measures over 12 weeks, trailed it on physician-rated improvement, and caused fewer side effects and less rebound after stopping.*

**Melasma, alpha-arbutin combined with laser (topical plus device).** A prospective case series treated 35 patients with refractory melasma using ten weekly Q-switched Nd:YAG laser sessions, two monthly follow-up treatments, and a topical 7% alpha-arbutin solution, with 6 months of follow-up. Independent grading found 30% of patients achieved excellent clearance (more than 81% pigment reduction) and 36.7% achieved good clearance (51% to 80%); adverse effects included transient discomfort, erythema, temporary lightening of fine facial hair, mottled hypopigmentation in 8.57% of patients, and melasma recurrence in 5.71%.[^polnikorn] *A meaningful share of patients achieved strong clearance, but the design cannot separate the laser's effect from arbutin's.*

**Formulation and concentration.** A pharmaceutical stability study formulated 7% w/w alpha-arbutin creams intended for melasma treatment and found the active ingredient remained above 90% (about 92%) of its starting level after 60 days of storage at room temperature or refrigerated, supporting a 60-day beyond-use date; the study addressed formulation stability, not clinical efficacy.[^teera]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"A natural, gentler alternative to hydroquinone."** The enzyme data do not support arbutin as a stronger or fundamentally different actor: on recombinant human tyrosinase, arbutin inhibited the enzyme about as weakly as hydroquinone itself, both in the millimolar IC50 range.[^mann] And products are not guaranteed to be hydroquinone-free in practice, since a review of arbutin research warns that hydroquinone "may be generated during product use."[^boo]
- **"Clinically proven to treat melasma."** The clinical record is one small, non-placebo-controlled split-face trial against an active hydroquinone-based comparator, plus an uncontrolled case series combining arbutin with laser treatment; there is no vehicle-controlled trial of arbutin alone.[^tanta][^polnikorn]
