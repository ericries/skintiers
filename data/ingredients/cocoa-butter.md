Cocoa butter (Theobroma Cacao Seed Butter) is the solid plant fat pressed from cocoa beans, a rich occlusive emollient used in body creams and lip balms and marketed heavily, and wrongly, for preventing stretch marks and fading scars.[^osman]

> Cocoa butter is a genuine emollient: as a firm, occlusive plant fat it softens and helps seal moisture into dry skin, the same generic benefit any heavy fat provides. What it does not do is the thing it is most famous for. A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial found a cocoa butter lotion did not reduce stretch marks, and the broader stretch-mark evidence is on [[stretch-marks]].[^osman] It is also comedogenic, so it suits body and lips more than acne-prone faces. Judge it as a pleasant moisturizing fat, not a stretch-mark or scar treatment.

## The Rubric

Cocoa butter is graded on the two things it is actually used for: moisturizing, and the stretch-mark/scar claim it is sold on.

**Moisturizing and softening dry skin (cosmetic)**
- **Effect size: modest.** As an occlusive, fatty plant butter, cocoa butter forms a softening film that slows water loss and leaves skin feeling smoother, which is real emollient value on dry body skin and lips. It is a generic property of heavy fats rather than a distinctive active effect, and shared by shea butter, [[petrolatum]], and other occlusives.
- **Evidence quality: established but generic.** That a rich occlusive fat moisturizes is uncontroversial; there is no strong controlled evidence that cocoa butter outperforms other emollients, and it is rated comedogenic, a drawback on acne-prone skin.

**Stretch marks and scars (the marketed use)**
- **Effect size: none demonstrated.** This is the claim cocoa butter is built on and the evidence is negative. In a double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trial of 175 first-time mothers, a cocoa butter lotion applied daily through pregnancy produced no difference in stretch-mark development versus placebo (45.1% versus 48.8%; P = 0.730), and no difference in severity.[^osman] The fuller stretch-mark evidence, including reviews reaching the same conclusion, is set out on [[stretch-marks]] and in [[osman-2008-cocoa-butter-for-prevention-of-striae-gravidarum-rct]] and [[ud-din-2016-topical-management-of-striae-distensae-review-pmid-26486318]]; it is not repeated here.
- **Evidence quality: solid (and negative).** A well-powered, blinded, placebo-controlled RCT plus concurring reviews; the finding is not that evidence is missing but that a controlled test found no benefit.[^osman]

## The Evidence

Cocoa butter's honest profile is short: it is a good occlusive moisturizer and a debunked stretch-mark treatment. Its moisturizing action is the generic behaviour of a heavy plant fat, real but not unique, and offset on facial skin by its comedogenicity. Its headline use, preventing or fading stretch marks and scars, was directly tested and failed: a double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT found a daily cocoa butter lotion did not reduce the likelihood or severity of stretch marks in pregnancy versus placebo.[^osman] The condition-level evidence, including the reviews that reach the same verdict, lives on [[stretch-marks]]; products built on cocoa butter, such as [[palmers-cocoa-butter-skin-therapy-oil]], are graded against that reality.
