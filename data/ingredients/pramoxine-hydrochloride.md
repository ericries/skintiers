Pramoxine hydrochloride is a topical local anesthetic formulated into OTC skincare and drug products, typically at 1%, to numb the sensation of itch rather than treat its underlying cause.[^dailymed][^cfr346] It is FDA-recognized as a local anesthetic active ingredient for anorectal use at 1% under the anorectal drug monograph, and the same 1% concentration is the active in a wide range of general anti-itch lotions, creams, and sprays marketed under the external-analgesic Drug Facts framework, including CeraVe Itch Relief Moisturizing Lotion.[^cfr346][^dailymed]

> A single randomized, double-blind, controlled trial in hemodialysis patients with uremic itch found a 1% pramoxine lotion cut itch intensity 61%, versus 12% for a control lotion, over 4 weeks.[^young] That is a real, controlled antipruritic effect for one difficult-to-treat itch condition; it is symptomatic relief, not a treatment for the disease causing the itch.

## The Rubric

**Symptomatic itch relief (topical, 1%)**
Effect size: notable for a symptomatic measure. In a randomized, double-blind, controlled trial of 28 hemodialysis patients with moderate-to-severe uremic pruritus, twice-daily 1% pramoxine lotion for 4 weeks produced a 61% decrease in itch intensity, versus a 12% decrease with a control lotion, with a faster rate of decline in the treatment arm.[^young] A smaller open-label pilot of a combined 2.5% hydrocortisone/1% pramoxine lotion found a significant drop in itch on a visual analog scale after a single day of use (mean reduction 31.7%, P=0.03), though that result mixes pramoxine with a topical steroid and cannot isolate pramoxine's own contribution.[^kircik] An open-label trial of pramoxine 1% combined with 12% lactic acid also reported significant self-rated itch improvement by day 1, again in a combination formula rather than pramoxine alone.[^grove] "Notable for the itch-numbing effect itself, best isolated in the hemodialysis trial; this is symptomatic relief of the sensation of itch, not a treatment for skin disease."

Evidence quality: mixed. The hemodialysis trial is the one controlled study to isolate pramoxine against a comparator, and it is small (28 patients, one center).[^young] The hydrocortisone-combination and lactic-acid-combination studies are open-label and cannot separate pramoxine's effect from its co-actives.[^kircik][^grove] Pramoxine 1% also carries longstanding FDA regulatory recognition as a local anesthetic active ingredient for anorectal itch and pain under 21 CFR 346.10(g), a regulatory-status fact rather than an efficacy trial.[^cfr346]

## The Evidence

Pramoxine hydrochloride numbs itch symptomatically; it is a local anesthetic, not an anti-inflammatory or barrier-repair ingredient. In the one controlled trial that isolated it against a comparator lotion, in hemodialysis patients with uremic pruritus, a 1% pramoxine lotion cut itch intensity 61% over 4 weeks versus 12% for the control.[^young] It carries FDA regulatory recognition as a 1% local anesthetic active ingredient for anorectal itch and pain,[^cfr346] and the same 1% concentration is the active ingredient in general OTC anti-itch lotions and creams for "minor skin irritations," per DailyMed structured product labels for marketed products such as CeraVe Itch Relief Moisturizing Lotion.[^dailymed] Because it treats the sensation of itch rather than its cause, it is typically paired with, not a substitute for, a treatment aimed at the underlying condition (for example the barrier and anti-inflammatory care covered on [[atopic-dermatitis]]).

## How It Works

Pramoxine is a surface local anesthetic. The FDA's anorectal-drug monograph lists it, alongside benzocaine, dibucaine, lidocaine, and tetracaine, as a "local anesthetic active ingredient" approved at 1% for anorectal use, and its indicated use across OTC labeling is "for the temporary relief of" pain, burning, or itching.[^cfr346] Unlike lidocaine or benzocaine, pramoxine is not chemically related to the amide or ester classes of local anesthetics, which is why it is commonly used in patients with a known allergy to those classes; the DailyMed label for CeraVe's pramoxine-based product lists its purpose simply as "external analgesic."[^dailymed]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Instant" or "fast" itch relief.** The controlled hemodialysis trial measured effect over 4 weeks, not instantly; the faster-onset data available (Kircik, single-day VAS reduction) come from an open-label pilot combining pramoxine with hydrocortisone, not pramoxine alone.[^young][^kircik]
- **"Treats" eczema, psoriasis, or other rashes.** Pramoxine numbs the itch sensation; it is not an anti-inflammatory or disease-modifying treatment. A 2006 review of itch in psoriasis lists pramoxine among several antipruritic options alongside corticosteroids, coal tar, and phototherapy, not as a treatment for the underlying disease.[^dawn]
