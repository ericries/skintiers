Urea is a small, water-soluble molecule that occurs naturally in the outer layer of skin as part of its natural moisturizing factor, and it is formulated into skincare and prescription products across a wide concentration range, from roughly 5% up to 40%, where it is described as acting as a humectant (binding water) at lower concentrations and as a keratolytic (loosening and softening excess keratin) at higher ones.[^friedman][^dalloglio] The two roles rest on different bodies of evidence: low-concentration urea creams have real controlled-trial support for easing dryness and delaying relapse in [[atopic-dermatitis]], while the high-concentration keratolytic use is best documented not as a stand-alone treatment but as a debriding step that helps antifungal drugs reach thickened, fungal nails.

> A Cochrane review of 77 eczema trials found that, in the one trial that isolated urea-containing cream against placebo (129 participants), investigators recorded more improvement in dryness (RR 1.40, 95% CI 1.14 to 1.71) and fewer flares (RR 0.47, 95% CI 0.24 to 0.92) with urea, at low-to-moderate certainty, alongside more reported adverse events (RR 1.65, 95% CI 1.16 to 2.34).[^cochrane] At the high end of the concentration range, 40% urea's best-controlled role is as a chemical nail-softening agent that raises the success rate of a topical antifungal, rather than as a keratolytic proven alone.[^bassiri][^tietz]

## The Rubric

**Dryness and barrier support, [[moisturizing]] (humectant, low concentration, roughly 5%-10%)**
- Effect size: modest. The Cochrane systematic review of 77 randomized eczema trials (6,603 participants), covered in full on [[van-zuuren-2017-emollients-moisturisers-eczema]], isolated one placebo-controlled trial of urea-containing cream (129 participants): participants reported skin improvement more often with urea (RR 1.28, 95% CI 1.06 to 1.53), investigators recorded improved dryness more often (RR 1.40, 95% CI 1.14 to 1.71) and fewer flares (RR 0.47, 95% CI 0.24 to 0.92), while adverse events were also reported more often with urea than with the vehicle (RR 1.65, 95% CI 1.16 to 2.34).[^cochrane] Across all the moisturizers the review pooled, not urea specifically, the authors concluded "we did not find reliable evidence that one moisturiser is better than another."[^cochrane]
- Separately, a randomized trial that enrolled 55 adults with [[atopic-dermatitis]], cleared their eczema with three weeks of topical corticosteroid, and randomized the 44 who cleared into two arms of 22 found that twice-daily use of a urea-containing barrier moisturizer over a 26-week maintenance phase held the median time to relapse beyond 180 days, versus 30 days with no treatment, and left 68% of patients eczema-free versus 32% of the untreated group.[^wiren] The abstract does not report the cream's urea concentration or a p-value for these figures.
- Evidence quality: mixed. The relapse-prevention trial compared urea moisturizer against no treatment at all, not against a plain non-urea moisturizer, so it shows a urea cream beats nothing rather than that urea outperforms another emollient.[^wiren] The Cochrane pooled urea estimate rests on a single trial rated low-to-moderate certainty.[^cochrane]

**Ichthyosis vulgaris and visible scaling (10%, overlapping humectant and keratolytic action)**
- Effect size: notable on a scaling/roughness score, cosmetic-and-symptomatic. In a two-center, randomized, right-versus-left trial in 30 patients with ichthyosis vulgaris, a 10% urea lotion cut a composite scaling-roughness-redness-cracking score by 65% at four weeks (from a baseline of 9.5) versus 40% for a glycerol-based emollient applied to the opposite side of the body, a between-group difference the authors reported as significant (P=0.0005 at four weeks), with overall efficacy also rated higher for urea (8.9 vs 7.3, P=0.0001).[^tadini] Three of 30 patients withdrew for itching or burning (two on the urea side, one on the glycerol side).[^tadini]
- Evidence quality: preliminary. One small trial (30 randomized, 27 completed), single-blinded to the patient and industry-sponsored (Ureadin Rx10 vs Dexeryl).[^tadini]

**Hyperkeratosis and thickened, fungal nails (keratolytic, higher concentration, roughly 15%-40%)**
- Effect size: notable as a debriding adjunct, not established as a stand-alone keratolytic. Two randomized trials tested 40% urea as a nail-softening step before an antifungal, not as a treatment on its own. In 70 patients with onychomycosis, adding 40% urea to fluconazole 1% nail lacquer raised efficacy from 62.8% with fluconazole alone to 82.8% with the combination, a difference the authors reported as statistically significant.[^bassiri] In a 692-subject, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, softening the nail with 40% urea paste for two to four weeks before four weeks of topical bifonazole produced a higher overall cure rate than urea ablation plus placebo (54.8% vs 42.2%, P=0.0024) and a higher mycological cure rate (64.5% vs 49.0%, P=0.0001).[^tietz] Neither trial isolates urea's own contribution against a no-urea control arm; both show 40% urea as a delivery step that improves an antifungal's result, not a tested-alone keratolytic.
- Evidence quality: solid for the adjunct claim on nails, thin for urea used alone on skin. Both nail trials are randomized and the larger one placebo-controlled, but a review of 15%-30% urea used by itself for xerosis and hyperkeratosis concluded that "limited evidence supports the use of these concentrations of urea in skin disorders characterised by xerosis and hyperkeratosis," even while describing the products as commonly used in clinical practice.[^dalloglio]

## How It Works

Urea is a normal, hygroscopic component of the natural moisturizing factor in the outermost epidermis.[^friedman] A 2016 review describes urea-containing formulations as being "used in a concentration-dependent manner to restore skin hydration, thin hyperkeratosis, debride dystrophic nails, and enhance topical drug penetration."[^friedman] The medium-concentration review frames the two mechanisms as distinct: humectant action "by enhancing stratum corneum hydration" at lower concentrations, and keratolytic action "by loosening epidermal keratin" as concentration rises, with the review noting that higher concentrations are more irritating on sensitive or broken skin.[^dalloglio]

## The Evidence

**Eczema, pooled across trials (Cochrane review).** A 2017 Cochrane systematic review pooled 77 randomized controlled trials (6,603 participants, mean age 18.6 years, mean trial duration 6.7 weeks) of emollients and moisturizers for eczema, comparing moisturizer against no moisturizer, specific ingredients (glycerol, urea, oat) against vehicle, and moisturizer added to active treatment.[^cochrane] The single trial isolating urea-containing cream against placebo (129 participants) found more participants reported skin improvement with urea (RR 1.28, 95% CI 1.06 to 1.53, low-certainty evidence) and investigators recorded improved dryness (RR 1.40, 95% CI 1.14 to 1.71, moderate certainty) and fewer flares (RR 0.47, 95% CI 0.24 to 0.92, low certainty), alongside more adverse events (RR 1.65, 95% CI 1.16 to 2.34, moderate certainty).[^cochrane] Full detail on the review's other comparisons (glycerol, oat, and pooled-moisturizer results) is on [[van-zuuren-2017-emollients-moisturisers-eczema]].

**Relapse prevention in atopic dermatitis.** A randomized controlled trial enrolled 55 adults with atopic dermatitis, cleared their eczema with three weeks of topical betamethasone valerate 0.1%, then randomized the 44 who cleared into two arms of 22 for a 26-week maintenance phase: twice-daily application of a urea-containing barrier-strengthening moisturizer on the previously affected areas, or no treatment.[^wiren] The median time to relapse was "> 180 days (duration of the study)" for the moisturizer group versus 30 days for the no-treatment group, and 68% of the moisturizer group stayed eczema-free versus 32% of the untreated group; the authors concluded the treatment "reduced the risk of relapse to approximately one third of that of no treatment."[^wiren]

**Ichthyosis vulgaris.** A two-center, randomized, controlled, single-blind, right-versus-left trial in 30 patients (ages 8-65) with ichthyosis vulgaris applied a 10% urea-based lotion to one side of the body and a glycerol-based emollient cream to the other for four weeks.[^tadini] A composite scaling-roughness-redness-cracking (SRRC) score fell from a baseline of 9.5 to 3.3 (65% relative reduction) on the urea side versus 5.7 (40% reduction) on the glycerol side at four weeks (P=0.0001 within urea; between-group difference P=0.0005 at both two and four weeks), and a global efficacy score was higher for urea (8.9 vs 7.3, P=0.0001), while global tolerability did not differ significantly (8.1 vs 8.4).[^tadini] Three of 30 patients withdrew for itching or burning (two on the urea side, one on the glycerol side).[^tadini]

**Onychomycosis, 40% urea as a debriding adjunct.** A randomized, double-blind trial in 70 patients with onychomycosis compared fluconazole 1% nail lacquer alone with fluconazole 1% combined with 40% urea; the authors reported the combination was more effective (82.8%) than fluconazole alone (62.8%) and described the difference as a "statistically significant superiority in clinical and mycological responses."[^bassiri] Separately, a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial across 51 centers enrolled 692 subjects with mild-to-moderate onychomycosis, softened and removed affected nail material with 40% urea paste over two to four weeks, then randomized subjects to four weeks of topical bifonazole 1% cream or placebo.[^tietz] At the two-week primary endpoint, overall cure was higher with bifonazole (54.8% vs 42.2% for placebo, P=0.0024) and mycological cure was higher (64.5% vs 49.0%, P=0.0001); both study arms had already received the same 40% urea nail ablation, so the trial demonstrates that urea debridement plus an antifungal beats debridement plus placebo, not that urea alone clears nails.[^tietz]

## Common Marketing Claims

**"Exfoliates and resurfaces skin texture" at any listed percentage.** The keratolytic evidence base is concentrated in nails and in ichthyosis-type scaling, tested at 10%-40% and often as an adjunct to another treatment; a review of 15%-30% urea used alone for general xerosis and hyperkeratosis found only limited supporting evidence for that use.[^dalloglio] A product's urea percentage does not by itself indicate which of humectant or keratolytic evidence applies.

**"Clinically proven" barrier repair from any urea moisturizer.** The strongest controlled result, delayed relapse in previously cleared eczema, compared a urea moisturizer against no treatment at all, not against a plainer humectant such as [[glycerin]] or [[hyaluronic-acid]], so it does not show urea specifically outperforms other moisturizing ingredients.[^wiren][^cochrane]
