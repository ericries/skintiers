Salicylic acid is a lipophilic beta-hydroxy acid used topically as a nonprescription acne active at 0.5% to 2%, as a keratolytic wart remover at 12% to 40%, and at roughly 20% to 30% for in-office chemical peels.[^cfr-acne][^cfr-wart][^arif]

It is the oil-soluble hydroxy acid, which lets it penetrate the sebum-filled pore rather than staying on the surface, and that is why it turns up in so many acne cleansers and toners. It sits in the US OTC drug monograph twice: as an acne active at 0.5% to 2% and as a wart remover at 12% to 40%.[^cfr-acne][^cfr-wart] Its evidence base is uneven across those uses. The largest controlled evidence is for wart removal, where a Cochrane review of 85 trials found it clears warts significantly more often than placebo.[^warts] For acne it is a genuine, guideline-recognized option that the American Academy of Dermatology recommends conditionally, but the controlled trials behind it are small and low-certainty, and dermatology guidelines rank benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids above it.[^aad][^cochrane]

> Salicylic acid has its strongest controlled evidence as a wart treatment (it beat placebo for clearance, RR 1.56, 95% CI 1.20 to 2.03) and is a legitimate but second-tier acne active: the American Academy of Dermatology recommends it only conditionally, while strongly recommending benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids, and Cochrane rated the acne trials low quality.[^warts][^aad][^cochrane]

## The Rubric

Grades differ by use. Framing below is relative and names comparators.

**Acne (topical, mild to moderate, 0.5% to 2%)**
- **Effect size: modest.** Salicylic acid is an FDA OTC acne active, and where it has been compared head to head it lands level with a topical retinoid rather than above it. In the one randomized trial Cochrane could pool, salicylic acid was no different from [[tretinoin]] on participants' global assessment of improvement (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.92 to 1.09; 46 participants).[^cochrane] But the drugs guidelines rank higher for acne, [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and the topical [[retinoids]], carry stronger recommendations than salicylic acid does.[^aad] "Modest, and on weaker evidence than the first-line acne topicals (benzoyl peroxide, topical retinoids) that guidelines place above it."
- **Evidence quality: weak.** A Cochrane systematic review and the 2024 American Academy of Dermatology acne guideline both cover it; Cochrane concluded that for salicylic acid and the other comparisons "the quality of evidence was low or very low," and the guideline recommends it only conditionally.[^cochrane][^aad]

**Chemical peels (in-office, roughly 20% to 30%)**
- **Effect size: modest, and comparable to other superficial peels.** A systematic review of 12 peel RCTs found salicylic acid peels about level with glycolic acid (good-or-fair improvement RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.85 to 1.18) and better than Jessner's solution for comedones (comedone reduction 53.4% vs 26.3%, p=0.001), with a salicylic-plus-mandelic combination beating glycolic acid (total-score improvement 85.3% vs 68.5%, p<0.001).[^peels] "Roughly interchangeable with other superficial acne peels; no clear winner among them."
- **Evidence quality: weak.** The peel trials were small and heterogeneous; the review rated their methodological quality "very low to moderate" and could not pool a meta-analysis.[^peels]

**Wart removal / keratolytic (12% to 40%)**
- **Effect size: notable.** This is the best-evidenced use. A Cochrane review of 85 topical-wart trials (8,815 participants) found salicylic acid more than doubled clearance versus placebo on the hands and increased it overall (all sites RR 1.56, 95% CI 1.20 to 2.03; hands RR 2.67, 95% CI 1.43 to 5.01).[^warts] It was statistically no different from cryotherapy overall, and salicylic acid combined with cryotherapy beat salicylic acid alone (RR 1.24, 95% CI 1.07 to 1.43).[^warts] "The one use with large, significant controlled evidence: clears warts better than placebo, on par with cryotherapy, and additive with it."
- **Evidence quality: solid for the placebo comparison.** The salicylic-acid-versus-placebo effect is pooled across many randomized trials and is significant, though the review noted many included studies were at high risk of bias.[^warts]

## What We Actually Know

Salicylic acid's strongest controlled evidence is as a wart treatment: a Cochrane review of 85 trials found it clears warts significantly more often than placebo (RR 1.56, 95% CI 1.20 to 2.03), roughly matches cryotherapy, and adds to it.[^warts] For acne it is a real but second-tier active. It is on the US OTC acne monograph at 0.5% to 2%, the 2024 American Academy of Dermatology guideline gives it a conditional recommendation, and the one head-to-head trial Cochrane could pool put it level with [[tretinoin]] (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.92 to 1.09), but Cochrane rated the acne evidence low to very low quality and guidelines rank [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and topical [[retinoids]] above it.[^cfr-acne][^aad][^cochrane] As an in-office peel it performs about the same as other superficial acne peels.[^peels] Systemic toxicity (salicylism) is rare from topical use but documented, particularly when high-concentration preparations are applied to large areas.[^salicylism]

## How It Works

Salicylic acid is the lipophilic (oil-soluble) member of the hydroxy-acid family, which is why it is used on acne: its lipid solubility lets it penetrate into the sebum-rich pilosebaceous unit rather than acting only on the skin surface.[^arif] Once thought of purely as a keratolytic that dissolves keratin, it is now described as acting as "a desmolytic agent, because of its ability to disrupt cellular junctions" between corneocytes, which loosens and sheds the outermost dead-cell layer (desquamation).[^arif] That desquamating and comedolytic action, clearing the plugged follicle, is the basis for its use in acne and acne peels; the Arif review states that "the comedolytic property of salicylic acid makes it a useful peeling agent for patients with acne."[^arif] At the higher concentrations used for wart removers, the same keratin-shedding effect is what strips away the thickened wart tissue.[^cfr-wart] A review of topical salicylic acid also attributes to it "keratolytic, bacteriostatic, fungicidal, and photoprotective properties."[^salicylism]

## The Evidence

Primary clinical evidence, by use:

**Acne (topical).** The 2020 Cochrane review of topical azelaic acid, salicylic acid, and several other actives pooled 49 trials (3,880 participants). For salicylic acid it found "little or no difference between salicylic acid and tretinoin" on participants' global assessment (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.92 to 1.09; 1 study, 46 participants, low-quality evidence), and could not measure a salicylic-acid-versus-benzoyl-peroxide global response at all.[^cochrane] The review's overall verdict was cautious: outside the azelaic-acid comparisons, "the quality of evidence was low or very low," limited by risk of bias and imprecision.[^cochrane] The 2024 American Academy of Dermatology acne guideline, built on a GRADE systematic review, placed salicylic acid in its lower recommendation tier: "Conditional recommendations are made for topical clascoterone, salicylic acid, and azelaic acid," whereas "Strong recommendations are made for benzoyl peroxide, topical retinoids, topical antibiotics, and oral doxycycline."[^aad]

**Chemical peels.** A 2018 systematic review of chemical peels for acne (12 RCTs, 387 participants) found the commonly used peels broadly interchangeable. Salicylic acid was about equal to glycolic acid (good-or-fair improvement RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.85 to 1.18) and to trichloroacetic acid (RR 0.89, 95% CI 0.73 to 1.10), superior to Jessner's solution for comedones (reduction 53.4% vs 26.3%, p=0.001), and a salicylic-plus-mandelic-acid peel beat glycolic acid on total acne score (85.3% vs 68.5%, p<0.001).[^peels] The authors concluded that "commonly used chemical peels appear to be similarly effective for mild-to-moderate acne vulgaris" while cautioning that the trials were of very low to moderate quality and too heterogeneous to meta-analyze.[^peels]

**Wart removal.** The 2012 Cochrane review of topical treatments for cutaneous warts (85 trials, 8,815 participants) reported that "trials of salicylic acid (SA) versus placebo showed that the former significantly increased the chance of clearance of warts at all sites" (RR 1.56, 95% CI 1.20 to 2.03), with a larger effect on the hands (RR 2.67, 95% CI 1.43 to 5.01) than the feet (RR 1.29, 95% CI 1.07 to 1.55).[^warts] Salicylic acid was not significantly different from cryotherapy overall (RR 1.23, 95% CI 0.88 to 1.71), and the two combined were more effective than salicylic acid alone (RR 1.24, 95% CI 1.07 to 1.43).[^warts]

**Safety.** A 2014 review of topical salicylic acid toxicity reported that although rare, "salicylic acid toxicity (salicylism) can occur from topical application," and warned of "the potential for salicylism or even death from topically applied salicylic acid," most plausibly with high-concentration preparations used over large surface areas.[^salicylism]

**Regulatory status.** US federal regulation lists salicylic acid as a monograph active for two OTC uses: "Salicylic acid, 0.5 to 2 percent" for acne, and "Salicylic acid 12 to 40 percent in a plaster vehicle" (or 5% to 17% in a collodion-like vehicle) for wart removal.[^cfr-acne][^cfr-wart]

## Uses

Best supported: wart removal, where salicylic acid at monograph strengths (12% to 40%) clears warts significantly more often than placebo and adds to cryotherapy.[^warts][^cfr-wart] Supported but second-tier: mild-to-moderate acne, where the 0.5% to 2% OTC active is level with [[tretinoin]] in the one pooled trial, carries a conditional guideline recommendation, and sits below [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and the topical [[retinoids]] that guidelines recommend more strongly.[^cochrane][^aad] Comparable to peers as an in-office superficial acne peel at roughly 20% to 30%.[^peels] The main documented safety limit is rare systemic salicylate toxicity when strong preparations are applied to large areas.[^salicylism]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"The best ingredient for acne."** Not what guidelines say. The 2024 American Academy of Dermatology guideline recommends salicylic acid only conditionally, while [[benzoyl-peroxide]], topical [[retinoids]], and topical antibiotics get its strong recommendations.[^aad] Its one pooled head-to-head put it level with, not ahead of, tretinoin.[^cochrane]
- **"Shrinks pores / gives glass skin."** Pore appearance and glow are cosmetic endpoints, distinct from the acne and wart clearance the trials measured.[^cochrane][^warts] The controlled evidence is about lesion and wart counts, not pore size.
- **"A gentle exfoliant for everyday use."** Salicylic acid is a drug-monograph active, and the concentrated preparations carry a real, if rare, systemic-toxicity risk (salicylism) when overused on large areas.[^salicylism][^cfr-wart]
- **"An anti-aging acid."** The controlled evidence covers acne, warts, and superficial peeling, not skin aging.[^cochrane][^warts][^peels] Any anti-aging positioning is extrapolation from the peeling effect rather than from aging trials.
