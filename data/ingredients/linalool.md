The floral, slightly citrus terpene alcohol behind a great deal of what skincare smells like, and "one of the most commonly used fragrance terpenes in consumer products".[^kuresepi] It is in a formula for scent, not for skin, which is why the grade here is `weak`. **Bottom line:** of the oxidised terpenes it is the one that tests positive most often, in two independent clinics on two different test concentrations, and it is also the one where the test itself is under the most suspicion.

> **7.0% in Sweden, 8.8% in Germany and Austria, and the second group thinks its own test over-calls.** Oxidised linalool out-scored oxidised limonene in both series.[^sukakul][^ivdk] But the German multicentre study found that most positives were weak, that doubtful and irritant readings together outnumbered the positives, and concluded that "both test preparations display an irritant potential with an increased risk of false positive reactions".[^ivdk]

## The Rubric

**Effect on skin: none claimed.** Linalool is there to smell of lavender and bergamot. No efficacy evidence was sought because no benefit is advertised for it.

**Oxidation is the whole mechanism, as with [[limonene]] and [[geraniol]].** The chemistry here is unusually well described: "While pure linalool is considered as non-allergenic because it has a very low skin sensitization potential, its autoxidation on air leads to allylic hydroperoxides that have been shown to be major skin sensitizers."[^kuresepi] Working in a reconstructed human epidermis model, that group traced the radicals those hydroperoxides form and showed they can attach directly to the thiol side chain of cysteine.[^kuresepi] This is why an old, often-opened bottle is not the same proposition as a fresh one.

**The most frequently positive of the oxidised terpenes, on two independent series.** 7.0% of 5,773 consecutive dermatitis patients in Malmo over 2013 to 2020,[^sukakul] and 8.8%, 483 of 5,511 consecutive patients, across the German and Austrian IVDK clinics over 2018 to 2020.[^ivdk] Both denominators are people already attending a contact dermatitis clinic, not the public.

**The two series are not directly comparable, and the test concentration is why.** IVDK patch tested linalool hydroperoxides at 1% in petrolatum against limonene hydroperoxides at 0.3%, more than three times the dose for the substance that scored higher.[^ivdk] The study reports the concentrations without attributing the gap to them, so treat `linalool beats limonene` as a finding that holds in both series rather than as a settled ratio.

**The strongest caution comes from the larger positive rate, not the smaller.** In the IVDK series most linalool positives were weak, 429 of 483, while doubtful readings numbered 578 and irritant readings 178, so unclear and irritant results together outnumbered the positives.[^ivdk] Reactivity also rose in patients who reacted irritantly to sodium lauryl sulphate.[^ivdk] Their recommendation is "Mindful interpretation of PT results and aimed patch testing of lower concentrations".[^ivdk]

**Timing changes the answer.** In the Swedish series, "More than 95% of contact allergy cases were identified on Day 3/4", so a patch test read earlier will miss most of them.[^sukakul]

## The Evidence

Two large consecutive-patient series, run independently, agreeing on the ranking and disagreeing on what it means. Together they cover roughly 11,000 patch-tested patients, which is the strength; both are specialist-clinic populations, which is the limit.

Neither establishes that a positive test explains a given person's rash. The Swedish authors ask for exactly that work before screening is recommended: "More studies concerning the clinical relevance before recommending these substances for screening are required."[^sukakul] The IVDK authors go further and question the preparation, asking that it be "chemically monitored in order to reduce irritancy".[^ivdk] A reader who takes 8.8% as `linalool causes dermatitis in one in eleven people` has misread the denominator, and, on the IVDK group's own reading, the test.

The mechanistic work is laboratory chemistry in an epidermis model, not a clinical study, and is cited here only for the fresh-versus-oxidised distinction it establishes.[^kuresepi]

## Common Marketing Claims

There are none to quarantine. No brand advertises linalool as a benefit; it appears in ingredient and allergen-disclosure lists rather than in marketing copy. A scent molecule with a measurable sensitisation rate and no claimed upside is judged purely on tolerability, as on [[citronellol]].
