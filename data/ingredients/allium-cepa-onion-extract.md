Allium Cepa (Onion Bulb) Extract is the INCI name for a botanical extract of common onion, best known as the active ingredient in Mederma (marketed as "Cepalin") and in the German product Contractubex, formulated into gels for surgical scars, hypertrophic scars, and keloids.[^chung] Head-to-head trials against plain petrolatum, the cheapest standard-of-care emollient, have repeatedly found no significant advantage for onion extract, and a 2021 meta-analysis of 13 randomized trials concluded it is not superior to other topical scar treatments and carries more irritation risk than doing nothing.[^chung][^jackson][^yuan]

> A 2021 meta-analysis of 13 RCTs found onion extract gel beat no treatment on investigator- and patient-rated improvement scores (both P < .00001), but showed no difference from other common topical scar treatments (investigator P = .56, patient P = .39), while increasing total adverse effects versus no treatment (P < .0001) and versus other treatments (P = .008).[^yuan]

## The Rubric

**Surgical/post-op scar appearance, as a standalone gel**
- **Effect size: none demonstrated versus petrolatum.** In a randomized, double-blinded, split-scar study of 24 patients, onion extract gel applied three times daily for 8 weeks showed no statistically significant difference from petrolatum-based ointment on blinded ratings of overall cosmesis, erythema, or hypertrophy, nor on patient-reported erythema, pruritus, burning, or pain.[^chung] An earlier 17-patient pilot after Mohs surgery found onion extract produced no significant pre/post improvement in erythema or pruritus, while the petrolatum comparator did significantly reduce erythema.[^jackson]
- **Evidence quality: reasonably good but consistently negative for the isolated product.** Two independent randomized trials against petrolatum, plus a 13-RCT meta-analysis, all point the same direction: no advantage over standard emollient or other topical treatments, and worse tolerability than no treatment.[^chung][^jackson][^yuan]

**Prevention of hypertrophic scars/keloids, as onion extract combined with silicone gel**
- **Effect size: modest, and the combination outperforms onion extract alone.** The same meta-analysis found onion extract blended into a silicone gel base increased total improvement scores versus other treatments on both investigator (P < .00001) and patient (P = .0007) ratings, and the authors conclude "OE in silicone gel might be the optimal topical choice for scar treatment."[^yuan] A separate 40-patient RCT comparing a silicone gel containing onion extract and aloe vera to standard silicone gel sheets found no significant difference in scarring incidence, POSAS score, erythema, or melanin, with pliability significantly better in the onion-extract-silicone group.[^pangkanon] A 3-month RCT in post-cesarean scars found silicone gel with 5% onion extract equivalent to silicone gel with vitamin C on POSAS/VSS and melanin, with a significant edge only on erythema.[^napavichayanun]
- **Evidence quality: weak-to-moderate; benefit tracks the silicone, not clearly the onion.** None of the combination trials include a silicone-alone arm without onion extract, so the meta-analysis's "OE in silicone" signal cannot rule out that silicone gel, itself supported by a Cochrane review as a modest scar treatment, is doing the work.[^yuan][^cochrane] Silicone gel sheeting itself has only "weak evidence of a benefit" per Cochrane, and the trials underlying it are "highly susceptible to bias," so even the base comparator is a low bar.[^cochrane]

## How It Works

Onion extract's proposed mechanism centers on flavonoids, principally quercetin, which industry and preclinical literature credit with anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic activity that could theoretically limit collagen overproduction in scar tissue; however, none of the clinical trials located here measured a biochemical or histological mechanism in treated human scars, so this remains a plausibility argument rather than a demonstrated pathway in vivo.[^chung] The clinical trial record is what actually adjudicates the ingredient, and it consistently fails to separate onion extract from its vehicle or from petrolatum.

## The Evidence

**Onion extract gel versus petrolatum, split-scar RCT (topical).** A prospective, double-blinded, split-scar study enrolled 24 patients with new surgical wounds at least 4 cm long; each scar was divided in half, with onion extract gel (Mederma) applied to one side and petrolatum-based ointment (Aquaphor) to the other, three times daily for 8 weeks. Using paired t-test and Wilcoxon signed rank test, the authors "found no statistically significant difference (p < .1) between the two treatment groups in any of the outcome variables studied," including blinded cosmetic appearance, erythema, hypertrophy, and patient-reported symptoms.[^chung] *A direct, blinded, within-patient comparison against the cheapest standard emollient found onion extract gel did not improve scar cosmesis or symptoms at all.*

**Onion extract versus petrolatum after Mohs surgery, pilot RCT (topical).** Seventeen patients with post-Mohs surgical scars applied either onion gel extract or a petrolatum-based ointment three times daily for one month; using Fisher's exact test, there was no statistically significant pre-to-post improvement in erythema or pruritus with onion extract, while the petrolatum group did show a statistically significant reduction in scar erythema. The authors concluded onion extract "was ineffective in improving scar erythema and pruritus."[^jackson] *A small early trial found the plain emollient comparator outperformed onion extract on the one endpoint that reached significance.*

**Onion extract gel, systematic review and meta-analysis of 13 RCTs (topical).** Yuan et al. pooled 13 randomized controlled trials of onion extract gel in scar management. Versus no treatment, onion extract improved investigator- and patient-rated total improvement scores (both P < .00001), but versus other commonly used topical treatments there was no difference on either investigator (P = .56) or patient (P = .39) ratings. Onion extract also increased total adverse effects versus no treatment (P < .0001) and versus other treatments (P = .008), and increased treatment dropout from intolerance (P = .0002). The authors' stated conclusion is that onion extract gel "has no superiority to commonly used topical treatments" and "has the potential to increase the incidence of adverse effects."[^yuan] *Pooling the available RCTs, onion extract beats doing nothing but not other standard scar treatments, and it irritates more often than either comparator.*

**Onion extract plus silicone gel versus silicone gel sheets, RCT (topical, combination).** In a prospective, assessor-blind RCT of 40 post-surgical patients, a silicone gel containing onion extract and aloe vera (SGOA) was compared to standard silicone gel sheets (SGS) over 12 weeks. There was no significant difference in scarring incidence, POSAS score, erythema, or melanin value between groups; pliability measured by Cutometer was significantly better with SGOA.[^pangkanon] *The onion-extract silicone gel matched, but did not clearly beat, plain silicone sheeting, the accepted (if weakly evidenced) standard.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Clinically proven to improve the appearance of scars" (as in Mederma's Cepalin positioning).** Not supported by the independent, blinded RCTs against petrolatum: both available split-scar/pilot trials found no significant benefit over a plain emollient, and a 13-trial meta-analysis found no advantage over other standard topical treatments.[^chung][^jackson][^yuan]
- **"Softens and flattens scars."** The one place onion extract shows a real, reproducible edge is pliability/texture when blended into a silicone gel base rather than sold alone, and even there the trials cannot separate onion extract's contribution from the silicone's, and silicone gel sheeting itself carries only "weak evidence of benefit" per Cochrane.[^yuan][^pangkanon][^cochrane]
