A small molecule sold to formulators as Symsitive and used in sensitive-skin products, where it is meant to blunt the stinging and burning those products are bought to stop. It works on TRPV1, the same receptor [[capsaicin]] activates.[^concept] **Bottom line:** the mechanism is real and the human testing is thin, short and conducted by the company that sells it, so the grade here is `weak`.

> **Every human result on this page comes from the manufacturer.** Both trials were authored entirely by Research and Development staff at Beiersdorf AG, the company behind [[eucerin]], and both appeared in the same 2016 journal supplement.[^concept][^split] That does not make them wrong. It does mean no independent group has reproduced them, and this page found none that tried.

## The Rubric

**The mechanism is specific and was shown directly.** In cell work, "4-t-butylcyclohexanol significantly reduced TRPV1 activation, while acetyl dipeptide-1 cetyl ester had no effect on receptor activation".[^concept] That is a clean comparison against the rival soothing ingredient, and it is the strongest thing on this page.

**The human endpoint is a sensation, not something you can see.** The trials measure self-reported stinging and burning after the skin is deliberately provoked with capsaicin. A reader hoping for visible calming should note that the only visible outcome reported, reduced shaving-induced erythema, came from a cream containing "a combination of 4-t-butylcyclohexanol and a licochalcone A-rich licorice extract".[^concept] That result cannot be assigned to this ingredient alone, and [[licochalcone-a]] has its own page for a reason.

**The head-to-head win is real but narrow.** In 31 women with self-perceived sensitive skin, split-face against a competitor product, the 4-t-butylcyclohexanol cream gave "significant lower values for burning/stinging after one, and two minutes post-application".[^split] Read the next sentence too: "No significant difference was determined between the two test products for the point in time with most intense burning sensation, the severity of burning and the duration of burning".[^split] So it acted faster, and by fifteen minutes both products had done the same job.[^split]

**A provocation model is not daily life.** Subjects had 40 ppm capsaicin cream applied to the nasolabial fold, then the test product.[^split] That isolates the receptor neatly and tells you little about how skin feels on an ordinary day.

## The Evidence

Two studies, same company, same supplement, n = 31 in the only comparative one.[^concept][^split] Both single-blind. No independent replication was found, and no trial of this ingredient in diagnosed [[rosacea]] was found, which matters because sensitive-skin marketing frequently gestures at rosacea.

What would change the grade: an independent, blinded trial with a visible endpoint, testing the ingredient without licochalcone A alongside it.

## Common Marketing Claims

Products built on it are sold for calming and soothing. Those words describe a sensation, which is the endpoint that was actually measured, so the register is fair. Watch for the step beyond it, where soothing becomes a claim about redness or barrier repair; the redness evidence here is from a combination formula, and nothing on this page speaks to the barrier.
