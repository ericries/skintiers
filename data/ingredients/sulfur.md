Sulfur is one of dermatology's oldest topical treatments, an elemental mineral valued for keratolytic, antibacterial, and antifungal activity and used historically in acne, rosacea, seborrheic dermatitis, dandruff, and pityriasis versicolor, alone or combined with agents such as sodium sulfacetamide or salicylic acid.[^guptanicol] Its modern controlled-trial base is thin and concentrated on the sodium sulfacetamide/sulfur combination for rosacea; for acne it has not been tested against benzoyl peroxide or the retinoids head to head, and a 2020 evidence review of topical acne treatments called its effect uncertain.

> A 12-week investigator-blinded trial found combination sodium sulfacetamide 10%/sulfur 5% cream modestly outperformed metronidazole 0.75% cream on rosacea lesion counts and erythema, though at some cost in tolerability.[^torok] For acne, a 2020 evidence review covering six topical treatments reported it was "uncertain about the effects of sulfur," leaving it well short of the trial base behind [[benzoyl-peroxide]], [[azelaic-acid]], and the [[retinoids]].[^cochrane]

## The Rubric

**Papulopustular rosacea, combined with sodium sulfacetamide 10% (topical)**
- **Effect size: modest.** In a 12-week investigator-blinded, randomized, parallel-group trial at 6 sites, sodium sulfacetamide 10%/sulfur 5% cream with sunscreens (n=75) produced a significantly greater reduction in inflammatory lesions than [[metronidazole]] 0.75% cream (n=77): 80% versus 72% (P=.04). More subjects on the sulfacetamide-sulfur cream showed improved erythema (69% vs 45%; P=.0007) and treatment success on global improvement at week 12 (79% vs 59%; P=.01), with no significant difference in investigator-rated global severity.[^torok] Tolerance ran the other way: good-or-excellent tolerance was reported by 85% of the sulfacetamide-sulfur group versus 97% of the metronidazole group, with 7 subjects showing poor tolerance possibly linked to a sulfa-drug allergy.[^torok] "Modest and comparator-beating on lesion counts and erythema in one solid RCT, at some cost in tolerability against metronidazole."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** One randomized trial supplies the real comparative numbers; a small, open-label, uncontrolled 8-patient case series of a fragrance-free, alcohol-free sulfacetamide-sulfur foam reported similar improvement and high patient satisfaction, which is supportive but cannot establish efficacy on its own.[^torok][^trumbore]

**Acne vulgaris (topical, tested at 5% sulfur in combination products)**
- **Effect size: uncertain.** A 2020 evidence-based review of topical acne treatments (azelaic acid, salicylic acid, nicotinamide, sulfur, zinc, and fruit acid) concluded plainly: "we were uncertain about the effects of sulfur and zinc."[^cochrane] Sulfur's acne use is long-standing, reviewed for keratolytic and antibacterial action,[^guptanicol] but it sits well behind the far larger controlled-trial base supporting [[benzoyl-peroxide]], [[azelaic-acid]], and the [[retinoids]] for acne. "Uncertain by a 2020 evidence review's own wording; the weakest-evidenced of the treatments it covered alongside zinc."
- **Evidence quality: weak.** The acne indication rests on older pharmacology and review-level literature rather than a modern controlled trial isolating sulfur.[^lin][^guptanicol]

## How It Works

Sulfur's keratolytic action runs through the formation of hydrogen sulfide from direct contact between sulfur particles and keratinocytes; the Lin review notes "the smaller the particle size, the greater the degree of such interaction and the greater the therapeutic efficacy."[^lin] About 1% of topically applied sulfur is absorbed systemically, and adverse effects are described as uncommon and mostly local, though the same review notes a fatal outcome after extensive application in an infant.[^lin] Sulfur's reputation for clogging pores has not held up under testing: a controlled patch-test study at two institutions, biopsying treated skin before and after six weeks of 5% sulfur patches, found "no trend or correlation... between the presence or absence of sulfur in the formulation and the appearance of comedones," while a surfactant (Triton X-100) used in one test vehicle appeared comedogenic instead.[^strauss] The Lin review notes the same tension: an earlier study found sulfur comedogenic on human and rabbit skin, "findings that were not reproduced in other studies."[^lin]

## The Evidence

**Rosacea.** Torok and colleagues randomized 152 rosacea patients across 6 sites to sodium sulfacetamide 10%/sulfur 5% cream with sunscreens or metronidazole 0.75% cream for 12 weeks in an investigator-blinded, parallel-group design. The sulfacetamide-sulfur group showed a larger reduction in inflammatory lesions (80% vs 72%, P=.04), more subjects with improved erythema (69% vs 45%, P=.0007), and more treatment successes on global improvement (79% vs 59%, P=.01); tolerance was good or excellent in 85% of the sulfacetamide-sulfur group versus 97% on metronidazole, and 7 subjects had poor tolerance to the sulfur cream, "possibly caused by a sulfa drug allergy."[^torok] A separate, much smaller open-label case series (8 patients, uncontrolled, not randomized) tested a fragrance-free, alcohol-free sodium sulfacetamide 10%/sulfur 5% emollient foam for papulopustular rosacea and reported clinically significant improvement in severity, a favorable safety profile, and high patient satisfaction; as an 8-patient uncontrolled series it cannot isolate the foam's effect from natural fluctuation.[^trumbore]

**Acne.** An abridged Cochrane systematic review of topical acne treatments (49 trials, 3,880 participants) covering azelaic acid, salicylic acid, nicotinamide, sulfur, zinc, and fruit acid stated the reviewers "were uncertain about the effects of sulfur and zinc," in contrast to azelaic acid, for which the review found more supportive evidence.[^cochrane] A 2004 review of sulfur's dermatologic uses describes it as having "antifungal, antibacterial, and keratolytic activity" and notes its use "alone or in combination with agents such as sodium sulfacetamide or salicylic acid" across acne, rosacea, seborrheic dermatitis, dandruff, pityriasis versicolor, scabies, and warts, adding that "in the past, its use was widespread" in these conditions, without citing a modern controlled trial isolating sulfur for acne.[^guptanicol]

## Common Marketing Claims

None of the claims below is established beyond what The Evidence above supports.

- **"Clears acne as effectively as benzoyl peroxide or retinoids."** A 2020 evidence review of topical acne treatments found the effect of sulfur uncertain, in a review that rated azelaic acid ahead of the other treatments it covered.[^cochrane] No trial in the sourcing for this page tests sulfur head to head against [[benzoyl-peroxide]] or the [[retinoids]].
- **"A natural mineral, so it is inherently gentle and safe."** Sulfur is generally well tolerated with mostly mild local reactions, but the Lin review records a fatal outcome after extensive topical application in an infant, and the Torok rosacea trial found 7 of 75 subjects had poor tolerance possibly linked to sulfa-drug allergy.[^lin][^torok] "Natural" is not the same as risk-free at every dose or in every patient.
- **"Sulfur clogs pores and causes new breakouts."** A controlled two-institution patch-test study found no correlation between sulfur in a formulation and comedone formation, with a surfactant in one test vehicle implicated instead; the Lin review notes the same comedogenicity question produced conflicting results across studies.[^strauss][^lin]
