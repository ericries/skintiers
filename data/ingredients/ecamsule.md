Ecamsule (terephthalylidene dicamphor sulfonic acid, sold as Mexoryl SX) is an organic UVA [[sunscreen-uv-filters|UV filter]], one of a small group of newer UVA filters, alongside [[bemotrizinol]] and [[bisoctrizole]], that were developed and adopted in Europe and Canada well before any US access.[^dsouza][^latha] It reached the US only through a narrow route: the FDA approved it by New Drug Application (NDA) for specific named L'Oreal-brand sunscreen products rather than adding it to the general over-the-counter monograph that any manufacturer can use.[^fda]

> Ecamsule is a photostable organic UVA filter used in Europe and Canada before the FDA approved it for any US product, which didn't happen until 2006, and even then only inside four specific L'Oreal-brand NDAs rather than as a monograph ingredient available to all sunscreen makers; a randomized trial of one of those formulas found removing ecamsule from an SPF 40 cream cut its success at preventing a sun-triggered rash from 56% to 11%.[^fda][^deleo]

## The Rubric

**UVA photoprotection, as part of a multi-filter sunscreen formula (topical)**
- **Effect size: notable, but only tested as one ingredient in a fixed combination.** In a double-blind, randomized, controlled trial in 144 people with a history of polymorphous light eruption (a UV-triggered rash), an SPF 40 cream containing ecamsule 3%, avobenzone 2%, octocrylene 10%, and titanium dioxide 5% was compared, on opposite sides of the same body, against versions of the same cream missing one UVA filter. The full formula prevented flares in 56% of participants (41 of 73) versus 11% (8 of 73) for the ecamsule-free version (P<.001), and in a separate side-by-side comparison, 36% of participants (26 of 71) with the full formula versus 16% (11 of 71) for the version missing avobenzone (P=.02).[^deleo] That is a real, statistically significant, ingredient-isolating result, but it is one trial of one manufacturer's fixed combination cream, not a test of ecamsule alone against a vehicle, and both UVA filters mattered: pulling either one out reduced protection. "Notable within one combination formula; removing ecamsule roughly halved the protection rate in a trial that isolated its contribution, but avobenzone contributed too, so this grades the pairing, not ecamsule in isolation."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** One randomized trial of one product formulation; no independent replication located, and the trial does not report who funded it.[^deleo]

**Photostability and formulation role (topical, chemistry claim, not a health outcome)**
- **Effect size: not independently quantified here.** An independent (non-manufacturer) review states Mexoryl "provides efficient ultraviolet A coverage, better photostability, and enhanced water resistance" than the older UVA option available in the US at the time, avobenzone, which the same review says has "limitations, such as degradation under ultraviolet exposure."[^dsouza] This is a narrative claim from a review article, not a measured photostability comparison we verified from primary data, so it is reported as attributed rather than graded.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable (attributed claim, no primary dataset reviewed).**

## The Evidence

Primary evidence, by question.

**Clinical effect, isolating ecamsule's contribution.** DeLeo and colleagues ran a double-blind, randomized, controlled trial in maximized outdoor conditions in 144 people with a history of polymorphous light eruption (PMLE), a sun-triggered rash used here as a UV-damage readout. Each participant had an SPF 40 cream containing ecamsule 3%, avobenzone 2%, octocrylene 10%, and titanium dioxide 5% applied to one side of the body, and a version of the same cream missing one UVA filter applied to the other side. Comparing the full ("tetrad") formula against the ecamsule-free ("triad") version, "a significantly greater number of successes were detected on the tetrad-treated side compared with either triad: 41 of 73 participants (56%) versus 8 of 73 participants (11%; P<.001)."[^deleo] The same design against the avobenzone-free version found 26 of 71 participants (36%) successful with the full formula versus 11 of 71 (16%) without avobenzone (P=.02).[^deleo] Both comparisons were statistically significant, so the trial supports a real contribution from ecamsule specifically rather than from adding UVA filter volume in general, but it is a single trial of one manufacturer's fixed combination cream (the same formula and concentrations as the FDA-approved Anthelios 40), not a test of ecamsule alone against a vehicle, and the published abstract does not disclose a funding source.[^deleo][^fda]

**Photostability and UVA coverage, as characterized in review literature.** An independent (non-manufacturer) review in a plastic surgery journal states that Mexoryl "provides efficient ultraviolet A coverage, better photostability, and enhanced water resistance," contrasting it with the older UVA option long available in the US, avobenzone ("Parsol 1789"), which it says has "limitations, such as degradation under ultraviolet exposure."[^dsouza] A separate dermatology review classifies "ecamsule (terephthalylidene dicamphor sulphonic acid)" alongside dometrizole trisiloxane, bemotrizinol, and bisoctrizole as one of the "organic UVA sunscreening agents."[^latha] Neither source reports a measured absorption spectrum or a quantitative photostability index for ecamsule itself, so this is reported as an attributed characterization rather than a graded finding.

**Regulatory history.** Ecamsule was used in European and Canadian sunscreens before it reached the US market.[^dsouza] FDA records show it was approved for the US not through the general OTC sunscreen monograph but through four separate New Drug Applications, all sponsored by L'Oreal USA and all for specific branded products: Anthelios SX (NDA 021502, ecamsule 2% with avobenzone 2% and octocrylene 10%, approved 2006-07-21), Anthelios 20 (NDA 021471, ecamsule 2% with avobenzone 2%, octocrylene 10%, and titanium dioxide 2%, approved 2006-10-05), Capital Soleil 15 (NDA 021501, ecamsule 3% with avobenzone 2% and octocrylene 10%, approved 2006-10-02), and Anthelios 40 (NDA 022009, ecamsule 3% with avobenzone 2%, octocrylene 10%, and titanium dioxide 5%, approved 2008-03-31).[^fda] The independent Mexoryl review states the FDA approval came on "July 24, 2006," consistent with that first Anthelios SX approval.[^dsouza] Because approval was by NDA rather than monograph inclusion, ecamsule could legally be used only in those named products, not by other manufacturers, which is why it remained rare on US shelves even after 2006; the general UVA-coverage gap this reflects, and how it compares with filters since added to the monograph such as bemotrizinol, is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^fda]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Superior/better UVA protection than avobenzone."** An independent review does describe Mexoryl as offering "efficient ultraviolet A coverage, better photostability, and enhanced water resistance" relative to avobenzone,[^dsouza] but that is a narrative characterization, not a head-to-head measured comparison verified here; the one controlled trial located tested ecamsule and avobenzone together in the same formula and found both contributed, not that either alone outperforms the other.[^deleo]
- **"FDA-approved" as a general safety or efficacy seal.** Approval here was narrow: four specific L'Oreal-brand products by New Drug Application, not addition to the OTC monograph that would let any manufacturer use ecamsule.[^fda] The approval does not extend beyond those named formulas.
