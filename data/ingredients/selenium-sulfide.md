Selenium sulfide (also sold as selenium disulfide) is an FDA over-the-counter anti-dandruff active, recognized in the OTC monograph at 1% for controlling dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis, and one of the best-evidenced topical antifungals for the Malassezia-driven scaling behind both.[^fda] It is the active in medicated shampoos such as [[selsun-blue-selenium-sulfide-shampoo|Selsun Blue]] and [[head-shoulders-clinical-strength-dandruff-defense-selenium-sulfide|Head & Shoulders Clinical Strength]].

> Selenium sulfide is a strong, first-line antidandruff antifungal, ranked alongside ketoconazole at the top of [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]]. A 2026 randomized trial found a 0.6% selenium disulfide shampoo statistically matched 2% ketoconazole for seborrheic dermatitis, which is about as good as topical antidandruff evidence gets. Its drawbacks are cosmetic: it can have an odor and, rarely, discolor bleached or treated hair.

## The Rubric

**Dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis (the Malassezia-driven scaling)**
- **Effect size: strong and first-line.** Selenium sulfide reduces the Malassezia yeast that drives dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis and is one of the best-evidenced OTC antifungals for it. In a 2026 randomized, double-blind trial, a 0.6% selenium disulfide shampoo statistically matched 2% ketoconazole on seborrheic dermatitis, the head-to-head detail carried on [[farris-2026-beyond-efficacy-0-6-selenium-disulfide-shampoo-matches-2-k]]. It sits at the top tier of [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]] with ketoconazole.
- **Evidence quality: FDA monograph plus a modern head-to-head.** Its standing rests on OTC-monograph recognition at 1% and on randomized comparisons against the reference antifungal, ketoconazole.[^fda] For seborrheic dermatitis and dandruff the evidence is solid; for [[fungal-acne]] specifically it is one of the better off-label options but less directly studied than the azoles.

**Practical notes (not an efficacy grade)**
- The real drawbacks are cosmetic rather than safety: a sulfur-like odor, and in rare cases discoloration of gray, blonde, bleached, or tinted hair, so treated hair should be rinsed thoroughly. It is used as a short-contact wash a couple of times a week.

## The Evidence

Selenium sulfide is an FDA OTC-monograph anti-dandruff active at 1%, reflecting long recognized use for dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis.[^fda] Its mechanism is antifungal and antiproliferative against Malassezia. What raises it above a merely established active is modern comparative data: a 2026 randomized, double-blind trial found a 0.6% selenium disulfide shampoo statistically matched 2% ketoconazole, the reference topical antifungal, for seborrheic dermatitis, set out on [[farris-2026-beyond-efficacy-0-6-selenium-disulfide-shampoo-matches-2-k]]. That places it firmly in the first-line tier on [[anti-dandruff-seborrheic-dermatitis-treatments-by-evidence]] and [[seborrheic-dermatitis]].

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Clinical strength" / higher percentages.** OTC selenium sulfide is a monograph active at 1%; higher prescription strengths exist, but the OTC evidence base is strong at monograph concentrations.[^fda]
- **Fungal acne treatment.** Plausible and sometimes effective off-label because the same Malassezia yeast is involved, but the [[fungal-acne]] evidence centers on azole antifungals; selenium sulfide is a reasonable secondary option there, not the best-documented one.
