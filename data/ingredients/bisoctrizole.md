Bisoctrizole (INCI methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol, sold as Tinosorb M) is an organic ultraviolet filter that absorbs across both the ultraviolet-B and ultraviolet-A bands and, unlike most organic filters, is manufactured as microfine particles that stay largely on the skin surface rather than penetrating it.[^latha][^herzog][^durand] It covers UVB (about 290 to 320 nm) and both UVA II (about 320 to 340 nm) and UVA I (about 340 to 400 nm), spanning roughly 280 to 400 nm, which makes it one of the few single filters that is broad-spectrum on its own; SPF is a UVB metric and UVA is a separate axis, both explained on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^latha] It is photostable, which separates it from [[avobenzone]], the main UVA filter in older US sunscreens, whose absorbance falls under sunlight unless a co-filter holds it stable.[^hojerova][^akintilo] Regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use is one of the few skincare practices with randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden, and bisoctrizole is one of the filters delivering that protection where it is sold; the health benefit is established for the practice of regular use, not for bisoctrizole in isolation, and is documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^latha] Its regulatory position is split: it is an authorised UV filter in the European Union at up to 10%, and is found in European, Asian, and South American sunscreens, but it is not in the United States OTC sunscreen monograph, so it is absent from sunscreens sold in the US.[^eu2000][^akintilo][^fda2019][^fda2026]

> Bisoctrizole is a photostable organic UV filter that, at up to its 10% European maximum, covers both UVB and the full UVA range in a single molecule, a spectral breadth and stability that older US filters such as avobenzone lack; it is manufactured as sub-200 nm particles and shows very low skin penetration in vitro, and it is authorised across the EU and used widely abroad while remaining outside the US OTC sunscreen monograph.[^latha][^herzog][^durand][^hojerova][^akintilo][^eu2000][^fda2019]

## The Rubric

The grades below are for bisoctrizole's job in a sunscreen, framed relative to the other filters it is measured against. Skin penetration and tolerance are a separate axis, graded separately and not folded into the photoprotection grades.

**Ultraviolet-A photoprotection**
- **Effect size: notable, and photostable.** Bisoctrizole is an organic UVA filter that covers long-wave UVA I (about 340 to 400 nm), the part of the spectrum that drives photoaging and that mineral titanium dioxide attenuates less well.[^latha] In model sunscreens tested in people by persistent pigment darkening, "UVA-protection factors (UVA-PF) obtained from the in vivo studies increased with the concentration of the UVA filter," and formulations reaching a UVA-PF at or above 4 "in most cases met also the conditions of the Australian Standard."[^herzog] Its distinguishing property is stability: an MBBT-containing sunscreen tested under natural sunlight showed "excellent UVA1 photostability," with a photostability index of 1.00.[^hojerova] A US dermatology review groups it with the newer filters that "offer better protection from UVA and free radicals than avobenzone."[^akintilo] "Notable and stable UVA coverage, filling the same UVA role as avobenzone but without avobenzone's photodegradation."
- **Evidence quality: solid for the spectral and photostability characterization; the health payoff is inherited, not filter-specific.** The UVA coverage and photostability are documented across independent laboratory, in-vivo, and formulation studies.[^latha][^herzog][^hojerova] What is not demonstrated for bisoctrizole on its own is a clinical-endpoint benefit; the randomized evidence that broad-spectrum sunscreen slows photoaging and lowers skin-cancer burden is for the practice of regular use, covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^latha]

**Ultraviolet-B photoprotection**
- **Effect size: notable contribution to SPF.** Unlike avobenzone, which is a UVA-only filter, bisoctrizole also absorbs UVB (about 290 to 320 nm) and contributes to a product's SPF; a review lists it among agents "effective against UVA and UVB rays ranging from 280 to 400nm."[^latha] In broad-spectrum combinations, prototypes pairing "bisoctrizole (USAN), bemotrizinol (USAN), avobenzone, and octocrylene" attained the "highest" category for UVA in an in-vitro test method.[^dueva] "Notable, adding both UVB and UVA coverage in one filter rather than covering a single band."
- **Evidence quality: solid characterization.** Its broad-spectrum absorption is documented in the review and formulation literature.[^latha][^dueva]

**Skin penetration and tolerance (a separate axis, not an efficacy grade)**
- Bisoctrizole is a large molecule delivered as a particulate dispersion, and it penetrates skin very little. In a Franz-cell study of sprayable sunscreens with high filter loads, MBBT showed a maximum penetration of "0.14 microg cm(-2) h(-1)," among the lowest of the organic filters tested, and "more than 50% of each sunscreen agent stayed on the surface on the skin."[^durand] The filter "is produced as particulate organic UVA-filter dispersion with average particle size smaller than 200 nm," which is the physical reason it stays on the surface.[^herzog] The European Union authorises it at up to 10%, a level assessed as "not likely to produce harmful effects for the health of users."[^eu2000] This is a lower systemic exposure than the older filters avobenzone and octinoxate, which FDA maximal-use trials found are absorbed into plasma above the agency's 0.5 ng/mL safety-testing threshold.[^akintilo] The main tolerance signal is contact allergy to the raw material: a Portuguese case series titled "The UV filter tinosorb M, containing decyl glucoside, is a frequent cause of allergic contact dermatitis" attributed the reactions to the decyl glucoside emulsifier in the commercial Tinosorb M dispersion.[^pereira]

## What We Actually Know

Bisoctrizole is a well-characterized organic UV filter with an unusual combination of properties: it absorbs across both UVB and UVA (roughly 280 to 400 nm), it is photostable, and it barely penetrates skin.[^latha][^hojerova][^durand] Its breadth means one filter covers what avobenzone (UVA only) and a UVB filter such as octinoxate cover between them, and its stability avoids avobenzone's central weakness, loss of absorbance under sunlight.[^latha][^hojerova][^akintilo] Because it is manufactured as sub-200 nm particles, most of an applied dose stays on the skin surface, and its measured in-vitro penetration is very low.[^herzog][^durand] The health value of using it is the value of regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use, which has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing melanoma and squamous-cell tumor burden and is documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; that benefit is a property of regular, adequate application rather than of any single filter.[^latha] Its main practical limit is availability: it is authorised and widely used in Europe, Asia, and South America, but it is not part of the US OTC sunscreen monograph, so US sunscreens cannot contain it.[^eu2000][^akintilo][^fda2019][^fda2026]

## How It Works

Bisoctrizole is an organic (chemical) filter, so it absorbs ultraviolet photons and releases the energy as heat, but it is formulated differently from most organic filters. Rather than being dissolved in the oil phase, it is milled into a suspension of microfine particles: it "is produced as particulate organic UVA-filter dispersion with average particle size smaller than 200 nm."[^herzog] Its absorption spans the terrestrial UV range, covering UVB (about 290 to 320 nm), UVA II (about 320 to 340 nm), and UVA I (about 340 to 400 nm); a review lists it among filters "effective against UVA and UVB rays ranging from 280 to 400nm."[^latha] It is also photostable, keeping its absorbance under prolonged sun exposure where avobenzone loses its.[^hojerova][^akintilo] The particulate form has a second consequence: the filter is a large aggregate rather than a small dissolved molecule, so it stays largely on the skin surface, which is the basis of its very low measured skin penetration.[^herzog][^durand]

## The Evidence

Primary evidence, by question.

**Spectral coverage.** A review in the Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology classifies bisoctrizole among the "organic UVA sunscreening agents" and states that newer broad-spectrum agents including MBBT "have been found to be effective against UVA and UVB rays ranging from 280 to 400nm."[^latha] Its UVA protection has been measured directly: in model formulations tested in vivo by persistent pigment darkening, "UVA-protection factors (UVA-PF) obtained from the in vivo studies increased with the concentration of the UVA filter," and formulations with a UVA-PF at or above 4 "in most cases met also the conditions of the Australian Standard."[^herzog]

**Photostability.** In a study of fifteen sunscreens with the same labeled SPF exposed to natural sunlight, the product built on MBBT (with ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate and ethylhexyl triazone) showed "excellent UVA1 photostability," quantified as an "Auc-UVA1 Index" of 1.00, the top of the scale.[^hojerova] That intrinsic stability contrasts with avobenzone, which a dermatology review calls "highly unstable" and which formulators stabilize with a co-filter such as octocrylene "in an attempt to retard its degradation."[^akintilo]

**Broad-spectrum performance in combination.** In work on UVA in-vitro test methods, "two experimental sunscreen prototypes with bisoctrizole (USAN), bemotrizinol (USAN), avobenzone, and octocrylene were tested" under the modified conditions and attained the "highest" category for UVA protection.[^dueva]

**Skin penetration.** An in-vitro Franz-cell study of sprayable emulsions with high filter concentrations measured how much of each filter crossed skin over 24 hours. MBBT gave a maximum penetration of "0.14 microg cm(-2) h(-1)," among the lowest measured, and across the filters "more than 50% of each sunscreen agent stayed on the surface on the skin."[^durand] The particle size behind this is documented separately: the filter is dispersed as particles "smaller than 200 nm."[^herzog]

**Skin tolerance.** A case series from a Portuguese dermatology unit reported the Tinosorb M raw material as "a frequent cause of allergic contact dermatitis," and its title attributes the reactions to the decyl glucoside emulsifier contained in the commercial dispersion rather than to the filter molecule.[^pereira]

**Regulatory status.** In the European Union, bisoctrizole is an authorised UV filter. Commission Directive 2000/6/EC added "2,2'-Methylene-bis-6-(2H-benzotriazol-2yl)-4-(tetramethyl-butyl)-1,1,3,3-phenol" to the permitted UV-filter list at a maximum concentration of 10%, having concluded it "is not likely to produce harmful effects for the health of users" as used in sunscreen products; it is now entry 23 of Annex VI to the EU Cosmetics Regulation.[^eu2000] A US dermatology review notes the newer filters including bisoctrizole are "frequently found in Asian, European, and South American sunscreens."[^akintilo] It is not part of the US framework: the FDA's 2019 proposed sunscreen rule addressed sixteen filters (bisoctrizole is not among them) and noted that filters submitted through the Time and Extent Application process under the Sunscreen Innovation Act are handled separately; the FDA's June 2026 final order adding a modern filter to the monograph added only bemotrizinol, at up to 6%, and did not add bisoctrizole.[^fda2019][^fda2026] Bisoctrizole therefore remains unavailable in US OTC sunscreens.

## Uses

Well-supported: as a broad-spectrum UV filter in a sunscreen, where bisoctrizole covers UVB and the full UVA range in one photostable molecule and, where it is sold, supplies the protection that regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use provides against photoaging and skin-cancer burden.[^latha][^herzog][^hojerova] That health benefit is a property of regular, adequate sunscreen use documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], not of bisoctrizole alone.[^latha] Its microfine-particle form gives it very low in-vitro skin penetration, and the EU authorises it to 10%.[^durand][^herzog][^eu2000] Its practical limit is geography: it is used across Europe, Asia, and South America but is not permitted in US OTC sunscreens.[^akintilo][^fda2019][^fda2026]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Next-generation broad-spectrum protection."** Bisoctrizole genuinely absorbs across both UVB and UVA (about 280 to 400 nm) and is photostable, which older single-band filters are not.[^latha][^hojerova] But the health payoff of any filter is the payoff of regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use, which depends on adequate application and reapplication, not on the filter's spectrum alone; that evidence is on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^latha]
- **"Does not absorb into the skin."** In-vitro data support very low penetration: a Franz-cell study measured a maximum of 0.14 microg cm(-2) h(-1) for MBBT, with most of the applied filter remaining on the surface, consistent with its sub-200 nm particulate form.[^durand][^herzog] This is a measured in-vitro finding, and the EU authorises the filter at up to 10% as not likely to harm users; it is a lower penetration than the older filters avobenzone and octinoxate, which are absorbed into plasma above the FDA's testing threshold.[^eu2000][^akintilo] One tolerance caveat is on record: the Tinosorb M raw material has been reported as a frequent cause of allergic contact dermatitis, attributed to its decyl glucoside content.[^pereira]
- **"Stabilizes avobenzone."** Bisoctrizole is itself photostable, and broad-spectrum formulas do combine it with avobenzone and octocrylene to reach top UVA categories.[^hojerova][^dueva] The filter shown in controlled photostability work to prevent avobenzone's breakdown is the related bemotrizinol (Tinosorb S), not bisoctrizole; a claim that bisoctrizole specifically rescues avobenzone is not supported by the evidence here.
