Panthenol (provitamin B5, also formulated as its more penetrant precursor dexpanthenol) is a humectant and skin-conditioning ingredient used across cleansers, moisturizers, and after-sun and wound-care products. Proksch and Nissen describe it as "converted in tissues to pantothenic acid, a component of coenzyme A," the cofactor that "catalyses early steps in the synthesis of fatty acids and sphingolipids which are of crucial importance for stratum corneum lipid bilayers and cell membrane integrity."[^proksch] Its best-supported role is soothing and short-term barrier/wound recovery, not a treatment active in its own right.

> Topical dexpanthenol has controlled-trial support for speeding barrier recovery, easing irritation, and modestly accelerating minor wound and post-procedure healing, though a notable share of that evidence is manufacturer-linked: the main clinical review is co-authored by Bayer Consumer Care employees, and Bayer owns the original Bepanthen dexpanthenol brand.[^anniversary] Its cosmetic use as a humectant that lowers water loss is supported by one dose-ranging trial at 1% and 5%.[^camargo] Anti-aging or wrinkle claims are not supported by controlled evidence; panthenol functions as a supporting soothing/hydrating ingredient, best paired with better-evidenced actives such as [[niacinamide]] rather than used as a treatment on its own.

## The Rubric

**Skin barrier repair, irritation recovery, and minor wound healing (topical dexpanthenol)**
- **Effect size: modest but consistent across several controlled comparisons.** On skin irritated with sodium lauryl sulphate, a dexpanthenol-containing cream produced "significantly accelerated skin barrier repair" and "significantly reduced skin redness as a sign of inflammation" compared with a vehicle cream, which "produced no effect" on redness.[^proksch] In a split-face trial of 38 patients after fractional ablative CO2 laser resurfacing, a dexpanthenol ointment gave "significantly faster cure of the lesions" on days 1 and 2 and "significantly better cosmetic results and re-epithelialization" on days 1, 2, and 5 versus plain petroleum jelly, though both sides reached "completed wound healing on day 14."[^heise] A review of the wider clinical literature reports dexpanthenol "accelerated re-epithelization in wound healing" and had "an anti-inflammatory effect on experimental ultraviolet-induced erythema," while noting it "is well tolerated, with minimal risk of skin irritancy or sensitization."[^ebner] "Modest and consistent: faster short-term healing and less redness than a plain vehicle or petrolatum, mainly over the first few days, with both arms converging by full healing."
- **Evidence quality: several controlled trials, heavily manufacturer-linked.** The 2017 "70th anniversary" review of dexpanthenol is co-authored by three Bayer Consumer Care AG employees, and Bayer owns the original Bepanthen brand;[^anniversary] an earlier clinical review is industry-linked through a different maker, its authors affiliated with Beiersdorf/Eucerin.[^ebner] The post-procedure laser trial carries no funding or conflict-of-interest statement on its PubMed record, and its full declaration was not accessible, so it is best read as manufacturer-adjacent but not confirmed-funded.[^heise] The SLS-irritation trial does not disclose a sponsor.[^proksch] Independent confirmation exists, but much of the literature clusters around parties with a commercial interest in dexpanthenol.

**Moisturizing and reducing water loss (topical panthenol, cosmetic concentrations)**
- **Effect size: modest and concentration-dependent.** In forearm formulations tested at 0.5%, 1.0%, and 5.0% panthenol over 30 days, "panthenol-containing formulations (1.0% and 5.0%) produced significant decreases in TEWL," and on skin freshly irritated by a sodium laureth sulphate wash, "significant reduction of TEWL was evident two hours after application of formulations loaded with panthenol when compared with control and vehicle."[^camargo] The authors concluded skin integrity was best maintained by "the improved protective effect of 1.0% panthenol added to the formulation," which is consistent with the roughly 1% to 5% range most leave-on products use.[^camargo] "Modest; a real, dose-dependent cut in water loss at 1% and 5% in one trial, useful as a supporting humectant rather than a standalone treatment."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** One instrumented, single-blind, vehicle-controlled formulation trial from an academic cosmetic-science group (randomization not reported).[^camargo]

**Anti-aging and wrinkle-reduction claims (topical)**
No controlled trial in the sources above tests panthenol or dexpanthenol against collagen synthesis, elastin, or wrinkle depth; the supported endpoints are transepidermal water loss, redness, and short-term wound closure.[^proksch][^camargo][^heise] Grading it against anti-aging claims is not supported by the evidence gathered here.

## How It Works

Dexpanthenol is "converted in tissues to pantothenic acid, a component of coenzyme A," and that coenzyme "catalyses early steps in the synthesis of fatty acids and sphingolipids which are of crucial importance for stratum corneum lipid bilayers and cell membrane integrity."[^proksch] Ebner and colleagues describe it as "the stable alcoholic analog of pantothenic acid," whose topical use "is based on good skin penetration and high local concentrations."[^ebner] As a small, water-binding molecule it also acts directly as a humectant, which is the more immediate mechanism behind its measured reduction in transepidermal water loss at cosmetic concentrations.[^camargo]

## The Evidence

**Barrier repair and irritation (topical dexpanthenol).** Proksch and Nissen induced irritation with sodium lauryl sulphate in patch-test chambers, then applied a dexpanthenol-containing cream or its vehicle twice daily. "Significantly accelerated skin barrier repair was found in treatments with the dexpanthenol-containing cream (verum) compared with vehicle-treated (placebo) or untreated skin." Both creams increased stratum corneum hydration and reduced roughness, "but significantly more so with the dexpanthenol-containing cream," and "the dexpanthenol-containing cream significantly reduced skin redness as a sign of inflammation in contrast to the vehicle, which produced no effect."[^proksch]

**Post-procedure wound healing (topical dexpanthenol).** Heise and colleagues randomized the wound area of 38 patients after fractional ablative CO2 laser resurfacing to a dexpanthenol-containing ointment on one side and petroleum jelly on the other, with dermatoscopic imaging and visual-analogue-scale scoring on days 1, 2, 5, and 14. "Measuring the diameter of laser-generated lesions revealed a significantly faster cure of the lesions in wound sections that were treated with the dexpanthenol-containing ointment on days 1 and 2," and "visual evaluation of the wounds revealed significantly better cosmetic results and re-epithelialization on days 1, 2 and 5." By day 14, "all patients exhibited a completed wound healing" on both sides.[^heise] The paper's PubMed record shows no funding or conflict-of-interest statement.

**Moisturizing and TEWL (topical panthenol, cosmetic concentrations).** Camargo and colleagues applied formulations containing 0.5%, 1.0%, or 5.0% panthenol to the forearms of healthy subjects daily for 30 days, measuring hydration and TEWL, and separately tested TEWL two hours after a sodium laureth sulphate wash. "Panthenol-containing formulations (1.0% and 5.0%) produced significant decreases in TEWL after 30-day applications," and after the surfactant wash, "significant reduction of TEWL was evident two hours after application of formulations loaded with panthenol when compared with control and vehicle." The authors concluded that "skin integrity is maintained by the improved protective effect of 1.0% panthenol added to the formulation."[^camargo]

**Broader clinical review (topical dexpanthenol).** Ebner and colleagues' review of dexpanthenol across wound care, skin grafts, scars, and burns reports that "monitoring of transepidermal water loss showed a significant acceleration of epidermal regeneration as a result of dexpanthenol therapy" and that it "has been shown to have an anti-inflammatory effect on experimental ultraviolet-induced erythema," concluding that topical dexpanthenol "is well tolerated, with minimal risk of skin irritancy or sensitization."[^ebner] A 2017 follow-up marking 70 years of the Bepanthen brand, co-authored by three Bayer Consumer Care AG scientists, reaffirms dexpanthenol's "moisturizing and skin barrier enhancing potential" and role in wound healing, while noting that "the exact mechanisms of action of dexpanthenol have not been fully elucidated yet."[^anniversary]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Anti-aging" or wrinkle-reduction claims for panthenol.** None of the controlled evidence above measures collagen synthesis, elastin, or wrinkle depth; the supported endpoints are water loss, redness, and short-term wound closure.[^proksch][^camargo][^heise] Treat anti-aging framing as unsupported by this evidence.
- **"Clinically proven skin barrier repair" on a generic cosmetic product.** The strongest repair and healing data come from dexpanthenol-specific formulations tested against a plain vehicle or petrolatum, funded largely by Bayer;[^heise][^anniversary] a product citing "clinically proven" barrier repair on the strength of panthenol alone is extrapolating beyond a single dose-ranging humectant trial.[^camargo]
