Hydroquinone is a topical depigmenting drug and the long-standing reference treatment for melasma, available in the United States only by prescription.[^triluma][^fda2006]

It works by suppressing melanin production, and its clinical record is real but middling: it beats placebo, yet in head-to-head rankings of melasma treatments it lands near the bottom, and the form with the strongest evidence and the only US drug approval is the fixed triple-combination cream that pairs it with a retinoid and a corticosteroid.[^haddad][^liu][^chan][^triluma] Its evidence and its safety questions both concentrate on one use, melasma.

> Hydroquinone reliably lightens melasma more than placebo, but a 59-trial network meta-analysis ranked it near the bottom of 14 treatments for efficacy while giving it one of the lower side-effect rates; the fixed triple combination with tretinoin and fluocinolone is its best-supported form and its only FDA-approved one, indicated for short-term use.[^haddad][^liu][^chan][^triluma]

## The Rubric

Grades differ by how hydroquinone is used.

**Melasma (as monotherapy)**
- **Effect size: modest to notable.** Hydroquinone clears melasma better than placebo but sits low when melasma treatments are ranked against each other. In a double-blind, split-face RCT (30 enrolled, 25 completed), the hydroquinone 4% side improved 76.9% versus its paired placebo side, with local side effects in 25%.[^haddad] A network meta-analysis of 59 RCTs ranked hydroquinone near the bottom of 14 treatments for efficacy while giving it one of the lower side-effect rates (18.2%), and found the fixed triple-combination cream and the Q-switched Nd:YAG laser more effective.[^liu] Azelaic acid is a well-evidenced depigmenting agent that beats hydroquinone 2% and roughly ties 4%; learn more on [[azelaic-acid]]. A 6-RCT meta-analysis (673 patients) found azelaic acid produced a slightly greater reduction in the Melasma Area and Severity Index than hydroquinone (mean difference -1.23; 95% CI -2.05 to -0.40; P=0.004).[^albzea] "Modest to notable: the reference depigmenting topical, beaten on efficacy rankings by the triple-combination cream and several lasers and edged by azelaic acid on MASI, but better tolerated than most alternatives."
- **Evidence quality: solid.** A placebo-controlled RCT, a 59-trial network meta-analysis, a comparative meta-analysis against azelaic acid, and decades of clinical use converge on a genuine but middling monotherapy effect.[^haddad][^liu][^albzea] Short of gold-standard because the placebo-controlled trials are small and older and the comparative rankings carry the heterogeneity of a broad network meta-analysis.

**Melasma (fixed triple combination with tretinoin and fluocinolone)**
- **Effect size: notable.** The hydroquinone 4% / tretinoin 0.05% / fluocinolone acetonide 0.01% cream is the FDA-approved form and the best-supported way hydroquinone is used. In a multicenter, investigator-blinded RCT in Asian patients with moderate-to-severe facial melasma, 77 of 120 patients (64.2%) on the triple combination reached a global severity score of "none" or "mild" at week 8 versus 48 of 122 (39.4%) on hydroquinone 4% alone (P<0.001), at the cost of more local adverse events (63/129, 48.8%, vs 18/131, 13.7%).[^chan] The same network meta-analysis rated the triple-combination cream the most favorable topical option for melasma.[^liu] Tretinoin, the retinoid in the combination, is the trial-validated benchmark retinoid and improves melasma on its own; learn more on [[tretinoin]]. "Notable: superior to hydroquinone alone and the top-ranked topical, though more irritating, and its FDA label restricts it to short-term use."[^chan][^liu][^triluma]
- **Evidence quality: solid.** A multicenter RCT, FDA approval on the strength of controlled trials, and a network meta-analysis agree.[^chan][^liu][^triluma]

## What We Actually Know

Hydroquinone lightens melasma more than placebo: a split-face RCT found a 76.9% improvement on the treated side.[^haddad] It is the reference agent the field measures newer options against, but it is not the strongest. A network meta-analysis of 59 RCTs placed hydroquinone monotherapy near the bottom of 14 treatments for efficacy, though with one of the lower rates of side effects, and it rated the fixed triple-combination cream the most favorable topical.[^liu] A head-to-head meta-analysis found azelaic acid edged hydroquinone on MASI reduction.[^albzea] The form with the most controlled support and the only US marketing approval is the prescription triple combination with tretinoin and fluocinolone, which beat hydroquinone 4% alone in a multicenter RCT while causing more irritation.[^chan][^triluma] Two safety issues sit beside the efficacy. Prolonged topical use is associated with exogenous ochronosis, a blue-black skin discoloration that "in severe cases" the FDA describes as causing "disfiguring and irreversible effects," and oral hydroquinone showed "some evidence" of carcinogenicity in animal bioassays, findings the FDA cited when it proposed in 2006 that over-the-counter skin-bleaching hydroquinone products are not generally recognized as safe and effective.[^fda2006]

## How It Works

Hydroquinone is a phenolic compound that reduces the skin's production of melanin. A review of depigmenting agents describes it as "a tyrosinase inhibitor," introduced in 1961, that blocks the rate-limiting enzyme of melanin synthesis and acts preferentially on hyperactive melanocytes; the same review notes that bleaching compounds are "fairly ineffective on dermal accumulation of melanin," so hydroquinone works on epidermal pigment rather than pigment deposited deeper in the skin.[^briganti] The FDA label for the triple-combination cream calls hydroquinone "a melanin synthesis inhibitor" but is candid about the details, stating that the mechanism of action of the cream's active ingredients in treating melasma "is unknown."[^triluma] In the triple combination, the retinoid ([[tretinoin]]) increases epidermal turnover and the penetration of the depigmenting agent, and the corticosteroid (fluocinolone acetonide) reduces the irritation the other two actives cause.[^chan][^triluma]

## The Evidence

Primary clinical evidence, by use:

**Melasma, hydroquinone versus placebo.** A double-blind, prospective, split-face RCT enrolled 30 patients (25 completed), applying hydroquinone 4% cream to one side of the face and placebo to the other at night, with a standardized SPF 25 sunscreen used on both sides. The hydroquinone side improved 76.9% relative to its paired placebo side, with side effects in 25% of that group.[^haddad] Melasma is aggravated by ultraviolet exposure, and this and other trials pair depigmenting treatment with daily sunscreen; how UV filters cover the UVB and UVA spectrum is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

**Melasma, triple combination versus hydroquinone alone.** A multicenter, randomized, investigator-blinded, parallel trial in East and South-East Asian patients with moderate-to-severe facial melasma compared the fixed triple combination (fluocinolone acetonide 0.01%, hydroquinone 4%, tretinoin 0.05%) against hydroquinone 4% over 8 weeks. The triple combination was superior on the primary endpoint: 77 of 120 patients (64.2%) reached a global severity score of "none" or "mild" at week 8 versus 48 of 122 (39.4%) on hydroquinone alone (P<0.001). Related adverse events were more common with the triple combination (63/129, 48.8%) than with hydroquinone (18/131, 13.7%), though most were mild and none was severe.[^chan]

**Melasma, comparative ranking.** A systematic review and network meta-analysis pooled 59 RCTs using the Melasma Area and Severity Index. Ranking efficacy against placebo in descending order, hydroquinone placed 13th of 14 therapies, below the Q-switched Nd:YAG laser, intense pulsed light, ablative fractional laser, the triple-combination cream, topical vitamin C, oral tranexamic acid, chemical peeling, azelaic acid, microneedling, topical tranexamic acid, tretinoin, and the picosecond laser; the Q-switched Nd:YAG laser was more effective than hydroquinone. On side effects, hydroquinone was among the better-tolerated options (18.2%), and the authors concluded the triple-combination cream "was found to be the most favorable selection among the topical drugs for melasma."[^liu]

**Melasma, hydroquinone versus azelaic acid.** A systematic review and meta-analysis of 6 RCTs (673 patients) comparing azelaic acid with hydroquinone found azelaic acid produced a greater reduction in the Melasma Area and Severity Index (mean difference -1.23; 95% CI -2.05 to -0.40; P=0.004), with no significant difference on an objective response scale or in adverse events.[^albzea]

**Safety and regulatory status.** The FDA describes exogenous ochronosis, a deposition of blue-black pigment in the skin, as reported "following the topical application of hydroquinone," and states that "in severe cases, ochronosis may cause disfiguring and irreversible effects."[^fda2006] Separately, National Toxicology Program 2-year oral gavage bioassays showed "some evidence" of carcinogenicity in rats and mice; this is oral animal data, not topical human data, but the FDA weighed it. Citing these data, the FDA proposed in 2006 that over-the-counter skin-bleaching drug products "are not generally recognized as safe and effective (GRASE) and are misbranded," and stated it intended to treat all hydroquinone skin-bleaching products, "whether currently marketed on a prescription or OTC basis, to be new drugs requiring an approved new drug application (NDA) for continued marketing."[^fda2006] The 1982 tentative final monograph the agency withdrew had proposed that "hydroquinone (1.5 to 2.0 percent) be GRASE."[^fda2006] Hydroquinone's one US drug approval is as part of the prescription triple-combination cream, indicated "for the short-term treatment of moderate to severe melasma of the face" and, per its label, "NOT indicated for the maintenance treatment of melasma."[^triluma]

## Uses

Well-supported: facial melasma, where hydroquinone is the reference depigmenting topical and is strongest as the FDA-approved fixed triple combination with tretinoin and fluocinolone, which outperformed hydroquinone 4% alone in a multicenter RCT.[^chan][^triluma] Supported but middle-tier: hydroquinone monotherapy for melasma, which beats placebo but ranks low against lasers, the triple-combination cream, and azelaic acid.[^haddad][^liu][^albzea] Hydroquinone acts on epidermal pigment and is relatively ineffective on dermal melanin.[^briganti] Its main practical limits are local irritation and, with prolonged use, exogenous ochronosis; its FDA-approved form is restricted to short-term use.[^triluma][^fda2006]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"The most powerful skin lightener / the gold standard for dark spots."** A network meta-analysis of 59 melasma RCTs ranked hydroquinone monotherapy 13th of 14 treatments for efficacy.[^liu] It is the long-used reference agent, not the strongest option, and azelaic acid edged it on MASI reduction in a head-to-head meta-analysis.[^albzea]
- **"Safe for continuous long-term use."** The FDA-approved triple-combination label restricts use to short-term melasma treatment and states the cream is "NOT indicated for the maintenance treatment of melasma."[^triluma] Prolonged topical hydroquinone is associated with exogenous ochronosis, which the FDA describes as capable of "disfiguring and irreversible effects."[^fda2006]
- **"Fades every kind of pigmentation."** Depigmenting compounds act on epidermal melanin and are "fairly ineffective on dermal accumulation of melanin," so hydroquinone does less for pigment deposited deep in the skin.[^briganti]
