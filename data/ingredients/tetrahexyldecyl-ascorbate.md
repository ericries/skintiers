Tetrahexyldecyl ascorbate (THD ascorbate, sometimes THDA or THDC) is an oil-soluble ester of vitamin C, built by attaching a fatty branched-chain alcohol to ascorbic acid so the molecule dissolves in oils and silicones rather than water; it is the vitamin C form behind serums such as Medik8's C-Tetra line.[^swindell] Ingredient lists sometimes name the same molecule "ascorbyl tetraisopalmitate" (ATIP); a structural comparison by cosmetic chemist Michelle Wong concluded the two INCI names refer to the same molecule in the vast majority of commercial ingredients, so products labeled either way are this same form rather than two different ingredients.[^wong] The pitch for lipophilic esters like this one is that they dodge the instability and low-pH penetration barrier that limits [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]], but the direct human trial evidence for THD ascorbate on its own is thin, and the most rigorous data available describe it as chemically fragile rather than a stable improvement on the parent molecule. It also does not act on skin directly: it must be hydrolyzed to release ascorbic acid, and the rate at which that conversion happens in skin is not quantified in the fetched literature.[^swindell]

> The manufacturer-affiliated lab that patents THD ascorbate has itself published that the compound "is a poor antioxidant that degrades rapidly when exposed to singlet oxygen" and reduced the viability of oxidative-stressed keratinocytes in vitro, an effect it says is fixed only by co-formulating it with a second patented antioxidant, acetyl zingerone.[^swindell] In the one randomized controlled trial isolating THD ascorbate against that combination, the THD-ascorbate-alone arm did not reliably improve wrinkle, pigment, or redness scores over 8 weeks and worsened on some measures, while the combination arm improved.[^afzal] There is no independent, non-sponsor-funded human trial of THD ascorbate isolated against L-ascorbic acid.

## The Rubric

**Chemical stability and antioxidant activity (in vitro, mechanism)**
THD ascorbate's own patent-holder describes it as chemically weak on its own: in cell-free and keratinocyte assays it "degrades rapidly when exposed to singlet oxygen" and is characterized in the paper's title as needing stabilization.[^swindell] That paper also found THD ascorbate reduced viability in oxidative-stressed keratinocytes, an effect "completely abrogated" only when acetyl zingerone was added.[^swindell] This is the opposite of a stability advantage over L-ascorbic acid; it is evidence that the isolated ester is itself reactive and short-lived once exposed to oxidative conditions, from the lab with the commercial and patent interest in the ingredient.

**Photoaging appearance, pigment, and redness (topical, cosmetic)**
- **Effect size: unclear when isolated, modest only in combination.** The one randomized, double-blind, sponsor-funded trial comparing 5% THD ascorbate plus 1% acetyl zingerone against 5% THD ascorbate alone over 8 weeks in 44 participants found the combination arm improved wrinkle severity (down 3.72% at week 8, P=0.048), pigment intensity (down 4.10%, P=0.0002), and redness (down 14.25%, P=0.045), while the THD-ascorbate-alone arm moved the wrong direction on several of the same measures (wrinkle severity up 4.48%, redness up 8.34% at week 8).[^afzal] An open-label, uncontrolled 12-week study of a commercial THD-ascorbate-plus-acetyl-zingerone serum, funded by the product's manufacturer, reported significant improvements in pigmentation, fine lines, smoothness, firmness, and elasticity, but had no vehicle or comparator arm.[^min] Neither trial isolates THD ascorbate against L-ascorbic acid or against a vehicle control.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary and industry-funded throughout.** The RCT (Afzal) was funded by Sytheon, the company that holds patents on THD ascorbate and acetyl zingerone; the open-label study (Min) was funded by Image Skincare, the maker of the finished product tested; neither has an independent, non-industry replication.[^afzal][^min]

**Skin tolerability**
A published report of allergic contact dermatitis associated with two eye creams containing THD ascorbate suggests it is a recognized, if uncommon, contact allergen; because only the title was fetchable, the patient count and whether the diagnosis was confirmed by patch testing are unresolved.[^scheman]

## What We Actually Know

The evidence base for THD ascorbate isolated from other actives is small and comes almost entirely from the company that patents it. The clearest controlled finding is a negative one for the ingredient's stability claim: in vitro, THD ascorbate "degrades rapidly when exposed to singlet oxygen" and lowers keratinocyte viability under oxidative stress unless paired with a second stabilizing antioxidant.[^swindell] The one RCT that isolates a THD-ascorbate-alone arm found that arm's wrinkle severity and redness got worse over 8 weeks (up 4.48% and 8.34% respectively) and its pigment intensity barely moved (down 0.69%, not separately tested for significance), while the same ester combined with acetyl zingerone improved on all three measures.[^afzal] THD ascorbate's reputation for penetrating skin well rests on a molecular-dynamics computer simulation of the same molecule under its other name, ascorbyl tetraisopalmitate, plus supplier in-vitro data, not on a measured human or ex-vivo skin-penetration study or on clinical efficacy tested head-to-head against [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]] itself.[^machado][^wong] THD ascorbate is also a recognized, if uncommon, contact allergen, per a published report of allergic contact dermatitis linked to two eye creams containing it (patch-test confirmation unresolved; only the title was fetchable).[^scheman]

## How It Works

THD ascorbate is an ester of ascorbic acid with tetrahexyldecanol, a branched fatty alcohol, giving the molecule an oil-soluble character that lets it be formulated in anhydrous or silicone-based serums instead of the acidic aqueous solutions [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]] needs to penetrate. Being an ester rather than ascorbic acid itself, it is not antioxidant-active on skin until it is hydrolyzed; Swindell and colleagues track this breakdown as "THDC fatty acid ester hydrolysis with carboxylesterase-2 (CES2)," but the fetched abstract does not report what fraction of applied THD ascorbate converts to ascorbic acid in skin or over what timeframe.[^swindell] A 2023 review by researchers affiliated with the ingredient's patent-holder describes THD ascorbate as an "ascorbic acid precursor" that acetyl zingerone stabilizes "in the dermal microenvironment," language that frames THD ascorbate itself as a delivery form requiring conversion and protection rather than an active molecule with independently demonstrated skin biology.[^meyer] The same review notes acetyl zingerone "may blunt pro-inflammatory effects of THDC, such as activation of type I interferon signaling," which implies the isolated ester can itself provoke an inflammatory signal in the skin.[^meyer] Its reputation for penetrating skin better than [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]] traces to a molecular-dynamics computer simulation of the related molecule ascorbyl tetraisopalmitate moving through modeled skin membranes, plus unpublished supplier in-vitro data, not to a measured human or ex-vivo skin-flux study.[^machado]

## The Evidence

**Stability and antioxidant activity (in vitro).** Swindell and colleagues, whose paper discloses the compound is patented by Sytheon Ltd. and lists authors with ownership, consulting, or employment ties to the company, reported that THD ascorbate (abbreviated THDC) "is a poor antioxidant that degrades rapidly when exposed to singlet oxygen," a degradation "prevented by combination with acetyl zingerone (AZ) as a stabilizing antioxidant." THD ascorbate alone "reduced viability of keratinocytes exposed to oxidative stress," an effect "completely abrogated by the addition of AZ"; the combination also increased collagen protein production and repressed MMP1 and MMP7 expression in dermal fibroblasts.[^swindell]

**Photoaging, pigment, and redness (topical, human, randomized).** Afzal and colleagues ran a prospective, randomized, double-blind, sponsor-funded (Sytheon) trial in 44 participants comparing 5% THD ascorbate plus 1% acetyl zingerone against 5% THD ascorbate alone for 8 weeks, assessed by photography and colorimetry. The combination arm showed wrinkle severity down 0.75% at week 4 (P=0.023) and 3.72% at week 8 (P=0.048), pigment intensity down 4.10% at week 8 (P=0.0002), and redness down 3.73% at week 4 (P=0.0162) and 14.25% at week 8 (P=0.045). The THD-ascorbate-alone arm moved the opposite direction on several of the same measures over the same period, with wrinkle severity up 7.88% at week 4 and 4.48% at week 8, and redness up 27.5% at week 4 and 8.34% at week 8. The authors concluded "daily use of combined THDA and AZ may improve facial wrinkle severity, pigment intensity, and erythema to a greater extent than THDA."[^afzal]

**Open-label serum study.** Min, Pérez Damonte, and Sivamani ran a 12-week, uncontrolled, open-label study of a commercial serum containing THD ascorbate and acetyl zingerone (funded by the product's manufacturer) in 35 participants aged 21-55, reporting significant decreases in pigmentation and fine lines and significant increases in smoothness, firmness, and elasticity versus each participant's own baseline (P<0.0001), plus a reduction in transepidermal water loss at week 4 (P=0.01). The study had no vehicle or comparator arm, so the specific contribution of THD ascorbate cannot be separated from the rest of the formula or from a placebo effect.[^min]

**Contact allergy.** Scheman, Fournier, and Kerchinsky reported allergic contact dermatitis associated with two eye creams containing THD ascorbate; only the title was fetchable, so the patient count and whether patch testing confirmed the diagnosis are unresolved.[^scheman]

**Penetration (in-silico simulation, not a measured human study).** Machado and colleagues modeled the transport of ascorbyl tetraisopalmitate (ATIP), the same molecule marketed under the THD ascorbate/THDA name, through simulated skin membranes using molecular dynamics with a Martini force field, comparing penetration via the stratum corneum route to the hair-follicle (infundibulum) route. The molecule showed greater affinity for a direct stratum-corneum pathway and lower, more laterally mobile affinity in the infundibulum route. This is a computational model of molecular behavior, not a measurement of penetration through excised or living human skin, and it is the primary source behind the popular claim that this ester "penetrates better" than L-ascorbic acid.[^machado]

## Uses

Preliminary, sponsor-funded evidence only: facial wrinkle appearance, pigment intensity, and redness, where the strongest signal in the one available RCT came from THD ascorbate combined with acetyl zingerone rather than THD ascorbate alone, which did not improve over 8 weeks.[^afzal] No fetched study measures THD ascorbate's own penetration by direct measurement (the available penetration evidence is a computer simulation, not a skin-flux measurement), its stability in a finished formula, or its clinical effect against a vehicle control or against [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]] head-to-head.[^machado] A patch-test case series documents it as a cause of allergic contact dermatitis in some users.[^scheman]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"More stable than L-ascorbic acid."** Not supported by the fetched primary evidence. The in vitro data available, from the ingredient's own patent-holder, describe the isolated ester as degrading rapidly under oxidative stress and needing a second stabilizing antioxidant to hold up, which is a stability liability, not a demonstrated advantage over [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid]].[^swindell]
- **"Penetrates better because it's oil-soluble."** The penetration claim traces to a molecular-dynamics simulation of ascorbyl tetraisopalmitate (the same molecule under a different INCI name) moving through modeled skin membranes, not to a measured human or ex-vivo skin-flux study, and no fetched study compares that penetration to [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|L-ascorbic acid's]] own pH-dependent penetration.[^machado][^wong] The oil solubility is a real chemical property; a measured penetration advantage over L-ascorbic acid is not established in the sources fetched here.
- **"Clinically proven to reduce wrinkles and pigment."** The one RCT isolating THD ascorbate alone did not show consistent improvement on wrinkle or redness scores over 8 weeks; the improvements reported for THD-ascorbate-containing serums came from formulas combined with acetyl zingerone, tested in sponsor- or manufacturer-funded trials.[^afzal][^min]
