Topical ivermectin 1% cream, sold under the brand Soolantra, is a prescription drug the FDA approved on December 19, 2014 for the once-daily treatment of the inflammatory lesions of rosacea.[^fda][^dailymed] It is not a cosmetic active; ivermectin is best known as an oral antiparasitic, and the topical cream is proposed to work through a dual anti-inflammatory and anti-parasitic action against the *Demodex* mites implicated in rosacea's inflammation.[^galderma][^schaller]

## The Rubric

**Papulopustular (inflammatory) rosacea**
- **Effect size: notable.** In two identically designed, randomized, double-blind, 12-week, vehicle-controlled trials, once-daily ivermectin 1% reached treatment success (Investigator's Global Assessment of "clear" or "almost clear") in 38.4% and 40.1% of patients versus 11.6% and 18.8% for vehicle (both P<.001), and reduced inflammatory lesion counts by 76.0% and 75.0% versus 50.0% for both vehicle groups.[^stein] In a separate 962-patient head-to-head trial against twice-daily metronidazole 0.75% cream, ivermectin was superior on lesion reduction (83.0% vs 73.7%, P<0.001) and on the share of patients rated clear or almost clear (84.9% vs 75.4%, P<0.001).[^taieb] "Notable: roughly triples the odds of a clear-or-almost-clear rating against vehicle, and beats metronidazole 0.75%, the older standard topical, by about 9 to 10 percentage points head-to-head."
- **Evidence quality: solid.** Two independent 12-week vehicle-controlled trials, an independent head-to-head superiority trial against metronidazole, a 40-week long-term extension against azelaic acid 15% gel, an abridged Cochrane systematic review that rated ivermectin-versus-placebo evidence high-quality, and FDA approval built on that trial base all converge.[^stein][^taieb][^steingold][^cochrane][^fda] Short of gold-standard for the same reason as [[azelaic-acid]]'s rosacea grade: the head-to-head comparisons are single-comparator trials, not a network replicated against every alternative topical.

## What We Actually Know

Topical ivermectin has genuine, independently replicated efficacy for the inflammatory papules and pustules of [[rosacea-papulopustular|papulopustular rosacea]], the subtype defined by inflammatory lesions: it beat an inert vehicle by a wide margin in two 12-week trials and outperformed the older topical metronidazole 0.75% in a large head-to-head trial.[^stein][^taieb] An abridged Cochrane review of the rosacea treatment field rated the ivermectin-versus-placebo evidence high-quality and found it slightly more effective than metronidazole in one comparative study.[^cochrane] The FDA approval and the underlying trials cover the papulopustular (inflammatory-lesion) form of rosacea specifically; the drug's own indication statement is limited to "inflammatory lesions," not the flushing and persistent redness of erythematotelangiectatic rosacea.[^dailymed]

## How It Works

The FDA label is candid that the mechanism is not settled: "the mechanism of action of SOOLANTRA cream in treating rosacea lesions is unknown."[^dailymed] The proposed account, described by manufacturer Galderma as ivermectin having "been reported to have both anti-inflammatory and antiparasitic activity," rests on two threads.[^galderma] The antiparasitic thread targets *Demodex* mites, present at higher density on rosacea-affected skin and implicated, though not established as the sole cause, in the inflammation.[^galderma] The anti-inflammatory thread is supported by a monocentric pilot study of 20 patients treated with ivermectin 1% cream for at least 12 weeks: mite density on skin-surface biopsies fell significantly by week 6 and week 12 (P<0.001), while gene expression of the inflammatory and innate-immune markers IL-8, LL-37 (the active cathelicidin peptide), HBD3, TLR4, and TNF-alpha was downregulated, reaching statistical significance for LL-37, HBD3, and TNF-alpha at both follow-up points and for TLR4 at week 12 (all P<0.05); 16 of the 20 patients reached an IGA score of 1 or less.[^schaller] The authors concluded the cream "acts by a dual, anti-inflammatory and anti-parasitic mode of action ... by killing Demodex spp. in vivo, in addition to significantly improving clinical signs and symptoms."[^schaller]

## The Evidence

**Regulatory approval.** The FDA approved Soolantra (ivermectin) Cream, 1% under NDA 206255 on December 19, 2014, as a Type 3 new dosage form; Galderma announced the approval publicly four days later.[^fda][^galderma] The current label indication reads: "SOOLANTRA cream is indicated for the treatment of inflammatory lesions of rosacea," with dosing instructions to "apply to the affected areas of the face once daily," using "a pea-size amount for each area of the face (forehead, chin, nose, each cheek) that is affected."[^dailymed]

**Vehicle-controlled trials (Stein and colleagues, 2014).** Two identically designed, randomized, double-blind, 12-week trials in subjects with moderate to severe papulopustular rosacea compared once-daily ivermectin 1% cream against vehicle. In both studies a greater proportion of the ivermectin group reached treatment success (IGA clear or almost clear): 38.4% and 40.1% versus 11.6% and 18.8% for vehicle (both P<.001), and inflammatory lesion counts fell 76.0% and 75.0% versus 50.0% for both vehicle groups, with statistically significant superiority from week 4 through week 12.[^stein] The authors concluded that ivermectin 1% cream was effective and safe in treating the inflammatory lesions of papulopustular rosacea.[^stein]

**Head-to-head against metronidazole (Taieb and colleagues, 2015, the ATTRACT study).** In a phase 3, investigator-blinded, randomized trial, 962 subjects with moderate to severe papulopustular rosacea received once-daily ivermectin 1% (n=478) or twice-daily metronidazole 0.75% (n=484) over 16 weeks. At week 16 ivermectin 1% was significantly superior to metronidazole 0.75% in reduction of inflammatory lesions from baseline (83.0% versus 73.7%; P<0.001), a difference the authors reported as early as week 3, and IGA success (clear or almost clear) also favoured ivermectin, 84.9% versus 75.4% (P<0.001).[^taieb] Adverse-event incidence was comparable between arms, and local tolerability favored ivermectin.[^taieb]

**Long-term comparison with azelaic acid (Stein Gold and colleagues, 2014).** Two 40-week extension studies followed the two 12-week vehicle-controlled trials: subjects originally on ivermectin continued it, and those originally on vehicle switched to azelaic acid 15% gel. Ivermectin "was safe throughout the study with a lower incidence of related adverse events (AEs) compared to AzA 15% gel," with no ivermectin-group discontinuations due to a related adverse event, and remained efficacious, with the share of patients rated clear or almost clear higher at the end of the extension than at baseline; the authors reported it "safe and effective for up to 52 weeks of total treatment."[^steingold] This is a within-protocol comparison of tolerability and continued response, not a randomized head-to-head efficacy trial against azelaic acid; azelaic acid's own rosacea evidence, including its own head-to-head trial against metronidazole, is graded separately on [[azelaic-acid]].

**Independent synthesis.** The abridged, updated Cochrane review of 106 randomized rosacea trials found "topical ivermectin was more effective than placebo based on two studies (high-quality evidence)," and "slightly more effective than metronidazole in one study."[^cochrane] Full detail on that review is on [[van-zuuren-rosacea-cochrane]].

## Uses

Well-supported: the inflammatory lesions (papules and pustules) of papulopustular rosacea, a prescription-only use where two vehicle-controlled trials and a large head-to-head trial against metronidazole 0.75% converge with FDA approval and a high-quality Cochrane rating.[^stein][^taieb][^cochrane][^fda] The approved indication and every cited trial address inflammatory lesions specifically; they do not cover the persistent facial redness and flushing of the erythematotelangiectatic subtype, covered on [[rosacea-erythematotelangiectatic]].[^dailymed] Within papulopustular rosacea, ivermectin sits alongside [[azelaic-acid]] at the top of the evidence-graded topicals, both rated high-quality by the Cochrane review, ahead of the older standard, metronidazole.[^cochrane]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Clears rosacea redness."** Not supported by the cited trials. The FDA indication and the vehicle-controlled and head-to-head trials measure inflammatory lesion counts and an IGA scale, not a dedicated erythema endpoint; the drug's own indication statement is limited to "inflammatory lesions."[^dailymed][^stein][^taieb]
- **"Kills the mites that cause rosacea."** Partly grounded, partly overreach. A pilot study found a significant reduction in *Demodex* density alongside clinical improvement, and Galderma describes an antiparasitic action.[^schaller][^galderma] But *Demodex* mites are described as implicated in rosacea's inflammation, not established as its sole cause, and normal skin carries the same mites at lower density.[^galderma]
- **"Works better than any other rosacea topical."** Overreach as a blanket claim. Ivermectin beat metronidazole 0.75% head-to-head, and the Cochrane review rated both ivermectin and azelaic acid high-quality against placebo, with azelaic acid also beating metronidazole in its own head-to-head trial; the trials here support standing alongside the best-graded alternative, not an outright best.[^taieb][^cochrane]
