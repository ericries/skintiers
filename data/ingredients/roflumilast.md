Roflumilast is a small-molecule phosphodiesterase-4 (PDE4) inhibitor, chemically 3-(cyclopropylmethoxy)-N-(3,5-dichloro-4-pyridinyl)-4-(difluoromethoxy)benzamide (C17H14Cl2F2N2O3), originally developed as an oral drug for COPD and reformulated as a topical for inflammatory skin disease.[^pubchem] Topical roflumilast is FDA-approved as a 0.3% cream for plaque psoriasis, a 0.15% cream for atopic dermatitis, and a 0.3% foam for seborrheic dermatitis, the active ingredient in [[zoryve-roflumilast-cream]]; across four identically structured phase 3 trials it beat vehicle on Investigator Global Assessment (IGA) success by roughly 17 to 40 percentage points, one of the larger effect sizes documented for a non-steroidal topical in this rubric.[^dermis][^integument][^stratum]

> In the DERMIS-1 and DERMIS-2 phase 3 trials, once-daily roflumilast cream 0.3% for 8 weeks produced IGA success in 42.4% and 37.5% of plaque psoriasis patients versus 6.1% and 6.9% with vehicle (P<0.001 for both).[^dermis]

## The Rubric

**Plaque psoriasis (topical 0.3% cream, including intertriginous/skin-fold sites)**
- **Effect size: large.** DERMIS-1 and DERMIS-2 (n=881 combined) found IGA success at week 8 in 42.4% vs 6.1% (trial 1) and 37.5% vs 6.9% (trial 2) of roflumilast vs vehicle patients, a roughly 30 to 40 percentage-point absolute difference.[^dermis] Intertriginous IGA success (a site poorly served by steroids) was even higher: 71.2% vs 13.8% and 68.1% vs 18.5%.[^dermis] Worst Itch success (a 4-point reduction on a 0-10 scale in patients starting at 4 or higher) was also met by a majority of treated patients (67.5% and 69.4% vs 26.8% and 35.6% on vehicle).[^dermis]
- **Evidence quality: strong.** Two identically designed, adequately powered, double-blind, vehicle-controlled phase 3 RCTs with consistent, statistically significant results (P<0.001) across nearly all 9 secondary endpoints in both trials.[^dermis] Both trials were industry-sponsored (Arcutis Biotherapeutics, the drug's maker) and short (8 weeks), so durability beyond that window and effectiveness relative to established actives like topical corticosteroids or calcipotriene remain unaddressed; the authors themselves note "further research is needed to assess efficacy compared with other active treatments."[^dermis]

**Atopic dermatitis (topical 0.15% cream)**
- **Effect size: moderate.** In INTEGUMENT-1 and INTEGUMENT-2 (n=1337 combined, ages 6+, mild to moderate disease), Validated IGA for AD success at week 4 was 32.0% vs 15.2% and 28.9% vs 12.0% for roflumilast vs vehicle (P<0.001 for both), roughly a 17 percentage-point difference, smaller in relative terms than the psoriasis result.[^integument] A 75% reduction in Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI-75) was achieved by 43.2% and 42.0% of roflumilast patients vs 22.0% and 19.7% on vehicle.[^integument]
- **Evidence quality: strong but shorter-duration.** Two large, well-designed, double-blind, vehicle-controlled phase 3 RCTs, but the treatment window was only 4 weeks, half the psoriasis trials' duration, and again industry-sponsored by the drug's maker.[^integument] Tolerability was favorable: at each visit, at least 95% of 885 roflumilast-treated patients showed no signs of application-site irritation and 90% reported no or mild application-site sensation, a notable point given crisaborole's and topical steroids' known tolerability issues in AD.[^integument]

**Seborrheic dermatitis (topical 0.3% foam)**
- **Effect size: large.** In a single phase 3, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trial, IGA success at week 8 was 79.5% with roflumilast foam vs 58.0% with vehicle (P<0.001), with the roflumilast arm already separating from vehicle by week 2 (43.0% vs 25.7%) and week 4 (73.1% vs 47.1%).[^stratum] The high vehicle-arm response (58%) is itself notable and typical of seborrheic dermatitis trials, where the foam vehicle alone has meaningful anti-scale/anti-flake effect.
- **Evidence quality: moderate.** A single phase 3 trial (not the two-trial replication pattern used for psoriasis and AD), industry-sponsored, with only an 8-week treatment window for a chronic, relapsing condition; the authors state "study limitations include the 8-week treatment period for this chronic condition" and call for longer trials to establish durability.[^stratum]

## How It Works

Roflumilast is a selective inhibitor of phosphodiesterase-4 (PDE4), the enzyme that degrades cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) inside inflammatory and skin cells; blocking PDE4 raises intracellular cAMP, which in turn suppresses production of pro-inflammatory mediators including interferon-gamma and tumor necrosis factor-alpha.[^review] This is the same target class as oral apremilast (Otezla) and topical crisaborole (Eucrisa). Pharmacokinetic data from phase 1 through phase 3 studies show topical bioavailability of only 1.5%, a flat, low plasma concentration-time curve (peak-to-trough ratio 1.2, unlike the sharper peaks seen with oral PDE4 inhibitors), and skin concentrations 62- to 126-fold higher than corresponding plasma trough levels, consistent with the drug forming a reservoir in the stratum corneum and being slowly released (elimination half-life about 4 days) rather than acting as a fast-clearing topical.[^pk] Because the drug reaches meaningful systemic exposure (mean plasma trough around 1.7 ng/mL of roflumilast across the two DERMIS trials, with an active N-oxide metabolite present at roughly 8-fold higher concentration), it carries the same class-level caution as oral roflumilast regarding GI and psychiatric adverse effects at high systemic exposure, though topical dosing keeps that exposure far below oral therapeutic levels.[^pk]

## The Evidence

**Plaque psoriasis, DERMIS-1 and DERMIS-2 (topical 0.3% cream, phase 3 RCTs).** Two identically designed, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, multicenter trials enrolled 881 patients (age 2+) with 2% to 20% body surface area plaque psoriasis, randomized 2:1 to roflumilast cream 0.3% or vehicle once daily for 8 weeks. IGA success (clear or almost clear plus a 2-grade improvement) was reached by 42.4% of roflumilast patients vs 6.1% of vehicle patients in trial 1, and 37.5% vs 6.9% in trial 2 (P<0.001 for both); 8 of 9 secondary endpoints reached significance in trial 1 and all 9 in trial 2, including 75% PASI reduction (41.6% vs 7.6%; 39.0% vs 5.3%) and intertriginous IGA success. Treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 25.2% vs 23.5% (trial 1) and 25.9% vs 18.4% (trial 2) of roflumilast vs vehicle patients, most mild to moderate; serious adverse events were rare and balanced between arms.[^dermis] *A large, well-powered, twice-replicated trial found roflumilast cream produced clear or almost clear skin in roughly 4 in 10 patients within 8 weeks, a strong result for a non-steroidal topical, though the trials only measured against vehicle, not against a corticosteroid or vitamin D analog comparator.*

**Atopic dermatitis, INTEGUMENT-1 and INTEGUMENT-2 (topical 0.15% cream, phase 3 RCTs).** Two phase 3, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials enrolled 1337 patients age 6+ with mild to moderate AD (mean 13.6% body surface area involved), randomized 2:1 to roflumilast cream 0.15% or vehicle once daily for 4 weeks. Validated IGA for AD success at week 4 was 32.0% vs 15.2% (trial 1) and 28.9% vs 12.0% (trial 2), both P<0.001; EASI-75 was reached by 43.2% vs 22.0% and 42.0% vs 19.7%. Tolerability was favorable, with at least 95% of roflumilast-treated patients showing no application-site irritation at each visit.[^integument] *Two large, well-designed trials found roflumilast cream roughly doubled the rate of clear-or-almost-clear skin versus vehicle over 4 weeks with good local tolerability, though the treatment window was short relative to the psoriasis trials and AD is a chronic, relapsing condition that a 4-week snapshot cannot fully characterize.*

**Seborrheic dermatitis, single phase 3 trial (topical 0.3% foam).** A phase 3, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trial randomized adolescent and adult patients with seborrheic dermatitis 2:1 to once-daily roflumilast foam 0.3% or vehicle foam for 8 weeks. IGA success at week 8 was 79.5% with roflumilast vs 58.0% with vehicle (P<0.001), with significant separation already visible at week 2 (43.0% vs 25.7%) and week 4 (73.1% vs 47.1%). The drug was well tolerated with a low rate of treatment-emergent adverse events.[^stratum] *A single, industry-sponsored 8-week trial found a large advantage over vehicle, but seborrheic dermatitis is chronic and relapsing, and the authors themselves flag that longer trials are needed to establish durability beyond the 8-week window studied.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Steroid-free, so you can use it long-term without skin thinning."** The PDE4 mechanism is genuinely distinct from corticosteroids and the trials found low rates of application-site irritation, a real advantage for sensitive or intertriginous skin.[^dermis][^integument] But none of the phase 3 trials ran longer than 8 weeks, so "long-term" safety and efficacy claims outrun the trial evidence; the seborrheic dermatitis authors explicitly call this out as a limitation.[^stratum]
- **"Clinically proven for psoriasis, eczema, and seborrheic dermatitis alike."** True that each indication has its own positive phase 3 data, but the effect sizes differ meaningfully: intertriginous psoriasis and seborrheic dermatitis show the largest gaps over vehicle, while the AD result, though statistically robust across 1337 patients, is a smaller relative improvement over a 4-week window.[^dermis][^integument][^stratum] Treat "works for all three" as accurate but not equally strong evidence across indications.
