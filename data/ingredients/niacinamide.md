Niacinamide (nicotinamide) is the amide form of vitamin B3 (niacin), used in leave-on skincare typically at 2% to 10% and taken orally as a supplement; in the body it is a precursor to the coenzymes NAD+ and NADP+, which run cellular energy metabolism and DNA repair.[^tosti][^ontrac] Its evidence splits by route of use: the strongest controlled data are for oral supplementation reducing skin cancers in high-risk people, while the topical serums most buyers reach for rest on smaller and frequently manufacturer-run trials.

> Oral niacinamide has real but population-dependent evidence for cutting nonmelanoma skin cancers in high-risk immunocompetent people, a 23% reduction in one phase 3 trial, yet a later trial in organ-transplant recipients found no benefit and a 2023 meta-analysis pooling both calls the overall evidence insufficient.[^ontrac][^ontrans][^tosti] Topical niacinamide is well tolerated and has modest evidence for supporting the skin barrier and for lightening facial hyperpigmentation, much of it from small or manufacturer-run studies; the oral skin-cancer data are for a 500 mg twice-daily pill and do not transfer to a topical serum.[^tanno][^hakozaki][^navarrete]

## The Rubric

Grades differ by route and use.

**Skin-cancer and actinic keratosis chemoprevention (ORAL, 500 mg twice daily)**
- **Effect size: modest and population-dependent.** In immunocompetent patients with a heavy skin-cancer history, the ONTRAC trial found the rate of new nonmelanoma skin cancers 23% lower than placebo (95% CI 4 to 38; P=0.02) and actinic keratoses 13% lower at 12 months (P=0.001).[^ontrac] In organ-transplant recipients, the ONTRANS trial found no difference (rate ratio 1.0; 95% CI 0.8 to 1.3; P=0.96).[^ontrans] This is an oral effect on a medical outcome, not something a topical serum has been shown to reproduce, and the benefit stopped when the pill was stopped. "Modest in immunocompetent high-risk skin, absent in transplant recipients; an oral supplement effect, not a serum effect."
- **Evidence quality: solid but conflicting.** Two phase 3 double-blind RCTs reached opposite results in different populations, and a 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis of four trials, pooling immunocompetent and immunosuppressed patients, found the pooled signal did not reach significance (NMSC rate ratio 0.82; 95% CI 0.61 to 1.12).[^ontrac][^ontrans][^tosti]

**Skin barrier support and dry skin (topical)**
- **Effect size: modest.** In cultured human keratinocytes, nicotinamide raised de novo synthesis of ceramides and other stratum-corneum lipids, and topical application increased stratum-corneum ceramide and free fatty acid levels and lowered transepidermal water loss in dry skin.[^tanno] This is a lipid-precursor mechanism that overlaps the barrier support attributed to [[ceramides]], useful for dry skin rather than a treatment for a barrier disease. "Modest; a barrier-lipid boost adjacent to what [[ceramides]] provide, shown mainly as reduced water loss in dry skin."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** Mechanistic keratinocyte work plus an in vivo transepidermal-water-loss measure, from a single manufacturer research group (Kanebo).[^tanno]

**Hyperpigmentation and melasma (topical)**
- **Effect size: modest.** A split-face study of 5% niacinamide reduced facial hyperpigmentation versus vehicle after four weeks, and in a small independent melasma RCT 4% niacinamide was comparable to hydroquinone 4% (good-to-excellent response 44% vs 55%, with no significant colorimetric difference) and better tolerated (side effects 18% vs 29%).[^hakozaki][^navarrete] It works by a different route than [[hydroquinone]], blocking pigment transfer rather than inhibiting tyrosinase. "Modest; roughly comparable to hydroquinone 4% in one small independent melasma trial and better tolerated, on a different mechanism."
- **Evidence quality: mixed.** One small independent RCT (27 patients) plus manufacturer split-face studies; small samples and heterogeneous designs.[^hakozaki][^navarrete]

**Acne (topical)**
- **Effect size: modest.** In a double-blind RCT, 4% nicotinamide gel was comparable to 1% clindamycin gel over 8 weeks (papule and pustule reduction -60% vs -43%, P=0.168; 82% vs 68% of patients improved on the Physician's Global Evaluation, P=0.19).[^shalita] It sits below the better-evidenced topical acne actives [[benzoyl-peroxide]] and [[azelaic-acid]]. "Modest; comparable to topical clindamycin in a single small RCT, on much thinner evidence than benzoyl peroxide or azelaic acid."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** The topical acne case rests largely on one small RCT (76 patients); an abridged Cochrane review found no included nicotinamide trial reported the global self-assessment outcome and rated the topical acne evidence outside azelaic acid "low- to very-low-quality evidence for other topical treatments."[^shalita][^cochrane]

**Photoaging appearance and wrinkles (topical, cosmetic)**
- **Effect size: modest and cosmetic-only.** A double-blind half-face trial (50 women, 12 weeks) reported significant improvement in the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles, hyperpigmented spots, red blotchiness, yellowing, and elasticity for 5% niacinamide versus its vehicle.[^bissett] These are appearance and instrumental-appearance endpoints, not measured collagen synthesis or histology, and they fall short of the demonstrated skin biology behind the [[retinoids]]. "Modest and cosmetic-only; appearance endpoints in a manufacturer half-face study, well below the retinoid class on demonstrated skin biology."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary and manufacturer-funded.** Half-face studies from Procter and Gamble laboratories, scoring appearance rather than histological outcomes.[^bissett]

## What We Actually Know

The best-evidenced niacinamide use is a health outcome from a pill, not a serum: in immunocompetent people with a heavy skin-cancer history, oral nicotinamide 500 mg twice daily lowered new nonmelanoma skin cancers by 23% (95% CI 4 to 38; P=0.02) and actinic keratoses by 13% at 12 months in the ONTRAC trial.[^ontrac] That benefit did not appear in organ-transplant recipients in the ONTRANS trial (rate ratio 1.0; 95% CI 0.8 to 1.3),[^ontrans] and a 2023 meta-analysis pooling both populations concluded there is "insufficient evidence that oral nicotinamide therapy significantly reduces the number of keratinocyte cancers."[^tosti] Topically, niacinamide is well tolerated and has modest, mostly small-trial evidence: it raises keratinocyte ceramide synthesis and lowers water loss in dry skin,[^tanno] lightens facial hyperpigmentation and performed on par with hydroquinone 4% in one small independent melasma trial,[^hakozaki][^navarrete] and matched topical clindamycin for inflammatory acne in a single RCT.[^shalita] Its topical anti-aging support is manufacturer half-face studies measuring appearance rather than skin biology.[^bissett]

## How It Works

Niacinamide is the amide of niacin (vitamin B3). Inside cells it is a building block for nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) and its phosphate (NADP+). The Tosti meta-analysis notes that nicotinamide "participates in cellular energy metabolism" and "modulates multiple cellular survival and death pathways."[^tosti] The skin-cancer rationale runs through that energy and repair role: the ONTRANS investigators note that nicotinamide "enhances the repair of ultraviolet (UV) radiation-induced DNA damage" and "reduces the cutaneous immunosuppressive effects of UV radiation," which is the mechanism ONTRAC tested for chemoprevention.[^ontrans][^ontrac] Its topical actions are separate and use-specific. For the barrier, nicotinamide raises the activity and messenger RNA of serine palmitoyltransferase, the rate-limiting enzyme of sphingolipid synthesis, increasing ceramide, free fatty acid, and cholesterol production; the Tanno group concluded it "improved the permeability barrier by stimulating de novo synthesis of ceramides."[^tanno] For pigment, it does not inhibit tyrosinase; Hakozaki and colleagues found it "had no effect on the catalytic activity of mushroom tyrosinase" and instead concluded niacinamide "works by inhibiting melanosome transfer from melanocytes to keratinocytes."[^hakozaki] For acne, the Shalita trial attributes its use to "potent antiinflammatory activity without the risk of inducing bacterial resistance," a non-antimicrobial route unlike clindamycin.[^shalita]

## The Evidence

Primary clinical evidence, by route and use:

**Skin-cancer and actinic keratosis chemoprevention (oral).** The phase 3, double-blind ONTRAC trial randomized 386 immunocompetent participants with at least two nonmelanoma skin cancers in the previous five years to oral nicotinamide 500 mg twice daily or placebo for 12 months. New nonmelanoma skin cancers were "lower by 23% (95% confidence interval [CI], 4 to 38)" with nicotinamide (P=0.02), squamous-cell carcinomas 30% lower (95% CI 0 to 51; P=0.05), basal-cell carcinomas 20% lower but not significant (95% CI -6 to 39; P=0.12), and actinic keratoses 13% lower at 12 months (P=0.001); the authors concluded "oral nicotinamide was safe and effective in reducing the rates of new nonmelanoma skin cancers."[^ontrac] The companion ONTRANS trial applied the same regimen to 158 organ-transplant recipients and was stopped early for poor recruitment; it found 207 new keratinocyte cancers on nicotinamide versus 210 on placebo (rate ratio 1.0; 95% CI 0.8 to 1.3; P=0.96), concluding "oral nicotinamide therapy did not lead to lower numbers of keratinocyte cancers."[^ontrans] A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis (four eligible trials) that pooled immunocompetent and immunosuppressed patients reported NMSC rate ratio 0.82 (95% CI 0.61 to 1.12) and concluded there is "insufficient evidence that oral nicotinamide therapy significantly reduces the number of keratinocyte cancers."[^tosti]

**Skin barrier and dry skin (topical).** In cultured normal human keratinocytes, nicotinamide at 1 to 30 micromol/L increased ceramide biosynthesis 4.1 to 5.5-fold over control, along with glucosylceramide (7.4-fold), free fatty acid (2.3-fold), and cholesterol synthesis (1.5-fold), by upregulating serine palmitoyltransferase. Topical application "increased ceramide and free fatty acid levels in the stratum corneum" and "decreased transepidermal water loss in dry skin."[^tanno]

**Hyperpigmentation and melasma (topical).** Hakozaki and colleagues tested 5% niacinamide moisturizer against vehicle in a paired split-face design in 18 subjects with hyperpigmentation, and 2% niacinamide plus sunscreen in 120 subjects with facial tanning; niacinamide "significantly decreased hyperpigmentation and increased skin lightness compared with vehicle alone after 4 weeks of use," and the authors concluded it "is an effective skin lightening compound."[^hakozaki] This work was conducted by Procter and Gamble researchers. An independent double-blind RCT in Mexico randomized 27 melasma patients to 4% niacinamide on one side of the face and 4% hydroquinone on the other for eight weeks, both under sunscreen; good-to-excellent improvement was seen in 44% of patients with niacinamide versus 55% with hydroquinone, colorimetry showed no statistical difference between sides, side effects were fewer with niacinamide (18% vs 29%), and the authors called niacinamide "a safe and effective therapeutic agent for this condition."[^navarrete]

**Acne (topical).** A double-blind RCT randomized 76 patients with moderate inflammatory acne to 4% nicotinamide gel (n=38) or 1% clindamycin gel (n=38) twice daily for eight weeks. Papule and pustule counts fell 60% with nicotinamide versus 43% with clindamycin (P=0.168), acne severity fell 52% versus 38% (P=0.161), and 82% versus 68% of patients improved on the Physician's Global Evaluation (P=0.19); the authors concluded "4% nicotinamide gel is of comparable efficacy to 1% clindamycin gel."[^shalita] An abridged Cochrane systematic review of topical acne treatments (49 trials, 3,880 participants) found no included nicotinamide trial reported the participants' global self-assessment outcome and rated the evidence for treatments other than azelaic acid "low- to very-low-quality evidence for other topical treatments."[^cochrane]

**Photoaging appearance (topical).** A double-blind, left-right randomized half-face trial applied 5% niacinamide to one side of the face and vehicle to the other, twice daily for 12 weeks, in 50 white women with photoaging. The Procter and Gamble authors reported significant appearance improvements for niacinamide in fine lines and wrinkles, hyperpigmented spots, red blotchiness, and skin sallowness, plus improved elasticity by cutometry.[^bissett] These endpoints measure appearance and skin properties rather than collagen synthesis or histology.

## Uses

Well-evidenced as an oral supplement: nonmelanoma skin cancer and actinic keratosis chemoprevention in immunocompetent people with a heavy prior skin-cancer burden, where the effect is real but modest and does not carry over to transplant recipients or to a topical product.[^ontrac][^ontrans][^tosti] Supported topically, on small trials: barrier support and dry skin, where nicotinamide raises stratum-corneum lipids and lowers water loss;[^tanno] facial hyperpigmentation and melasma, comparable to hydroquinone 4% in one small independent trial and better tolerated;[^hakozaki][^navarrete] and mild-to-moderate inflammatory acne, comparable to topical clindamycin in a single RCT.[^shalita] Cosmetic and manufacturer-evidenced: the appearance of photoaging, from half-face studies scoring appearance rather than skin biology.[^bissett] Across the topical uses it is generally well tolerated, with side effects described as mild and transient.[^navarrete][^shalita]

## Common Marketing Claims

None of the claims below is established beyond what The Evidence above supports.

- **"Prevents skin cancer" on a serum.** The chemoprevention evidence is for an oral 500 mg twice-daily pill, is population-dependent (present in immunocompetent high-risk patients, absent in transplant recipients), and did not reach significance when pooled across populations.[^ontrac][^ontrans][^tosti] None of it tests a topical niacinamide product.
- **"Brightens and evens skin tone."** The pigment evidence is specifically for facial hyperpigmentation and melasma in small trials, where 4% niacinamide was comparable to hydroquinone 4%.[^hakozaki][^navarrete] Generic radiance or glow claims for already even-toned skin are extrapolation beyond what was tested.
- **"Anti-aging, reduces wrinkles."** The supporting data are Procter and Gamble half-face studies scoring the appearance of lines and spots, not collagen synthesis or histology, and they sit below the [[retinoids]] on demonstrated skin biology.[^bissett]
- **"Repairs the skin barrier."** Grounded mechanistically: nicotinamide increases keratinocyte ceramide synthesis and lowers water loss in dry skin.[^tanno] But that evidence is from one manufacturer research group and does not establish it as a treatment for barrier diseases such as eczema.
