Polydeoxyribonucleotide (PDRN) is a "DNA-derived drug from Oncorhynchus mykiss (Salmon trout) or Oncorhynchus keta (Chum Salmon) sperm with molecular weights between 50 and 1500 KDa," purified by high-temperature extraction into a mixture of DNA fragments.[^galeano] It is the active ingredient behind "salmon DNA" facials and skin boosters such as Rejuran, and it has a real, well-characterized mechanism, but almost all of that evidence comes from PDRN that is injected or applied directly to an open wound, not from a leave-on cosmetic serum.[^galeano][^lampridou]

> PDRN activates the adenosine A2A receptor, which "reduces inflammatory infiltration, promotes endothelial cell proliferation and migration, and VEGF production, stimulates fibroblast differentiation and maturation," a mechanism confirmed across in vitro, animal, and clinical wound-healing studies using local or systemic (largely injected) administration.[^galeano] For injected polynucleotide fillers, a 2025 systematic review found "promising outcomes in reducing wrinkles, improving skin texture, and enhancing elasticity" across nine studies, but rated them "low and moderate quality" with only 219 total patients.[^lampridou] For topical, leave-on cosmetic PDRN, the picture is thinner: one 2026 randomized split-face trial of a specially engineered small-molecule PDRN eye cream reported it outperformed retinol, but it is a single industry-affiliated study of a proprietary, penetration-optimized variant, not evidence that a standard salmon-DNA serum works the same way.[^ye] Injectable results do not automatically transfer to a topical product; a large DNA polymer's ability to cross intact stratum corneum is a real, largely unresolved delivery question, distinct from delivering it under the skin with a needle.[^ye][^galeano]

## The Rubric

**Injected / in-clinic use (skin boosters, dermal biorevitalization)**
- **Effect size: modest, based on a small evidence base.** The 2025 systematic review of polynucleotide injections in esthetic medicine found "promising outcomes in reducing wrinkles, improving skin texture, and enhancing elasticity, with statistically significant results in several studies," and reports "patient satisfaction is moderate to high."[^lampridou] A 2026 split-face RCT of an injectable polynucleotide filler (n=15) found "significant improvements in CFGS and GAIS scores" (wrinkle and global-aesthetic-improvement scales) for both a standard and a reformulated version.[^jeon]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** The systematic review covers only "nine studies, of low and moderate quality," a population of "219 patients," with "limited consensus regarding their optimal use," and one review author discloses being a paid clinical trainer for a polynucleotide distributor.[^lampridou] This is a real but early-stage clinical literature, not a mature one.

**Topical, leave-on cosmetic use (serums, creams)**
- **Effect size: minimal to preliminary, and not established for standard PDRN.** The only topical, leave-on human trial identified is a 2026 randomized, double-blind, split-face study of a "medium-length PDRN preparation (PDRN-850K)" 0.1% eye cream against 0.1% retinol over 28 days, which reported "approximately two-fold greater improvements in periocular wrinkles, dermal thickness, density, and eye bag parameters compared with retinol."[^ye] That is a single, very recent, industry-run study of a proprietary engineered variant built specifically to penetrate skin, run by the ingredient developer's own research affiliates; it is not independent replication, and it is not evidence for the unmodified PDRN sold in most "salmon DNA" serums.[^ye]
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal to preliminary.** Outside that one study, the skin-regeneration literature for PDRN is built on injectable and wound-application data, and the review literature explicitly frames PDRN's regenerative record as coming "through local and systemic administration," a category that in the cited wound-healing studies spans "from topical application to injection" on open wounds, not leave-on application to intact skin.[^galeano] Whether a large DNA polymer penetrates healthy stratum corneum well enough for a cosmetic serum to work like the injectable data is an open question the field itself is still trying to answer.[^ye]

## What We Actually Know

PDRN is not a single molecule but a purified mixture of DNA fragments, extracted from salmon trout or chum salmon sperm, "with molecular weights between 50 and 1500 KDa and a chain length ranging from 50 bp to 2000 bp," processed into a ">95% pure active principle."[^galeano] Its biological activity is grounded in real receptor pharmacology: PDRN fragments bind the adenosine A2A receptor, which is "expressed on most cell types involved in wound healing, including macrophages, fibroblasts and microvascular endothelial cells," and this activation "reduces inflammatory infiltration, promotes endothelial cell proliferation and migration, and VEGF production, stimulates fibroblast differentiation and maturation."[^galeano] That mechanism is well replicated in vitro, in animal wound models, and in clinical wound care, almost entirely using injected, locally infiltrated, or open-wound application.

The gap is topical, leave-on cosmetic use. A 2025 systematic review of polynucleotide injections in aesthetic medicine found real but early-stage benefit for wrinkles, texture, and elasticity, based on only nine low-to-moderate-quality studies and 219 patients.[^lampridou] For a standard leave-on serum applied to intact skin, the independent evidence is essentially absent: the one topical human trial found used a proprietary, penetration-engineered "medium-length" PDRN variant developed by the same commercial lab that ran the study, tested only as a 28-day eye cream against retinol, and has not been independently replicated.[^ye] Marketing that cites "salmon DNA" collagen or regeneration claims for a standard topical PDRN serum is borrowing evidence generated almost entirely from injectable and wound-care contexts, not from leave-on cosmetic use. See [[copper-peptides]] for the same broader pattern: a molecule with strong laboratory and mechanistic evidence whose cosmetic, topical-serum case is much thinner and largely unproven independently.

## How It Works

PDRN's active fragments bind and activate the adenosine A2A receptor, one of four adenosine-responsive G-protein-coupled receptors (A1, A2a, A2b, A3) that "modulate adenylate cyclase activity in an inhibitory (A1, A3) or stimulatory (A2a, A2b) fashion."[^galeano] "The binding of PDRN derivates to A2a receptors stimulates an increase in intracellular cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) with consequent activation of the protein kinase A (PKA)," a signaling step involved in cell growth, survival, and differentiation.[^galeano] Because A2A receptors sit on "macrophages, fibroblasts and microvascular endothelial cells," their activation touches most of the cell types involved in wound repair at once, reducing inflammatory infiltration while promoting endothelial proliferation, VEGF production, and fibroblast maturation.[^galeano] PDRN's fragments can also feed the cellular "salvage pathway," the route cells use to recycle purine bases into new nucleotides rather than synthesizing them from scratch, which the review literature credits alongside A2A signaling for PDRN's regenerative effects.[^galeano]

That mechanism is not in dispute; the open question is delivery. The 2026 topical trial that found a benefit used a purpose-built "medium-length" PDRN fraction and confirmed its skin delivery separately, by "confocal Raman spectroscopy in a reconstructed epidermal model" plus "ex vivo and in vivo penetration studies," specifically because standard PDRN's ability to cross intact stratum corneum was not a given.[^ye] A more recent, industry-affiliated nomenclature review notes that PDRN and the related, longer-chain polynucleotides (PN) are sold commercially in "gels, creams, serums, masks, and injectables," but does not resolve how efficiently the DNA fragments in a leave-on gel or serum actually reach living skin cells the way an injection does.[^marques]

## The Evidence

**Mechanism (primary, durable).** The clearest primary evidence is mechanistic: PDRN activates the adenosine A2A receptor and the purine salvage pathway, and "all data obtained proved the ability of PDRN in promoting physiological tissue repair through adenosine A2A receptor activation and salvage pathway," reviewing 25 years of PubMed literature on PDRN and wound healing.[^galeano]

> **Bottom line.** The receptor mechanism is well established across in vitro, animal, and clinical wound-healing work, but nearly all of that clinical work used injected or wound-applied PDRN, not a leave-on cosmetic product.[^galeano]

**Injectable / in-clinic skin quality.** A systematic review registered with PROSPERO searched Embase, Medline, and Cochrane for polynucleotide esthetic-medicine studies and found "nine studies, of low and moderate quality," covering "219 patients," concluding polynucleotide injections show "promising outcomes in reducing wrinkles, improving skin texture, and enhancing elasticity, with statistically significant results in several studies," while cautioning "rigorous, high-quality studies are essential to validate the effectiveness and safety."[^lampridou] A 2026 split-face RCT (n=15) of an injectable polynucleotide filler found "significant improvements in CFGS and GAIS scores" for wrinkle appearance, with both a conventional and a reformulated (lower-pain) version performing similarly on efficacy.[^jeon]

> **Bottom line.** Injected PDRN/polynucleotide has a real, if still small and heterogeneous, clinical evidence base for skin quality and wrinkles, distinct from anything shown for a topical serum.[^lampridou][^jeon]

**Topical / leave-on cosmetic use.** The only topical human trial found is a 2026 randomized, double-blind, split-face study comparing a 0.1% "PDRN-850K" eye cream to 0.1% retinol over 28 days, reporting the PDRN cream "achieved approximately two-fold greater improvements in periocular wrinkles, dermal thickness, density, and eye bag parameters compared with retinol, with good tolerability."[^ye] The study was run by researchers affiliated with the ingredient's own commercial developer (a Shanghai-based skin-research institute and biotechnology company that also supplied the "PDRN-850K" material), used a proprietary medium-length PDRN fraction specifically engineered and tested for skin penetration rather than standard PDRN, and has not been independently replicated.[^ye]

> **Bottom line.** A topical PDRN trial exists, but it is one industry-linked study of a specialized, penetration-optimized ingredient variant compared only to retinol over four weeks; it does not establish that ordinary PDRN serums or "salmon DNA" creams work the same way.[^ye]

## Uses

Well documented mechanistically: PDRN activates the adenosine A2A receptor and the purine salvage pathway, driving anti-inflammatory, pro-angiogenic, and fibroblast-stimulating effects confirmed across decades of in vitro, animal, and injected or wound-applied clinical research.[^galeano] Emerging, moderate-confidence evidence for injected use: PDRN and related polynucleotide "skin boosters" used in-clinic show statistically significant, but early-stage, benefit for wrinkles, texture, and elasticity across a small pooled literature.[^lampridou][^jeon] Preliminary, largely unproven for topical use: leave-on PDRN serums and "salmon DNA" creams marketed for collagen or regeneration rest on the injectable and wound-care evidence above, not on trials of the leave-on product itself; the single topical human trial identified used a proprietary, delivery-engineered PDRN variant, not standard PDRN, and has not been independently confirmed.[^ye] See [[copper-peptides]] for a parallel case of strong mechanism paired with thin topical-serum evidence, and [[peptides]] for the broader category of biologically active but often under-tested cosmetic actives.

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Salmon DNA repairs and regenerates skin."** The regeneration and repair evidence is real but comes almost entirely from injected PDRN or PDRN applied directly to open wounds, not from a leave-on serum on intact skin.[^galeano]
- **"Collagen-boosting skin booster."** The clinical wrinkle and skin-quality data behind "skin booster" claims are for in-clinic injections, evaluated in a small, low-to-moderate-quality literature (nine studies, 219 patients), not for a topical product applied at home.[^lampridou]
- **"Works like Rejuran, but in a bottle."** Injectable PDRN products such as Rejuran deliver the ingredient under the skin; a topical serum has to cross the stratum corneum first, a step the cosmetic literature has barely begun to test, with one industry-run trial of a specially engineered variant as the only human data found.[^ye]
- **"Anti-aging DNA repair."** PDRN's DNA fragments act as a receptor ligand and salvage-pathway substrate; there is no evidence in the sources here that topical PDRN repairs a user's own genomic DNA.[^galeano]
