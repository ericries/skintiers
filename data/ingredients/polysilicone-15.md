Polysilicone-15 (INCI Polysilicone-15; chemical names dimethicodiethylbenzalmalonate and benzylidene malonate polysiloxane; sold by DSM as Parsol SLX) is an organic ultraviolet-B sunscreen filter authorized in the European Union at up to 10% but absent from the United States over-the-counter sunscreen monograph, which does not list it among its permitted actives.[^cosing][^sccs][^ecfr] It is built on a polysiloxane (silicone) backbone of roughly 60 repeating silicon-oxygen units carrying pendant benzylidene-malonate ester groups that do the UV absorbing; the European Commission's own absorption-spectrum data for the compound show a single peak centered at 312 nm, inside the ultraviolet-B band (roughly 290 to 320 nm), with the curve already back near baseline by 360 nm, well short of reaching deep into the ultraviolet-A band (320 to 400 nm), so it functions as a UVB co-filter rather than a broad-spectrum filter on its own.[^sccs] Regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use has randomized-trial support for slowing photoaging and reducing skin-cancer burden, and polysilicone-15 supplies part of the UVB share of that protection in the non-US products that contain it; the health benefit belongs to the practice and the whole formula, not to this filter in isolation, and is documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Two questions shape how it is graded: how much protection a secondary UVB co-filter actually contributes, and what its large polymer size means for skin penetration.[^couteau][^sccs]

> Polysilicone-15 (Parsol SLX) is a silicone-backbone organic UVB filter authorized in the European Union at up to 10% and used in Korean and other non-US sunscreens; the European Commission's own spectral data center its absorption at 312 nm, inside the UVB band, and an independent in vitro efficacy comparison ranks it toward the lower end of 18 EU-authorized filters. A percutaneous-absorption study reviewed by EU regulators found none of the applied dose in the receptor fluid representing systemic uptake after 16 hours on rat skin, and EU safety review found no evidence of irritation, sensitization, or photo-toxicity, but it is not part of the US FDA sunscreen monograph, so US-sold sunscreens do not use it.[^sccs][^couteau][^cosing][^ecfr]

## The Rubric

The grade below is for polysilicone-15's job in a sunscreen, ultraviolet-B photoprotection as a secondary filter, framed relative to the other UVB filters it is measured against. Skin penetration and systemic safety are a separate axis and are graded separately, not folded into the photoprotection grade.

**Ultraviolet-B photoprotection (as a secondary/co-filter)**
- **Effect size: minimal to modest.** Polysilicone-15 absorbs in the UVB band, peaking at 312 nm per the European Commission's own spectral characterization.[^sccs] An independent in vitro study measuring SPF-type efficacy for 18 EU-authorized filters, each at its maximum permitted concentration, ranked polysilicone-15 at 3.64, above oxybenzone (3.01), octylsalicylate (3.12), and PABA (3.36), but below methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol (3.68), homosalate (4.33), and far below octocrylene (10.41) and octinoxate (10.42).[^couteau] "Minimal to modest as a standalone filter. It contributes some UVB absorbance at its 10% EU maximum but sits toward the weaker end of authorized filters and is formulated as a co-filter, not the primary UVB or UVA workhorse." Its spectrum falls off well before the UVA band, so it supplies no meaningful UVA coverage and is combined with dedicated UVA filters.[^sccs]
- **Evidence quality: solid for the chemistry, regulatory status, and spectral characterization; modest for the specific efficacy figure; the health payoff is inherited, not filter-specific.** The chemical structure, 312 nm absorption peak, and EU authorization are documented in an official EU scientific-committee opinion.[^sccs] The 3.64 efficacy value comes from a single independent in vitro study rather than a body of trials.[^couteau] What is not demonstrated for polysilicone-15 on its own is any clinical-endpoint benefit; the randomized evidence that regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use slows photoaging and lowers skin-cancer burden is for the practice and the whole formula, covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

**Skin penetration and systemic safety (a separate axis, not an efficacy grade)**
- Polysilicone-15 is a large polymer, molecular weight approximately 5,987 for the main homologue.[^sccs] In a percutaneous-absorption study reviewed by the EU Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety (SCCS), the compound was applied to isolated rat and pig skin in a Franz-cell setup; on rat skin, "after 16 hours, none was found in the receptor fluid," the compartment representing systemic uptake, while pig-skin trials across several vehicles recovered roughly 97 to 98% of the applied dose from the skin surface and about 2% from the stratum corneum plus stripped skin, again with none reaching the receptor fluid.[^sccs] The SCCS itself flagged limits in how the report was documented, writing "it is difficult to interpret this interesting report, as no details are given of the number of experiments performed, the statistical analysis carried out, etc," while still concluding "it would appear that about 2% of an applied amount is found in the stratum corneum + the stripped skin, and none in the receptor fluid."[^sccs] That low ex vivo uptake is consistent with the compound's large size. Separately, the same 2010 SCCS review addressed a newer finding that polysilicone-15 is toxic by the inhalation route and, using exposure modeling, concluded there was no consumer risk from its use in pressurized hairsprays or pump sprays; that inhalation question does not apply to the topical, non-aerosol sunscreens covered here.[^sccs] Skin-irritation, sensitization, phototoxicity, and photoallergy testing reviewed in the same opinion found no adverse reactions, and the committee's predecessor concluded in 1999 that "Polysilicone-15 (Dimethicodiethylbenzalmalonate) is safe for use in cosmetic products as a UV light absorber at a maximum concentration of 10%," a conclusion reaffirmed in the 2010 opinion.[^sccs]

## What We Actually Know

Polysilicone-15 is a silicone-backbone organic UVB filter with an official EU absorption-spectrum peak at 312 nm and little coverage beyond the UVB band, so it is used as a co-filter alongside stronger UVB and dedicated UVA actives rather than on its own.[^sccs] An independent in vitro comparison of 18 EU-authorized filters placed its efficacy in the lower range, above a few older filters like PABA and oxybenzone but well below octocrylene, octinoxate, and the newer triazine filters.[^couteau] It is authorized in the European Union under Annex VI of the EU Cosmetics Regulation at up to 10% and is used in Korean and other non-US sunscreens, but it is not part of the US FDA over-the-counter sunscreen monograph (21 CFR 352.10), so it does not appear in US-sold sunscreens.[^cosing][^ecfr] On safety, an EU-reviewed percutaneous-absorption study found none of the applied dose in the receptor fluid representing systemic uptake, consistent with the compound's large polymer size (molecular weight approximately 5,987), and EU toxicological and dermal-safety testing found no evidence of irritation, sensitization, phototoxicity, or photoallergy; both the 1999 and 2010 EU scientific-committee opinions concluded it is safe at up to 10%.[^sccs] The health value of using a sunscreen that contains it is the value of broad-spectrum protection generally, documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], not a property of polysilicone-15 in isolation.

## How It Works

Polysilicone-15 is an organic (chemical) filter: a polysiloxane backbone of roughly 60 repeating silicon-oxygen units, where about 92% of the side-chain positions are simple methyl groups and most of the rest carry a benzylidene-malonate ester group, a cinnamate-like chromophore that absorbs a UV photon and re-emits the energy as heat rather than scattering light the way mineral filters do.[^sccs] The European Commission's own absorption-spectrum plot for the compound shows a single peak centered at 312 nm, inside the UVB band (about 290 to 320 nm), with the curve already declining toward baseline by about 360 nm, so it contributes little to a product's UVA coverage and is paired with a dedicated UVA filter.[^sccs] The EU's cosmetic-ingredient database additionally classifies its function as a light stabilizer as well as a UV absorber and UV filter.[^cosing] Its large size, with a molecular weight around 5,987 for the main homologue, distinguishes it from the smaller organic filters it is typically combined with and is consistent with the low ex vivo skin penetration measured in EU-reviewed studies.[^sccs]

## The Evidence

Primary evidence, by question.

**Chemistry and spectral coverage.** The SCCS opinion describes polysilicone-15 by its chemical names "Benzylidene malonate polysiloxane" and "Dimethico-diethylbenzalmalonate," with a structural formula showing a polysiloxane backbone (n approximately 60) bearing benzylidene-malonate ester substituents, and reports the molecular weight of the main homologue as 5987.[^sccs] Its absorption-spectrum figure, reproduced in the same opinion, plots absorbance against wavelength with the curve peaking at the value labeled 312 (nanometers) on the axis, squarely inside the UVB band.[^sccs]

**UVB efficacy.** An independent in vitro study measured SPF-type efficacy for 18 sun filters authorized in the EU, each "by using the highest concentration allowed by European legislation," and reported ascending order of efficacy including "oxybenzone (3.01) < octylsalicylate (3.12) < PABA (3.36) < polysilicone 15 (3.64) < methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol (3.68) ... < homosalate (4.33) ... < octocrylene (10.41) < octylmethoxycinnamate (10.42)."[^couteau] Polysilicone-15's 3.64 places it in the lower band of the 18 filters tested.

**Percutaneous absorption.** A percutaneous-absorption study reviewed by the SCCS used a modified Franz cell with rat and pig skin. On "naked" rat skin, "after 16 hours, none was found in the receptor fluid," with 1.4% recovered from stripping, 0.4% from the remaining skin, and 98.2% from the skin surface; on pig skin, the figures (chamber/strippings/remaining skin/surface) were "0, 0.2, 2.1, and 97.7," with similar results across isopropyl myristate and petrolatum vehicles.[^sccs] The SCCS's own summary: "it would appear that about 2% of an applied amount is found in the stratum corneum + the stripped skin, and none in the receptor fluid," while noting the underlying report lacked detail on replicate numbers and statistical analysis.[^sccs]

**Regulatory status.** Polysilicone-15 is authorized in the European Union as a UV filter under Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, Annex VI, entry 26, at a maximum concentration in ready-for-use preparations of 10%, alongside its CAS number (207574-74-1) and EC number (426-000-4).[^cosing] The SCCS's 2010 opinion states plainly: "Polysilicone-15 is used as an UV-filter in cosmetic sunscreen products as well as in other cosmetic products at a maximum concentration of 10%," and its conclusion section records that "In 1999, the SCCNFP concluded that Polysilicone-15 (Dimethicodiethylbenzalmalonate) is safe for use in cosmetic products as a UV light absorber at a maximum concentration of 10%."[^sccs] In the United States, 21 CFR 352.10 lists the FDA's permitted over-the-counter sunscreen active ingredients from aminobenzoic acid through zinc oxide, and polysilicone-15 is not among them, so it is not a recognized US sunscreen active.[^ecfr]

## Uses

Well-supported: as a secondary UVB filter in EU-authorized and other non-US sunscreens, where polysilicone-15 adds some UVB absorbance at up to 10% alongside stronger UVB filters and a dedicated UVA filter.[^sccs][^couteau] The health benefit of using such a sunscreen regularly, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer burden, is a property of the practice and the whole formula documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], not of polysilicone-15 on its own. Its practical limits are that it is a mid-to-lower-strength UVB absorber relative to other EU-authorized filters, that it supplies no meaningful UVA coverage, and that it is absent from the US OTC monograph, so products relying on it are not sold as compliant US sunscreens.[^couteau][^ecfr]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Broad-spectrum protection" (attributed to polysilicone-15).** Its absorption spectrum peaks in the UVB band at 312 nm and is essentially back to baseline by 360 nm, so it provides essentially no UVA coverage; any broad-spectrum performance of a product containing it comes from its other filters.[^sccs]
- **"A powerful sunscreen filter."** An independent in vitro efficacy ranking places it in the lower band of 18 EU-authorized filters, below homosalate, octocrylene, and octinoxate.[^couteau]
- **"Not FDA-approved, so it must be unsafe."** Its absence from the US monograph is a listing gap, not a stated safety finding; EU toxicological, irritation, sensitization, and photoallergy testing found no adverse effects, and EU scientific committees concluded it is safe at up to 10% in both 1999 and 2010.[^sccs][^ecfr]
- **"Silicone-based filters don't penetrate skin."** The demonstrated fact is specific to this compound: an EU-reviewed ex vivo study found none of the applied dose in the receptor fluid representing systemic uptake, but the SCCS itself noted the underlying report lacked replicate and statistical detail, so this is a modest, qualified finding rather than a general property of silicone filters.[^sccs]
