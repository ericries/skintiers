Mexoryl 400, INCI methoxypropylamino cyclohexenylidene ethoxyethylcyanoacetate (MCE; 2-ethoxyethyl (2Z)-2-cyano-2-[3-(3-methoxypropylamino)cyclohex-2-en-1-ylidene]acetate, CAS 1419401-88-9), is an organic UV filter developed by L'Oreal with peak absorption at 385 nm that extends a sunscreen's coverage out toward 400 nm, into the long-wave UVA1 band that older filters leave largely unaddressed.[^marionnet][^mercurio] It was authorized by the EU's Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety and added to the bloc's list of permitted UV filters,[^marionnet] making it newer than, and not part of, the US OTC list [[avobenzone]] belongs to; the wider gap between the older filters standard in US sunscreens and the newer ones authorized in the EU and elsewhere is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]. Almost all of the clinical evidence for MCE published so far comes from L'Oreal's own research group; the one independent report is a case of allergic contact dermatitis.[^loretan]

> A peer-reviewed L'Oreal study found an MCE-containing sunscreen extended in-vitro absorption to 400 nm and reduced UVA1-induced pigmentation in a 19-person trial, and a second L'Oreal trial in 52 women found four weeks of an MCE sunscreen improved appearance measures like lip wrinkles and pigmentation versus the same formula without it. Both effects are real findings but come from the filter's inventor; the only fully independent report on MCE published so far is a case of allergic contact dermatitis to it.[^marionnet][^mercurio][^loretan]

## The Rubric

**Extending spectral coverage into long-wave UVA1 (370-400 nm)**
- Effect size: notable, but a spectral/mechanistic measurement, not yet a clinical-outcome one. In vitro, an MCE filter with a peak absorption at 385 nm (absorption coefficient 63,052) widened a reference sunscreen's absorption profile from a ceiling of about 370 nm to 385 nm at 0.7% MCE and 400 nm at 1.5% MCE.[^marionnet] That closes exactly the gap the newer L'Oreal trial describes: conventional sunscreens shield the skin from UV rays up to 370 nm, leaving wavelengths between 370 and 400 nm unfiltered despite their potentially harmful biological and clinical effects.[^mercurio] This is a formulation-chemistry finding about what the molecule absorbs, not an independent replication.
- Evidence quality: preliminary. A single manufacturer research group (L'Oreal Research and Innovation, with the contract lab Episkin), tested at 0.7% and 1.5% MCE.[^marionnet]

**Reducing pigmentation from UVA1 exposure specifically**
- Effect size: modest, small trial. In a randomized, double-blind, 19-volunteer trial, artificial UVA1 exposure (50 J/cm2) left most vehicle-treated skin high on the 0-13 pigmentation scale (74% of volunteers having a score of 9 or 10 at 2 hours); a sunscreen with 1.5% MCE significantly lowered the mean pigmentation score compared with the same sunscreen without it, shifting more volunteers into the 4-5 range.[^marionnet] This is a UVA1-specific pigmentation endpoint, not a photoaging or skin-cancer outcome.
- Evidence quality: preliminary. Nineteen volunteers, one manufacturer-run monocentric trial (NCT04865094).[^marionnet]

**Photoaging appearance with regular use (cosmetic)**
- Effect size: modest and cosmetic-only. In a 4-week, randomized, hemiface trial in 52 Brazilian women (phototypes I-III) using an SPF50 sunscreen with 1% MCE on one side of the face and the same SPF50 formula without MCE on the other, an expert panel rated significant improvement on the MCE side for upper-lip wrinkles, crow's feet, texture, pigmentation, and vascular signs (P<.0001), with weaker but still significant gains in lower-face ptosis (P=.003), lateral-face pigmentation (P=.005), and a naive panel's skin-homogeneity rating (P=.043).[^mercurio] These are appearance and photograph-scored endpoints, not measured collagen, DNA damage, or skin-cancer outcomes, and seven of the eight authors are L'Oreal employees (the eighth, dermatologist Thierry Passeron, is a university-hospital researcher).
- Evidence quality: preliminary. One manufacturer-funded trial, staffed mostly by manufacturer employees.[^mercurio]

**Tolerability**
An independent case report from Geneva University Hospital's dermatology department describes a patient with well-demarcated, infiltrated, erythematous facial plaques who patch-tested positive to MCE ("positive reaction (++) to MCE 1% 50 aq/50 alc at 96 h"), consistent with allergic contact dermatitis to the filter.[^loretan] A single case does not establish how common a reaction this is, but it is so far the only MCE evidence published outside L'Oreal's own research group.

## The Evidence

**Spectral coverage (in vitro).** L'Oreal researchers measured MCE's absorption directly: "the MCE filter exhibited a peak of absorption at 385 nm with an absorption coefficient of 63,052." Added to a reference sunscreen absorbing up to about 370 nm, MCE "enlarged the profile of absorption up to" 385 nm at 0.7% and 400 nm at 1.5%.[^marionnet]

**UVA1-induced pigmentation (clinical, n=19).** In the same paper's randomized, double-blind trial, 19 volunteers had zones of skin irradiated with UVA1 (50 J/cm2) after applying either a reference sunscreen or the same formula with 1.5% MCE. Untreated/vehicle zones scored high on a pigmentation scale ("74% of volunteers having a score of 9 or 10 at 2 hours"); the MCE formula "significantly decreased the mean pigmentation score compared with Reference vivo."[^marionnet]

**UVA1 protection gap and photoaging appearance (clinical, n=52).** A separate L'Oreal trial frames the rationale: conventional sunscreens shield the skin from UV rays up to 370 nm, leaving wavelengths between 370 and 400 nm unfiltered despite their potentially harmful biological and clinical effects. In a 4-week hemiface trial in 52 Brazilian women (phototypes I-III) comparing an SPF50 sunscreen with 1% MCE against the same SPF50 formula without it, an expert panel found significant improvement on the MCE side for upper-lip wrinkles, crow's feet, texture and pigmentation, and vascular abnormalities (P<.0001), plus smaller but significant gains in lower-face ptosis (P=.003), lateral-face pigmentation (P=.005) and a naive panel's homogeneity rating (P=.043).[^mercurio]

**Allergic contact dermatitis (independent case report).** Dermatologists at Geneva University Hospital, unaffiliated with L'Oreal, reported a patient who developed "well-demarcated infiltrated erythematous plaques on the face" and patch-tested positive to MCE ("positive reaction (++) to MCE 1% 50 aq/50 alc at 96 h").[^loretan]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Protects up to 400 nm."** Grounded in the in-vitro absorption data: MCE measurably extends a formula's absorption profile toward 400 nm at higher use levels (1.5% in the cited trial).[^marionnet] Whether that translates into a measurable reduction in a real-world outcome like photoaging or skin cancer, rather than the pigmentation and appearance surrogates studied so far, has not been tested.
