Bentonite is an absorbent clay largely composed of the mineral montmorillonite, formed from weathered volcanic ash.[^zafar] It is also the INCI name for the raw clay used as a base in rinse-off face masks. A 2024 review in Archives of Dermatological Research surveys its proposed cutaneous benefits, oil absorption, anti-inflammatory, wound-healing, drug-delivery, photoprotective, and anti-aging effects, and concludes the evidence behind them is drawn from in vitro experiments, animal studies, and preliminary clinical trials, with larger randomized trials needed before safety and efficacy are established.[^zafar]

> Bentonite masks absorb surface oil and reduce shine right after use, an effect shown in an uncontrolled, manufacturer-run study of a mixed clay mask, but no controlled human trial isolates bentonite alone for a lasting skin benefit; the one on-site RCT testing a bentonite mask for acne combined it with an unrelated botanical as an add-on to oral antibiotics, and its result did not hold up after adjusting for a baseline age imbalance.[^zhang][^salimi]

## The Rubric

**Oil control and mattifying (topical, cosmetic)**
- **Effect size: real but immediate and short-lived, and it is a cosmetic effect, not a treatment of skin disease.** In an open-label study of 60 completers with oily or combination skin run by L'Oreal China, a single application of a commercial clay mask (containing kaolin and bentonite among other ingredients, not bentonite in isolation) reduced measured skin oiliness by 68.97% immediately after use (p < 0.001), with reductions of roughly 24% to 30% recorded at subsequent weekly assessments over 4 weeks of twice-weekly use.[^zhang] The same study also reported reductions in acne lesion counts and transepidermal water loss over the 4 weeks.[^zhang]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary and uncontrolled.** The study had no placebo or vehicle-mask comparison arm, so the reductions cannot be separated from washing the face, expectation, or regression to the mean; it was designed and run by the manufacturer of the tested product (L'Oreal), and it tested a multi-ingredient commercial mask rather than bentonite alone.[^zhang]

**Acne (topical, as an add-on to treatment)**
- **Effect size: not established for bentonite alone.** The only on-site controlled trial of a bentonite mask, [[salimi-2025-bentonite-alcea-facial-mask-acne-rct]], tested bentonite combined with a Traditional Persian Medicine botanical (Alcea sulphurea) that has no independent corroborating trials, added on top of oral azithromycin in both study arms, so it cannot show what bentonite does by itself. Its between-group advantage over placebo was significant unadjusted but lost significance once the trial adjusted for a baseline age difference between arms, and participants were not fully blinded because the mask and placebo gel looked and felt different.
- **Evidence quality: a single small add-on trial of a two-ingredient combination, not bentonite in isolation.** No other controlled human trial of bentonite for acne exists on this evidence base.

## The Evidence

**Oil absorption (topical).** The L'Oreal-run study enrolled 75 adults with oily, sensitive, or combination skin in Shanghai and analyzed 60 completers using a clay mask (La Roche-Posay Effaclar Sebo-Controlling Mask) twice weekly for 4 weeks, with no control or vehicle-mask arm. Skin oiliness (measured by Sebumeter) fell 68.97% immediately after a single application (p < 0.001), and further reductions of 24.26%, 24.22%, and 29.90% were measured at weeks 1, 2, and 4 of continued use (all p < 0.001). The same participants also showed reductions in closed and open comedone counts (46.44% and 65.77% at week 4) and a 20.41% reduction in transepidermal water loss at week 4, and increases in stratum corneum water content.[^zhang] Without a comparison arm, the trial cannot attribute these changes to the clay ingredients specifically, and the product tested combines bentonite with kaolin and other ingredients rather than isolating bentonite.

**Acne (topical, add-on).** [[salimi-2025-bentonite-alcea-facial-mask-acne-rct]] randomized 60 patients with moderate acne to a bentonite-and-Alcea-sulphurea facial mask plus oral azithromycin, or a placebo gel plus the same antibiotic, for 8 weeks. Total lesion count and Acne Severity Index fell significantly more in the mask group on unadjusted analysis, but the between-group difference was no longer significant once the trial adjusted for a baseline age gap between arms (about 24 versus 19 years). Because both arms received the antibiotic, the trial measures the mask's incremental effect on top of treatment, not what bentonite does on its own, and the tested product paired bentonite with an unrelated, uncorroborated botanical.

**Broader claims (anti-inflammatory, wound-healing, photoprotective, anti-aging).** A 2024 review of bentonite and montmorillonite in dermatology describes evidence for these effects as coming from in vitro experiments, animal studies, and preliminary clinical trials, and states that larger randomized clinical trials are needed to establish safety and efficacy.[^zafar] The review itself is a survey of that preliminary literature, not a new human trial.

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Detoxifies" or "draws out impurities."** The controlled evidence behind a visible effect is a single uncontrolled, manufacturer-run study of a multi-clay commercial mask showing a large but immediate, transient drop in surface oil, not a demonstration that bentonite removes anything from the skin.[^zhang]
- **"Clears acne."** The one on-site trial of a bentonite mask for acne tested it as an add-on to oral antibiotics, combined with an unrelated botanical, and its advantage over placebo did not survive adjustment for a baseline group difference.[^salimi]
