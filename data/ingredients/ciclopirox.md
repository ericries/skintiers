Ciclopirox (as the free acid or its ciclopirox olamine salt, INCI names Ciclopirox and Ciclopiroxolamine, C12H17NO2) is a synthetic hydroxypyridone antifungal used in leave-on shampoos, creams, and lacquers at concentrations of roughly 0.1% to 1.5%, most commonly 1% for [[seborrheic-dermatitis]] and dandruff.[^pubchem][^altmeyer] Unlike azole antifungals such as [[ketoconazole]], it does not inhibit ergosterol synthesis; instead it chelates trivalent metal cations to disable fungal peroxide-degrading enzymes, and a 2015 Cochrane review of 51 trials (9,052 participants) found ciclopirox 1% significantly outperformed placebo for clearing seborrheic dermatitis, with an effectiveness similar to ketoconazole.[^subissi][^cochrane]

> In the Cochrane review's meta-analysis, ciclopirox 1% produced a lower failed-remission rate than placebo at 4 weeks (risk ratio 0.79, 95% CI 0.67 to 0.94, 8 studies, moderate-quality evidence), with side-effect rates similar to placebo (risk ratio 0.90, 95% CI 0.72 to 1.11, 4 studies).[^cochrane]

## The Rubric

**Seborrheic dermatitis and dandruff of the scalp (topical, leave-on or shampoo)**
- **Effect size: real but modest, and a step behind ketoconazole in some head-to-head data.** The Cochrane pooled result (RR 0.79 vs. placebo for failed remission at 4 weeks) means a meaningful minority of extra patients clear versus vehicle, not dramatic superiority; the review's direct ciclopirox-versus-ketoconazole comparison found the two agents produced similar remission-failure rates (RR 1.09, 95% CI 0.95 to 1.26, 3 studies), so ciclopirox is not clearly weaker, but it is not clearly stronger either.[^cochrane] A separate systematic review and meta-analysis of 9 vehicle-controlled trials rated ketoconazole's evidence "strong" and ciclopirox's "moderate," with ciclopirox's pooled relative risk of improvement over vehicle at 3.00 (95% CI 1.86 to 4.84) versus ketoconazole's 5.78 (95% CI 2.17 to 15.40), a smaller effect on that particular metric.[^apasrawirote] A dose-ranging, vehicle-controlled RCT of 203 patients found 1% ciclopirox shampoo (twice weekly, 4 weeks) reduced the sum symptom score from 8.3 to 4.4 versus vehicle (P=0.0372), with higher concentrations outperforming lower ones on the same schedule.[^altmeyer]
- **Evidence quality: solid, resting on a large Cochrane-graded evidence base.** The Cochrane review pooled 11 ciclopirox trials (N=3,029) against placebo or vehicle and rated the placebo comparison "moderate-quality evidence"; it is one of the better-evidenced antifungal actives for this condition, on par with ketoconazole in trial volume.[^cochrane] The caveat the reviewers themselves flag: 24 of the 51 included trials across all agents had some conflict of interest (pharmaceutical funding), and most trials measured outcomes at 5 weeks or less, so durability of remission beyond a month is not well characterized.[^cochrane]

**Comparison against a keratolytic acid shampoo, isolated ingredient (topical)**
- **Effect size: comparable to a lipohydroxy-acid shampoo, and numerically worse on tolerance/acceptability in one trial.** A 100-subject RCT comparing a 1.5% ciclopiroxolamine (plus 3% salicylic acid) shampoo to a 0.1% lipohydroxy acid shampoo found both reduced scale, erythema, itching, discomfort, and dryness from baseline over 4 weeks, but at day 28 the LHA shampoo's tolerance (P=0.03) and global efficacy (P=0.01) were significantly better than the ciclopirox formulation's.[^seite] This trial was funded by La Roche-Posay (the maker of the LHA shampoo), so the direction of the funding bias should be weighed, but the underlying data on ciclopirox's own improvement from baseline are consistent with the Cochrane result.
- **Evidence quality: single industry-funded head-to-head trial; not independently replicated.** One RCT with a funding conflict of interest on the comparator side; useful as context, not as a standalone verdict on ciclopirox.[^seite]

## How It Works

Ciclopirox belongs to the hydroxypyridone chemical class, structurally unrelated to azole, allylamine, or polyene antifungals.[^subissi] Where azoles like ketoconazole block ergosterol synthesis in the fungal cell membrane, ciclopirox works through "high affinity... for trivalent metal cations, resulting in inhibition of the metal-dependent enzymes that are responsible for the degradation of peroxides within the fungal cell," a mechanism its reviewers describe as the "major determinant of its antimicrobial activity."[^subissi] This peroxide-handling disruption gives it a broad spectrum against dermatophytes, yeasts (including the Malassezia species implicated in seborrheic dermatitis and dandruff), and moulds, plus activity against some azole-resistant Candida strains.[^subissi] Because its mechanism does not depend on the ergosterol pathway that azole resistance mutations typically target, the same review reports this "multilevel mechanism of action provides a very low potential for the development of resistance in pathogenic fungi, with cases of resistance rarely reported."[^subissi] Ciclopirox also shows "mild anti-inflammatory effects in biochemical and pharmacological models," attributed in part to scavenging reactive oxygen species released by inflammatory cells, which may contribute to symptom relief independent of its antifungal action.[^subissi]

## The Evidence

**Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis of topical antifungals for seborrhoeic dermatitis (topical, multiple formulations).** The 2015 Cochrane review (CD008138, update) pooled 51 randomized controlled trials with 9,052 total participants across all antifungal agents studied for seborrheic dermatitis of the face and scalp in adults and adolescents; 11 trials (N=3,029) tested ciclopirox against placebo or vehicle. At 4 weeks, ciclopirox 1% produced a significantly lower failed-remission rate than placebo (RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.67 to 0.94, 8 studies, "moderate-quality evidence"), with side-effect rates statistically indistinguishable from placebo (RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.72 to 1.11, 4 studies, "moderate-quality evidence"). A direct comparison against ketoconazole across 3 studies found similar remission-failure rates (RR 1.09, 95% CI 0.95 to 1.26, "low-quality evidence"). The authors' overall conclusion: "Ketoconazole and ciclopirox are more effective than placebo, but limited evidence suggests that either of these agents is more effective than any other agent within the same class."[^cochrane] *The largest and most rigorously graded evidence base available: ciclopirox reliably beats placebo, and the head-to-head data with ketoconazole do not show either drug as clearly superior, though most trials only tracked outcomes for a month or less.*

**Dose-ranging, vehicle-controlled RCT of ciclopirox shampoo (topical).** A randomized, double-blind trial enrolled 203 patients with scalp seborrheic dermatitis and compared vehicle against ciclopirox shampoo at 0.1%, 0.3%, and 1%, each applied twice weekly for 4 weeks, grading scaling, inflammation, and itching on 6-point scales. The 1% group showed the most pronounced change, from a baseline sum score of 8.3 to 4.4 (P=0.0372 versus vehicle), with efficacy increasing alongside concentration; all concentrations were "safe and well tolerated."[^altmeyer] *A clean dose-response signal, with the 1% concentration used in most marketed products reaching statistical significance over vehicle in just 4 weeks.*

**Ciclopiroxolamine shampoo versus lipohydroxy acid shampoo, industry-funded head-to-head (topical).** A randomized trial of 100 subjects with mild-to-moderate scalp seborrheic dermatitis compared a shampoo with 1.5% ciclopiroxolamine, 3% salicylic acid, and 0.5% menthol against a shampoo with 0.1% lipohydroxy acid and 1.3% salicylic acid, each used every 2 days for 4 weeks. Both shampoos reduced scale, erythema, itching, discomfort, and dryness from baseline; at day 28 the LHA shampoo's global efficacy (P=0.01) and tolerance (P=0.03) were significantly better than the ciclopirox formulation's, and cosmetic acceptability favored LHA on several endpoints. The study was run by La Roche-Posay Pharmaceutical Laboratories, maker of the LHA product.[^seite] *Ciclopirox shampoo worked, but a competitor's product edged it out in a trial funded by that competitor, a result that should be read with the funding source in mind rather than taken as a clean loss for ciclopirox.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Clinically proven for dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis."** Fair. The Cochrane meta-analysis of 11 trials found ciclopirox 1% significantly reduced failed-remission rates versus placebo, and an independent dose-ranging RCT confirmed a dose-dependent benefit at 4 weeks.[^cochrane][^altmeyer] This is a genuinely well-evidenced claim, not marketing puffery.
- **"As effective as prescription antifungals."** Reasonably supported for ketoconazole specifically: the Cochrane review's direct comparison found similar remission-failure rates between the two, though that comparison rested on only 3 studies and "low-quality evidence," so treat it as comparable, not proven equivalent.[^cochrane] It should not be over-generalized to every antifungal or every trial metric; a separate meta-analysis rated ketoconazole's evidence "strong" and ciclopirox's "moderate."[^apasrawirote]
