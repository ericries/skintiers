Homosalate (INCI homomenthyl salicylate) is an organic sunscreen filter that absorbs ultraviolet-B radiation, the sunburn band, and is one of the older filters carried in United States over-the-counter sunscreens at up to 15%.[^holt][^fda2019] Its absorption is confined to UVB (roughly 290 to 320 nm); it contributes to a product's SPF but adds essentially nothing in the UVA band (320 to 400 nm), so it is combined with UVA filters such as [[avobenzone]] and with other UVB filters, and SPF is a UVB metric while UVA is a separate axis, both explained on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].[^holt][^chaudhuri] The health value of using it, the prevention of sunburn and UV-induced DNA damage that regular broad-spectrum sunscreen delivers, is a property of the whole formula used regularly and lives on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; homosalate supplies part of the UVB half.[^holt] Two questions shape how it is graded: how much photoprotection it actually contributes, given that it is UVB-only, and what its systemic absorption, the highest of the salicylate filters and the reason European regulators cut its permitted concentration over endocrine concerns, means.[^matta2020][^eu2022]

> Homosalate is a UVB-only organic filter (about 290 to 320 nm) that is photostable but covers none of the UVA band, so it works as a co-filter alongside avobenzone or a mineral filter rather than as a broad-spectrum filter on its own.[^holt][^chaudhuri] It is permitted in US sunscreens at up to 15% and sits in the FDA's Category III (insufficient safety data), and in maximal-use testing it reached the highest plasma concentrations of any salicylate filter tested (up to 23.1 ng/mL).[^fda2019][^matta2020] Over concerns about possible endocrine activity, the EU restricted it to 7.34% in face products only after its scientific committee found the previously permitted 10% "not safe."[^eu2022]

## The Rubric

The grade below is for homosalate's job in a sunscreen, ultraviolet-B absorption, framed relative to the other filters it is measured against. Its ultraviolet-A coverage is graded separately because it has essentially none, and its systemic absorption and the associated endocrine question form a third, separate axis.

**Ultraviolet-B photoprotection**
- **Effect size: modest, and as a co-filter rather than a standalone.** Homosalate absorbs in the UVB region (about 290 to 320 nm), the band that drives sunburn and the DNA photodamage implicated in keratinocyte cancers, and contributes to a product's SPF.[^holt] It is one of the older US UVB filters (with octisalate, [[octinoxate]], and octocrylene) and is permitted at up to 15%, the joint-highest concentration ceiling in the US monograph, which reflects that salicylate filters are used at high loadings.[^fda2019] A study of its excited-state behavior found the molecule dissipates absorbed UV energy harmlessly and concluded homosalate "exhibits mostly favourable photophysical characteristics of a UV filter for inclusion in sunscreen formulations."[^holt] It is commonly combined with the UVA filter avobenzone in broad-spectrum products.[^chaudhuri] "Modest UVB contribution used at high concentration; a co-filter, not a broad-spectrum filter on its own, and short of the mineral filters and modern organic filters on overall spectral breadth."
- **Evidence quality: solid for the spectral and photostability characterization; the health payoff is inherited, not filter-specific.** The UVB absorption and its photostable, non-radiative energy dissipation are documented by ultrafast-spectroscopy and formulation studies.[^holt][^chaudhuri] What is not demonstrated for homosalate on its own is a clinical-endpoint benefit; the randomized evidence that broad-spectrum sunscreen slows photoaging and lowers skin-cancer burden is for the practice of regular use, covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], and homosalate earns its place as one of the UVB filters delivering the sunburn-protection half of that spectrum.[^holt]

**Ultraviolet-A photoprotection**
- **Effect size: none.** Homosalate's absorption is confined to UVB; it provides essentially no coverage of UVA (320 to 400 nm), the deeper-penetrating band that is the principal driver of photoaging.[^holt] A product that relies on homosalate for its UVA protection has none, and depends entirely on a co-filter for that half of the spectrum: the UVA workhorse in older US formulas is avobenzone, which covers long-wave UVA but degrades in sunlight unless stabilized (see [[avobenzone]]), while mineral [[zinc-oxide]] attenuates across UVA and UVB. The general UVA/UVB mechanism and why UVA coverage matters for photoaging live on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].

**Systemic absorption and the endocrine question (a separate axis, not an efficacy grade)**
- In the FDA's maximal-use trial, homosalate was absorbed into plasma at the highest concentrations of any salicylate filter tested: geometric mean maxima of 23.1 ng/mL (aerosol spray; coefficient of variation 68.0%), 17.9 ng/mL (nonaerosol spray; CV 61.7%), and 13.9 ng/mL (pump spray; CV 70.2%), all above the 0.5 ng/mL threshold and surpassing it on day 1 after a single application.[^matta2020] That 0.5 ng/mL figure is the level below which the FDA may waive some nonclinical safety testing; exceeding it triggers a request for further study, not a finding of harm, and the trial authors wrote that the results "do not indicate that individuals should refrain from the use of sunscreen."[^matta2020] The FDA classifies homosalate "Category III" (insufficient data for a positive safety determination) in its 2019 proposed rule.[^fda2019] The EU acted on the same absorption question: after a call for data over "potential endocrine disrupting properties," its Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety concluded homosalate "is not safe when used as a UV-filter in cosmetic products at concentrations of up to 10 %," then found it safe only "up to 7,34 % when used in face products," and the Commission restricted it accordingly.[^eu2022] An independent human toxicokinetic study reached margins of safety "between 11 and 92" after a single whole-body application of a 10% product, below the SCCS's target of 100.[^ebert] No health harm from homosalate at label use has been demonstrated; the absorption and the regulatory divergence are the established facts.

## What We Actually Know

Homosalate is a well-characterized organic UVB filter. Its absorption sits in the UVB band (about 290 to 320 nm), where it is photostable, dissipating absorbed energy non-radiatively, and it contributes to SPF while adding essentially nothing in the UVA range.[^holt] For that reason it is used as a co-filter, typically alongside the UVA filter avobenzone and other UVB filters, and at high concentration (up to 15% in the US).[^chaudhuri][^fda2019] Its health contribution is the UVB, sunburn-and-DNA-damage, half of the protection that regular broad-spectrum sunscreen use provides, documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]], not in any homosalate-specific clinical-endpoint trial. Its distinguishing issue is systemic absorption: in the FDA's randomized maximal-use trial homosalate reached the highest plasma levels of the salicylate filters (up to 23.1 ng/mL), well above the agency's 0.5 ng/mL testing threshold, which is why it sits in Category III and why the FDA has asked for more data.[^matta2020][^fda2019] The EU went further, restricting homosalate to 7.34% in face products only, over concerns about possible endocrine activity, after its scientific committee found the previously permitted 10% "not safe."[^eu2022][^ebert] No harm at label use has been shown; the absorption itself is well established.

## How It Works

Homosalate is a salicylate ester (the homomenthyl ester of salicylic acid), an organic (chemical) filter that absorbs a UV photon and re-emits the energy as heat rather than scattering light the way mineral filters do.[^holt] A combined spectroscopy and computational study mapped the pathway: the enol form of the molecule "undergoes ultrafast excited state intramolecular proton transfer (ESIPT) upon photoexcitation in the UVB (290-320 nm) region; once in the keto tautomer, the excess energy is predominantly dissipated non-radiatively," the rapid, harmless energy release that underlies a filter's photostability.[^holt] Because that absorption is concentrated in UVB, homosalate contributes to SPF, a UVB-only number, and provides little coverage of UVA (about 320 to 400 nm), so it is paired with UVA filters.[^holt] Homosalate is also used to build photostable broad-spectrum formulas around avobenzone: one formulation study describes "combining avobenzone, DESM and UV-B sunscreens, such as homosalate, octisalate or other UV-B sunscreens" to achieve photostable, high-SPF products.[^chaudhuri]

## The Evidence

Primary evidence, by question.

**Spectral coverage and mechanism.** A 2020 study in Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics characterized homosalate's photophysics with time-resolved laser spectroscopy, steady-state absorption spectroscopy, and computation, describing it as "a salicylate molecule that is commonly included within commercial sunscreen formulations to provide protection from the adverse effects of ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure."[^holt] It located the absorption and the protective mechanism in the UVB, reporting that the enol tautomer "undergoes ultrafast excited state intramolecular proton transfer (ESIPT) upon photoexcitation in the UVB (290-320 nm) region," and concluded that "overall, HMS exhibits mostly favourable photophysical characteristics of a UV filter for inclusion in sunscreen formulations."[^holt] A formulation study identifies homosalate specifically as a UVB filter, listing it among "UV-B sunscreens, such as homosalate, octisalate" used together with avobenzone in broad-spectrum products.[^chaudhuri]

**Systemic absorption.** The FDA's randomized maximal-use trial (48 participants, 44 completing; four spray and lotion products applied at 2 mg/cm2 to 75% of body surface, four times daily) measured six filters, including homosalate. Homosalate reached geometric mean maximum plasma concentrations of 23.1 ng/mL (coefficient of variation 68.0%) for the aerosol spray, 17.9 ng/mL (CV 61.7%) for the nonaerosol spray, and 13.9 ng/mL (CV 70.2%) for the pump spray, the highest levels among the salicylate filters and second only to oxybenzone across all six.[^matta2020] "Geometric mean maximum plasma concentrations of all 6 active ingredients were greater than 0.5 ng/mL, and this threshold was surpassed on day 1 after a single application for all active ingredients."[^matta2020] The authors stated the findings "do not indicate that individuals should refrain from the use of sunscreen."[^matta2020] An independent German toxicokinetic study had four volunteers apply "a commercial sunscreen containing 10% HMS to their whole body under regular-use conditions" and confirmed dermal absorption, noting that "concerns have been raised about possible endocrine activity of HMS" and calculating margins of safety "between 11 and 92" after a single whole-body application.[^ebert]

**Regulatory status.** Homosalate is a permitted US OTC sunscreen active at a maximum concentration of 15%.[^fda2019] In the FDA's 2019 proposed sunscreen rule the agency stated that "the public record does not currently contain sufficient data to support positive GRASE determinations" for homosalate and eleven other organic filters and proposed them as "Category III," requesting more safety data, with systemic absorption among the reasons; only zinc oxide and titanium dioxide were proposed as generally recognized as safe and effective.[^fda2019] The EU reached a different limit on the same question. Homosalate had been allowed there as a UV filter at up to 10%; after a 2019 call for data over potential endocrine-disrupting properties, the Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety concluded in June 2021 that homosalate "is not safe when used as a UV-filter in cosmetic products at concentrations of up to 10 %," and in December 2021 that it "is safe as a UV-filter at concentrations up to 7,34 % when used in face products in the form of cream and pump spray."[^eu2022] Commission Regulation (EU) 2022/2195 restricted homosalate to "face products with the exception of propellant spray products" at a maximum of 7.34%, with non-compliant products barred from being placed on the EU market from 1 January 2025.[^eu2022] Homosalate therefore remains widely available in US products at up to 15% while the EU permits it only in face products at less than half that concentration.

## Uses

Well-supported: as a UVB filter in a broad-spectrum sunscreen, where homosalate absorbs in the sunburn band (about 290 to 320 nm), contributes to SPF, and is combined with a UVA filter such as avobenzone or a mineral filter to cover the rest of the spectrum.[^holt][^chaudhuri] The health benefit, preventing sunburn and UV-induced DNA damage and, with regular broad-spectrum use, slowing photoaging and lowering skin-cancer burden, is a property of the whole formula used regularly and is documented on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; homosalate supplies part of the UVB half.[^holt] Its practical limits are two: it provides essentially no UVA protection on its own, and it is absorbed systemically to the highest levels of the salicylate filters, which is why the FDA lists it Category III and the EU restricted it to 7.34% in face products over endocrine concerns.[^holt][^matta2020][^fda2019][^eu2022] It is not used as a standalone broad-spectrum filter.[^holt]

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Broad-spectrum protection."** Homosalate itself absorbs only in the UVB band (about 290 to 320 nm) and covers essentially none of the UVA range; any broad-spectrum claim on a homosalate-containing product comes from its co-filters (avobenzone, octocrylene, a mineral filter), not from homosalate.[^holt] A US "Broad Spectrum" label is a single low-bar UVA threshold, not a graded UVA strength, and older US filter sets allow more UVA through than European products; that landscape is covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]].
- **"Chemical sunscreens are toxic / hormone disruptors."** The demonstrated facts are systemic absorption to the highest plasma levels of the salicylate filters (up to 23.1 ng/mL, above the FDA's 0.5 ng/mL testing threshold) and a regulatory concern about "potential endocrine disrupting properties" that led the EU to cut the permitted concentration to 7.34% in face products.[^matta2020][^eu2022] Those triggered requests for more data and a precautionary restriction, not a demonstrated harm at label use; the FDA classifies homosalate Category III (insufficient data), not unsafe, and the maximal-use trial authors stated the results "do not indicate that individuals should refrain from the use of sunscreen."[^fda2019][^matta2020]
- **"High SPF from a gentle filter."** Homosalate is permitted at up to 15%, the joint-highest ceiling in the US monograph, which reflects that it is used at high loadings rather than that it is an unusually strong absorber; its contribution is UVB-only and it is one component of a formula's SPF, not the whole of it.[^fda2019][^holt]
