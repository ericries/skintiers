Tacrolimus ointment (Protopic; 0.03% and 0.1%) is a prescription-only topical calcineurin inhibitor approved as second-line therapy for moderate to severe [[atopic-dermatitis]], used in place of a topical corticosteroid when steroid side effects such as skin thinning are a concern.[^fdalabel] Its efficacy against a steroid is genuinely well demonstrated in a large trial, but it also carries the FDA's strongest label warning, a boxed warning over a malignancy signal that the largest follow-up study to date has not been able to confirm.

> In a 570-patient trial, 0.1% tacrolimus ointment performed on par with the mid-potency steroid hydrocortisone butyrate, while the pediatric-strength 0.03% dose was significantly weaker than both, and tacrolimus caused more application-site burning and itching than the steroid.[^reitamo] The FDA's boxed warning restricts continuous long-term use and blocks use under age 2, based on rare malignancy case reports rather than an established causal link; a subsequent cohort of nearly 8,000 children followed for 44,629 person-years found cancer rates matching the general population and no cases of lymphoma.[^fdalabel][^paller2020]

## The Rubric

Tacrolimus has one real use, steroid-sparing treatment of atopic dermatitis, and one safety question that shadows it, the boxed warning.

**Atopic dermatitis, second-line steroid-sparing therapy (0.1% adults; 0.03% children 2 to 15)**
- **Effect size: notable.** A multicenter, randomized, double-blind trial in 570 adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis compared 0.03% tacrolimus, 0.1% tacrolimus, and 0.1% hydrocortisone butyrate ointment. Median modified-EASI area-under-the-curve was 47.0% of baseline on 0.03% tacrolimus, 36.5% on 0.1% tacrolimus, and 36.1% on hydrocortisone butyrate; the authors concluded "the efficacy of 0.1% tacrolimus ointment was similar to that of 0.1% hydrocortisone butyrate ointment and was lower for 0.03% tacrolimus ointment."[^reitamo] Skin burning and pruritus at the application site were more frequent in the tacrolimus groups than with the steroid (P<.05).[^reitamo] "Notable: the adult 0.1% strength matches a mid-potency topical steroid on a validated eczema-severity score; it trades the steroid's skin-thinning risk for more burning and stinging at the site, and the lower pediatric-labeled 0.03% strength is weaker than either."
- **Evidence quality: solid.** One large multicenter double-blind RCT with an active steroid comparator, plus FDA approval restricting use by age and concentration.[^reitamo][^fdalabel] The comparator was a single mid-potency steroid, not the full range of corticosteroid strengths dermatologists also use.

**The FDA boxed warning (malignancy signal)**
- **Signal strength: unconfirmed in the largest follow-up so far.** The tacrolimus ointment label carries a boxed warning stating that "although a causal relationship has not been established, rare cases of malignancy (e.g., skin and lymphoma) have been reported in patients treated with topical calcineurin inhibitors, including tacrolimus ointment," and on that basis restricts continuous long-term use in any age group and bars use in children under 2, with only the 0.03% strength indicated for ages 2 to 15.[^fdalabel] The APPLES cohort study followed 7,954 children and adolescents on topical tacrolimus for 44,629 person-years across 314 sites and recorded 6 confirmed cancers and no lymphomas, a standardized incidence ratio of 1.01 (95% CI 0.37-2.20) against matched background population rates; the authors concluded this "provides no support for the hypothesis that topical tacrolimus increases long-term cancer risk in children with atopic dermatitis."[^paller2020] The cohort was observational, not placebo-controlled, and covers children rather than the adults who use the higher 0.1% strength, so it narrows but does not close the question the label still warns about.

## The Evidence

**Tacrolimus versus a mid-potency steroid.** The multicenter, randomized, double-blind trial randomized 570 adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis to 0.03% tacrolimus (n=193), 0.1% tacrolimus (n=191), or 0.1% hydrocortisone butyrate ointment (n=186), with the modified Eczema Area and Severity Index area-under-the-curve as the primary endpoint. Median results were 47.0% of baseline on 0.03% tacrolimus, 36.5% on 0.1% tacrolimus, and 36.1% on hydrocortisone butyrate, and the 0.03% dose was significantly worse than both 0.1% tacrolimus and the steroid; skin burning and pruritus at the application site were more common with tacrolimus than with the steroid (P<.05).[^reitamo]

**The boxed warning and its evidentiary basis.** The FDA label states that "although a causal relationship has not been established, rare cases of malignancy (e.g., skin and lymphoma) have been reported in patients treated with topical calcineurin inhibitors, including tacrolimus ointment," and instructs that continuous long-term use "in any age group should be avoided" and that the ointment "is not indicated for use in children less than 2 years of age," with only the 0.03% strength indicated for ages 2 to 15.[^fdalabel] The APPLES study, a prospective cohort of 7,954 children and adolescents with atopic dermatitis using topical tacrolimus, accumulated 44,629 person-years of follow-up across 314 sites in 9 countries and identified 6 confirmed incident cancers and no lymphomas, for a standardized incidence ratio of 1.01 (95% CI 0.37-2.20) against age-, sex-, and race-matched national cancer-registry rates; the authors concluded the finding "provides no support for the hypothesis that topical tacrolimus increases long-term cancer risk in children with atopic dermatitis."[^paller2020]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"A steroid-free alternative with no downside."** Tacrolimus avoids the corticosteroid skin-thinning risk, but the same trial that showed it matches a mid-potency steroid also found more application-site burning and itching with tacrolimus than with the steroid, and the FDA label still carries a boxed warning restricting continuous long-term use.[^reitamo][^fdalabel]
