Phloretin is a plant-derived flavonoid, extracted from apple tree bark and leaves, used topically as a supporting antioxidant in vitamin C serums, most prominently as the third active in [[skinceuticals-phloretin-cf|SkinCeuticals Phloretin CF]] alongside [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c|vitamin C]] and [[ferulic-acid]].

> Phloretin's own topical evidence base is thin. The one human trial behind it tested a research antioxidant mixture of vitamin C, ferulic acid, and phloretin together against UV damage, not phloretin alone, and a controlled skin-permeation study found phloretin did not significantly improve penetration in human skin, only in rat and porcine skin, despite its common billing as a penetration enhancer.[^oresajo][^auner] Its tyrosinase-inhibiting and antioxidant chemistry is documented in cell-free enzyme and lab studies, not in controlled human skin trials of phloretin by itself.[^chen][^casarini]

## The Rubric

**Antioxidant combination for UV photodamage (topical, as part of a multi-antioxidant mixture)**
- **Effect size: unresolved for phloretin specifically.** In a randomized study of ten subjects, a topical antioxidant mixture containing vitamin C, ferulic acid, and phloretin applied before UV exposure attenuated erythema, sunburn cell formation, thymine dimer formation, matrix metalloproteinase-9 expression, p53 expression, and UV-induced loss of Langerhans cells compared with vehicle.[^oresajo] The formula tested is a research combination from the group associated with the C-E-Ferulic serum work, not a named retail product, and the design cannot separate phloretin's own contribution from that of the vitamin C and ferulic acid it was mixed with. "Real protection was shown for the three-antioxidant mixture as a whole in a small trial; nothing here isolates what phloretin itself adds beyond what [[ascorbic-acid-vitamin-c]] and [[ferulic-acid]] already provide."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** One small trial (n=10), unblinded design not detailed in the abstract, testing a combination rather than phloretin alone.[^oresajo]

**Penetration enhancer for co-formulated actives (topical)**
- **Effect size: not supported in human skin.** Phloretin is often marketed as helping other actives penetrate skin, but a controlled in vitro diffusion study across rat, porcine, and human skin found phloretin "had no significant influence compared to the control in human skin," with a significant enhancing effect only in rat and porcine skin after 30 hours; a co-tested compound, 6-ketocholestanol, was the one that enhanced permeation across all three skin types tested.[^auner] "Unresolved to negative in human skin: the one controlled human-skin permeation study found no measurable boost from phloretin, only in rodent and pig skin."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** One in vitro diffusion study using a marker dye (sodium fluorescein), not a co-formulated skincare active, and not a living human trial.[^auner]

**Tyrosinase inhibition and pigmentation (topical, cosmetic)**
- **Effect size: unresolved for skin.** In a cell-free enzyme assay, phloretin reversibly inhibited mushroom tyrosinase with an IC50 of 169.36 micromol/L and also acted as a substrate, reducing o-dopaquinone in a manner the authors compared to ascorbic acid.[^chen] This is a purified-enzyme biochemistry result, not a study in melanocytes, skin, or human subjects. "Unresolved: a test-tube enzyme-inhibition number, several steps removed from any evidence it lightens pigmentation on skin."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** A single cell-free biochemical and computational (molecular docking) study.[^chen]

**Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-aging chemistry (topical, cosmetic and mechanistic)**
- **Effect size: unresolved for skin outcomes.** A pharmacology review describes phloretin's antioxidant activity, its inhibition of elastase and matrix metalloproteinase-1 in laboratory assays, and an animal-skin study in which it suppressed a marker of inflammation (COX-2 expression) after chemical irritant exposure, but the review itself notes phloretin's "penetration profile in different skin layers has not yet been evaluated" and that use is "limited because of its photoinstability and poor aqueous solubility."[^casarini] "Unresolved: the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory chemistry is documented in cell and animal work, but the review that compiles it flags phloretin's own skin penetration and stability as still unclear."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** A narrative pharmacology review compiling in vitro and animal studies, not itself a source of new clinical data; cited here for background only.[^casarini]

## The Evidence

**Antioxidant mixture and UV photodamage.** Oresajo and colleagues randomized ten subjects (age 18-60, Fitzpatrick skin types II and III) to a topical antioxidant mixture of vitamin C, ferulic acid, and phloretin or a vehicle control, applied to lower-back skin for four consecutive days before UV exposure, then measured biomarkers at 24 hours. "All these changes were attenuated by the antioxidant composition," covering erythema, sunburn cell formation, thymine dimer formation, matrix metalloproteinase-9 expression, and p53 expression; the mixture also blocked UV-induced loss of CD1a-expressing Langerhans cells. The authors concluded the mixture demonstrated "the protective role of a unique mixture of antioxidants containing vitamin C, ferulic acid, and phloretin on human skin from the harmful effects of UV irradiation" and proposed it "will complement and synergize with sunscreens in providing photoprotection." The trial tested the three-ingredient combination as a whole; it does not isolate phloretin's individual contribution.[^oresajo]

**Skin penetration.** Auner, Valenta, and Hadgraft incorporated phloretin and a co-tested compound, 6-ketocholestanol, into liposomes and tested their effect on the diffusion of a marker dye (sodium fluorescein) across rat, porcine, and human skin in Franz-type diffusion cells, confirmed with confocal microscopy. "6-Ketocholestanol" showed a positive effect on diffusion "in all skin types," while phloretin "had no significant influence compared to the control in human skin," though it did show a significant positive effect in rat and porcine skin after 30 hours. This directly tests the claim that phloretin helps other ingredients penetrate skin, and finds no support for that claim in human skin specifically.[^auner]

**Tyrosinase inhibition.** Chen and colleagues ran a cell-free biochemical assay (spectroscopy, molecular docking, and chromatography) on purified tyrosinase and found phloretin "reversibly inhibits tyrosinase in a mix-type manner" with an "IC50 of 169.36 micromol/L," and that it can also act as a substrate, reducing o-dopaquinone to L-dopa "just like ascorbic acid." This is enzyme chemistry in a test tube, not a study in melanocytes or skin.[^chen]

**Background chemistry and gaps.** A pharmacology review of phloretin's dermatological applications compiles laboratory and animal findings: antioxidant activity, inhibition of elastase and matrix metalloproteinase-1 in assays, and suppression of the inflammation marker COX-2 in an animal-skin irritation model. The same review states phloretin's "penetration profile in different skin layers has not yet been evaluated" and that practical use is "limited because of its photoinstability and poor aqueous solubility." Cited here for background context only; it is a review, not a source of new clinical data.[^casarini]

## Common Marketing Claims

None of the claims below is established beyond what The Evidence above supports.

- **"Boosts penetration of vitamin C and other actives."** The one controlled skin-permeation study of phloretin found no significant enhancement in human skin, only in rat and porcine skin.[^auner]
- **"Fights hyperpigmentation" / "brightens."** The tyrosinase-inhibition data are a cell-free enzyme assay, not a study in melanocytes, skin, or human subjects.[^chen]
- **"Protects skin from UV damage" as phloretin's own effect.** The human UV-photodamage evidence tested phloretin only as part of a three-antioxidant mixture with vitamin C and ferulic acid; it does not isolate what phloretin itself contributes.[^oresajo]
