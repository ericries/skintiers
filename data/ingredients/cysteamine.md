Cysteamine is a small aminothiol applied as a 5% cream, marketed as a non-hydroquinone option for melasma. The trial record is real but modest: it beats placebo, and in head-to-head and pooled comparisons it lands close to hydroquinone 4% without ever clearly beating it, while its characteristic sulfur odor and higher rate of irritation make it a harder product to tolerate.

> A placebo-controlled RCT found cysteamine 5% cream cut Melasma Area and Severity Index (MASI) scores more than placebo after 4 months (7.2 vs 11.6, P=0.02),[^mansouri] and a 2024 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs found it more effective than placebo on MASI (SMD -0.84, P<0.00001) and not significantly different from hydroquinone 4% (SMD 0.16, P=0.42).[^mawu] A head-to-head trial found hydroquinone reduced pigmentation more in absolute terms (32% vs 21.3% mMASI reduction) though the difference did not reach significance, and rated hydroquinone better tolerated.[^nguyen]

## The Rubric

**Melasma (topical, 5% cream)**
- **Effect size: modest.** Against placebo, a double-blind RCT (50 patients, 4 months) found cysteamine 5% cream lowered MASI scores versus placebo (7.2 ± 5.5 vs 11.6 ± 7.9, P=0.02) and produced a larger drop on colorimetric measurement of pigmentation (P<0.001 at 4 months).[^mansouri] Against [[hydroquinone]], the picture is closer to parity than superiority: a head-to-head RCT found hydroquinone 4% reduced modified MASI more than cysteamine (32% vs 21.3%), a gap that did not reach statistical significance (P=0.3) in a small, high-dropout trial (20 enrolled, 14 completed).[^nguyen] Pooling 7 RCTs, a 2024 meta-analysis found cysteamine 5% beat placebo on MASI (SMD -0.84, 95% CI -1.19 to -0.49, P<0.00001) and did not differ significantly from hydroquinone 4% (SMD 0.16, 95% CI -0.22 to 0.53, P=0.42), concluding it may become a suitable alternative to hydroquinone.[^mawu] "Modest and roughly comparable to hydroquinone 4% on pooled trial data, not clearly superior to it in any single head-to-head trial."
- **Evidence quality: mixed, small trials.** The evidence base is placebo-controlled and head-to-head RCTs, which is a real design tier, but individual trials are small (20 to 50 patients), one head-to-head trial lost 30% of participants to follow-up,[^nguyen] and most of the work has come from a small number of research groups, largely in Iran.[^ahramiyanpour]

**Tolerability**
- **Effect size: worse than placebo and comparable to hydroquinone on side effects; a known odor problem.** The 2024 meta-analysis found adverse events (erythema, irritation, burning, itching, dryness) more common with cysteamine 5% than with placebo, but similar in frequency to hydroquinone 4%.[^mawu] In the head-to-head trial, the authors reported hydroquinone cream was "generally better tolerated" than cysteamine cream, with cysteamine side effects described as mild and reversible.[^nguyen] A 2021 systematic review noted the 5% cysteamine hydrochloride formulation used in modern trials has "a less foul odor than its prior counterparts," implying earlier cysteamine preparations had a more pronounced sulfurous smell that formulation changes have since reduced but not eliminated.[^ahramiyanpour]
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** Tolerability data come from the same small RCTs and pooled reviews above, with adverse-event reporting inconsistent across trials.[^mawu][^ahramiyanpour]

## The Evidence

**Placebo-controlled trial.** A double-blind RCT randomized 50 patients with epidermal melasma to cysteamine 5% cream or placebo, applied once daily at bedtime for 4 months. MASI scores at 4 months were lower with cysteamine than placebo (7.2 ± 5.5 vs 11.6 ± 7.9, P=0.02), and mexameter colorimetry (difference between pigmented and normal skin) fell from 75.2 ± 37 to 26.2 ± 16 with cysteamine versus 68.9 ± 31 to 60.7 ± 27.3 with placebo (P<0.001 at 4 months).[^mansouri]

**Head-to-head against hydroquinone.** A randomized, double-blinded trial in Australia compared cysteamine cream to hydroquinone in melasma over 16 weeks. Of 20 patients enrolled, 14 completed (5 cysteamine, 9 hydroquinone). Modified MASI fell 1.52 ± 0.69 (21.3%) with cysteamine versus 2.96 ± 1.15 (32%) with hydroquinone, a difference the authors reported as not statistically significant (P=0.3). The authors state "hydroquinone cream was generally better tolerated" than cysteamine, with cysteamine's side effects described as mild and reversible, and concluded cysteamine "provides a possible alternative to patients and clinicians who wish to avoid or rotate off topical hydroquinone" while calling for larger studies.[^nguyen]

**Meta-analysis.** A 2024 meta-analysis pooling 7 RCTs found cysteamine 5% more effective than placebo on MASI (SMD -0.84, 95% CI -1.19 to -0.49, P<0.00001, I2=0%) and not significantly different from hydroquinone 4% (SMD 0.16, 95% CI -0.22 to 0.53, P=0.42, I2=55%). Adverse events (erythema, irritation, burning, itching, dryness) were more frequent with cysteamine than placebo but similar in frequency to hydroquinone 4%.[^mawu]

## Common Marketing Claims

None of the claims below is established beyond what The Evidence above supports.

- **"A non-hydroquinone alternative that works as well as hydroquinone."** The strongest comparative evidence is one small, high-dropout head-to-head trial that found no significant difference from hydroquinone 4%, and a pooled meta-analysis with the same non-significant result;[^nguyen][^mawu] neither trial shows cysteamine outperforming hydroquinone, and the head-to-head trial's point estimate favored hydroquinone.
- **"Gentle and well tolerated."** Pooled data show cysteamine causes more adverse events (erythema, irritation, burning, itching, dryness) than placebo, and a head-to-head trial rated it less well tolerated than hydroquinone.[^mawu][^nguyen] Its formulation has historically carried a sulfurous odor that a 2021 review says has been reduced, not eliminated, in the 5% formulation used in modern trials.[^ahramiyanpour]
