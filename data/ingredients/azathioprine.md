Azathioprine is an oral immunosuppressant, not a skincare ingredient. It appears on this site because dermatologists have used it off-label for years in severe [[atopic-dermatitis]], and because it is the comparator that modern drugs are measured against. **Bottom line:** one placebo-controlled trial shows it genuinely works, the effect is modest, it is poorly tolerated, it carries a boxed malignancy warning, and in a 2026 head-to-head it was beaten decisively by a newer drug.[^bj][^malekan][^label]

> **It is not approved for eczema anywhere on its label.** The Imuran label lists two indications: preventing rejection in renal transplantation, and managing active rheumatoid arthritis.[^label] Every dermatologic use is off-label. That is not a scandal, it is ordinary practice for an old generic, but it means no regulator has reviewed its benefit for skin disease.

## The Rubric

**Severe atopic dermatitis, as a systemic option**
- **Effect size: modest, and real.** In the only double-blind placebo-controlled trial, the SASSAD sign score "fell by 26% during treatment with azathioprine vs. 3% on placebo (P < 0.01)".[^bj] A 26% fall in a sign score is a genuine improvement, not clearance.
- **Evidence quality: mixed.** The placebo-controlled evidence is one crossover trial in 37 adults from 2002, and 16 of those 37 withdrew.[^bj] A 2026 open-label head-to-head adds a second data point and it is unflattering.[^malekan]

**Against modern systemic drugs**
- **Effect size: minimal by comparison.** In the 2026 trial, 15% of the azathioprine plus topical steroid group reached EASI 75 at 12 weeks against 65% on baricitinib plus topical steroid, "difference 50%, 95% confidence interval (CI) 23.9-76.1; P = 0.002".[^malekan] That is a large, statistically clear gap, not a close call.
- **Evidence quality: preliminary for the comparison.** One single-centre, open-label trial with 20 patients per arm.[^malekan] Open-label design inflates the apparent gap when one drug is the novel one, and the authors state the limitation of their own design in the title.

**Tolerability, which is where it most often fails**
- **Effect size: not applicable; a harms finding.** In the placebo-controlled trial, "Gastrointestinal disturbances were reported by 14 patients during azathioprine treatment and four were withdrawn as a result of severe nausea and vomiting. Leukopenia was observed in two patients and deranged liver enzymes in eight".[^bj] Its own authors concluded it "is not always well-tolerated" and that blood count and liver enzyme monitoring "is advisable".[^bj]
- **Evidence quality: solid for the harms.** These are counted events in a blinded trial, plus a boxed warning on the label.[^bj][^label]

**Overall tier: mid.** Real placebo-controlled evidence of a modest effect, offset by poor tolerability and clear inferiority to current options. It is not graded lower because the controlled evidence is positive, and not higher because the effect is small and the newer drugs beat it.

## The Evidence

**The boxed warning is about cancer, and it is the first thing on the label.** It states that "Chronic immunosuppression with IMURAN, a purine antimetabolite increases risk of malignancy in humans", naming post-transplant lymphoma and hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma, and tells physicians to be familiar with that risk "as well as with the mutagenic potential to both men and women and with possible hematologic toxicities".[^label] Nothing on this site's topical pages carries a warning of this kind, which is the clearest possible illustration of why a systemic drug is a different category of decision.

**Two genes decide whether a standard dose is safe for you.** The label explains that reduced activity of the TPMT or NUDT15 enzymes causes active metabolites to accumulate, so such patients "are at higher risk for severe myelosuppression", and that "patients with TPMT or NUDT15 deficiency require alternative therapy or dose modification".[^label] This is why azathioprine is a drug with pre-treatment testing and ongoing blood monitoring, not a prescription you simply fill.

**The modern alternatives are better studied, and this site covers several.** The 2024 living network meta-analysis of systemic treatments for atopic dermatitis, spanning 97 trials and 24,679 patients, found "high-dose upadacitinib and abrocitinib demonstrating numerically highest relative efficacy".[^nma] Its abstract does not report where azathioprine ranks, so this page does not state a rank for it. Both of those drugs have pages here: [[upadacitinib]] and [[abrocitinib]]. For topical options in the same disease see [[tacrolimus-topical]] and [[delgocitinib]].

**Medical note.** This is educational content and emphatically not a substitute for a clinician. Azathioprine is prescription-only, off-label in dermatology, and requires genetic and laboratory monitoring. Nothing here is a reason to seek it out or to stop it.
