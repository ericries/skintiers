Clotrimazole is an over-the-counter topical azole antifungal, sold at 1% in creams such as Lotrimin AF. On the Bayer label its purpose is simply "Antifungal", and its stated use is that it "cures most ringworm" and "relieves itching, redness, irritation and discomfort which accompany this condition".[^dailymed] It is an OTC Monograph Drug under citation M005 rather than an approved NDA, so the regulatory basis is the monograph for the active, not trials of this product.[^dailymed]

> **Two limits sit on the label itself and decide most of what you need to know.** It is a treatment for a specific fungal infection, not a skincare active, and it "is not effective on the scalp or nails".[^dailymed] The label also sets a clear stopping rule: use it daily for four weeks, and "Stop use and ask a doctor if" there is "no improvement within 4 weeks".[^dailymed] And if the target is a foot infection, a Cochrane review of 11 head-to-head trials found treatment failure lower with allylamines than azoles, "RR 0.63 (95% CI 0.42 to 0.94) in favour of allylamines", which points to [[terbinafine]] over this.[^cochrane] For flaking on the scalp or face, [[ketoconazole]] is the better-studied choice and is covered on [[seborrheic-dermatitis]].

## The Rubric

**Curing tinea (ringworm) on the body**
- **Effect size: large for the indication, and the label uses the word "cures".** OTC Drug Facts language is tightly regulated, so "cures most ringworm" is a meaningful claim rather than marketing copy; the hedge in "most" is the honest part.[^dailymed] This is a resolvable infection, so the endpoint is clearance rather than improvement.
- **Evidence quality: solid at the class level, monograph-grade for this product.** The marketing category is OTC Monograph Drug M005, so the FDA accepted the active without requiring trials of this cream.[^dailymed] But the azole class itself has real randomised evidence: a Cochrane review of 67 trials found a pooled risk ratio of treatment failure for "azoles RR 0.30 (95% CI 0.20 to 0.45)" against placebo for fungal skin infections of the foot.[^cochrane] That is class-level and foot-specific rather than a trial of this product on body ringworm.

**Choosing an azole over an allylamine**
- **Effect size: azoles lose this comparison.** The same Cochrane review pooled 11 head-to-head trials and found a risk ratio of treatment failure of "RR 0.63 (95% CI 0.42 to 0.94) in favour of allylamines".[^cochrane] Allylamines means [[terbinafine]] and relatives. So where both are options for a foot infection, the evidence points away from clotrimazole.
- **Evidence quality: solid, and directly comparative.** Eleven randomised trials pooled in a Cochrane review is the strongest evidence on this page, and it is the kind of comparison that rarely exists between two drugstore products.

**Anything on the scalp or nails**
- **Effect size: none, by the manufacturer's own statement.** The label says plainly that "this product is not effective on the scalp or nails".[^dailymed] Nail and scalp fungal infections need different drugs and usually a prescriber.
- **Evidence quality: not applicable; this is an explicit exclusion rather than an untested use.**

## The Evidence

**What the label directs, in full.** Wash and dry the affected area, "apply a thin layer over affected area twice daily (morning and night)", and "use daily for 4 weeks".[^dailymed] The four-week figure does double duty: it is both the course length and the decision point, since the label tells you to stop and ask a doctor if nothing has improved by then.[^dailymed]

**The safety section is short and worth reading as written.** It is "For external use only", you should "avoid contact with the eyes", and it should not be used "on children under 2 years of age unless directed by a doctor".[^dailymed] The only listed reason to stop early is irritation.[^dailymed]

**A naming trap worth knowing.** The Lotrimin family does not all share one active. The DailyMed index lists Lotrimin AF Jock Itch and Lotrimin Antifungal powders as **miconazole nitrate**, Lotrimin Daily Prevention as **tolnaftate**, and Lotrimin Ultra Jock Itch as **butenafine hydrochloride**, alongside this clotrimazole cream.[^dailymed] Buying by brand name does not tell you which antifungal you are getting, so read the active-ingredient line.

**Where it sits against the alternatives on this site.** [[ketoconazole]] is the azole with the deeper dermatological literature, particularly for Malassezia conditions, and [[terbinafine]] is an allylamine rather than an azole. The conditions themselves are on [[athletes-foot]] and [[seborrheic-dermatitis]]. This page does not repeat those cases.

**On fungal acne, be careful.** Malassezia folliculitis is a different organism and a different problem from ringworm, and nothing on this label covers it. The hub is [[fungal-acne]], and any use of clotrimazole there is off-label rather than supported by the claim above.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. A rash that is not clearly ringworm can look very like one, and treating the wrong thing with an antifungal wastes four weeks.
