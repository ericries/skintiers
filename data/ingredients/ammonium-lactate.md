Ammonium lactate is the ammonium salt of [[lactic-acid]], made by neutralizing 12% lactic acid with ammonium hydroxide to a pH of 4.4-5.4, and is the active ingredient in FDA-approved prescription creams and lotions (sold generically and as brands including Lac-Hydrin) indicated for ichthyosis vulgaris and xerosis, as well as in over-the-counter moisturizers such as AmLactin.[^dailymed]

> A 12% ammonium lactate lotion outperformed a 5% lactic acid lotion, a petrolatum-based cream, and its own vehicle across several double-blind trials in patients with xerosis and ichthyosis, and it is the form of lactic acid the FDA has approved as a prescription drug for both conditions; head-to-head against other over-the-counter keratolytics (salicylic acid plus urea, a liposome-based moisturizer) it performed about the same, not better.[^dailymed][^rogers][^buxman][^jennings][^uy]

## The Rubric

**Xerosis (moderate to severe dry skin, twice-daily 12% cream or lotion)**
- Effect size: modest to notable, and consistently ahead of lower-concentration lactic acid or a plain emollient across independent trials, though the published abstracts report statistical significance rather than the underlying percentage scores. A 21-day, randomized, double-blind, contralateral trial in 60 patients found 12% ammonium lactate lotion, 5% lactic acid lotion, and a nonlactated emollient all significantly reduced xerosis severity during treatment, but only the 12% ammonium lactate lotion produced significantly greater reductions than the plain emollient during the two weeks after treatment stopped, and it beat the 5% lactic acid lotion on total severity scores at that point too.[^dahl] A separate double-blind study found 12% ammonium lactate lotion significantly more effective than a petrolatum-based cream during and after treatment.[^wehr] A third double-blind trial, again comparing 12% ammonium lactate lotion with 5% lactic acid lotion, found the ammonium lactate lotion significantly more effective at reducing xerosis severity during both the treatment and the post-treatment regression periods.[^rogers] "Notable relative to a plain emollient and to a lower-strength lactic acid lotion, across three independently reported double-blind trials, though none report the raw severity-score numbers in their published abstracts."
- Evidence quality: solid. Multiple independent double-blind, controlled trials with consistent direction, underlying the drug's FDA-approved xerosis indication.[^dailymed][^dahl][^wehr][^rogers]

**Ichthyosis vulgaris (Rx, 12% ammonium lactate lotion, twice daily for 4 weeks)**
- Effect size: notable. A double-blind trial found 12% ammonium lactate lotion significantly more effective than both its own vehicle and a petrolatum-based cream, with improvement reported across several ichthyosis subtypes (vulgaris, lamellar, sex-linked, Netherton's, and epidermolytic hyperkeratotic forms), though again the published abstract does not give the specific improvement percentages.[^buxman] This is the key evidence behind the drug's FDA-approved ichthyosis vulgaris indication.[^dailymed] "Notable; beat both vehicle and a comparator cream in the one published vehicle-controlled trial, though the exact effect size isn't stated in the available abstract."
- Evidence quality: solid for a vehicle-controlled dermatologic drug trial, but resting on one published study.[^buxman]

**Head-to-head against non-lactic-acid keratolytics (salicylic acid + urea, or a liposome-based moisturizer) for foot xerosis**
- Effect size: none differential. Two double-blind trials comparing 12% ammonium lactate lotion against other marketed foot moisturizers, a 5% salicylic acid + 10% urea ointment in one, a liposome-based lotion in the other, found both treatments improved xerosis significantly from baseline but found no statistically significant difference between the two arms.[^jennings][^uy] "None differential; effective, but not shown to beat other keratolytic or moisturizing options for foot dryness specifically."
- Evidence quality: preliminary to mixed. Two modest-sized double-blind comparator trials in a narrow (plantar/foot) use case, not vehicle-controlled.[^jennings][^uy]

## What We Actually Know

Ammonium lactate is lactic acid buffered to a less acidic, better-tolerated pH by neutralizing it with ammonium hydroxide, and this buffered form is what the FDA has approved as a prescription drug (12% cream or lotion) for two specific keratinization disorders: xerosis and ichthyosis vulgaris.[^dailymed] Across several independent double-blind trials run to support that approval and its later over-the-counter marketing, 12% ammonium lactate outperformed a plain emollient, a petrolatum-based cream, and a lower-strength 5% lactic acid lotion for xerosis, with its advantage most consistent in the weeks after treatment stopped (the "regression" period), suggesting a lasting rather than merely surface-level effect.[^dahl][^wehr][^rogers] A separate vehicle-controlled trial found it effective across several forms of ichthyosis, beyond the vulgaris subtype named in its approved indication.[^buxman] When compared against other marketed keratolytics rather than against vehicle or plain emollient, in two smaller trials limited to foot xerosis, ammonium lactate performed about as well as, not clearly better than, a salicylic-acid-and-urea ointment or a liposome-based moisturizing lotion.[^jennings][^uy] The FDA-approved labeling documents rash, erythema, irritation, and burning/stinging as the most frequent adverse reactions, seen in roughly 10-15% of ichthyosis-vulgaris patients and in a smaller 2-3% of xerosis patients in the controlled trials behind that labeling.[^dailymed]

## How It Works

As the salt form of [[lactic-acid]], ammonium lactate shares the general alpha-hydroxy-acid mechanism covered in depth there: it is thought to act as a humectant that influences stratum corneum hydration and to decrease corneocyte cohesion, promoting shedding of the outer skin layer, though the FDA-approved labeling notes the precise mechanism for the cohesion effect "is not yet known."[^dailymed] Percutaneous absorption is limited: an in vitro study using human cadaver skin found about 6.1% of the applied material was absorbed after 68 hours.[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

**Xerosis, 12% ammonium lactate lotion vs. 5% lactic acid lotion and a nonlactated emollient.** A 21-day, randomized, double-blind, contralateral study in 60 patients with moderate to severe xerosis applied a 12% ammonium lactate lotion, a 5% lactic acid lotion, and a nonlactated emollient lotion, grading severity on days 0, 7, 14, and 21, then again on days 28 and 35 after treatment stopped. All three preparations significantly reduced xerosis severity scores during treatment. During the two-week post-treatment "regression" period, the 12% ammonium lactate lotion produced significantly greater reductions in severity than the plain emollient (lateral calf area at days 28 and 35, and total severity scores at day 35), and significantly greater reductions than the 5% lactic acid lotion on total severity scores at days 28 and 35.[^dahl]

**Xerosis, 12% ammonium lactate lotion vs. a petrolatum-based cream.** A controlled, double-blind, two-center study in patients with moderate to severe xerosis found the 12% ammonium lactate lotion "significantly more effective" than a petrolatum-based therapeutic cream in reducing xerosis severity, both during treatment and in the post-treatment phase.[^wehr]

**Xerosis, 12% ammonium lactate lotion vs. 5% lactic acid lotion, second trial.** A separate double-blind study, run by a different author group, again compared 12% ammonium lactate lotion with 5% lactic acid lotion for moderate to severe xerosis and found the ammonium lactate lotion "significantly more effective" at reducing severity across both the treatment period and the following no-treatment regression period.[^rogers]

**Ichthyosis, 12% ammonium lactate lotion vs. vehicle and vs. a petrolatum-based cream.** A double-blind trial treated patients twice daily for 4 weeks, with weekly evaluations during treatment and for 2 weeks afterward. The 12% ammonium lactate lotion was "significantly more effective than both its vehicle and a petrolatum-based cream," with improvement reported in vulgaris, lamellar, sex-linked, Netherton's, and epidermolytic hyperkeratotic forms of ichthyosis.[^buxman]

**Foot xerosis vs. salicylic acid + urea.** A randomized, double-blind, paired (one foot per treatment) trial in patients with mild to moderate foot xerosis compared 5% salicylic acid + 10% urea ointment against 12% ammonium lactate lotion. Of 70 enrolled patients, 54 were evaluated at 2 weeks and 39 at 4 weeks. Both treatments significantly reduced xerosis severity, but "there was no statistically significant difference between treatment groups."[^jennings]

**Foot xerosis vs. a liposome-based moisturizing lotion.** A double-blind trial in patients with bilateral plantar xerosis (43 of 57 enrolled completing the protocol) applied ammonium lactate 12% lotion to one foot and a liposome-based moisturizing lotion to the other, twice daily for 4 weeks, then followed for 2 more weeks. Both lotions produced significant improvement in xerotic grade beginning in week 2, with "no significant differences between the two lotions" over the full 6-week course.[^uy]

> **Bottom line.** In every published vehicle- or emollient-controlled trial located, 12% ammonium lactate beat the comparator; in two head-to-head trials against other marketed keratolytic or moisturizing products for foot dryness specifically, it matched but did not clearly beat them. None of the located abstracts report the underlying numeric severity scores, only that the differences were or were not statistically significant.[^dahl][^jennings][^uy]

## Uses

Supported on FDA-approved, controlled clinical-trial evidence: 12% ammonium lactate cream or lotion for xerosis and for ichthyosis vulgaris, the two indications named in its prescribing information.[^dailymed] Supported on trial evidence in a broader ichthyosis population than the approved label names: lamellar, sex-linked, Netherton's, and epidermolytic hyperkeratotic ichthyosis, in one vehicle-controlled study.[^buxman] Not shown to be superior, only comparable, to other marketed keratolytics or moisturizers when compared head-to-head for foot xerosis specifically.[^jennings][^uy] The general alpha-hydroxy-acid evidence for photoaging appearance, skin smoothness, and hydration is covered in depth on [[lactic-acid]].

## Common Marketing Claims

None of the claims below is established beyond what The Evidence above supports.

- **"Clinically proven."** True for its FDA-approved xerosis and ichthyosis vulgaris indications, each backed by published double-blind controlled trials,[^dailymed][^dahl][^wehr][^rogers][^buxman] but the specific numeric results of those trials are not disclosed in the available published abstracts.
- **"More effective than other moisturizers."** True against a plain nonlactated emollient and a petrolatum-based cream in controlled trials,[^dahl][^wehr] but not shown against other marketed keratolytics: two head-to-head foot-xerosis trials found no significant advantage over a salicylic-acid-and-urea ointment or a liposome-based lotion.[^jennings][^uy]
