Capryloyl salicylic acid, better known as beta-lipohydroxy acid (LHA) or C8-LHA, is a lipophilic derivative of [[salicylic-acid]] (chemically 2-hydroxy-5-octanoyl benzoic acid) used as an exfoliant in cleansers, toners, and serums.[^saintleger] It was developed by L'Oreal researchers in the 1980s and, per a 2016 clinical review, "is found only in L'Oreal brand skin care products," which is also why almost every study of it comes from L'Oreal-affiliated researchers or L'Oreal-brand products.[^zeichner]

> LHA's added fatty chain makes it penetrate skin more slowly than plain salicylic acid, producing a milder, cell-by-cell exfoliation in small studies, but it is not an FDA-recognized OTC acne active, and nearly all of the clinical evidence for it comes from L'Oreal-run or L'Oreal-funded research on L'Oreal-brand products.[^zeichner][^saintleger]

## The Rubric

**Exfoliation / mild acne (topical, cosmetic concentrations)**
- **Effect size: modest, mostly from small or industry-run studies.** An in vitro Franz-chamber skin assay found only 6% of LHA penetrated past the stratum corneum, versus 58% of plain salicylic acid, giving it a slower, more surface-limited exfoliating action.[^saintleger] In a randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind comparison, 2% LHA "mimicked" 0.05% retinoic acid on histologic measures of photoaged epidermis "but with somewhat lesser efficacy," while 10% glycolic acid "appeared almost inactive" in the same trial.[^pierard] A 66-patient, multicenter, double-blind 12-week trial found an OTC 5.5% benzoyl-peroxide/LHA combination performed comparably to a prescription 5% benzoyl-peroxide/1% clindamycin combination (both alongside 0.025% tretinoin) on acne lesion counts, with statistically less erythema at week 2 in the LHA arm (p=0.042); because it tested a combination product, this trial cannot isolate LHA's own contribution.[^zeichner]
- **Evidence quality: weak.** LHA is not included in the FDA's OTC acne monograph (only benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid, and sulfur qualify as OTC acne actives), so any acne claim on an OTC LHA product is legally attributed to one of those ingredients, not LHA.[^zeichner] The two foundational review papers characterizing LHA's biology and penetration were authored by L'Oreal Recherche researchers, and the 2016 clinical review synthesizing the acne data discloses its author "has served as a consultant and advisory board member for L'Oreal."[^saintleger][^zeichner] No independent, non-L'Oreal-linked randomized trial of LHA alone (not in an OTC combination product) against a placebo or an established comparator was found.

## How It Works

LHA is chemically salicylic acid with an added eight-carbon fatty chain (octanoyl group), which raises its molecular weight and lipophilicity relative to the parent molecule.[^zeichner] That extra bulk slows its penetration into skin: an in vivo tape-strip analysis found 17.1% of applied LHA retained in the stratum corneum after four days, versus 9.7% for salicylic acid, and the Franz-chamber assay above found far less of it crossing the stratum corneum.[^saintleger][^zeichner] The clinical review frames this as a mechanistic explanation for a "cell-by-cell" pattern of exfoliation (breaking individual corneocyte junctions) rather than the more rapid, sheet-like exfoliation attributed to salicylic acid, though the review states that whether this actually translates into better tolerability "needs continued evaluation."[^zeichner]

## The Evidence

**Photoaging (topical, small comparative trial).** A randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind study by Pierard et al. applied 2% LHA (called "LSA" in the paper), 10% glycolic acid, or 0.05% all-trans-retinoic acid to one forearm and vehicle to the other in women volunteers, then compared epidermal changes by histochemistry and immunohistochemistry. Retinoic acid produced the largest improvement; "the LSA-treated site also exhibited similar positive changes, although to a lesser degree"; glycolic acid "showed no significant effect."[^pierard]

**Comedolytic effect (topical, small studies).** Per the 2016 clinical review, one split-face study of comedonal acne patients using once-daily LHA for a month found a 47% reduction in follicular-cast number and a 54% reduction in their size versus the untreated side (both p<0.01), measured by cyanoacrylate skin-strip sampling; a separate 14-patient study using UV-light video imaging reported a decrease in comedone size and number over 14 days, though the review notes the paper did not publish exact figures.[^zeichner]

**Acne combination trial.** In a multicenter, double-blind, 12-week trial, 66 patients aged 18 to 50 with mild-to-moderate acne were randomized to an OTC 5.5% benzoyl peroxide/LHA product or a prescription 5% benzoyl peroxide/1% clindamycin gel, both used alongside prescription 0.025% tretinoin cream. Both arms showed statistically significant improvement in lesion counts and global assessment from baseline at weeks 4, 8, and 12 (p<0.05), with reductions "similar in both groups"; the benzoyl peroxide/clindamycin arm had significantly more erythema at week 2 (p=0.042).[^zeichner] Because this trial tested LHA only as part of a combination product against another combination product, it does not isolate what LHA itself contributed. Zeichner's review also cites an LHA-monotherapy-versus-benzoyl-peroxide acne study reporting "comparable efficacy and greater tolerability," but that study is L'Oreal-linked and reported without its design, size, or endpoints, so it is thin, hard-to-weigh evidence rather than a clean isolation of LHA's effect.

**Safety.** A case report published in Contact Dermatitis documents contact allergy to capryloyl salicylic acid.[^allergy]

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Gentler exfoliation than salicylic acid."** Supported by penetration data (LHA crosses the stratum corneum less than salicylic acid in vitro) and by the mechanistic "cell-by-cell" exfoliation theory, but the clinical review itself says whether this produces better real-world tolerability "needs continued evaluation," and no head-to-head tolerability trial against plain salicylic acid was found.[^saintleger][^zeichner]
- **"Treats acne."** LHA is not an FDA-recognized OTC acne active. The one sizeable trial testing an LHA-containing product for acne tested it only as part of a benzoyl-peroxide combination, so it cannot show LHA works as an acne treatment on its own.[^zeichner]
