Imiquimod is a prescription topical cream used to clear actinic keratoses and some superficial skin cancers. It is graded here because [[actinic-keratosis]] and [[basal-cell-carcinoma]] both name it and neither had anything to link to, and because its own label contains a sentence worth reading twice.

The original brand, Aldara, was approved as NDA 020723 on 27 February 1997 and is now listed as discontinued, with the record carrying the Federal Register determination that it was not discontinued or withdrawn for safety or effectiveness reasons. Zyclara, at 3.75% and 2.5%, followed as NDA 022483 in March 2010, and generic 5% and 3.75% creams are approved and marketed.[^fda]

## The Rubric

**Actinic keratosis on the face or scalp (health)**
Graded notable on gold-standard evidence. Two double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials randomised 436 subjects to twice-weekly cream for 16 weeks, assessed 8 weeks after the last application. Complete clearance, meaning no clinically visible lesions left in the treatment area, reached 46% (49/107) against 3% (3/110) on vehicle in one trial and 44% (48/108) against 4% (4/111) in the other. Clearing at least 75% of baseline lesions reached 60% against 10% and 58% against 14%.[^label] Those are among the largest absolute differences from vehicle on this site.

**Superficial basal cell carcinoma (health)**
Graded notable on gold-standard evidence, with one important caveat below. Two double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials treated 364 subjects five times weekly for six weeks. Composite clearance, requiring both visual and histological clearance 12 weeks after treatment, was 70% (66/94) against 2% in one trial and 80% (73/91) against 1% in the other, pooling to 75% (139/185) against 2% (3/179).[^label]

**Reading a clear result on skin cancer**
The label reports that 6% (11/178) of treated subjects who appeared clinically clear, and who had both assessments, still had tumour found when the treated area was excised.[^label] Looking clear and being clear are not the same thing here, which is why the indication requires biopsy confirmation first and why this is a physician-managed treatment rather than something to judge by eye.

## How It Works

Not known, and the label says so plainly: "The mechanism of action of Imiquimod Cream in treating AK, sBCC, and EGW lesions is unknown."[^label] A pharmacodynamics trial of 18 subjects with actinic keratosis reported rises from baseline in CD3, CD4, CD8, CD11c and CD68 biomarkers at week two, and the label adds that the clinical relevance of those findings is unknown.[^label] The drug is routinely described as an immune response modifier, which describes a plausible class rather than a demonstrated pathway for these lesions.

## The Evidence

The efficacy case is unusually strong for a topical: four vehicle-controlled double-blind trials across two indications, all summarised in the label itself, with vehicle arms clearing 1% to 14% of subjects. What limits the reading is who was studied. In the actinic keratosis trials, subjects ranged from 37 to 88 years, 55% had Fitzpatrick skin type I or II, and the label records that every imiquimod-treated subject was White. In the superficial basal cell carcinoma trials, 65% had Fitzpatrick I or II.[^label] Nothing in those trials speaks to darker skin.

The indications are also narrower than the drug's reputation. Actinic keratosis is covered only for clinically typical, nonhyperkeratotic, nonhypertrophic lesions on the face or scalp in immunocompetent adults. Superficial basal cell carcinoma is covered only when biopsy-confirmed, primary, no more than 2.0 cm across, and on the trunk excluding anogenital skin, the neck, or the extremities excluding hands and feet.[^label] Field therapy generally and the alternatives are on [[actinic-keratosis]]; the other topical covered here is [[fluorouracil-topical]].

One number belongs with the expectations rather than the efficacy. During treatment, 48% (103/215) of imiquimod-treated subjects in the actinic keratosis trials had MORE visible lesions than at baseline, and the label notes those subjects responded similarly to those without an increase.[^label] Lesions appearing during treatment is the expected course, not a failure.

The label carries no boxed warning, verified absent from the current SPL. Its warnings are intense local inflammatory reactions including weeping and erosion, severe vulvar swelling that can cause urinary retention, localised complete depigmentation that has persisted, flu-like systemic symptoms, and increased sensitivity to ultraviolet light.[^label] The depigmentation warning matters for anyone weighing a cosmetic-sounding treatment: it can be permanent.

## Common Marketing Claims

Quarantined: the phrase most often attached to this drug describes a hypothesis about its class, and the label says the mechanism is unknown.

"Immune response modifier" is how imiquimod is habitually described, including in clinical writing. Set it beside the label's own sentence that the mechanism in these lesions is unknown.[^label] The class label is a hypothesis about how it might work, and the trials establish that it works without establishing why.
