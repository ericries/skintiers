Palmitoyl pentapeptide-4, better known by its trade name Matrixyl, is the synthetic lipopeptide pal-KTTKS: a five-amino-acid fragment of type I procollagen with a fatty palmitoyl tail attached, added to leave-on anti-aging products as a "signal peptide" intended to prompt skin cells to make more collagen. Its human evidence rests almost entirely on one manufacturer-run, placebo-controlled facial trial that found a statistically significant improvement in the look of fine lines; the rest of the record is laboratory work and supplier data.

> In the one substantial clinical test, a 12-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled, split-face trial in 93 women conducted by The Procter & Gamble Company, a moisturizer with 3 ppm pal-KTTKS "provided significant improvement vs. placebo control for reduction in wrinkles/fine lines by both quantitative technical and expert grader image analysis."[^robinson] An independent review of KTTKS found "a sound in vitro basis for its action on fibroblasts" but "a surprising absence of in vitro skin penetration data in the literature" and "relatively few clinical studies using these materials."[^abusamah] The endpoints measured are cosmetic (the appearance of wrinkles, not a medical outcome), and dermatology reviews place peptides in the mid-tier "cosmeceutical" band, below prescription [[tretinoin]], the retinoid "considered to be the clinical 'gold standard'" for topical rejuvenation.[^griffiths]

## The Rubric

**Appearance of wrinkles and fine lines (topical, cosmetic)**
- Effect size: modest, cosmetic. The Robinson trial found significant improvement versus placebo on wrinkle/fine-line image analysis, and the peptide "was well tolerated by the skin."[^robinson] In the same trial's self-assessments, subjects "reported significant fine line/wrinkle improvements and noted directional effects for other facial improvement parameters," meaning the other appearance measures did not clearly separate from placebo.[^robinson] Modest and cosmetic: a measurable change in the look of fine lines in one trial, on appearance endpoints, from an ingredient class that dermatology reviews rank below prescription retinoids.[^griffiths]
- Evidence quality: preliminary. The key trial was conducted by Procter & Gamble, the company that developed and sells the ingredient's benefit story.[^robinson] The independent systematic review of collagen-stimulating cosmetic peptides identified 12 papers reporting 15 studies, of which "only six used a placebo control," and concluded "the literature on the topic is sparse."[^michalek] A 2026 meta-analysis of 19 peptide RCTs found a "modest pooled effect on wrinkle reduction (MD = 0.27, p = 0.04)" that "was largely driven by oral polypeptides" rather than topical ones.[^nukaly]

**Collagen synthesis in living skin (the mechanism the marketing claims)**
- Effect size: demonstrated in cell culture, not in living skin. An independent University of Reading group showed C16-KTTKS "stimulates collagen production in a concentration-dependent manner" in human fibroblasts in vitro.[^jones] The Robinson trial measured the appearance of wrinkles, not collagen in the skin of its participants.[^robinson] The KTTKS review found the in-vitro stimulatory effect "specific to collagen types I and III and fibronectin expression" while noting the absence of published skin-penetration data for the peptide.[^abusamah]
- Evidence quality: experimental. A 2022 review of anti-wrinkle peptide delivery concluded that "most anti-wrinkle peptides are not appropriate candidates for skin permeation" without enhancement methods and "might not reach their targets in the skin at right concentrations."[^mortazavi]

## How It Works

KTTKS (lysine-threonine-threonine-lysine-serine) is, in the words of a 2020 peer-reviewed review, "the well-known signal peptide fragment of the C-terminal propeptide of type I collagen": a piece of the collagen precursor molecule that, when detected by fibroblasts, is proposed to act as a feedback signal to make more collagen and other extracellular-matrix proteins.[^errante] Its developers described it as "a synthetic material that was designed as a topical agent to stimulate collagen production and thus provide a skin anti-wrinkle benefit."[^robinson] The palmitoyl tail is the delivery rationale: per the same review, "the conjugation with the palmitoyl moiety results in more effective delivery across the skin and better stability to skin proteases."[^errante] How much of the applied peptide actually reaches the dermis is not established; the dedicated KTTKS review found no published skin-penetration data for it, and the wider peptide-delivery literature treats poor skin permeation as the default for molecules of this size and charge.[^abusamah][^mortazavi]

Cosmetic peptides as a class are well-tolerated ingredients with a modest, mostly preliminary effect on the appearance of aging, and the pooled wrinkle signal comes more from ingested than topical peptides; the class-level evidence, by peptide type, is set out on [[peptides]].

## One Trade Name, Three Different Products

Sederma, the French ingredient supplier behind the Matrixyl trademark, sells several distinct peptide products under the name, and they do not contain the same molecules.

- **Matrixyl** is pal-KTTKS itself, palmitoyl pentapeptide-4.[^jones][^errante]
- **Matrixyl 3000** is a blend of two other palmitoylated peptides. Errante and colleagues describe it as the supplier's Biopeptide CL "in combination with Pal-GQPR in Matrixyl 3000, both developed by Sederma as an anti-aging serum, stimulating collagen production."[^errante] On retail labels the name travels with the ingredients palmitoyl tripeptide-1 and palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7.[^ordinary]
- **Matrixyl synthe'6** appears on products whose ingredient lists carry palmitoyl tripeptide-38, a third, different peptide.[^ordinary]

The Robinson trial evidence belongs to pal-KTTKS at 3 ppm and does not transfer to these successor blends.[^robinson] Their own peer-reviewed record is largely supplier-connected. A 2015 study of "a blend of palmitoyl oligopeptide and palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7," authored by Sederma researchers, reported by echography that "the peptide blend reduced subepidermal low-echogenic band thickness and improved its density" and that "the peptides improved the ECM structure vs. placebo," in a methods paper its authors call "this preliminary MALDI-MSI study."[^mondon] For palmitoyl tripeptide-38, a 2020 human study was open-label with no control group: 35 women applied a serum containing 15% l-ascorbic acid, tocopheryl acetate, and 5 ppm of the peptide for 56 days, so nothing attributes the reported 8-9% drop in skin-roughness parameters to the peptide rather than the [[vitamin-c]]; the study was funded by Alumier MD Laboratories and all three authors are consultants to Alumier Labs.[^lintner]

## The Evidence

**The key trial (manufacturer-run).** Robinson and colleagues at The Procter & Gamble Company's Miami Valley Laboratories ran a 12-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled, split-face, left-right randomized study in 93 Caucasian women aged 35-55, comparing a moisturizer against the same moisturizer with 3 ppm pal-KTTKS. The peptide side "provided significant improvement vs. placebo control for reduction in wrinkles/fine lines by both quantitative technical and expert grader image analysis," was "well tolerated by the skin," and in self-assessments subjects "reported significant fine line/wrinkle improvements and noted directional effects for other facial improvement parameters."[^robinson] *One well-designed but manufacturer-run trial found a statistically significant improvement in the look of fine lines; the abstract reports no effect magnitude, and the non-wrinkle appearance measures were directional rather than significant.*

**Independent in-vitro confirmation.** Jones and colleagues at the University of Reading, noting the peptide "is used in antiwrinkle cosmeceutical applications (trade name Matrixyl)," found that C16-KTTKS "stimulates collagen production in a concentration-dependent manner" in cultured human fibroblasts, close to the concentration at which the molecule self-assembles into nanotape structures, suggesting "self-assembly and the stimulation of collagen production are inter-related."[^jones] *The collagen-signaling mechanism is real in a dish, confirmed outside the manufacturer; a dish is not facial skin.*

**The dedicated independent review.** Abu Samah and Heard at Cardiff University reviewed KTTKS and its derivatives in 2011. They found "a sound in vitro basis for its action on fibroblasts due to its stimulatory effect on extracellular matrix synthesis, where the stimulatory effect of KTTKS is specific to collagen types I and III and fibronectin expression," and that the peptide "features in numerous up-market cosmetic products." They also found "a surprising absence of in vitro skin penetration data in the literature" and "relatively few clinical studies using these materials."[^abusamah] *The only independent review devoted to this peptide accepts the lab mechanism and flags that the delivery step and the clinical base are thin.*

**The delivery problem.** Mortazavi and Moghimi's 2022 review of anti-wrinkle peptide permeability concluded that "most anti-wrinkle peptides are not appropriate candidates for skin permeation and the use of enhancement methods is essential to increase their permeability," and that without such methods "most anti-wrinkle peptides might not reach their targets in the skin at right concentrations"; KTTKS is among the peptides whose permeability the reviewed enhancement studies addressed.[^mortazavi] *Peptides of this size penetrate skin poorly by default; the palmitoyl tail is an attempt at a fix whose adequacy in a retail cream has not been independently measured for this molecule.*

**The field around it.** Michalek and colleagues' systematic review of extracellular-matrix-stimulating cosmetic peptides found 12 papers reporting 15 studies, "only six used a placebo control," double-blinding in five, and concluded "the literature on the topic is sparse" with "a need for better planned and controlled clinical trials."[^michalek] The 2026 meta-analysis of 19 peptide RCTs (1,341 participants) found peptides well tolerated, with a "modest pooled effect on wrinkle reduction (MD = 0.27, p = 0.04)" that "was largely driven by oral polypeptides (MD = 1.5, p = 0.01)."[^nukaly] Griffiths and colleagues' 2023 review of topical rejuvenation names tretinoin, "a prescription-only medicine considered to be the clinical 'gold standard,'" as the most efficacious topical treatment, with peptides among the "cosmeceutical" options below it.[^griffiths] *Set against the wider evidence, pal-KTTKS is one of the better-tested cosmetic peptides, and the class it leads still sits well below prescription retinoids.*

## Common Marketing Claims

- **"Stimulates collagen production" / "rebuilds the skin's matrix."** The collagen-signaling effect is demonstrated in cultured fibroblasts, including by an independent laboratory.[^jones] No study cited here measured collagen in the living skin of people using a retail pal-KTTKS product; the key trial measured the appearance of wrinkles.[^robinson]
- **"Clinically proven anti-wrinkle peptide."** There is one substantial placebo-controlled facial trial, run by Procter & Gamble, positive on fine-line appearance by image analysis.[^robinson] The independent review of the field calls the clinical literature "sparse" and mostly not placebo-controlled.[^michalek]
- **"Matrixyl 3000 / Matrixyl synthe'6: the next generation of Matrixyl."** These are different molecules sold under the same trademark.[^errante][^ordinary] The blend study is authored by Sederma researchers and described by them as preliminary,[^mondon] and the palmitoyl tripeptide-38 human study is open-label, uncontrolled, mixed with 15% vitamin C, and funded by the serum's maker.[^lintner]
- **A gentler substitute for retinoids.** Peptides are well tolerated,[^nukaly] and the same dermatology reviews that cover them rank prescription tretinoin, not any peptide, as the most efficacious topical rejuvenation treatment.[^griffiths]
