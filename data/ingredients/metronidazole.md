Topical metronidazole is a prescription-only nitroimidazole antibiotic, FDA-approved as a gel, cream, or lotion (0.75% or 1%) for "the topical treatment of inflammatory lesions of rosacea."[^dailymed] It has long been a standard topical for that use, the reference against which newer options are measured.[^elewski][^taieb] Its own FDA label is candid that the reason it works there is unresolved: "the mechanism of action of metronidazole in the treatment of rosacea is unknown."[^dailymed] Its effect on [[rosacea|rosacea's]] inflammatory bumps and pustules ([[rosacea-papulopustular|papulopustular rosacea]]) is real and independently replicated, but it is modest, and in head-to-head trials it has been outperformed by both [[azelaic-acid|azelaic acid 15%]] and [[ivermectin-topical|ivermectin 1%]].[^elewski][^taieb]

## The Rubric

**Inflammatory lesions (papules and pustules) of papulopustular rosacea**
- **Effect size: modest.** A 40-patient split-face, double-blind trial of metronidazole 0.75% gel twice daily found papule-and-pustule reductions of 36.7%, 48.5%, and 65.1% at 3, 6, and 9 weeks on the treated side, against a maximum 14.9% reduction on the vehicle side.[^bleicher] The trial cited in the FDA label for the once-daily 1% gel (746 subjects, 10 weeks) found a 50.7% reduction in inflammatory lesions versus 32.6% for vehicle, and an Investigator's Global Assessment of clear-or-almost-clear in 38.42% of the drug group versus 27.51% on vehicle.[^dailymed] Against the two newer topicals it trails on the same measures: in a 251-patient head-to-head, azelaic acid 15% gel cut lesions 72.7% versus 55.8% for metronidazole 0.75% (P<.001);[^elewski] in a 962-patient head-to-head (the ATTRACT study), ivermectin 1% cream cut lesions 83.0% versus 73.7% for metronidazole 0.75% (P<0.001), with treatment success in 84.9% versus 75.4% (P<0.001).[^taieb] "Modest: clearly better than vehicle, and effective enough to remain first-line for decades, but beaten by both azelaic acid 15% and ivermectin 1% in direct comparison."
- **Evidence quality: solid.** Two independent vehicle-controlled RCTs three decades apart (0.75% twice daily and 1% once daily), a randomized maintenance trial, two independent head-to-head trials against newer comparators, and FDA approval all converge.[^bleicher][^dailymed][^dahl][^elewski][^taieb] It falls one tier short of the "high-quality" rating an abridged Cochrane systematic review gave azelaic acid and ivermectin: the same review rated topical metronidazole "moderate-quality evidence" against placebo, and reported ivermectin "slightly more effective than metronidazole in one study."[^cochrane]

**Persistent facial redness (erythema)**
- **Effect size: minimal.** Metronidazole's own FDA-approved indication is limited to inflammatory lesions, not erythema.[^dailymed] Where erythema was measured, the effect was secondary and smaller than the lesion effect: in the 1987 split-face trial, erythema "also responded but was less improved" than lesions by nine weeks;[^bleicher] in the head-to-head against azelaic acid, only 42% of metronidazole-treated patients were rated improved for erythema versus 56% on azelaic acid (P=.02).[^elewski]
- **Evidence quality: mixed.** No trial in this set was designed around erythema as a primary endpoint; the erythema findings are secondary readouts inside trials built to measure inflammatory lesions.[^bleicher][^elewski]

## What We Actually Know

Topical metronidazole has a genuine, independently replicated effect on the inflammatory papules and pustules of papulopustular rosacea, confirmed against vehicle in trials thirty years apart and carrying an FDA approval built on that evidence.[^bleicher][^dailymed] A randomized maintenance trial also found that continuing metronidazole gel after initial clearing cut relapse: 9 of 39 subjects (23%) relapsed on metronidazole versus 18 of 43 (42%) on vehicle over six months (P<.05).[^dahl] Against the two topicals that came after it, though, metronidazole is the weaker option on both drugs' own head-to-head trials: azelaic acid 15% and ivermectin 1% each beat metronidazole 0.75% on inflammatory-lesion reduction and on the share of patients rated clear or almost clear.[^elewski][^taieb] Its effect on persistent redness, as distinct from lesions, is smaller and not what its FDA approval covers.[^dailymed][^bleicher][^elewski]

## How It Works

Metronidazole is classed as an antibiotic (a nitroimidazole, also used systemically against anaerobic bacteria and protozoa), but the FDA label does not attribute its rosacea benefit to killing bacteria: "the mechanism of action of metronidazole in the treatment of rosacea is unknown."[^dailymed] Systemic exposure from topical use is minimal: a one-gram dose of the 1% gel applied to the face produced a mean peak plasma concentration of 32 ± 9 ng/mL, "less than 1% of the value reported for a single 250 mg oral dose."[^dailymed] The label's neurologic-disease warning covers both routes: peripheral neuropathy, "characterized by numbness or paresthesia of an extremity, has been reported in patients treated with systemic metronidazole," and the label adds that "peripheral neuropathy has been reported with the post approval use of topical metronidazole," a rare post-marketing report rather than a finding from the controlled trials.[^dailymed] The topical product's own precautions are narrower: it should be used with care in patients with a history of blood dyscrasia (metronidazole is a nitroimidazole), and it can cause irritant or allergic contact dermatitis and eye irritation on contact.[^dailymed]

## The Evidence

**Vehicle-controlled trials.** A randomized, double-blind, split-face trial (n=40) compared metronidazole 0.75% gel against vehicle, one on each side of the face, twice daily for nine weeks: the treated side showed mean reductions in papules and pustules of 36.7%, 48.5%, and 65.1% at 3, 6, and 9 weeks, versus a maximum 14.9% reduction on the vehicle side; erythema "also responded but was less improved" by the end of the trial, and the gel was "locally well tolerated."[^bleicher] The current FDA label for once-daily metronidazole 1% gel cites a randomized trial of 746 rosacea subjects (557 on the drug, 189 on vehicle) over 10 weeks: inflammatory lesion counts fell from a baseline of 18.3 to 8.9 (a 50.7% reduction) on the drug versus 18.4 to 12.8 (32.6%) on vehicle, and an Investigator's Global Assessment of clear or almost clear was reached by 214 of 557 (38.42%) on the drug versus 52 of 189 (27.51%) on vehicle.[^dailymed]

**Maintenance.** A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled 113 subjects with moderate to severe rosacea in an open combination phase of oral tetracycline plus topical metronidazole gel; 88 subjects who cleared were then randomized to continue metronidazole 0.75% gel or switch to vehicle, alone, for up to six months. Relapse occurred in 9 of 39 (23%) on metronidazole versus 18 of 43 (42%) on vehicle (P<.05), and metronidazole-treated subjects had significantly fewer papules and pustules at six months (P<.01); erythema also relapsed less often on metronidazole (55%) than on vehicle (74%).[^dahl]

**Head-to-head against azelaic acid.** A multicenter, double-blind, randomized trial (n=251, 15 weeks) compared azelaic acid 15% gel against metronidazole 0.75% gel. Azelaic acid produced a larger percent reduction in inflammatory lesions (72.7% vs 55.8%, P<.001), a larger nominal lesion-count reduction (-12.9 vs -10.7, P=.003), more patients rated improved for erythema (56% vs 42%, P=.02), and a significant advantage on both the Investigator's Global Assessment (P=.02) and patients' overall improvement rating (P=.005), with azelaic acid's improvement continuing through week 15 while metronidazole's plateaued around week 8.[^elewski]

**Head-to-head against ivermectin.** A phase 3, investigator-blinded, randomized trial (the ATTRACT study, n=962: 478 on ivermectin, 484 on metronidazole) compared once-daily ivermectin 1% cream against twice-daily metronidazole 0.75% cream over 16 weeks. Ivermectin reduced inflammatory lesions further (83.0% vs 73.7%, P<0.001), a difference reported as early as week 3, and produced more clear-or-almost-clear ratings (84.9% vs 75.4%, P<0.001); adverse-event rates were comparable between arms, and local tolerability favored ivermectin.[^taieb]

**Independent synthesis.** An abridged, updated Cochrane systematic review of 106 randomized rosacea trials reported that pooling was feasible for topical metronidazole and azelaic acid, and "both appeared to be more effective than placebo (moderate and high-quality evidence, respectively)"; the same review found topical ivermectin "more effective than placebo based on two studies (high-quality evidence), and slightly more effective than metronidazole in one study."[^cochrane] Full detail on that review is on [[van-zuuren-rosacea-cochrane]].

## Uses

Well-supported: the inflammatory papules and pustules of papulopustular rosacea, a prescription-only use with a decades-long trial record and FDA approval, though it is now the third-place option among the graded topicals, behind azelaic acid and ivermectin on their own head-to-head trials against it.[^bleicher][^dailymed][^elewski][^taieb] A randomized maintenance trial supports continuing it after initial clearing to reduce relapse.[^dahl] Its effect on persistent facial redness, apart from the inflammatory lesions, is smaller and outside its FDA-approved indication.[^dailymed][^bleicher][^elewski]

## Common Marketing Claims

Marketing framings not established by the graded evidence above:

- **"Antibiotic that clears rosacea by killing bacteria."** Metronidazole is classed as an antibiotic, but its own FDA label does not credit an antibacterial action for the rosacea effect and instead states the mechanism is unknown.[^dailymed]
- **"The gold-standard rosacea treatment."** Not supported by the comparative trial record. Two independent head-to-head trials, one against azelaic acid and one against ivermectin, both found metronidazole the weaker of the pair on inflammatory-lesion reduction and on the share of patients rated clear or almost clear.[^elewski][^taieb]
- **"Fixes rosacea redness."** The FDA-approved indication is for inflammatory lesions, not erythema, and the erythema data that exists is a secondary finding smaller than the lesion effect.[^dailymed][^bleicher][^elewski]
