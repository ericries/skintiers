Terbinafine is an allylamine antifungal, dispensed as an oral tablet by prescription (generic; the Lamisil tablet brand is discontinued) and as an OTC topical cream, gel, or spray (Lamisil AT and store-brand equivalents), never as a cosmetic ingredient.[^tablet][^cream] The oral tablet's FDA-approved indication is onychomycosis of the toenail or fingernail caused by dermatophytes, confirmed by KOH preparation, fungal culture, or nail biopsy before treatment starts.[^tablet] The topical 1% cream is FDA-monograph OTC labeled to "cures most athlete's foot (tinea pedis)" and "cures most jock itch (tinea cruris) and ringworm (tinea corporis)."[^cream] Both forms work the same way: terbinafine inhibits squalene epoxidase, blocking ergosterol synthesis in the fungal cell membrane, and the tablet label states this mechanism makes it active against dermatophytes such as *Trichophyton rubrum* and *Trichophyton mentagrophytes*, while activity against *Candida albicans*, a yeast, is only an in vitro finding whose "safety and efficacy... in treating clinical infections... have not been established in adequate and well-controlled clinical trials."[^tablet]

> **Bottom line:** Terbinafine is a well-evidenced antifungal for dermatophyte infections, the most effective oral option for nail fungus and an OTC-labeled topical treatment for athlete's foot and ringworm, but its mechanism and clinical trial base are specific to dermatophytes. It is not established for the Malassezia yeast conditions a skincare audience most often asks about; fungal acne (Malassezia folliculitis), tinea versicolor, and seborrheic dermatitis call for an azole such as [[ketoconazole]] instead.[^tablet][^saunte]

## The Rubric

**Onychomycosis (toenail and fingernail fungal infection), oral terbinafine, the drug's own FDA-approved indication.**
- **Effect size: strong and directly compared against placebo and against azoles.** A 2017 Cochrane systematic review of 48 randomized trials (10,200 participants) found "high-quality evidence that terbinafine is more effective than placebo for achieving clinical cure (risk ratio 6.00, 95% CI 3.96 to 9.08... 1006 participants) and mycological cure (risk ratio 4.53, 95% CI 2.47 to 8.33... 1006 participants)."[^cochrane] The same review reports "moderate-quality evidence that terbinafine was probably more effective than azoles for achieving clinical cure... and mycological cure," making it the review's best-performing oral option among the antifungals compared.[^cochrane]
- **Evidence quality: the strongest tier available.** A Cochrane review pooling 48 RCTs with a graded, high-quality rating on the placebo comparison is about as rigorous as dermatologic drug evidence gets.

**Tinea pedis, tinea corporis, and tinea cruris (athlete's foot, ringworm, jock itch), topical terbinafine.**
- **Effect size: not independently quantified here.** The OTC label states the 1% cream "cures most" of these infections in one to two weeks of twice- or once-daily application, but the label is a drug-facts monograph statement, not a reported trial statistic, so no percentage figure can be sourced from it.[^cream] This use is consistent with terbinafine's approved dermatophyte spectrum and its long OTC track record, but the specific cure rate is not claimed here because it is not verifiable from the sources reviewed.
- **Evidence quality: label-level, not a cited trial.** The claim is qualitative (an FDA-cleared OTC drug-facts label), not a graded systematic review like the onychomycosis data above, which is the main reason this use does not push the overall tier past "good."

**Malassezia yeast conditions, fungal acne, tinea (pityriasis) versicolor, and seborrheic dermatitis: terbinafine is not the tool.**
- **Effect size: not established, with one narrow topical exception.** Terbinafine's own label lists activity against the yeast *Candida albicans* as an unestablished in vitro finding only, not a treated indication.[^tablet] A 2020 review of Malassezia-associated skin diseases does not include terbinafine among the treatments discussed for Malassezia folliculitis ("fungal acne"), and states specifically that for pityriasis versicolor, "terbinafine 1% cream, but not oral terbinafine, is also effective," a narrow exception limited to one yeast condition and one topical formulation.[^saunte]
- **Evidence quality: this is a spectrum gap, not a graded trial finding.** [[fungal-acne]] is a Malassezia-driven condition that terbinafine does not treat; [[ketoconazole]] is the azole with the evidence base for it, and the site's sibling oral antifungals [[itraconazole]] and [[fluconazole]] are the systemic options reviewed for Malassezia disease when topical treatment fails.

## The Evidence

The onychomycosis numbers above come from a 2017 Cochrane systematic review, the most rigorous evidence behind any use discussed on this page, pooling 48 randomized trials against placebo, azoles, and griseofulvin.[^cochrane] The tinea pedis, corporis, and cruris uses rest on the topical product's own FDA-monograph OTC label rather than a cited trial, a real but less rigorously sourced basis than the onychomycosis data.[^cream] The yeast-condition boundary rests on the oral tablet's own label, which does not claim efficacy against *Candida*, and on a 2020 review of Malassezia-associated skin disease that omits terbinafine from its Malassezia folliculitis discussion and explicitly separates topical from oral terbinafine for pityriasis versicolor.[^tablet][^saunte]

## Safety

Oral terbinafine carries a hepatotoxicity warning: the tablet label states "liver failure, sometimes leading to liver transplant or death, has occurred with the use of oral terbinafine," in patients with and without preexisting liver disease, and instructs prescribers to obtain pretreatment liver function tests and monitor periodically during therapy.[^tablet] It is contraindicated in patients with chronic or active liver disease.[^tablet] The label separately warns of taste disturbance, including taste loss, that "can be severe enough to result in decreased food intake, weight loss, anxiety, and depressive symptoms" and "may be prolonged (greater than 1 year), or may be permanent," along with a parallel warning for smell disturbance.[^tablet] Additional label warnings cover depressive symptoms, severe neutropenia, and serious skin reactions including Stevens-Johnson syndrome and DRESS syndrome.[^tablet] Topical terbinafine, by contrast, is a well-tolerated OTC drug-facts product: the cream label's only cautions are for external use, avoiding the eyes and mucous membranes, and stopping use if irritation worsens, none of the systemic risks that apply to the oral tablet.[^cream]
