A preservative and solvent, not an active. It makes formulas keep; it is not sold on doing anything for your skin, which is why the grade here is `weak`. **Bottom line:** the thing to know about it is tolerability, and in 2026 that changed status.

> **The American Contact Dermatitis Society named it Allergen of the Year for 2026.** The designation is intended "to increase awareness of its allergenic potential and encourage more comprehensive patch testing when clinically indicated".[^aoty] The reason given is a gap rather than an epidemic: benzyl alcohol "is not included in many standard patch test series, which may result in underrecognition of its role as an allergen".[^aoty] That is a statement about what gets tested, not a claim that reactions are common.

## The Rubric

**Functional preservative and solvent, not a skin-health active**

- **Effect size on skin: none claimed.** It is "an aromatic alcohol widely used as a preservative, solvent, and fragrance ingredient in personal care products, topical medications, and injectable formulations".[^aoty] No efficacy evidence was sought, because no benefit is advertised.
- **Tolerability: a recognised allergen, in a population that is mostly fine.** The review's own framing is that it is "generally considered safe" and that it "can cause allergic contact dermatitis, particularly with prolonged or repeated exposure".[^aoty] Both halves matter. Allergen of the Year is an awareness designation from a professional society, which is a different thing from evidence that the ingredient is dangerous or that most people react to it.
- **No prevalence figure is given here**, because the abstract read for this page provides none. Anyone quoting a percentage for benzyl alcohol contact allergy is not quoting this paper.
- **Occupational exposure is a separate story.** Two of the recent case reports in this literature concern epoxy hardeners and epoxy floor paint rather than cosmetics.[^occupational] A hand dermatitis from industrial epoxy does not tell you what a preservative at fractional percent in a lotion does.

## The "alcohol-free" wrinkle

Benzyl alcohol is an aromatic alcohol and behaves nothing like the ethanol or SD alcohol people avoid for dryness. That gap gets used. [[altreno-tretinoin-0-05-lotion]] is marketed as alcohol-free while the FDA label lists benzyl alcohol among its inactive ingredients, and the honest reading is that the claim excludes drying or denatured alcohols rather than every alcohol-derived excipient.[^altreno] Technically defensible, and not what most buyers hear.

## Common Marketing Claims

One claim involves this ingredient directly, and it is quarantined here rather than endorsed. Altreno is marketed as "alcohol-free" while its FDA label lists benzyl alcohol among the inactive ingredients.[^altreno] The claim is the manufacturer's; the ingredient list is the FDA's. Nothing on this page says the claim breaches any labelling rule, only that the two statements sit together and mean different things by the word alcohol.

## The Evidence

One source carried the weight here: the 2026 Allergen of the Year review, read from its PubMed abstract.[^aoty] It is a narrative review and designation rather than a trial, which is the appropriate form for what it is doing and also its limit. No controlled study of benzyl alcohol sensitisation rates is cited on this page because none was read.

Where it turns up on this site: twenty-five pages mention it, including [[altreno-tretinoin-0-05-lotion]], [[avar-sodium-sulfacetamide-10-sulfur-5]] and [[aveeno-daily-moisturizing-lotion]], and it is in the vehicle of [[luzu-luliconazole-1-cream]].

> **Bottom line.** Not worth avoiding on principle. Worth knowing the name if you have stubborn dermatitis that a standard patch test did not explain, because the society's own point is that many standard series do not test it.
