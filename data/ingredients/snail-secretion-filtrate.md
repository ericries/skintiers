Snail secretion filtrate, sold on cosmetic labels as "snail mucin," is the filtered mucus of land snails, and nearly all of its controlled research is on one patented extract, the secretion of the mollusk *Cryptomphalus aspersa* (SCA), rather than on the high-percentage mucin essences popular in K-beauty.

Two things are easy to run together and worth keeping apart. The ingredient that dermatology journals have actually studied is SCA, a specific *Cryptomphalus aspersa* secretion developed and supplied by its manufacturer, and even that research is thin at the human level: one small split-face wrinkle trial and one radiation-dermatitis pilot, supported by cell-culture work on fibroblasts and keratinocytes.[^fabi][^fondevilla][^brieva][^cruz] The separate, far more famous product, the 92-to-96% snail-mucin essence, is not what those trials tested, and no controlled trial of such an essence is cited here. Where snail secretion has a plausible mechanism, it is as a source of humectant and antioxidant material rather than as a proven treatment for any skin condition.[^brieva]

> The human evidence for snail secretion is a single 25-patient split-face trial of the SCA extract that improved a cosmetic wrinkle measure (P=.03) and a 72-patient pilot suggesting less radiation dermatitis, both using material from the ingredient's makers; the regeneration story rests on cell-culture experiments, not controlled human wound trials.[^fabi][^fondevilla][^brieva][^cruz]

## The Rubric

Grades differ by use, and the cosmetic uses are separated from the one health signal. Framing is relative and names comparators.

**Facial and periocular wrinkles / photoaging (cosmetic)**
- **Effect size: minimal to modest.** The one randomized human trial is a 14-week, double-blind, split-face study (n=25) of the SCA extract, where the treated side showed improvement in periocular wrinkles measured by silicone skin impression at 12 weeks (P=.03).[^fabi] That is a cosmetic surrogate on a small sample, and it sits well below the collagen-level anti-aging evidence for prescription retinoids. "Minimal to modest on a cosmetic wrinkle measure, far short of [[tretinoin]] and the wider retinoid class, whose anti-aging case rests on collagen and histology endpoints."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** One small split-face RCT plus in vitro mechanism, most of it produced by groups affiliated with the ingredient's manufacturer, with no independent replication of the clinical result cited here.[^fabi][^brieva][^cruz]

**Radiation dermatitis prevention (health)**
- **Effect size: not established; one pilot signals possible benefit.** In a 72-patient head-and-neck cancer pilot, a 10-to-15% SCA concentration was associated with fewer grade II or worse radiodermatitis cases (34% and 38%) than a 0-to-5% concentration (58%), reported at p=0.017 and p=0.045.[^fondevilla] This is the only human health-outcome signal, and it is a single, non-replicated pilot. "Promising but unproven, on one manufacturer-supplied pilot, not a controlled standard of care."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** A single-institution pilot in which the snail secretion was supplied by a manufacturer, with no randomized replication.[^fondevilla]

**Wound healing / skin regeneration**
- **Effect size: none demonstrated in humans.** The "regenerative" framing comes from cell-culture experiments in which SCA increased fibroblast and keratinocyte proliferation and migration, not from controlled human wound trials.[^brieva][^cruz] No graded human effect can be assigned. "Cell-culture mechanism only; no controlled human wound-healing trial cited."
- **Evidence quality: preliminary.** In vitro assays from a small number of related laboratories, several of them tied to the ingredient's manufacturer.[^brieva][^cruz]

**Hydration and general "moisturizing" / "glass skin" (cosmetic)**
- **Effect size: not established for the marketed essences.** The mucin's water-binding, film-forming character makes a humectant effect plausible, but no controlled trial of a high-percentage snail-mucin essence is cited, and the well-evidenced humectants carry that ground instead. "Untested for the specific mucin essences; hydration is better evidenced for [[hyaluronic-acid]] and [[glycerin]]."
- **Evidence quality: anecdotal.** No controlled trial of the generic snail-mucin product is cited.

## What We Actually Know

The controlled human evidence is small and narrow, and it is almost entirely on the SCA extract rather than on generic snail mucin. A double-blind, randomized, split-face trial (n=25) found the SCA-treated side improved on a periocular wrinkle measure at 12 weeks (P=.03), a cosmetic endpoint.[^fabi] A separate 72-patient pilot in head-and-neck cancer patients found that a higher SCA concentration (10 to 15%) tracked with fewer cases of moderate-or-worse radiation dermatitis than a lower one, a health signal that has not been replicated in a randomized trial.[^fondevilla] Underneath these sit cell-culture studies showing that SCA increases fibroblast and keratinocyte proliferation and migration and carries antioxidant enzyme activity.[^brieva][^cruz] Two honest limits sit beside all of this: much of the work comes from research groups affiliated with the ingredient's manufacturer, and the popular high-percentage snail-mucin essences are a different material from the SCA extract these trials used.[^fabi][^fondevilla][^brieva]

## How It Works

Snail secretion filtrate is a mixture rather than a single active. A mechanistic study of the SCA extract reported that "SCA contained antioxidant SOD and GST activities," referring to superoxide dismutase and glutathione S-transferase, two enzymes that neutralize reactive oxygen species, and that SCA "stimulated fibroblast proliferation and rearrangement of the actin cytoskeleton."[^brieva] The same work described SCA stimulating extracellular matrix assembly and regulating metalloproteinase activity, the enzymes that remodel collagen and other matrix proteins.[^brieva] A later cell-culture study found that "SCA promotes proliferation and migration of HaCaT cells," a keratinocyte line, along with dermal fibroblasts, in a dose- and time-dependent way, and that it increased adhesion-protein expression and cell survival.[^cruz] These are the proposed mechanisms behind the "regenerative" positioning; they are demonstrated in cells, and the human trials test whether any of it translates to skin.

## The Evidence

Primary evidence, by use:

**Photoaging (cosmetic wrinkle endpoint).** Fabi and colleagues ran a 14-week, double-blind, randomized, two-center split-face trial in 25 patients with moderate-to-severe facial photodamage, applying an 8% SCA emulsion and a 40% SCA serum to one side of the face and placebo to the other for 12 weeks.[^fabi] The active side showed significant improvement in periocular rhytides at 12 weeks (P=.03), assessed by silicone skin impressions, with improved texture through week 14, and the authors reported the products effective and well tolerated for coarse periocular and fine facial rhytides.[^fabi] The endpoints are cosmetic (wrinkle appearance), the sample is small, and the study was conducted on the manufacturer's SCA material.

**Radiation dermatitis (health outcome).** Fondevilla and colleagues reported a single-institution pilot in 72 head-and-neck cancer patients undergoing radiotherapy, testing SCA at 0, 5, 10 and 15% concentrations.[^fondevilla] At the higher concentrations (10 and 15%), 34% and 38% of patients respectively developed grade II or worse radiodermatitis, compared with 58% at the 0-and-5% concentrations (p=0.017 and p=0.045), and the authors concluded a 10-to-15% concentration was the best at reducing grade II or worse radiodermatitis after adjusting for other factors.[^fondevilla] The snail secretion samples were supplied by a laboratory (Tahe), and this is a single non-randomized pilot rather than a confirmatory trial.

**Combined-formula cosmetic study.** Addor reported an open-label, single-center study in 120 women aged 40 to 65 of a formulation combining SCA with "green coffee oil, olive oil, ectoine, hyaluronic acid, and peptides," and described improvement in hydration, firmness and elasticity from 45 days of use.[^addor] Because SCA was combined with several other active ingredients, including a well-known humectant, this study cannot attribute the effect to snail secretion, and it was open-label without a placebo arm; the author states "The author reports no conflicts of interest in this work."[^addor]

**Mechanism (cell culture).** Brieva and colleagues characterized SCA's antioxidant enzyme content (SOD and GST) and its effects on fibroblast proliferation, cytoskeletal rearrangement, extracellular matrix assembly and metalloproteinase activity.[^brieva] Cruz and colleagues found SCA promoted proliferation, migration and survival of keratinocytes (HaCaT) and dermal fibroblasts in vitro, with increased adhesion-molecule expression and FAK phosphorylation.[^cruz] These are cell-culture mechanisms, not clinical outcomes, and several of the involved authors are affiliated with the ingredient's manufacturer.

## Uses

Weakly supported for cosmetic wrinkle improvement: one small split-face RCT of the SCA extract shows a periocular-wrinkle benefit on a cosmetic measure, well short of the retinoid class.[^fabi] A single early health signal exists for reducing radiation dermatitis during head-and-neck radiotherapy, from one non-randomized pilot that has not been replicated.[^fondevilla] The "skin regeneration" and wound-healing positioning rests on cell-culture mechanism rather than controlled human wound trials.[^brieva][^cruz] The hydration and "glass skin" claims attached to high-percentage mucin essences are not backed by a controlled trial of those products cited here; humectant hydration is far better evidenced for [[hyaluronic-acid]] and [[glycerin]].

## Common Marketing Claims

This section is quarantined and discounted; treat everything here as unproven or overstated unless it appears in The Evidence above.

- **"Repairs and regenerates skin."** The regeneration language traces to cell-culture experiments in which SCA increased fibroblast and keratinocyte proliferation and migration.[^brieva][^cruz] No controlled human wound-healing or "repair" trial is cited, so this is a mechanistic claim, not a demonstrated clinical outcome.
- **"92% (or 96%) snail mucin, clinically proven."** The clinical trials cited here tested the SCA *Cryptomphalus aspersa* extract at 8-to-40% in specific formulations, not a 92-to-96% snail-mucin essence.[^fabi] A high mucin percentage on a label is not the material that the trials studied.
- **"Anti-aging."** The only randomized human trial measured a cosmetic wrinkle surrogate over 12 weeks and improved it at P=.03 in 25 patients.[^fabi] That is a modest cosmetic signal, not evidence of the collagen-level anti-aging effect established for [[tretinoin]]; general anti-aging positioning overstates it.
- **"Deeply hydrating / glass skin."** A humectant effect is plausible from the mucin's water-binding character, but no controlled hydration trial of a snail-mucin essence is cited; the water-binding claim is far better evidenced for [[hyaluronic-acid]] and [[glycerin]].
