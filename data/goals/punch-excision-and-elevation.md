Punch excision and punch elevation are small surgical techniques, named among the surgical options for atrophic acne scars alongside needling and subcision.[^narrative] They are reached for when a scar is narrow and deep, and the honest summary is that the technique is better established than its evidence. Neither source read for this page ties either technique to a particular scar subtype, so no such targeting is asserted here; the subtypes themselves are on [[atrophic-acne-scar-subtypes]] and the procedures with their own evidence here are on [[acne-scar-procedures-by-evidence]].

## The Evidence

A 2026 review of punch-based techniques across dermatologic surgery is the most direct source read for this page, and its verdict on acne scars is carefully hedged: punch elevation, "as an adjunctive technique, may improve outcomes in selected atrophic acne scars."[^punch] Adjunctive, may, selected. The same review says that scar applications of punch techniques are heterogeneous and that their efficacy "often depends on disease stability, combination treatment strategies, and operator experience", and that across all its indications "most evidence derives from small observational studies or case series, and long-term controlled data remain limited."[^punch]

That review is worth reading for its internal contrast. For follicular unit extraction in androgenetic alopecia it reports "generally predictable and reproducible outcomes"; for scar improvement it reports no such thing. The same family of instruments carries very different evidence depending on what it is pointed at.

**One comment does not transfer.** The review notes that in benign cutaneous and subcutaneous lesions, "punch excision offers favorable cosmetic advantages due to its limited incision length."[^punch] That is a statement about removing lesions, not about acne scars, and neither source read for this page makes an efficacy claim for punch excision in acne scarring.

A 2024 narrative review of atrophic post-acne scar treatment lists punch excision and punch elevation among the surgical methods alongside needling and subcision, and reaches two conclusions that bear directly on choosing any of them: "There are limited randomized controlled trials that aimed to determine which treatment options should be considered the gold standard", and "Combining interventions would likely produce more benefit compared to the implementation of a single method."[^narrative] Neither review's abstract mentions icepick or boxcar scars by name, which was checked directly. Nothing on this site establishes a subtype-specific indication for either technique either, so a reader should treat the narrow-deep-scar rationale as clinical convention rather than as a finding.

## What this means in practice

The procedure most often paired with these on the same face, [[subcision]], does have its own page and its own evidence here. So do the resurfacing options, [[fractional-co2-laser-resurfacing]] and [[erbium-yag-fractional-laser-resurfacing]], and the volumising route, [[dermal-fillers-for-acne-scars]]. If the 2024 review is right that combinations outperform single methods, those pages are the ones carrying the comparative numbers; this one carries a technique whose evidence has not caught up with its reputation.
