Standard surgical excision is the operation most people with a skin cancer actually get, and it is the thing every newer technique is measured against. **Bottom line:** in the one randomised trial with ten-year follow-up, excision of high-risk facial basal cell carcinoma recurred more often than [[mohs-micrographic-surgery]], and the difference was statistically clear only when the cancer had already come back once. The cancers themselves are at [[basal-cell-carcinoma]] and [[keratinocyte-carcinoma]].

> **Standard does not mean best-studied.** The same trial's own background says it plainly: "Surgical excision (SE) is considered standard treatment, though randomised trials with long-term follow-up are rare."[^trial] The default treatment for the most common cancer in people with light skin rests on a thinner randomised base than its status suggests.

## The Evidence

**The excision arm's own numbers.** In 408 high-risk facial primary BCCs, the 10-year cumulative probability of recurrence after excision was **12.2%**, against 4.4% after Mohs, at p=0.100.[^trial] In 204 facial *recurrent* BCCs it was **13.5%** after excision against 3.9%, at p=0.023.[^trial] Read as absolutes rather than as a comparison: roughly one in eight of these high-risk facial lesions came back within ten years after standard excision, in both groups.

**Who those numbers describe, and who they do not.** The trial enrolled facial lesions meeting a specific high-risk definition, "diameter at least 1cm, H-zone location or aggressive histological subtype".[^trial] A small, low-risk BCC on a shoulder is not that population, and nothing here estimates its recurrence rate. The head-to-head argument, including why the primary-BCC difference is weaker than the headline implies, belongs to [[mohs-micrographic-surgery]] and is not repeated here.

**The finding that outlives the comparison.** "A substantial proportion of recurrences occurred after more than 5years post-treatment: 56% for pBCC and 14% for rBCC."[^trial] More than half the primary-BCC recurrences appeared after year five, whichever operation was used. Any five-year clearance figure quoted for excision is therefore a milestone and not a final answer, and the authors say so, stressing "the need for long-term follow-up".[^trial]

**Why this page carries no ranking.** A tier list of two operations would have to pick one, and this evidence does not support a single ordering: Mohs separated from excision statistically in recurrent facial BCC and did not in primary facial BCC, from the same trial on the same day. The ranking that can honestly be built is per-population, not overall, and it needs more than one trial.
