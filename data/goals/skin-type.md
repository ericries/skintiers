Skin type is the first question most people ask and the one the industry answers most confidently. It is a sorting heuristic for choosing products, not a measured property of your skin, and the dermatologist whose test is reproduced below says so in the same breath as giving it.

> **Useful for picking a cleanser. Not a diagnosis, and not fixed.** The dry, combination and oily labels decide how rich a moisturizer you want and how often you can tolerate an active. They do not describe a stable biological category you belong to, and nothing on a shelf can test you for one. If your actual problem is redness, breakouts, flaking or stinging, those have their own pages and are more useful to you than a type.

## The test, and what its author says about it

Board-certified dermatologist Fatima Fahs gives a version most people can run tomorrow morning: wash your face, wait half an hour, and notice what it does on its own.[^fahs] Skin that feels "really tight and dry and itchy" puts you on the dry side; skin that feels normal with "a little bit of oiliness still in the center" is the combination pattern; skin "getting a little oily or blotchy again soon after washing" is the oily one.[^fahs]

She then says the part the industry leaves out. "Now, these are not specific scientific types, but this does help us help you find products that are suitable for your skin type, or at least adjust the frequency of how often you use certain products so that you don't end up with irritation."[^fahs] That is the honest frame for the whole idea: the categories earn their keep by setting richness and frequency, not by telling you what your skin is.

## What the label should change

- **How rich your moisturizer is, and how often you reapply.** This is the decision the type actually informs. The evidence for the ingredients that do the work is on [[moisturizing]], [[ceramides]] and [[petrolatum]].
- **How fast you introduce an active.** Dry and easily irritated skin tolerates a slower ramp. That is a frequency decision, not a different product list.
- **Very little else.** A type does not tell you whether to use sunscreen, a retinoid or vitamin C. Those are decided by what you are trying to change, not by how your cheeks feel at 8am.

## Where to go instead

The type labels are a starting point that most readers outgrow within one page. If you already know what bothers you, go straight to it:

- [[oily-skin]] and [[dry-skin]] for the two ends of this scale as conditions, with what actually helps each
- [[sensitive-skin]] if the issue is stinging or reacting, which is a different axis from oiliness and is frequently confused with it
- [[acne]] if the real question is breakouts, which is not the same thing as oily skin
- [[skin-barrier-repair]] if skin has become tight, flaky or reactive recently, since that is usually something that happened rather than a type you are

There is no combination-skin page here yet. The pattern is real enough as a description, but it sits between the two pages above rather than needing a third.
