Pore size is largely fixed by genetics, sex hormones, and age; DermNet states plainly that "treatments that focus on preventing and shrinking large pores are not very effective."[^1] What you can actually change is how full and visible pores look: less oil, less debris, and better surface texture make the same follicle openings read as smaller. Nothing sold over the counter closes a pore.

## Why pores look the way they do

A pore is just the visible opening of a hair follicle and its attached oil (sebaceous) gland.[^1] DermNet lists the drivers of enlarged pores as increased sebum production, larger hair follicle size, comedogenic product use, loss of skin elasticity with age, and sun damage.[^1] Pores also appear larger in [[oily-skin]], since excess oil and trapped debris stretch the follicle opening and catch light differently than a flat, matte surface. Ethnic background and age both affect baseline pore size, and in women pore size fluctuates across the menstrual cycle, peaking around ovulation.[^1]

## What doesn't work, and what might

DermNet's own review of the evidence is skeptical: the topical options it lists for pores (avoiding comedogenic creams, weight loss, chemical peels, topical nicotinamide, plant-derived copper chlorophyllin, L-carnitine, and topical retinoids) are all flagged as "not very effective" at preventing or shrinking pores, and the more reliable results come from physical treatments aimed at the sebaceous glands, like lasers and radiofrequency microneedling, which are procedures rather than skincare.[^1] Oral options such as combined oral contraceptives, spironolactone, and isotretinoin are used for related acne and oil control but are prescription medical treatments, not routine skincare.[^1] This is an educational summary, not medical advice; prescription medications and in-office procedures are decided with a qualified clinician.

Given that, the treatments below aren't pore-shrinkers. They work by controlling the things that make pores look worse: excess oil, clogged follicles, and rough, sun-damaged texture.

## The Evidence

DermNet: "Enlarged pores are depressions in the facial skin surface that contain one or more openings to the ducts carrying sweat and oil from their respective eccrine glands and sebaceous glands."[^1] On causes: "Factors that may lead to enlarged pores include: Increased sebum production, Hair follicle size, Use of comedogenic products, Loss of skin elasticity with age, Sun damage."[^1] On treatment: "Treatments that focus on preventing and shrinking large pores are not very effective."[^1]

A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of topical 2% niacinamide measured sebum output directly rather than pore size: in 100 Japanese subjects, "the 2% niacinamide treated group demonstrated significantly lowered SER [sebum excretion rate] after 2 and 4 weeks of application," while in a separate 30-person Caucasian split-face arm, "the CSL [casual sebum level] was significantly reduced, but the SER was not significantly reduced."[^2] That is evidence for less oil on the skin, an honest proxy for pore appearance, not a pore-size measurement itself.
