Subcision is an in-office procedure for depressed acne scarring: a needle or cannula is passed under the skin to cut the fibrous tethers that pull a rolling scar downward. The scars themselves are on [[acne-scars]] and the disease behind them on [[acne]].

> **The procedure is widely used and the evidence for it alone is surprisingly thin.** The two most relevant recent trials are a split-face study in **five patients** against an untreated side, which concluded only that multiple subcisions "may improve the appearance of rolling acne scars", and a 27-patient split-face trial in which **both sides received subcision**, so it cannot separate the procedure from the passage of time.[^multiple][^suction] Where subcision looks strongest is in combination: a 2026 network meta-analysis found "Fractional CO2 laser combined with subcision or PRP showed the most consistent benefits".[^combo] Treat it as a reasonable component of a clinician's plan rather than a proven stand-alone fix.

## The Evidence

**The one trial with an untreated comparator is very small.** A split-face, evaluator-blinded study at Northwestern enrolled "Five patients with rolling acne scars on both sides of their face", randomising one side to five sequential subcisions four weeks apart with follow-up at weeks 20 and 36, while "the contralateral side of the face received no treatment".[^multiple] It reported "a greater decrease in global scarring scores in the multiple subcision side compared to the control side" and a greater change in appearance scores at week 36.[^multiple] Two caveats matter more than the direction. The abstract reports **no p-values at all**, and its headline satisfaction figure is that "50% of patients reported being more satisfied with the treatment side compared to the control side" which, at n=5, is two or three people, and means roughly half were not more satisfied even against no treatment.[^multiple] The authors' own conclusion is hedged to "may improve".[^multiple]

**The larger trial asks a different question and loses it on objective measures.** A randomized, split-face, patient-blinded trial in 27 patients with bilateral atrophic scars gave **both** sides cannula subcision, then added 10 suction sessions over two weeks to one side against sham pressure on the other.[^suction] Both sides improved significantly over time (p < .05), but "No significant differences were detected between sides for objective outcomes", while physician- and patient-rated improvement scales favoured the suction side (p = .002 and p = .026).[^suction] Two readings follow: suction is not established as an objective add-on, and because neither side went untreated, this trial cannot tell you how much of the improvement was subcision rather than healing time.

**Combination is where it earns its place.** The 2026 network meta-analysis of laser, microneedling and combination therapies for moderate-to-severe atrophic scars found that "Combination therapies generally outperformed monotherapies" and singled out fractional CO2 laser with subcision or PRP.[^combo] Its own caution applies: treatment choice "should consider scar type, Fitzpatrick skin phototype, downtime, pain, adverse-effect risk, and patient preference", and it called for larger long-term randomized trials.[^combo] The needling comparison and its own limits are on [[microneedling]].

**Scar type decides whether it applies at all.** Subcision targets tethered, rolling depressions specifically, which is why the trials above recruit rolling or atrophic scars rather than acne scarring in general. Which type you have is a clinical judgement, set out on [[acne-scars]].

**No tier list on this page.** The same problem as [[microneedling]]: the real comparators are lasers, PRP and fillers, none of which has a graded page here, so there is nothing to rank against. The other procedure page on this site is [[laser-hair-removal]], a different goal entirely.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. Subcision is a surgical procedure performed under local anaesthetic, with bruising expected, and it is not something to attempt at home.
