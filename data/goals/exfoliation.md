Three different things get sold under one word. Removing dead cells from the surface, scrubbing them off, and speeding up how fast new cells are made are not the same mechanism, and conflating them is how people end up using a scrub, an acid and a retinoid at once and wondering why their skin is raw.

> **The distinction in one line each.** Chemical exfoliation loosens the bonds holding dead cells together so they shed. Physical exfoliation abrades them off. Neither is cell turnover, which is the rate at which the skin makes and matures new cells from below, and which is what a retinoid changes. An acid works on the layer that is already dead; a retinoid works on the living layer underneath.

## What chemical exfoliation actually does

Honest starting point: the mechanism is not fully settled. A review of AHA-based procedures states that "Although their exact mechanism of action is unknown, it has been demonstrated that AHAs improve these disorders by thinning the stratum corneum, promoting epidermolysis, dispersing basal layer melanin, and increasing collagen synthesis within the dermis".[^aha] Note what that sentence contains beyond exfoliation: pigment dispersal and dermal collagen, which is why acids are used for more than texture.

Which acid and at what strength is a separate question, and [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]] owns it. The individual cases are on [[glycolic-acid]], [[lactic-acid]], [[salicylic-acid]] and [[mandelic-acid]], and [[urea]] is the keratolytic that belongs in the same conversation without being marketed as an exfoliant.

## Why a retinoid is not an exfoliant

Retinoids act on the living epidermis, changing how keratinocytes proliferate and differentiate, which is a different intervention from dissolving the cement between cells that are already dead. The flaking people get when starting one is a side effect of that change, not the mechanism doing its job. Mechanism and evidence are on [[retinoids]] and [[tretinoin]], and the practical consequence is the useful part: stacking an acid on top of a retinoid adds irritation without adding a second mechanism to the same layer.

## The Evidence

**Chemical exfoliation is the half with the literature.** The acids have trials behind them for texture, pigment and acne, graded individually on the ingredient pages above rather than restated here.

**Physical exfoliation is not graded on this page, and that is deliberate.** A search of PubMed on 2026-10-26 found a small literature on mechanical exfoliation and a larger one on microdermabrasion, which is a professional procedure rather than a home scrub, and none of it was read for this page. So nothing here supports or dismisses scrubs; the gap is recorded rather than filled. What can be said without any trial is mechanical: a scrub's effect depends on particle shape, pressure and how long you do it, three variables the user controls and the label does not.

**The practical rule that follows from the distinction.** Pick one mechanism at a time and change one thing at a time. If skin is already irritated, the answer is not a gentler exfoliant but fewer of them, which is the case [[skin-barrier-repair]] makes.
