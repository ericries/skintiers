Microneedling creates controlled micro-injuries in skin to provoke a repair response, and its best-studied use is atrophic acne scarring. This page is about the procedure; the scars themselves are on [[acne-scars]] and the disease that causes them on [[acne]].

> **The split that matters is clinic versus bathroom, and the evidence lands very differently on each.** In the clinic, needling genuinely improves scars, but a 2026 review found ablative fractional lasers "may offer greater efficacy, whereas needling-based therapies may provide better tolerability and pigment safety".[^needlelaser] At home it is the one device category a GRADE-assessed review singled out negatively: home microneedling devices "were supported by low-quality evidence (Level D) and associated with infectious complications", while home fractional lasers reached Level A and LED and radiofrequency reached Level B.[^home] So the same technique is a reasonable in-office option and the weakest of the at-home choices.

## The Evidence

**In-office, for acne scars: it works, and it is probably not the most effective option.** A 2026 systematic review and exploratory meta-analysis comparing needling-based therapies against ablative fractional lasers concluded that "both modalities can improve scars", with the laser possibly more effective and needling possibly better tolerated and safer for pigment.[^needlelaser] The authors immediately qualify it: these conclusions "should be interpreted cautiously because of clinical heterogeneity and the sensitivity of the exploratory pooled estimate to individual studies".[^needlelaser]

**The pigment-safety point is the real reason to consider it.** Better "pigment safety" matters most in deeper skin tones, where energy-based resurfacing carries more risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation; the pigment side of that is on [[hyperpigmentation]]. That is a tolerability argument rather than an efficacy one, and this page does not have a trial that quantifies it.

**Nobody can yet rank the options honestly.** A 2026 network meta-analysis of minimally invasive interventions for atrophic acne scars reported that "the network is too sparse and uncertain to support a dependable treatment hierarchy or any definitive between-treatment claim", grading its own evidence certainty as "Low to very low across network comparisons".[^network] A separate 2026 network meta-analysis found combination approaches generally beat monotherapy, with "Fractional CO2 laser combined with subcision or PRP" showing the most consistent benefit, and still called for larger long-term randomized trials with standardized outcomes.[^combo] Read together: microneedling is a legitimate tool inside a combination plan chosen by a clinician, not a ranked winner.

**At home, the evidence is the weakest of any home device reviewed.** The GRADE-assessed scoping review above placed home microneedling at Level D with infectious complications, in a paper that was otherwise willing to recommend several home modalities.[^home] An at-home roller or stamp also cannot reproduce clinic conditions: depth control, sterile single-use needles, and a clinician deciding whether your scar type suits needling at all.

**No tier list on this page.** The comparison that matters here is microneedling against lasers, subcision and PRP, none of which has a graded page on this site yet, so there is nothing to rank against. The one procedure page that does exist is [[laser-hair-removal]], which is a different goal entirely.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. Microneedling breaks the skin barrier deliberately, so infection risk is real rather than theoretical, and scar treatment should follow a diagnosis of which scar type you have.
