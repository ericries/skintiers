Double cleansing is a two-step method of first using an oil-based cleanser or balm to dissolve makeup, sunscreen, and sebum, then following with a water-based cleanser to wash off the residue and everyday grime. The evidence-based bottom line is narrow: it is genuinely useful on days you wear water-resistant sunscreen, and unnecessary as a universal daily ritual.

## What the evidence says

The one controlled test that isolates the oil step is a small study that measured how much sunscreen was left on the face after washing with water only, a foaming cleanser, or a cleansing oil.[^chen] For a non-waterproof sunscreen, both the foaming cleanser and the cleansing oil cleaned it off well (15.6% and 13.4% residue, versus 54.0% for water and 9.9% at clean baseline), so a single water-based cleanser was enough.[^chen] For a waterproof sunscreen, only the cleansing oil got it off (5.8% residue, statistically the same as the 3.2% clean baseline), while the foaming cleanser left 36.8% and water left 59.3%.[^chen] The oil step also caused less trouble: eight of the participants reported dry skin after the foaming cleanser versus one after the cleansing oil.[^chen]

## When it helps, and when to skip it

The takeaway is practical, not magical. If you wear water-resistant or waterproof sunscreen (the kind covered on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]), an oil cleanser or balm first genuinely removes what a water-based wash leaves behind, and the second cleanser then clears the residue. The same oil-dissolves-oil logic is commonly extended to long-wearing or waterproof makeup, though this study tested only sunscreen. On a bare-skin day, or with a non-waterproof sunscreen, a single gentle cleanse does the job, and washing more than needed risks the barrier rather than helping it (the frequency case is on [[how-often-to-wash-your-face]] and the over-cleansing case on [[skin-barrier-strengthening]]).

## Choosing cleansers

The first-step oil cleanser or balm should rinse clean; the second, water-based step is where the gentle-cleanser evidence applies, so see the [[best-gentle-cleansers-sensitive-skin]] and [[best-cleansers-for-oily-acne-prone-skin]] lists for picks.
