An in-office procedure that drives insulated needles into the dermis and delivers radiofrequency energy at the tip. It sits between the two things this site already covers, plain [[microneedling]] and [[fractional-co2-laser-resurfacing]], and the interesting question is whether it keeps the efficacy of the laser without the pigment risk. The downtime-and-pain comparison across all the options is on [[acne-scar-treatments-by-downtime-and-pain]].

> **On atrophic acne scars it looks equivalent to laser, with far less pigmentary trouble.** A 2023 meta-analysis of eight randomised trials found fractional radiofrequency "as effective as the laser group in treating atrophic acne scars" on both observer and patient assessment, with risk ratios of 0.92 (95% CI 0.78 to 1.08) and 1.15 (0.99 to 1.34), neither significant in either direction.[^meta] In the same pooled analysis post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation was far less common after radiofrequency, RR 0.12 (95% CI 0.04 to 0.35), and erythema lasted a shorter time, SMD -0.78 (-1.37 to -0.18).[^meta] Equivalent result, much lower pigment risk, is a different trade from the one most of this category offers.

## Why this is not the same claim as plain microneedling

A 2026 systematic review of needling therapies against ablative fractional lasers, 27 studies and 1,137 participants, found that for atrophic acne scars the lasers "suggested greater improvement than microneedling", while needling had "shorter downtime, better tolerability, and fewer pigmentary adverse effects".[^review] That is the familiar trade. What the same review says about the radiofrequency version is different: "Microneedling radiofrequency appeared to provide efficacy closer to that of ablative lasers in several individual studies and demonstrated a more favorable safety profile."[^review] Note what that sentence is and is not. It is a narrative observation about several individual studies, carrying no pooled number of its own; the review synthesised narratively because of heterogeneity and labels its own pooled work exploratory.[^review] The pooled numbers in the standfirst come from the separate 2023 meta-analysis, not from this review.

## The Evidence

**The head-to-head trial reads the same way.** A 2026 prospective, randomised, evaluator-blinded split-face trial put radiofrequency microneedling on one side of the face and fractional CO2 on the other in 33 patients, 30 of whom completed, across three sessions eight weeks apart.[^splitface] Mean ECCA scar scores fell from 54.73 to 30.53 with radiofrequency and from 53.67 to 31.03 with the laser, "without a significant between-treatment difference", and physician-rated improvement was comparable.[^splitface] The radiofrequency side had "shorter erythema, pain, swelling, and scab-detachment durations and fewer post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation events".[^splitface] The authors call the trial exploratory and the sample is small, so this is one well-designed study rather than a settled answer.

**Equivalent is the finding, not superior.** Both confidence intervals in the pooled scar comparison cross 1, so the honest reading is that neither modality was shown to beat the other on atrophic scars, not that radiofrequency won.[^meta] The one comparison that did favour radiofrequency on efficacy was patient-evaluated improvement of ACNE rather than scars, RR 1.35 (95% CI 1.01 to 1.80), and the meta-analysis concludes it "could be a better choice for atrophic acne scar patients with active acne".[^meta] That is a narrower recommendation than the headline suggests.

**Pain and crusting were not better.** In the same pooled analysis there was no difference in pain level or crusting time, SMD 0.20 (95% CI -0.72 to 1.12) and SMD -0.93 (-2.38 to 0.52), both intervals crossing zero.[^meta] So the tolerability advantage is specifically about pigment and redness duration, not about comfort during the procedure.

**Give it twelve weeks.** The meta-analysis reports that subgroup analysis showed "at least a 12-week follow-up was required to observe the full effects of FRF".[^meta] Judging a course earlier than that is judging it early.

**Who the pigment finding matters most for.** A twelve-fold lower relative risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation is most consequential in the skin types where PIH after resurfacing is the main deterrent, which is the concern [[hyperpigmentation]] and [[post-inflammatory-erythema]] set out. Neither source read here reports results broken down by Fitzpatrick type, so this page does not claim one.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. This is a clinic procedure performed with needles entering the dermis, not a home device; the home radiofrequency category and what its own FDA files contain is a separate subject, covered on [[home-rf-devices-by-whose-evidence-the-file-rests-on]]. Scar treatment choice depends on scar type, skin type and what downtime you can accept, and belongs in a conversation with a clinician who has seen your skin.
