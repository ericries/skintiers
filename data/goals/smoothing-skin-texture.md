"Texture" is the vaguest word in skincare marketing, which is why it is worth pinning down before spending anything. Here it means the surface feel and evenness of skin: roughness, flakiness, small bumps, a dull or uneven finish. It is not the same problem as the four things it gets sold alongside, and each of those has its own page.

> **Most of what smooths texture is already ranked elsewhere on this site, by category. This page ranks across them.** If you want acids ordered by strength, that is [[exfoliating-acids-by-strength]]. If you want retinoids ordered by potency, that is [[retinoids-by-potency-ranked]]. What neither answers is which CATEGORY to reach for first, which is what the list below is for. The short version: a retinoid has the strongest evidence of anything here, and a keratolytic is the faster fix for roughness you can feel.

## What You Probably Mean

Five neighbouring problems get called texture, and picking the wrong hub wastes months.

- **Rough, flaky, or bumpy to the touch.** This page. If the bumps are specifically on upper arms or thighs, go to [[keratosis-pilaris]].
- **Visible pores.** A different mechanism and a different evidence base, on [[enlarged-pores]], with the blackhead case on [[blackheads-clogged-pores]].
- **Indentations left by acne.** Scarring, which topicals barely touch, on [[acne-scars]].
- **Lines and laxity.** Photoaging, on [[anti-aging]].
- **One patch that is thick, scaly, and stays put.** Not this page, and not a reason to exfoliate harder. A fixed localised plaque can be [[psoriasis]], a patch of chronically rubbed and thickened [[atopic-dermatitis|eczema]], or ordinary localised [[dry-skin]]. A rough scaly spot on sun-exposed skin that will not clear needs [[actinic-keratosis]] ruled out by a clinician first, because that one is a precursor lesion rather than a cosmetic complaint. Calluses, corns, warts and seborrhoeic keratoses are the other common causes of thickened skin and have no pages here yet.

## The Evidence: How to Read the List

The tier on each row is that ingredient's own evidence grade from its page, not a texture-specific regrade, and the note says what it does to the skin surface. Two consequences worth stating. A high grade earned mostly in another indication does not transfer automatically, and the notes flag where that applies. And the strongest-graded option is not automatically the one to start with, because the retinoids work by changing cell turnover, which is inherently slow, while a keratolytic acts directly on the dead surface layer.

**Irritation is the real constraint.** Everything in the top half of this list works by increasing cell turnover or dissolving the bonds between surface cells, and all of it can leave skin rough if overused, which is the failure mode that sends people back to the shop. The barrier case is on [[skin-barrier-repair]], and it is the one page here that matters more than picking the right active.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. Persistent rough patches that do not respond to any of this, or that are scaly and fixed in one spot, are worth showing to a dermatologist rather than exfoliating harder.

Two framing statements above are mechanistic rather than quantitative and are deliberately kept that way. That keratolytics act on the dead surface layer while retinoids act by changing cell turnover is a statement about mechanism, not about how fast either works, and no timeline is claimed here. Where an ingredient's grade was earned in a different indication, the note on its row says so rather than letting the grade imply texture evidence it does not have.
