Mohs is the skin-cancer operation in which the surgeon removes tissue and reads the margins under a microscope during the procedure, taking more only where cancer remains. **Bottom line:** one randomized trial with ten-year follow-up is the best evidence for it, and read closely it supports Mohs most clearly for *recurrent* facial basal cell carcinoma. For high-risk *primary* facial BCC the same trial's difference did not reach statistical significance. The cancers themselves are covered at [[basal-cell-carcinoma]] and [[keratinocyte-carcinoma]].

> **The headline and the p-values point to different conclusions.** In 408 high-risk facial primary BCCs, "the 10-year cumulative probabilities of recurrence were 4.4% after MMS and 12.2% after SE (Log-rank test chi(2) 2.704, p=0.100)".[^trial] Nearly a threefold numerical difference, and it did not clear significance. In 204 *recurrent* facial BCCs the figures were "3.9% and 13.5% for MMS and SE, respectively (Log-rank chi(2) 5.166, p=0.023)", which did.[^trial] Anyone told flatly that Mohs has the lowest recurrence rate is hearing the numerator without the p-value.

## The Evidence

**What the trial actually was.** 408 facial high-risk primary BCCs and 204 facial recurrent BCCs were randomly allocated to surgical excision or Mohs between October 1999 and February 2002, analysed by modified intention to treat, with recurrence as the primary outcome.[^trial] High risk was defined in the trial as "diameter at least 1cm, H-zone location or aggressive histological subtype".[^trial] That is a specific population: faces, high-risk lesions, and in the second arm cancers that had already come back once.

**The authors' own interpretation, and where it runs ahead of the data.** They conclude "Fewer recurrences occurred after treatment of high risk facial BCC with MMS compared to treatment with SE."[^trial] As a direction that is fair. As a claim about high-risk primary BCC specifically it is carried by a comparison the same paper reports at p=0.100. The honest reading is that Mohs looked better in both groups and was statistically separable from excision only in the recurrent group.

**The most useful number here has nothing to do with Mohs.** "A substantial proportion of recurrences occurred after more than 5years post-treatment: 56% for pBCC and 14% for rBCC."[^trial] More than half the primary-BCC recurrences showed up after year five. Every five-year cure rate quoted for any skin-cancer treatment is therefore an underestimate of lifetime recurrence, and the authors say the finding stresses "the need for long-term follow-up".[^trial] A patient told they are five years clear has had a useful milestone, not an all-clear.

**Who ran it.** The trial came out of Maastricht University Medical Centre, and two of the ten authors list affiliations with dedicated Mohs practices, the MohsA Expertise Centre for Mohs Micrographic Surgery and Mohsklinieken.[^trial] That is recorded here because this site records it consistently, and in this instance it cuts the other way: a trial run partly by Mohs specialists reported a non-significant result for the larger of its two groups, which is not what an interested party would engineer.

**What this page does not tell you.** The trial compared Mohs with standard surgical excision only. It says nothing about curettage, radiotherapy, or the topical options used for superficial BCC, and neither surgical excision nor those topicals have pages here yet, so there is no ranking to draw. Nothing here applies to [[melanoma]], which is a different disease with different surgery.

**No tier list sits on this page.** Grading would require pages for the comparators, which do not exist yet. A ranked comparison belongs on [[basal-cell-carcinoma]] or [[keratinocyte-carcinoma]] once those pages exist, not on the page for one of the options.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. Which operation suits a given skin cancer depends on the tumour type, its size, its location and its history, and that is a decision for a surgeon who has examined it. If you are weighing options, the finding above about late recurrence is worth raising with them directly.
