Lip care covers three different asks that get sold as one: lips that are chapped and sore, lips you want to keep comfortable, and lips you want to look fuller. Only the first two have evidence on this page, and one of them has a serious tail risk that lip-balm marketing never mentions.

> **A persistently dry, scaly lower lip in someone with a lot of sun exposure is not a balm problem.** It may be actinic cheilitis, which a 2022 review describes as "a precursor of malignancy or even an in situ squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) of the lip, located most frequently on the lower lip", with a rate of malignant transformation that "ranges from 10 to 30%".[^ac] The review calls lip SCC "one of the deadliest non-melanoma skin cancers".[^ac] If chapping on the lower lip has not resolved, that is a clinician visit, not another product.

## How to know what you have

**Chapped lips is a symptom, not a diagnosis.** A 2024 review of cheilitis lists the causes as "irritant and allergic contact dermatitis, atopic cheilitis, actinic cheilitis, infectious etiologies, nutritional deficiencies, drug-induced cheilitis", plus rarer ones.[^cheil] Note what comes first on that list. The review also says that "Distinguishing among the various etiologies of cheilitis is clinically difficult, as many causes may produce similar erythema and superficial desquamation of mucosal skin", and that patients report "dryness, redness, irritation, burning, fissuring, and itch" across many of the underlying causes.[^cheil]

**So the same look has many causes, and one of them is the product.** Irritant and allergic contact dermatitis heads the list in that review, and lip products are applied many times a day to a site people also lick. If a balm is not working, using more of it is the one response the differential does not support. The review notes the specific cause "is often difficult to diagnose, requiring extensive testing and treatment trials", which is an honest description of why patch testing exists.[^cheil]

**What actinic cheilitis looks like.** The 2022 review describes it as "dryness, scaling, atrophy, indistinct borders, and erosions", and warns that "Ulceration and the appearance of a nodule often suggest the progression to invasive SCC".[^ac] Its listed risk factors are "chronic UV exposure, increasing age, male gender, fair phototypes, chronic scarring, immunosuppressive therapy, and tobacco use".[^ac] The cancer it leads to is on [[keratinocyte-carcinoma]], and the related precursor on ordinary skin is [[actinic-keratosis]].

## The Evidence

**Sun protection is the one lip measure with a serious reason behind it.** Chronic UV exposure heads the actinic cheilitis risk list, and the condition's malignant transformation rate is the 10 to 30% quoted above.[^ac] The graded evidence for photoprotection generally is on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; what is specific here is that the lower lip is the site most often affected, and it is routinely left out of sunscreen application.

**For ordinary chapping, the ingredient evidence is borrowed.** The occlusives and humectants ranked below are graded on skin. None of the sources here tested them on the vermilion lip, so the list reflects general evidence for holding water in skin rather than lip trials.

**On plumping, there is nothing here to grade.** Neither source consulted evaluates lip-plumping cosmetics, so whether they work is an open question on the evidence gathered. The one relevant fact either source does establish is that irritant contact dermatitis is among the commonest causes of lip inflammation.[^cheil] Whether that is worth weighing against a product whose effect you can feel is a judgement for the reader.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. Persistent chapping, a lip lesion that does not heal, ulceration, or a nodule are reasons to be seen, and the 2022 review stresses that "early diagnoses and treatment are essential in preventing the development of invasive SCC".[^ac]
