Lines around the eyes, and a regulatory term worth knowing because several home devices are cleared for this area and nothing else. **Bottom line:** periorbital means around the eye, and when a device's FDA indication names it, that indication does not cover the rest of your face. Under-eye darkness and puffiness are a different target, covered at [[dark-circles]].

> **Periorbital and perioral are one letter apart and mean opposite ends of the face.** Periorbital is around the eyes. Perioral is around the mouth. Both appear in home-device clearances on this site, and reading one for the other turns a lip-area device into an eye-area one. [[revive-light-therapy-lux-lip-care]] is cleared perioral; [[dpl-nuve]] is cleared periorbital.

## What the cleared indications actually say

The clearest statement of the target comes from the only consumer fractional laser whose file this site has read, the Tria FANp: "The FANp is an over-the-counter device indicated for the treatment of periorbital wrinkles (crow's feet)."[^fanp] That parenthesis is the whole definition. That device is [[tria-fanp-age-defying-laser]] and the modality is at [[home-fractional-nonablative-laser]], which also notes that an infrared laser used near the eye carries real risk and that eye protection matters more here than for any topical.

**The eye area is where several device clearances stop.** [[dpl-nuve]] (K101382) is cleared for periorbital wrinkles rather than facial ones. Its successor [[dpl-nuve-for-wrinkles]] (K141181) widened to full-face, so within one product family the cleared region grew. At the other end of the face, [[revive-light-therapy-lux-lip-care]] covers a perioral clearance, and the perioral region turns up again in the circulation half of the [[nuface-trinity-plus-wrinkle-reducer]] indication. Each of those pages carries the filing and the quotations; none of it is restated here.

## What gets used here

The eye area attracts ingredients that are studied, when they are studied at all, specifically on it: [[caffeine]] for periorbital puffiness and darkness, plus [[coenzyme-q10]], [[palmitoyl-tetrapeptide-7]], [[palmitoyl-tripeptide-1]], [[retinyl-retinoate]] and [[vitamin-k-topical]] among the actives whose pages reference this region. Whether any of them earns its place is argued on those pages and on [[peptides]] and [[retinoids]], not here.

What this page will not do is rank them. A ranking of eye-area treatments needs evidence gathered on the eye area, and most of what exists for these ingredients was measured on the face generally and then applied here by assumption. [[anti-aging]] holds the general wrinkle evidence.

> **Bottom line.** A narrow target with a specific name. If a device says periorbital, that is the only area its clearance covers, and if it says perioral it is not an eye product at all.

## The Evidence

There is no body of evidence specific to this page, and saying so is more useful than assembling one by inference. What exists splits three ways and each part lives elsewhere.

The device side is the only place where the eye area is named as the tested target rather than assumed, because FDA indications are written per region: see [[home-fractional-nonablative-laser]] for the one cleared OTC fractional laser and [[dpl-nuve]] for the periorbital LED clearance. Those are statements about cleared scope, not about effect size, and [[home-device-wrinkle-claims-by-cleared-scope]] exists precisely to keep those two apart.

The ingredient side is mostly face-wide evidence applied here by assumption, which is argued on each active's own page. The procedural side, including injectables for crow's feet specifically, is outside what this site currently covers.
