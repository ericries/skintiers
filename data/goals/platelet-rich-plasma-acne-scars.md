Platelet-rich plasma is the patient's own blood, spun in a centrifuge to concentrate the platelets, then applied to the skin or injected into it during another procedure. For acne scarring it is almost never used alone. The scars themselves are on [[acne-scars]] and the disease behind them on [[acne]].

> **PRP is an add-on that reliably improves the procedure it is added to, and never the procedure itself.** In a 2025 Bayesian network meta-analysis of 68 randomised trials and 4,480 patients, "Laser + platelet-rich plasma (PRP) ranked best in reducing ECCA scores" with a SUCRA of 98.4%.[^nma] That is the strongest single number on this page, and two things immediately qualify it. The same analysis ranked **a different combination best on the other scar scale**, laser plus filler on the Goodman and Baron Scale, and **a third best on satisfaction**, laser plus chemical peels.[^nma] And when a separate meta-analysis centred the comparison on [[microneedling]] rather than laser, microneedling plus chemical peels beat microneedling plus PRP.[^mn] Which add-on is "best" moves with the base procedure and with the scale you measure.

## The Evidence

**The strongest result, stated precisely.** The 2025 network meta-analysis searched four databases to September 2024, enrolled "A total of 68 randomized controlled trials ... comprising 4,480 patients with acne scarring", and was registered with PROSPERO.[^nma] Its headline is that "Laser + platelet-rich plasma (PRP) ranked best in reducing ECCA scores (surface under cumulative ranking curve to the total area (SUCRA): 98.4%), laser + filler injection ranked best in reducing GBS (SUCRA: 72.1%), and laser + chemical peels ranked the best in patient satisfaction (SUCRA: 89.6%)".[^nma] Read all three clauses together rather than only the first. **Three different winners on three outcomes** is what a SUCRA ranking looks like when the underlying comparisons are close, and the authors' own conclusion is the broader one, that "Laser combined with other therapies should be considered to optimize treatment of acne scarring".[^nma]

**The PRP-specific review is small and heterogeneous.** A 2026 systematic review searched to 1 September 2025 for studies combining ablative fractional CO2 laser with autologous PRP, and found "Seventeen studies (n = 420) met inclusion criteria", of which "Nine (53%) were randomised studies, six (29%) non-randomised, and two (12%) retrospective".[^prp] It reports that "AFCL+PRP showed superior efficacy to AFCL alone, with significant improvements in scar texture, pigmentation, erythema, and depth", and that "Patient satisfaction was consistently higher with combination therapy, and topical PRP notably reduced downtime".[^prp] Its own limitation is the one that matters: "Heterogeneity in laser parameters, PRP preparation, and outcome measures limits direct comparison. Robust multicentre randomised trials are needed".[^prp] Four hundred and twenty patients across seventeen mixed-design studies is a thin base for a procedure sold at this price.

**PRP is not standardised, which is why the trials are hard to pool.** In that review PRP "was predominantly prepared via double-spin centrifugation and delivered topically (47%) or intradermally (59%)", and the laser settings ranged from 10 to 30 W.[^prp] Two clinics can both offer "laser with PRP" and deliver materially different treatments. There is no single product here to grade, which is the structural difference between this page and an ingredient page.

**Where PRP loses.** The microneedling-centred meta-analysis pooled 24 studies and 1,546 participants and found that "MN combined with chemical peels (CP) exhibited the best results in terms of degree of improvement, patient satisfaction, and treatment efficacy compared to other treatments examined".[^mn] The comparators it beat included microneedling with PRP, and PRP alone. The same analysis found that "The results for MN combined with additional treatments were obviously better than for MN alone", which is the honest general lesson: combining helps, and PRP is one of several things worth combining rather than the one that must be chosen.

**Nothing here reduces the risk of redness or dark marks.** The network meta-analysis found "no strong evidence suggesting a treatment reduced the risk of erythema nor post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation compared to the other treatments", and the microneedling analysis agreed that "Side effects such as erythema, pain, and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation showed no significant differences across all treatments assessed".[^nma][^mn] Two independent analyses reaching the same null is worth more than either alone. If post-procedure pigment is your concern, the choice of add-on is not the lever; [[post-inflammatory-erythema]] and [[hyperpigmentation]] cover what is.

**The safety argument for PRP is what it is made of.** It is autologous, meaning it comes from the patient's own blood, which removes the questions of allergy and rejection that a foreign filler raises. Neither review reports a safety signal specific to PRP. That is a genuine point in its favour and it is separate from efficacy.

**No tier list on this page.** The same gap as [[microneedling]] and [[subcision]]: the comparators that actually matter here are ablative fractional CO2 laser, fillers and chemical peels as procedures, and none has a graded page on this site yet. Until those pages exist there is nothing to rank against.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. PRP for scarring involves drawing blood and injecting or applying it during a procedure performed by a practitioner, and the evidence above is for it as an addition to laser or needling, not as a treatment on its own.
