For most people, a short routine done consistently beats a long one done inconsistently, and the evidence backs only a handful of steps as essential. The rest, the serums, the ten-step layering, the weekly *treatments*, is optional at best and irritating at worst. The small evidence-essential core is below, with the few additions worth making only if a specific goal calls for them.

> **Bottom line:** a gentle cleanser, a moisturizer if your skin needs one, and daily sunscreen cover the morning. At night, the same cleanse and moisturize, plus one active worth adding on purpose: a retinoid. Everything else is optional.

## The Evidence-Backed Core

**Morning:** a gentle cleanser (or just water if skin is not visibly oily or dirty), a moisturizer if skin feels dry or tight, and a daily broad-spectrum [[sunscreen-uv-filters|sunscreen]], the single most evidence-backed step in any routine.

**Night:** the same gentle cleanse and moisturizer, plus the one optional active with the deepest evidence base for both anti-aging and acne, a [[retinoids|retinoid]]. Retinoids are typically started slowly, a few nights a week at first, to let skin adjust; the gradual-introduction guidance is covered on [[retinoids]].

**Add only if a specific goal calls for it:** a [[vitamin-c]] antioxidant serum in the morning, or a targeted active pulled from the [[anti-aging]], [[acne]], or [[hyperpigmentation]] pages. None of these are part of the baseline; they are additions for a specific concern, layered onto the core above rather than replacing it.

## Less Is More

More products is not automatically better, and a crowded routine has real costs. Stacking actives without a plan raises the odds of irritation, and a compromised [[skin-barrier-repair|skin barrier]] undoes whatever the actives were supposed to accomplish. The American Academy of Dermatology's own tips for healthier skin warn against over-scrubbing even when skin looks like it needs it: "If you've been sweating heavily or have a serious acne flare, it may seem natural to scrub your skin. Don't!"[^aad] The standard dermatologist approach is to add one active at a time and watch for irritation before adding another, and the two additions with the strongest evidence behind them are a sunscreen and a retinoid rather than a full shelf of serums.
