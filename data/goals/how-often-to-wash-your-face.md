The evidence-based answer is twice a day, morning and night, plus after heavy sweating. The American Academy of Dermatology advises to "limit washing to twice a day and after sweating," washing "once in the morning and once at night, as well as after sweating heavily."[^aad] The bigger risk is usually the method, not the number of washes: scrubbing and harsh, high-pH cleansers strip the [[skin-barrier-strengthening|skin barrier]] and can make skin worse, not cleaner. As the trial evidence below shows, washing frequency itself was not the main driver of harm.

## What the evidence says

The common twice-daily recommendation has surprisingly little trial evidence behind it, but the one controlled trial that tested washing frequency head-to-head supports it. A single-blinded, randomized, controlled trial in males with mild to moderate [[acne]] had everyone wash twice daily for two weeks, then randomized them to wash once, twice, or four times a day for six weeks. Between the three groups there were no statistically significant differences overall, but the twice-a-day group showed significant improvement in open comedones and total noninflammatory lesions, while the once-a-day group got worse, with significant increases in redness, papules, and total inflammatory lesions.[^choi] The authors concluded there is "slight support" for washing twice daily with a mild cleanser, while noting that "excessive face washing may not be as culpable as previously thought."[^choi]

## How to wash

The AAD's practical guidance is to use "a gentle, non-abrasive cleanser that does not contain alcohol," wet the face with lukewarm water, and apply cleanser with your fingertips, because "using a washcloth, mesh sponge, or anything other than your fingertips can irritate your skin."[^aad] It also advises resisting the urge to scrub, rinsing with lukewarm water, patting dry with a soft towel, and applying [[moisturizing|moisturizer]] if skin is dry.[^aad]

## Choosing a cleanser

Frequency matters less than using a gentle formula that does not strip the skin.
