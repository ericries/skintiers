Medical ultraviolet treatment delivered in a clinic, for eczema that topical treatment has not controlled. **This is not a red light mask.** Phototherapy uses ultraviolet, most often narrowband UVB; the consumer devices at [[home-led-light-therapy]] use visible red and blue light and are a different technology for different claims. Conflating them is the single commonest error about this treatment. The condition is at [[atopic-dermatitis]].

> **Thirty-two trials, and the headline numbers come from about forty people.** A 2021 Cochrane review "included 32 trials with 1219 randomised participants, aged 5 to 83 years (mean: 28 years)".[^cochrane] That sounds like a solid literature until you read which trials carry the key comparisons: narrowband UVB against placebo at twelve weeks rests on "1 trial, 41 participants" for physician-assessed signs, "1 trial, 40 participants" for itch, and "1 trial, 41 participants" for global improvement.[^cochrane] The review judged "that all reported outcomes were supported with low-certainty" evidence.[^cochrane]

## What it is

Ultraviolet light delivered under medical supervision, usually in a cabin, over a course of weeks. The Cochrane review's included trials ran "on average, 13 weeks (range: 10 days to one year)", with participants "recruited mainly from secondary care dermatology clinics".[^cochrane] This is a referral treatment, not something bought.

Eleven distinct modalities appear across the 32 trials. Narrowband UVB dominates at 13 trials, then UVA1 at 6, broadband UVB at 5, UVAB and PUVA at 2 each, and single trials of UVA, unspecified UVB, full spectrum light, a Saalmann selective ultraviolet phototherapy cabin, saltwater bath plus UVB, and excimer laser.[^cochrane] Most of that list has been tested once.

## The Evidence

**Narrowband UVB probably helps, modestly, and the word is may.** Against placebo or no treatment at twelve weeks, the review reports "a larger reduction in physician-assessed signs with NB-UVB compared to placebo", a mean difference of "-9.4, 95% confidence interval (CI) -3.62 to -15.18" on a 0 to 90 scale.[^cochrane] More people reported reduced itch, at a risk ratio of "1.72, 95% CI 1.10 to 2.69", and more reached moderate or better global improvement, at "2.81, 95% CI 1.10 to 7.17".[^cochrane] The authors' own summary is that narrowband UVB "may improve physician-rated signs, patient-reported symptoms, and IGA after 12 weeks, without a difference in withdrawal due to adverse events".[^cochrane]

**The quality caveat is not a formality.** The review assessed risk of bias "for all key outcomes as having some concerns or high risk, due to missing data, inappropriate analysis, or insufficient information to assess selective reporting".[^cochrane] Comparisons between modalities fare worse still: evidence for UVA1 against narrowband UVB or PUVA, and for narrowband UVB against PUVA, was "very low certainty".[^cochrane]

**What nobody can tell you yet.** The review closes by saying "More information is needed on the safety and effectiveness of all aspects of phototherapy for treating AE".[^cochrane] Long-term safety is not addressed by a literature whose average trial runs thirteen weeks.

**No tier list on this page.** Ranking the eleven modalities would mean ranking nine of them off a single trial each, against head-to-head comparisons the review itself rates very low certainty.
