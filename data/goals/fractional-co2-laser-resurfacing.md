Fractional CO2 laser resurfacing is an in-office ablative procedure: a 10,600 nm carbon dioxide laser removes microscopic columns of skin, leaving untreated skin between them to speed healing.[^prp] It is the procedure the other acne-scar treatments on this site are usually measured against. The scars are on [[acne-scars]] and the disease behind them on [[acne]].

> **It wins on response and loses on tolerability, and only one of those findings is solid.** A 2026 meta-analysis comparing fractional CO2 with erbium-based lasers found erbium had a lower chance of better-than-50% improvement, "RR, 0.69; 95% CI, 0.49-0.97", which favours CO2.[^co2] But restrict that to randomised trials only and the advantage stops being significant, "RR, 0.76; 95% CI, 0.54-1.08".[^co2] The costs, by contrast, hold up: CO2 brought more pain, "MD, 1.86; 95% CI, 1.33-2.39", and "longer downtime (MD, 3.67 days; 95% CI, 1.90-5.44)".[^co2] **The benefit over erbium is uncertain; the extra few days of recovery are not.** And the whole comparison rests on "Five studies involving 130 analyzed participants".[^co2]

## The Evidence

**Read the sensitivity analysis, not the headline.** The primary result and the randomised-only result point the same way, but only the first excludes the null, and heterogeneity was moderate at "I²=45.9%".[^co2] When a significant finding loses significance as soon as non-randomised studies are dropped, the honest description is a signal rather than a settled difference. The authors say as much, concluding only that there is "a favorable clinical-response signal for fractional CO2 laser, accompanied by greater procedural pain and longer recovery".[^co2]

**On pigmentation the two lasers were indistinguishable, which is not the same as safe.** The meta-analysis found no significant difference between CO2 and erbium for post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, "OR, 1.75; 95% CI, 0.67-4.57", or for acneiform events, "OR, 0.73; 95% CI, 0.12-4.25".[^co2] Both intervals are wide and both cross 1, so this is an absence of a detected difference in 130 people, not a demonstration that either is gentle. Against needling the comparison is less flattering: a 2026 review of needling versus ablative fractional lasers concluded that "Ablative fractional lasers may offer greater efficacy, whereas needling-based therapies may provide better tolerability and pigment safety".[^needle] Its own caution is that the conclusions "should be interpreted cautiously because of clinical heterogeneity".[^needle]

**Where CO2 is clearly at its best is in combination.** A 2026 network meta-analysis of moderate-to-severe atrophic scarring found "Combination therapies generally outperformed monotherapies" and singled out that "Fractional CO2 laser combined with subcision or PRP showed the most consistent benefits".[^combo] A separate 2025 network meta-analysis of 68 randomised trials and 4,480 patients put "Laser + platelet-rich plasma (PRP)" first on the ECCA scar scale with a SUCRA of 98.4%.[^nma] The qualifier that matters is on [[platelet-rich-plasma-acne-scars]]: that same analysis ranked laser plus filler best on the other scar scale and laser plus chemical peels best on satisfaction, so the winning partner depends on the outcome measured.

**Do not confuse this with the home lasers.** A 2026 GRADE review of home-use devices rated "home-use fractional nonablative lasers" at "high-quality evidence (Level A)" for facial rejuvenation.[^home] That is a different wavelength class, a different power, a different setting and a different endpoint from in-office ablative fractional CO2 for scarring. A Level A grade for a home nonablative device says nothing about this procedure, and the home-device picture is on [[home-led-light-therapy]].

**What is still missing.** Across every source here the recurring complaint is the same: heterogeneous laser settings, inconsistent outcome definitions, and small samples. The CO2-versus-erbium question rests on 130 participants.[^co2] None of these reviews reports a durable long-term follow-up for CO2 specifically. Anyone quoting a precise percentage improvement for this procedure is quoting a single study, not a body of evidence.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not a substitute for a clinician. Ablative fractional CO2 resurfacing is a medical procedure with real downtime, performed under anaesthesia or numbing, and risk varies with skin tone in ways the trials above are too small to resolve.
