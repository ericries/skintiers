The only way to find out which ingredient is causing a reaction. Patch testing is a dermatology procedure, not a product or a home test, and this page exists because it is the real answer to the commonest unanswerable question in skincare. The conditions it diagnoses are at [[contact-dermatitis]].

> **It does not test for the thing most people think it tests for.** Patch testing looks for delayed allergic contact dermatitis. DermNet states it "is not useful in investigating irritant dermatitis", and separately that patch tests "are not the same as skin prick tests", which it says are the ones used to diagnose hay fever allergy.[^dermnet] If your skin stings on contact, that is usually irritation, and this test is the wrong instrument for it.

## What happens

Diluted allergens are taped to the back on discs, usually Finn chambers holding "between 10 and 12" allergens per strip, with "Anywhere between 30 and over a hundred allergens" applied at once.[^dermnet] The panels come off at 48 hours and the skin is inspected 48 hours after that, because the reactions "usually take 48-96 hours to develop".[^dermnet] That delay is the whole point: a type IV hypersensitivity reaction is slow, which is why nothing you can do at home in an afternoon substitutes for it.

Practicalities DermNet lists for the test to work: avoid potent topical steroids on the site for three days beforehand, do not wash the test area, and avoid heavy exercise that could dislodge the tapes. Antihistamines are fine and "do not interfere with the type IV hypersensitivity reaction."[^dermnet]

## What the result means

Reactions are graded negative, irritant, doubtful, weak, moderate or strong.[^dermnet] Two cautions matter more than the grade.

**A baseline series is not a complete answer.** DermNet puts its yield at "about 70% of contact dermatitis", with extra series added for occupation, site, suspected exposure and the patient's own products.[^dermnet] A negative result can mean the dermatitis is endogenous or irritant, or that the allergen was simply never tested.[^dermnet]

**A positive result is not automatically your answer either.** Positives are separately interpreted for current, past, future or uncertain relevance, and irritant reactions in people with atopic eczema, particularly to metals, perfumes and some rubber accelerators, "are not relevant."[^dermnet] Some allergens also stain the skin, which can be mistaken for a reaction.[^dermnet]

## Bringing your own products

DermNet advises bringing the suspect product and its packaging, since cosmetics and moisturisers can be tested directly at the correct dilution.[^dermnet] It also notes a stopgap while waiting: repeat open application testing, where the product goes on a 5 cm patch of forearm twice a day for 5 to 10 days, and no redness or swelling means contact allergy to it is unlikely.[^dermnet] Highly irritant products cannot be tested this way without dilution.[^dermnet]

Ingredients covered here that are documented contact allergens include [[coco-glucoside]], [[lanolin]] and [[propylene-glycol]]. Avoidance once an allergen is named is covered at [[contact-dermatitis]]; it is not restated here.
