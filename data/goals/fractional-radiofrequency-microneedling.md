The procedure that sits between [[microneedling]] and a laser: needles deliver radiofrequency energy into the dermis rather than only wounding it. **The short version:** the evidence is wide rather than deep, nobody has pooled an effect size, and the two most recent syntheses were both led from a device manufacturer's medical affairs department.

> **Who wrote the reviews matters here more than usual.** The 2026 systematic review covering 41 studies and the 2026 review of facial rejuvenation covering 22 share a first author whose affiliation is "Global Medical Affairs, Jeisys Medical Inc.", a company that manufactures radiofrequency microneedling devices.[^kumar41][^kumar22] The second states it plainly in its conflict statement, which also records that a co-author "is a speaker for Jeisys Medical Inc."[^kumar22] Neither review pools results; both synthesise narratively.[^kumar41][^kumar22]

## The Evidence

**Volume is not the problem.** The broader review included 41 studies, of which 15 were randomised controlled trials, the rest prospective non-randomised studies, cohorts and case series.[^kumar41] The facial review included 22 studies and synthesised 20 of them, covering 558 participants.[^kumar22] For an aesthetic procedure that is a substantial literature.

**The missing thing is a number.** Both reviews are narrative or thematic syntheses rather than meta-analyses, so neither produces a pooled effect size, a confidence interval or a between-group difference for any indication.[^kumar41][^kumar22] The broader review is labelled "LEVEL OF EVIDENCE III" by its own journal.[^kumar41] So a reader can learn that the direction is favourable and cannot learn how much.

**A manufacturer review reporting a loss is worth crediting.** In primary axillary hyperhidrosis, the broader review records that "botulinum toxin A outperformed RFMN for symptom reduction/comfort".[^kumar41] A synthesis written inside a device company naming an indication where its category loses to a competing treatment is a point in its favour.

**The device settings are not reported consistently.** The same review notes that "Reporting of technical parameters, especially temperature, pulse width, and cooling, was inconsistent", and calls for "rigorous reporting of device settings" to allow head-to-head comparison.[^kumar41] This is the same disclosure problem the site tracks for home light devices on [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]]: an energy treatment whose dose is not published cannot be reproduced or compared.

**Safety reads consistently well.** Both reviews describe adverse events as predominantly mild and transient, chiefly erythema and oedema, with post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation "infrequent and self-limited" and no serious complications reported in the facial set.[^kumar41][^kumar22]

**Is it separable from plain microneedling?** Not from these two reviews, which study the combined modality rather than isolating what the radiofrequency adds. That question is worked through for the one indication where the trials exist, on [[rf-microneedling-acne-scars]], and is not re-argued here.
