Erbium:YAG is the gentler of the two ablative resurfacing lasers, and the 2026 evidence says the gentleness is real and so is the cost in results. It runs at 2940 nm, the wavelength carried in the device names on its own FDA filings, against 10600 nm for carbon dioxide.[^fda] This page does not source the optical explanation for why the shorter wavelength ablates more shallowly, so it does not assert one; what the trials measure, less pain and a shorter recovery for a smaller gain, is below. The CO2 procedure itself is on [[fractional-co2-laser-resurfacing]] and the recovery period that follows either is on [[post-ablative-laser-aftercare]].

## The Evidence

For atrophic acne scars there is now a head-to-head meta-analysis, and it is small. Five comparative studies with 130 analysed participants, four of them contributing to the efficacy pool, harmonised on a single outcome of better-than-50% clinical response. Erbium-based lasers came out lower than fractional CO2 on that outcome, with a relative risk of 0.69 and a confidence interval of 0.49 to 0.97.[^meta] That interval only just excludes 1, and in the sensitivity analysis restricted to randomized studies it stopped excluding it: relative risk 0.76, confidence interval 0.54 to 1.08.[^meta] The direction of the finding is consistent. Its statistical strength depends on which studies you allow in.

What erbium buys is quantified in the same analysis. Fractional CO2 came with greater treatment-related pain, a mean difference of 1.86 points with a confidence interval of 1.33 to 2.39, and longer recovery, a mean difference of 3.67 days with a confidence interval of 1.90 to 5.44.[^meta] On the two complication outcomes the review could pool, neither favoured either laser: post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation gave an odds ratio of 1.75 with an interval of 0.67 to 4.57, and acneiform events an odds ratio of 0.73 with an interval of 0.12 to 4.25.[^meta] Those intervals are wide enough that the review cannot rule out a meaningful difference in either direction, which is not the same as showing the two lasers are equally safe.

A second and much larger 2026 review, 27 studies and 1137 participants across scar types, sets both ablative lasers against needling. It reports that ablative fractional lasers, chiefly CO2 and in some studies erbium systems, suggested greater improvement than microneedling, while needling carried shorter downtime, better tolerability and fewer pigmentary adverse effects.[^needling] Its authors synthesised narratively because of heterogeneity and warn that the exploratory pooled estimate was sensitive to individual studies, so that comparison is a direction of travel rather than an effect size.[^needling]

## What clearance does and does not tell you

Searching the federal device database for clearances whose device name contains "erbium" returns 21 records, 20 of them under a single product code, GEX, which the agency defines as a "Powered Laser Surgical Instrument" in the general and plastic surgery specialty.[^fda] That code is deliberately broad: its own definition spans general surgery, dermatology and aesthetics, podiatry, ear nose and throat, gynaecology and neurosurgery, and several of the 21 erbium records are dental lasers rather than skin devices.[^fda] A clearance in that code establishes equivalence to an existing surgical laser. It contains no finding about acne scars. The general point is on [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]].
