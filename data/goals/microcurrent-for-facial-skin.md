Microcurrent devices are sold on lift, contour and tone. Their FDA clearances say "stimulation". That gap is what this page is for.

**Seven microcurrent devices are covered here, and six of their clearances permit exactly one thing.** Read across them, the wording barely changes: facial stimulation, or facial and neck stimulation, or body skin stimulation, each "indicated for over-the-counter cosmetic use". None of the six names lifting, toning, firming or wrinkles as an outcome, even where the product's own NAME is a toning device. The seventh, [[foreo-luna-4-plus]], is the only one whose clearance names a wrinkle outcome, and that belongs to its red and near-infrared light mode rather than to its microcurrent.

## What the evidence says

Very little, and not what the category implies. Three PubMed searches on 2026-11-10 returned six records between them, and not one is a clean trial of facial microcurrent for a cosmetic skin outcome.[^searches] The nearest hit tests a clinic device, not a home one: Plasma IQ "generates microcurrent RF energy that produces controlled heating, leading to targeted tissue ablation and subsequent remodelling", reporting a 22.51% improvement in skin elasticity in thirty patients.[^plasmaiq] Controlled heating and tissue ablation are not what a handheld toning device does, so that result does not transfer. Another record in the same searches tests microcurrent for sinus pain.

So the honest position is an absence rather than a negative. Nothing found here shows facial microcurrent does not work; what is missing is a trial that tests it on its own terms.

## What this means when buying one

The clearance is the document a regulator reviewed, and on every device here it permits stimulation and nothing more. Where a box, a brand name or an advertisement promises lift or contour, that claim is the manufacturer's, not the clearance's, and the individual product pages record which is which.

Two practical notes the clearances and filings make plain. These devices are used with a separately sold conductive gel, covered at [[nuface-gel-primer]] and [[nuface-fix-serum-activator]], so there is a running cost beyond the device. And [[el-global-trade-deepskin-dep100]] states outright that no clinical testing was gathered for it, which is normal for this pathway and worth knowing before reading a clearance as evidence.

The broader question of what a clearance does and does not mean is on [[fda-approved-vs-cleared-skincare]]; the firmness goal itself is on [[skin-firmness-elasticity]].
