Blackheads and whiteheads are comedones, the non-inflammatory clogged pores of [[acne]]: an open comedone (a blackhead) is a plug whose surface is exposed to air, while a closed comedone (a whitehead) is sealed over. "Congestion" is an informal term for skin dotted with them. They are cleared and prevented with the same evidence-based topicals used for acne, led by topical [[retinoids]]; the American Academy of Dermatology's 2024 acne guideline makes a strong recommendation for topical retinoids and benzoyl peroxide, and a conditional one for salicylic acid and azelaic acid.[^aad]

> A blackhead is not trapped dirt: the dark color is oxidized keratin and oil at the surface of the pore, so scrubbing harder or "detoxing" does not clear it, and the tiny grey dots on the nose that come back within a day of a pore strip are usually normal sebaceous filaments rather than comedones. What actually reduces comedones over weeks is a topical retinoid, with salicylic acid or azelaic acid as options; squeezing and pore strips give a brief cosmetic result and can leave irritation, marks, or scarring instead.

## The Evidence

The graded actives above are ranked by how well the evidence supports them for comedones, using the AAD guideline's recommendation strengths.[^aad] A topical retinoid is the one treatment that both clears existing comedones and prevents new ones; the acids help, and benzoyl peroxide earns its strong overall rating mainly on inflammatory acne. Concentrations, formulations, and the trial detail are on each linked ingredient page.

## What does not clear them

Pore strips lift surface plugs and sebaceous filaments for a day, but they remove nothing that prevents comedones from re-forming and can irritate the skin; squeezing risks pushing the contents deeper and leaving a mark or scar. Charcoal or clay "detox" masks and harsher scrubbing do not address the reason comedones form, which is how the pore lining sheds and how much oil it holds, the process a [[retinoids|retinoid]] actually changes. Persistent or scarring acne is a reason to see a dermatologist rather than pick.
