Pregnancy-safe skincare means choosing actives with a reassuring safety profile during pregnancy, favoring ones with low systemic absorption or a long track record, over ones tied to birth-defect case reports or high absorption into the bloodstream. Direct evidence is thin because controlled trials in pregnant women are largely unethical to run, so most of this guidance is precautionary rather than proven.

> **The bottom line:** the safest defaults are gentle basics plus a sunscreen, mineral if you want the most conservative option. A short list of actives (topical retinoids, hydroquinone) has well-established reasons to avoid. Most of the rest is "probably fine, but the data is limited, so ask your clinician if you are unsure."

Pregnancy also commonly triggers or worsens [[melasma]] and [[stretch-marks]], both covered on their own pages.

## The Evidence

Randomized trials that deliberately expose pregnant women to a skincare active are considered unethical, so this field runs on absorption data, case reports, and comparisons to related drugs rather than the controlled trials used elsewhere on this site. The anchor source here is Bozzo, Chua-Gocheco, and Einarson's 2011 review in Canadian Family Physician, which tabulates how much of each common topical actually reaches the bloodstream and flags hydroquinone and topical retinoids as the two exceptions to an otherwise reassuring picture.[^bozzo] A 2023 narrative review by Ly and colleagues adds more recent, acne-focused evidence on azelaic acid, benzoyl peroxide, and salicylic acid,[^ly] and a 2017 review of cosmetic procedures during pregnancy corroborates the acid-peel picture.[^trivedi] Because so few actives have been studied this way, some common cosmetic ingredients, including niacinamide, vitamin C, and hyaluronic acid, are left off this tier list rather than assigned a rating those reviews do not support.

## Honest limits

This list only covers actives that already have a page on this site and that at least one of the reviews above discusses directly. Ordinary cosmetic ingredients without a documented absorption or outcome study, and ingredients this site does not yet cover, are not rated here, not because they are known to be risky, but because there is no source to grade them against. A "safe" or "caution" rating here reflects the state of a small evidence base, not a guarantee.

**Medical disclaimer.** This page is educational content, not medical advice, and it does not evaluate any individual's pregnancy or skin. Pregnancy risk depends on trimester, dose, area of application, and individual health history in ways a general page cannot capture. Talk with an OB-GYN or dermatologist before starting or stopping any skincare product during pregnancy, and do not use this page to delay or replace medical care.
