Ablative resurfacing leaves an open wound, and the question people actually ask is what to put on it. The procedure is on [[fractional-co2-laser-resurfacing]]; this page is only about the days afterwards. Nothing here replaces the instructions from whoever performed the procedure.

> **The most telling fact is which product the trials treat as the control.** In the post-laser aftercare literature, plain petroleum jelly or white petrolatum is what novel dressings and branded balms are measured against.[^dexpanthenol][^splitface][^film] That is what a standard of care looks like from the inside. The honest question is therefore not whether petrolatum works, but whether anything beats it by enough to matter.

## What the controlled trials actually found

**One ointment did beat petroleum jelly, and only early.** In 38 patients having fractional ablative CO2 resurfacing, the wound area was split so each person got a dexpanthenol-containing ointment on one section and petroleum jelly on another under occlusive care for 7 days.[^dexpanthenol] Lesion diameter shrank significantly faster on the dexpanthenol side on days 1 and 2, with significantly better re-epithelialization and cosmetic scores on days 1, 2 and 5.[^dexpanthenol] The sentence that keeps it in proportion is from the same abstract: "All patients exhibited a completed wound healing on day 14 after laser treatment."[^dexpanthenol] So the benefit is a faster first week, not a better endpoint. [[panthenol]] carries the ingredient case.

**The branded-balm trial is ten people and reports preference, not effect size.** A double-blinded split-face study randomised a plant-based hypoallergenic balm against Aquaphor Healing Ointment on opposite halves of 10 faces after fractionated CO2 resurfacing.[^splitface] Its results are given as the share of patients scoring better on one side, for example "improved erythema (50%), edema (50%), crusting (40%), and percentage healing (60%)" at day 4, and by day 14 "Crusting was the same between the two products".[^splitface] Ninety percent preferred the test product.[^splitface] No p-value appears in the abstract, and ten subjects cannot separate a real healing difference from how an ointment feels. [[aquaphor-healing-ointment]] is the comparator product.

**The film-dressing study is five volunteers and surrogate endpoints.** A split-face pilot compared a semipermeable film-forming dressing against spring thermal water plus Vaseline after full-face fractional CO2 resurfacing, in "Five female volunteers", measuring haemoglobin, surface temperature, texture, melanin and follicular porphyrins rather than healing time.[^film] Its own conclusion is hedged, that the study was "performed on a small number of volunteers".[^film] Treat it as a hypothesis.

## The Evidence

**The branded post-procedure cream category has a design problem on top of a size problem.** Two trials of such creams are covered here in detail and both share the same hole: no vehicle arm. [[monteil-2025-retinaldehyde-niacinamide-cream-post-procedure-rejuvenation-rct]] compared a three-active cream against an untreated half-face, so it cannot tell an active apart from the act of applying a moisturiser. [[karimi-2025-neurocosmetic-post-procedure-cream]] is worse and is logged specifically as unusable: it names neither product, reports no sample size and publishes no statistic of any kind. A page about aftercare has to say that the marketing in this category runs far ahead of the evidence.

**Timing matters more than product choice.** In the split-face study treated on [[monteil-2025-retinaldehyde-niacinamide-cream-post-procedure-rejuvenation-rct]], subjects began applying the test cream only after re-epidermization, not onto a fresh wound, which is that study's own design rather than a separate finding. An acid or a retinoid on skin that has not closed is a different proposition from one applied to healed skin, which is the distinction [[exfoliation]] and [[retinoids]] set out.

**Pigment is the risk that outlasts the healing.** Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation after ablative resurfacing is the complication that turns a good result into a long one, and it is why photoprotection is part of every aftercare instruction set. The evidence for what reduces established pigment is on [[hyperpigmentation]] and the filter comparison is on [[sunscreen-uv-filters]]; no post-ablative sunscreen trial was read for this page, so nothing here grades one.

**What was deliberately not graded.** [[centella-asiatica]] and [[colloidal-oatmeal]] are both used in post-procedure products and neither has a controlled post-ablative trial read for this page, so they are recorded as considered rather than ranked. [[cerave-healing-ointment]] is a petrolatum-based ointment whose own page carries its composition; no post-laser trial tested it specifically.

**Medical note.** This is educational content, not medical advice. Ablative resurfacing is a medical procedure with a real infection risk during healing, and signs of infection, spreading pain or a wound that is not closing are reasons to contact the treating clinician rather than to change ointment. Follow the aftercare protocol you were given.
