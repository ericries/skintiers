The Ordinary is the flagship skincare brand of DECIEM Beauty Group, a Toronto-based multi-brand company that The Estée Lauder Companies fully acquired in 2024.[^1][^2][^3]

## Ownership and history

DECIEM, which markets itself as "The Abnormal Beauty Company," was, in the words of Estée Lauder's own announcement, "founded in 2013 by the late Brandon Truaxe," alongside "Founder, Pasquale Cusano and Co-Founder, Nicola Kilner"; Kilner now leads the company as CEO.[^1]

The Estée Lauder Companies Inc. (NYSE: EL, "ELC") bought into DECIEM in three stages. ELC dates its "initial investment in June 2017," which left it holding about 29% of the company.[^1] On February 23, 2021, ELC announced an agreement to increase its stake "from approximately 29% to approximately 76%," a transaction it valued at "approximately USD $1.0 billion" at closing and put at "a total enterprise value of approximately USD $2.2 billion" for that phase; it reported DECIEM's net sales for the 12 months ended January 31, 2021 as "approximately USD $460 million."[^1] The company's Form 10-K records that this majority purchase closed on May 18, 2021.[^3]

The same 2021 agreement set terms to buy the rest of the company after three years. On June 3, 2024, ELC announced it had "completed its acquisition" of DECIEM, having "closed on the purchase of the remaining interests on May 31, 2024 using cash on hand for an estimated $860 million," and stated that "the total investment, net of cash, approximated $1.7 billion over the three tranches."[^2] The Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended June 30, 2024 states the same: "On May 31, 2024, we purchased the remaining interest in the Deciem Beauty Group Inc.," after "increasing our investment in 2021 to 76%."[^3]

## Positioning and technology

- The brand's own tagline is "Clinical Formulations with Integrity."[^4] Estée Lauder describes DECIEM's "dedication to ingredient transparency" and characterizes it as "a Toronto-based, vertically integrated multi-brand beauty company rooted in a consumer-focused and functional approach."[^2][^3]
- Ingredient-led range. ELC's Form 10-K describes The Ordinary as "an ingredient-focused brand."[^3] Consistent with that, the brand organizes its own catalog by single active (retinoids, vitamin C, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, peptides, copper peptides, salicylic acid, squalane) as well as by routine step.[^4]
- Scale and market position. ELC reports that during the quarter ended March 31, 2024 The Ordinary "ranked top two in prestige skin care" in its home markets of Canada and the United States, and places it in a tier of brands "each with net sales between $500 million and $1 billion."[^2]

The brand does not attach a proprietary delivery-technology name to its formulas in the way some competitors do; its positioning rests on named actives at stated concentrations.

## Portfolio

Estée Lauder's Form 10-K names DECIEM's portfolio as including "The Ordinary, an ingredient-focused brand, and NIOD, a science-driven skin care brand."[^3] The 2021 announcement said DECIEM's portfolio then "encompasses six brands."[^1] The brand's own site currently lists The Ordinary alongside sister brands NIOD and LOoPHA.[^4] The Ordinary's range spans facial cleansers, toners and essences, serums, face oils, moisturizers, exfoliators, eye care, and suncare.[^4]
