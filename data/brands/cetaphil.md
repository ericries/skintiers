Cetaphil is a mass-market brand of fragrance-free cleansers and moisturizers positioned for sensitive, dry, and reactive skin, sold in more than 70 countries and currently owned by the Swiss dermatology company Galderma.[^1][^4][^6]

## Origin

Cetaphil traces its origin to 1947. Galderma's own account of the brand's anniversary states it was "invented in 1947 by a pioneering pharmacist in Texas," and that its first product, "Cetaphil Cleansing Lotion," known today as [[cetaphil-gentle-skin-cleanser|Cetaphil Gentle Skin Cleanser]], "still uses the same formula."[^2] Cetaphil's own current site gives the same account: "From humble Texan roots, Cetaphil was designed by a pharmacist to help patients more effectively treat their sensitive skin."[^1] Neither source names the pharmacist or the city. Galderma's own corporate history separately lists "the invention of the first Cetaphil formulation" in 1947 as a landmark predating Galderma's own 1981 founding.[^3]

## Ownership and history

Cetaphil is a brand of Galderma Group AG, a Swiss company that describes itself as "the leading company solely dedicated to the human body's largest organ: the skin," operating across injectable aesthetics, dermatological skincare, and therapeutic dermatology.[^3][^4] Galderma's own corporate history dates its founding to 1981, as a joint venture between L'Oreal and Nestle.[^3]

Nestle became Galderma's sole owner in 2014, completing the purchase of L'Oreal's remaining 50% stake on July 8 of that year; Galderma, with about 5,000 employees in 80 countries, then operated as "the pharmaceutical arm of Nestle Skin Health S.A."[^5] Nestle later sold Galderma to an investor consortium: on October 2, 2019, Galderma announced the completion of a CHF 10.2 billion carve-out from Nestle Skin Health led by EQT and the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority, becoming, in its own words, "the world's largest independent global dermatology company."[^6] Galderma went public on the SIX Swiss Exchange in 2024.[^3] L'Oreal, Galderma's former part-owner, has since bought back into the company, taking an initial stake in August 2024 and announcing on December 8, 2025 that it would acquire an additional 10%, bringing its total holding to 20%, a transaction Galderma said was expected to close in the first quarter of 2026.[^7]

## Positioning

The statements in this section are the brand's own positioning claims. They are reproduced here with attribution and are not independently established findings; the product-level evidence for any specific Cetaphil product is assessed on that product's page.

Galderma's own Cetaphil brand page states that "CETAPHIL is a gentle skincare brand that has been recommended by dermatologists for over 70 years."[^4] Independent of the brand's own marketing, board-certified dermatologists interviewed by TODAY (NBC) describe Cetaphil as "gentle and dermatologist designed" and among "the most commonly recommended skin care brands by dermatologists."[^8]

Cetaphil's own site describes its current range as spanning "cleansers, moisturizers, sun protection to baby products and solutions for sensitive skin conditions such as acne, eczema and rosacea."[^1] This page does not catalog individual SKUs; the Cetaphil products profiled here are listed below.
