CeraVe is a United States skincare brand whose products are built around ceramides, and it has been owned by the French cosmetics company L'Oreal since 2017.[^1][^2][^3]

## Ownership and history

On January 10, 2017, Valeant Pharmaceuticals International announced that it had "entered into an agreement to sell its CeraVe, AcneFree and AMBI skincare brands to L'Oréal for $1.3 billion in cash." Valeant put the three brands' combined annualized revenue at "approximately of $168 million" and said the transaction was "expected to close in the first quarter of 2017."[^1] Valeant's 2017 annual report records the completion: "In March 2017, we completed the sale of the CeraVe, AcneFree and AMBI skincare brands to a global beauty company for $1,300 million in cash."[^2] L'Oreal's 2017 annual report lists CeraVe among that year's acquisitions and places it in the company's Active Cosmetics Division.[^3]

The seller, Valeant Pharmaceuticals International, later renamed itself Bausch Health Companies. Both names refer to the same US securities registrant, filed with the SEC under CIK 0000885590; the "Valeant Pharmaceuticals International, Inc." name was in use from August 2010 to July 2018, after which the registrant reports as "Bausch Health Companies Inc." (ticker BHC).[^4]

The $1.3 billion was the price for all three brands together (CeraVe, AcneFree, and AMBI), not for CeraVe alone.[^1][^2]

## Positioning and technology

The statements in this section are the brand's own positioning and technology claims. They are reproduced here with attribution and are not independently established findings; the product-level evidence for any specific CeraVe product is assessed on that product's page.

CeraVe states that "all of our products are developed alongside dermatologists and feature three skin-identical ceramides, plus MVE technology, to help protect against moisture loss, hydrate the skin, and restore the skin's natural protective barrier."[^5]

- "Developed with dermatologists." The brand presents this as part of how its products are made.[^5] The seller's 2017 filing used the same phrase, describing CeraVe as "developed with dermatologists."[^1] It is a statement about the brand's development process, not a measured clinical outcome.
- Ceramide focus. The brand markets its products as containing three ceramides.[^5] Whether a ceramide-containing moisturizer performs better than a plainer emollient is a product-level question. The cited evidence for its ceramide moisturizer is on [[cerave-moisturizing-cream]]; the general ingredient background is on [[ceramides]].
- "MVE technology." The brand's term for a multivesicular-emulsion delivery system it says releases ingredients over time.[^5] This is a claim about the product vehicle; the [[cerave-moisturizing-cream]] page notes that no independent trial reports its clinical effect.

## Portfolio

Valeant's 2017 filing described the CeraVe range as "a range of advanced skincare products, including cleansers, moisturizers, sunscreens, healing ointments and a dedicated baby line."[^1] The brand's own site adds facial serums, including a retinol line, and acne treatments to the current range.[^5] This page does not catalog individual SKUs; the CeraVe products profiled here are listed below.
