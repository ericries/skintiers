La Roche-Posay is a French dermatological skincare brand built around the thermal spring water of the town of La Roche-Posay, and it has been part of the French cosmetics company L'Oreal since 1989.[^1][^2]

## Ownership and history

L'Oreal's history of acquisitions dates the brand's entry to the group as "1989 • La Roche-Posay."[^1] The town's thermal spa was a separate, later purchase: L'Oreal's list of key acquisitions records that in 2018 it added the "Thermes de la Roche-Posay,"[^1] which its 2024 annual report describes as the "Acquisition of the thermal centre La Societe des Thermes de La Roche-Posay."[^2] The spa company appears in L'Oreal's 2024 register of consolidated entities as "CENTRE THERMAL DE LA ROCHE-POSAY," France, held 100.00%, alongside the brand's operating company "LA ROCHE-POSAY LABORATOIRE DERMATOLOGIQUE."[^1]

Within L'Oreal, La Roche-Posay sits in the Dermatological Beauty Division, whose portfolio L'Oreal lists as "La Roche-Posay, CeraVe, Vichy, SkinCeuticals, Skinbetter Science, etc." and whose stated goal is "to provide sustainable and life-changing dermatological solutions."[^1] The same division holds [[cerave]], which L'Oreal bought in 2017.[^1][^2] In L'Oreal's 2024 accounts, La Roche-Posay is a separate cash-generating unit carrying 169.6 million euros of goodwill; the Dermatological Beauty Division's goodwill totals 1,868.5 million euros.[^1]

## Positioning and technology

The statements in this section are L'Oreal's own corporate and marketing claims about the brand, attributed to the company.

- **Dermatological positioning and scale.** L'Oreal reports that in 2024 La Roche-Posay "has become the world's third largest skincare brand across all channels," driven by contributions from Europe and North America.[^1] In the United States, L'Oreal's 2024 annual report states, "La Roche-Posay is the biggest contributor to total dermatological market growth," a figure it attributes to its own internal market consolidation.[^2]
- **Thermal spring water.** The brand's signature ingredient is La Roche-Posay thermal spring water, which it markets as a prebiotic thermal water used as the aqueous base of many of its formulas. That positioning, and the barrier and microbiome claims attached to it, are documented for a specific product on [[la-roche-posay-toleriane]], the brand's fragrance-free ceramide and niacinamide moisturizer.
- **Mela B3 and the Melasyl molecule.** L'Oreal's 2024 annual report says the Mela B3 range, "launched by La Roche-Posay in 40 countries" and featuring the Melasyl molecule, "matches the efficacy of dermatological gold standard in hyperpigmentation."[^2] Its 2024 registration document describes Melasyl as "the result of 18 years of research in the Group's laboratories" and states it is "Validated by more than 121 scientific studies" and "has proved effective in treating localised pigmentation problems for all skin colours."[^1] These are the company's own claims; the evidence for any specific La Roche-Posay product is assessed on that product's page.

## Portfolio

L'Oreal groups La Roche-Posay's ranges under dermatological skincare, spanning sunscreens, sensitive-skin and barrier moisturizers, and treatment ranges such as the Mela B3 line for localised hyperpigmentation.[^1][^2] This page does not catalog individual products; the La Roche-Posay products profiled here are listed below.
