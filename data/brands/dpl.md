dpl is one of two device lines sold by LED Technologies, Inc., the other being [[revive-light-therapy]]; both sit under the same single applicant across all 15 of the firm's clearances.[^openfda] The applicant address on those filings moves over time, from Boulder and Coralville to Greenwood Village, Colorado on the 2017 to 2019 filings that cover the dpl devices here, and Largo, Florida on the four most recent.[^openfda] No founder is named here: the only individuals in the company's FDA filings are a compliance manager and a specification contact, which does not establish who founded it.

No brand claims are quoted on this page. ledtechnologies.com returned a Cloudflare challenge rather than a page on both 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-21, so no brand copy, price or photograph could be read for any dpl product.

The FDA record is the usable source instead. LED Technologies holds 15 device clearances, every one filed as a 510(k) Summary rather than a Statement, so the testing description is public in each case.[^openfda] Eight carry the dpl name: DPL Therapy System, DPL Nuve, Nuve for Wrinkles, dpl II Panel, dpl SpectraLite, dpl IIa Panel, dpl SpotLite and dpl Faceware.[^openfda]

## Products reviewed here

- [[dpl-spotlite]]: a blue and red LED acne device whose only human study is a 16-person usability test.
- [[dpl-faceware]]: a five-wavelength face device cleared against three of the firm's own earlier products.
- [[dpl-nuve]]: a 2010 clearance whose Summary names no irradiance, treatment area or wavelength heading at all.
- [[dpl-ii-panel]]: a 2017 full-face panel cleared for wrinkles, and one of only two filings from this firm the site has read that publish an actual irradiance figure.

What the firm publishes against its rivals is ranked on [[home-led-devices-by-what-the-file-publishes]].
