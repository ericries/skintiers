SkinCeuticals is an American professional skincare brand built around antioxidant serums, distributed mainly through dermatology offices, plastic surgeons, and medical spas; the French cosmetics company L'Oreal has owned it since 2005.[^1][^2]

## Ownership and history

L'Oreal announced on May 18, 2005 that it had signed an agreement to acquire SkinCeuticals, calling it "a leading professional skin care brand in the USA." The announcement dated the company's founding to 1994 and its headquarters to Dallas, Texas, described it as a privately held company that sold "to dermatologists, plastic surgeons, and high end spas," and put its 2004 sales at $35 million. It placed the acquired brand in L'Oreal's Active Cosmetics Division.[^1] SkinCeuticals' own current corporate history instead dates "the company's founding" to 1997, the same year it launched its first product, Serum 10.[^2][^7]

The company's research foundation is credited to Sheldon Pinnell, whom L'Oreal's own SkinCeuticals corporate page names as the brand's "founding scientist."[^2] Pinnell held the title of J. Lamar Callaway Professor Emeritus of Dermatology and Chief Emeritus of the Division of Dermatology at Duke University, where he returned in 1973 as an associate professor and served as Chief of Dermatology for 15 years before retiring in 2010; he died in Durham, North Carolina, on July 4, 2013, at age 76.[^3] A tribute in the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology independently dates his life 1937 to 2013 and describes him as "a visionary pioneer in cosmeceutical research."[^4] Pinnell's laboratory published the pH and concentration parameters that govern how L-ascorbic acid penetrates skin, the research the brand's flagship [[skinceuticals-c-e-ferulic|C E Ferulic]] serum is built on.[^5] Two other people, Alden Pinnell and Russell Moon, are credited as SkinCeuticals' co-founders in both L'Oreal's 2005 acquisition announcement and a 2013 tribute to Sheldon Pinnell issued by the company.[^1][^3]

By 2024, L'Oreal reports SkinCeuticals inside its Dermatological Beauty Division, whose stated goal is "to provide sustainable and life-changing dermatological solutions," alongside [[la-roche-posay]], [[cerave]], Vichy, and Skinbetter Science.[^6] In L'Oreal's 2024 accounts, SkinCeuticals is grouped with Skinbetter Science, also L'Oreal-owned, as a single cash-generating unit, which carried 794.6 million euros of goodwill at the end of 2024, up from 727.9 million euros a year earlier; the Dermatological Beauty Division's total goodwill was 1,968.1 million euros at the end of 2024, up from 1,868.5 million euros a year earlier.[^6]

## Positioning

The brand's own current site states its products are "dispensed by 9,000+ international dermatologists, plastic surgeons, and medi-spas," the same professional channel L'Oreal described at the 2005 acquisition.[^2][^1] Its products are also sold through select retailers such as Dermstore and Amazon alongside the brand's own site, as listed on [[skinceuticals-c-e-ferulic]]. That product page sets out the formula's studies, its patent, and its reviewer reputation, together with the recurring price critique that cheaper serums use the same actives and names lower-cost alternatives such as [[maelove-the-glow-maker]].

## Products reviewed here

- [[skinceuticals-c-e-ferulic]]: the brand's flagship morning antioxidant serum, a fixed combination of 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% vitamin E, and 0.5% ferulic acid, introduced in 2005.[^7]
