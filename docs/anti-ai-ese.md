# Anti-AI-ese style guide (write like a person, not a language model)

Based on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing." Automated by `scripts/sk style <slug>`. Clear it
before publishing. Warnings are advisory, but treat a clean `sk style` as the target.

## Punctuation and formatting to avoid
- **Em dashes (`—`) and en dashes (`–`) used as sentence punctuation.** This is the single
  biggest tell. Use a period, comma, colon, parentheses, or restructure the sentence. (Hyphens
  in ranges and compound words are fine.)
- **Curly/smart quotes and apostrophes** (' ' " "). Use straight ASCII quotes.
- **Bold used to hammer emphasis** on scattered words. Bold sparingly.
- **Rule of three:** "adjective, adjective, and adjective" triads where two words (or none) do
  the job.
- Emoji as list markers; Title Case In Headings; horizontal rules before headings.

## Stock words and phrases to avoid (LLM vocabulary)
delve, tapestry, testament (to), underscore(s)/underscoring, boasts (meaning "has"), meticulous,
pivotal, robust, showcase(s)/showcasing, "plays a crucial/vital/key/pivotal role", "stands as",
"serves as", "it is worth noting", "it is important to note", "in the realm of", "in today's ...",
"ever-evolving", "navigating the", "game-changer", "seamless", "leverage", "harness", "unlock",
"elevate", "myriad", "plethora", "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art", "rich (tapestry/history)",
"vibrant", "renowned", "nestled", "in the heart of", "a diverse array", "furthermore/moreover"
as filler, "at the end of the day", "in conclusion", "overall,".

## Constructions to avoid
- **Negative parallelism:** "not just X, but Y", "it's not X, it's Y", "not only ... but also".
- **Significance inflation:** "underscores its importance", "a testament to", "marks a turning
  point", "reflects a broader trend".
- **Vague attribution:** "experts say", "some critics argue", "studies show" without a specific
  citation. Name the source.
- **Present-participle padding** that editorializes a fact: "..., highlighting its role in ...".
- **Promotional tone / superlatives:** "the best", "revolutionary", "gold standard" (unless a
  named source uses the term and you attribute it).

## SkinTiers substance rules (why we care, beyond style)
- **Inform, do not instruct.** State what the evidence shows. Do not tell the reader what to do.
  No "pick it", "skip it", "don't be fooled", "don't overpay", "you should". The reader decides.
- **No uncited editorial or contestable claim.** Every substantive statement is backed by a
  named authority or citation. "Oversold" / "not worth it" / "the best" are opinions; instead
  report the cited fact ("the RCT found no difference in symptom scores between the ceramide
  cream and vehicle [cite]") and let the reader conclude. This also limits our liability.
- **Attribute, quote, cite.** Prefer "the [source] reports X [^n]" over asserting X in our voice.
- **Plain and neutral.** Short sentences. Concrete nouns and verbs. No hype, no defensiveness,
  no throat-clearing.

## Quick before/after
- AI-ese: "CeraVe boasts three essential ceramides, underscoring its commitment to barrier
  repair, but don't be fooled, it's not a game-changer."
- SkinTiers: "CeraVe Moisturizing Cream contains three ceramides. A randomized trial of a
  ceramide cream found it improved a barrier measure (TEWL) but did not reduce eczema severity
  more than a vehicle [^n]."
